Ur the Ascended & the Sol Blade

The Sol Blade is a legendary weapon once wielded by Ur
the Ascended, a mythical holy knight of the Azerai faith. In
the legends, Ur was a great warrior and pious follower of the
faith who transcended humanity through his valorous acts
and holy deeds.
   Ur’s sword, the Sol Blade, was said to radiate with the
brilliant energy of the sun and hold the power to vanquish
demons, fiends, and other nightmarish entities. The myths
are commonly known among the citizens of the realm as the
Azerai faith is integral to everyday life. However, whether the
stories of Ur’s deeds and weapon are meant to be taken
figuratively or literally remains to be seen.

CHAPTER 3:
THE CURSED KEEP

Ascending from the dungeons, the party will access the upper levels of Calden Keep, taking part in the ancient feud
between Lord Æthelric and the peasant revolutionaries.
  As they search for the Sol Blade and find a way to escape, the party will be presented with difficult choices. Do they side
with the Undying Lord and seek his favor by slaying their friend, the Nameless Prisoner? Or do they sacrifice the princess
Aislinn to activate the Sol blade and kill the Undying Lord?
  Keep in mind the ways in which the party may escape, as the party will be working towards this goal throughout their
journey.


Escaping Calden
The Fog Cloud is a magical embodiment of the powers of Æthelric the Undying, Lord of Calden. It surrounds the Keep
forming barrier between the real world and the shadow realm dominated by the Undying Lord. There are only two ways the
party may escape once inside the Keep.


Killing Lord Æthelric

If the party kills Lord Æthelric, his powers will be dispelled and the fog clouds will dissipate. Lord Æthelric and Morwen are
a challenging fight and the players will likely need to activate the Sol Blade to vanquish them.
  The Sol Blade is dormant at this time. To activate it, the party must do the unthinkable, sacrificing a pure and innocent
being, the princess Aislinn. However, killing Lord Æthelric will dispel Calden’s curse.


Gaining Æthelric’s Favor

Alternatively, the party may escape by gaining Lord Æthelric’s favor. However, this will not be an easy task either.
  First, they must not attack any of the royal family members. Morwen, the Shadow of Calden, stands outside of the throne
room barring entry. Before granting an audience with Æthelric, she will task the party with slaying the Resurrected Goliath,
a monstrous amalgamation of metal armor and desecrated souls.
                                 After defeating the Goliath, Lord Æthelric will demand the head of the Nameless Prisoner,
                                   their friend during their adventures. If they complete all of these tasks,
                                     they will be allowed to leave.

APPENDIX A:
FACTION OVERVIEW



       Though contemporary texts seek to portray Doaden's past as a golden age of
       enlightenment and bounty, one needs only to look at the ruins and remains for proof our
       forebears were not free of the soul-rot that pervades us today.

         In the west of the realm lies the keep named Calden, though I was unable to find any
       instance of the place or its name in records and archives. The locals I interviewed do not
       go there, and what little information I could garner was that the place is cursed. After
       months searching for answers, one old crone living alone on the outskirts of a nameless
       village regaled me of a tale told by her own grandmother.

         A tale of cruel lords and knights, who heaped such brutality upon their vassals that the
       peasant folk could take no more, and rose up in revolt to dethrone their cruel lord. I must
       say, good reader, that I do not usually place much faith in such tales, for I know it is
       common for such legends to exist amongst the peasant folk as a palliative for their sad
       lots in life.

         Though I must admit there is something about Calden Keep, it sits heavy on the hillside
       as I write, like some dark tumour on the hillside. That crone's tale has stuck with me, and
       one thing I have grown certain of in my travels is that whether it be times of bounty or
       famine, mankind has always been capable of unparalleled cruelty.

                                                                   - Dreyen Solius, Journal of the World’s Ending.




 Though Calden may have fallen unknown ages ago, it is still a technological marvel, preserved in the mists of the western
mountains to this day. The Keep’s great ramparts and halls serve as a testament to Doaden’s more prosperous and
enlightened past.
  Though the Undying Lord and his servants may have fallen, revenant soldiers continue to rise among the dead and peter
the living. For a more sinister evil works its way through the earthen depths below the Keep, in the dwellings of the dark
Judge.
 The souls of those in Calden are sworn for eternity, and maybe brought back to continue their torments at the dark entity’s
whim.
  Behind Calden’s wandering armies is the ever pressing and undying duty to subjugate the common folk of the realm to
their lord’s will. Though more meaningful in their life, the revenant soldiers of Calden now tend towards slaughtering all that
cross their paths. Slowly, they forget themselves and their memories, driven now by a seething hatred, desperate anger,
and unquenchable thirst for violence.

Passage from The Lonely Path

This passage is from the historical study of Ur’s life titled The Lonely Path, which may be found in the Histories section of
the library, area E6.




        In the many variations of the tales of Ur that are told throughout the lands, we see that beliefs
      about the holy knight’s life are as inconsistent as they are prolific. Barring those relatively
      inconsequential incongruities between stories, such as whether his sword shone with a solar
      brightness, or whether it erupted with a great flame, or even his wife’s name as Marya or Marithe,
      there are some significant differences in information that radically alter the meaning and purpose of
      his story.


        In analyzing the few remaining fragments of Ur’s writings, we see a vastly different expression of
      his life than those commonly told. Though the tales of his valor and greatness have inspired so
      many over the ages, it must be asked whether he would still be regarded as the hero he is known
      as today, had the true nature and consequences of his actions been commonly known.


        It does appear true that Ur’s sword possessed great magical powers. It also appears true that he
      rid the world of the physical manifestation of a great demon. But Ur himself viewed his life not as
      an example of saintly piety, but with deep pain, remorse, and conflict. In the surviving transcriptions
      written during his waning years, we see nothing but sorrow. Ur speaks only of the burdens of power
      and the prices paid by the pious.


        Ur writes of horrendous deeds, questioning his sanity and the principles of morality. He makes
      mention of an indomitable demon which grasped the lands, and writes too of the magical blade
      given to him by an otherworldly entity. But most significantly, he writes in pained prose of the
      sacrifices demanded of him to ignite its blade. He torments himself, reliving the act of plunging the
      blade through his wife’s heart, a being of superlative purity, the fairest of our kind and gentle to all.


        Here, we see Ur’s writings diverge greatly from the tales. They indicate not that he saved his love
      by ending the reign of the demon prince, but that he sacrificed her so that he might ascend to that
      great power. True, the sacrifice of innocence may be justified in the fight against evil. But as his
      writing makes evident, the morality of an act does little to assuage the guilt of those who have
      committed an abominable act that the greater collective requires of us.


      Of interest too is the name of the sword. Colloquially called the Sol Blade, it is a common
      assumption that the sword derives its name from the radiance of the sun, with which the blade is
      said to share a likeness. However, considering the testimony of Ur himself, this blade may draw its
      name from the unthinkable act necessary to gain its power - the sacrifice of a pure soul, from which
      it draws its energy.

Æthelric the Undying,
Lord of Calden
  The Lord of Calden is a twisted beast, his
monstrous form an expression of the great
powers that have been vested within him
by his covenants with dark entities. The
Undying Lord rules from his throne,
dominating the shadow realm of Calden
Keep with an iron will.
   He serves both as its lord and captive,
trapped forever in a conflict with the souls
of the tormented rebels who sought to
overthrow him. He now rules for all eternity
within a half-reality, trapped in a realm
between the physical and spirit, cursed to
live a never-ending cycle of paranoia,
hatred, and vengeance.

                                                                                  74
Courtiers
  Not all the denizens of Calden were warriors, creeping
sycophants and jealous relatives haunted the shadows of
the Lord’s Keep in numbers nearly as great as the soldiery,
seeking some boon or opportunity to climb higher in the
Lords regard than their compatriots. When the rebellion
came, they too fell to the clubs and blades of the peasant
folk, though instead of finding death at the hands of their
desecrators, the Courtiers of Calden met their ends by their
own means, drinking deep of the opiate draughts more often
used in moderation to soothe the humours.
  Risen again, these courtiers stalk the halls of Calden, still
whispering their poison and seeking opportunities to exceed
their fellows and perhaps, if they dare to wish, supplant the
Lord himself.

Dark Acolytes
The Dark Acolytes walk the halls of Calden in service to the
Undying Lord. These masked sorcerers conduct the Lord's
dark rituals, binding him to the dark entities which have
granted his power and fulfilling his forbidden covenants.

The Executioner was a cruel servant of the Lord of Calden, beheading the multitude of political enemies and common folk
who were misfortunate enough to fall under the Lord's paranoid suspicions. The Executioner relished in his work, executing
those condemned with swift efficiency, numbering thirty or more per day in his zenith.
  Otherwise faced with the unpleasant alternative of indefinite torture within the Lord's dungeons, execution was often the
preferred punishment in Calden
  .

Golgotha
Deep underground, far below Calden Keep
and cloaked behind a veil of magical
darkness, lies the magnificent city of a long-
extinct peoples. Within the great spires of this
city, the Golgotha stalks, a shambling
construct animated by arcane methods long
forgotten, protecting this wondrous tomb-city
from any who would be foolish enough to
enter there.

Morwen, the Shadow of Calden
While Calden's court was not exactly subtle in its
cruelties, it was still subject to the same intrigues
and machinations amongst the Court Hierarchy.
Those snide Dukes and grasping Duchesses who
overreached in their plans would find themselves
visited in the dead of night, by the Shadow of
Calden, the Lord's Deadly Consort. She too has
arisen with the rest of her dark compatriots to
serve at the whims of her Lord.




                                                        79
Phalanx
Lithe and Powerful Warriors, bearing great pavise shields,
the Phalanx patrol the corridors of Calden Keep with
unnatural vigor. They march in lockstep throughout the halls
of the Keep watching for any who would seek an unplanned
audience with their Lord.
  On those rare occasions that they find some soul foolish
enough to enter the Keep, they quickly form an impenetrable
shield wall, spanning the width of the hall. With defense set,
they march forward inexorably, stabbing out their spears
with pinpoint precision.

                                                                                  82
Torment Collective
  For all his cruelty, the Old Torturer of Calden Keep was an
ingenious and creative soul. Though his canvas was the
pain and suffering of the weak, he crafted such wonders that
were never before seen, nor rivaled since.
  The Torment Collective is the pinnacle of his art, a great
construct of pain engines and victims, mounted upon spikes
and encased within Iron Maidens. Their shared anguish
drives the machine with a foul animus, and it stalks
the deeper parts of Calden’s dungeon,
lashing out wildly with the snap reactions of a soul
in a state of heightened and perpetual pain.




                                                                83
Tormented Souls
  Many were the victims of the Lord's Torturer. Driven mad
by pain before slow, miserable death, their fates too made a
strong imprint on the soul of Calden. With the Castle's
reawakening, these poor souls have returned, constant
reminders of the terrible past and an ever-present threat to
any foolish enough to venture within Calden's walls.
   Perhaps more terrifying, it is clear amongst the Tormented
that some are not risen victims of ancient cruelty, but the still
living, perhaps those stolen away from the ruined villages
and farmsteads that neighbor the shadowed foothills at the
base of Calden. Those poor souls not killed in the initial
attack suffer a worse fate. Handed to the Lord's Torturer,
they are broken in both body and mind, an example to the
descendants of the insurrectionists that Calden will take its
due revenge.




                                                                    84
Torturer
  The torturer of Calden was a much feared
individual, the cruelties he visited upon the Lord's
subjects were nightmarishly creative. During life
many met a slow and painful end under his twisted
ministrations. Despite receiving a violent end under
his own blades, the Lord's pain-maker has returned
to visit pain upon of the folk of the realm once again.

Torturer’s Assistant
Many were the enemies of the Lord of Calden, whether they
were true rebels or simply folk who committed some
perceived slight on the Lord. As such, the dungeons under
Calden were full to bursting, and the Torturer alone was not
enough to visit pain upon the incarcerated masses.




                                                               86
Shadow Monk
  Across Doaden there exist myriad religious orders and
sects. All generally stem from the tenets of the Azerai
Church, albeit warped and altered to match the
considerations and cultures of the lands in which they are
based.
  One commonality is that of the mendicant and itinerant
monk, religious individuals who dedicate their lives to the
promulgation of the faith, traveling from village to village
proselytizing the holy word to those that would hear it.
  As is well established at this point, traveling across
Doaden is not a safe prospect, and as such, it is common
for these traveling holy men to possess a surprisingly degree
of martial skill.
  This monk became lost during his long forgotten quest.
Now a Shadow, he stalks the halls of Calden Keep, lost in
an endless rage.




                                                                87
Shadow Warlock
  Consorting with demons is unanimously vilified across Doaden, but that does not stop the practice. For those who are
willing to pledge their souls to the dark entities from beyond the veil, untold powers and magical prowess await. Across the
world, as has been the practice for thousands of years, shadow covenants meet in basement catacombs, learning to
summon fiends and bind demons to their will.
  This warlock with her demon servant became lost during her long forgotten quest. Now a Shadow, she stalks the halls
of Calden Keep, lost in an endless rage.
