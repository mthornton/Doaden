# Combat, in one page
### For Dave — everything you actually need to know on your first night

You do not need to memorize any of this. **The only thing you really need to know is at the very bottom.** Everything above it is context.

---

## The one-sentence version

**On your turn, you can move and do one thing.** That's it. That's combat.

---

## How a fight runs

When a fight starts, everyone rolls **initiative** — a d20 plus your Dexterity bonus — and we go around in that order, highest first. Same order every round until it's over. A round is about six seconds of story time, so everything you see happening in one round is happening *at the same time*.

When it's your turn, you get three things:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Movement** | Your speed in feet (on your sheet). You can use it before your action, after it, or split it. |
| **An action** | The main thing you do. Attack, or something clever. One per turn. |
| **A bonus action** | A small extra, *only if* you have something that specifically says "bonus action." Early on you may not. |

---

## Attacking

Two rolls, always in this order:

**1. Did I hit?** Roll a **d20** and add the attack bonus listed next to that weapon on your sheet. If the total is **equal to or higher than the target's AC** (their armor — Michael knows the number), you hit. He'll just tell you yes or no.

**2. How much?** Roll the damage dice for that weapon and add the bonus. Tell him the number. Done.

**Rolling a 20 on the die is a critical hit** — you hit no matter what, and you **roll your damage dice twice** (not the bonus, just the dice). Rolling a 1 is an automatic miss.

**Advantage and disadvantage.** Sometimes Michael will say you have one of these. **Advantage: roll two d20s, use the higher. Disadvantage: roll two, use the lower.** That's the whole mechanic, and it's how the game handles "this is easier than normal" or "this is harder than normal."

---

## Getting hurt

You have **hit points**. Damage comes off them. They are not a measure of how injured you are so much as how much more you can take.

**At 0 hit points you are not dead.** You fall unconscious and start making **death saving throws** — a straight d20 each turn, 10 or higher is a success. Three successes and you stabilize; three failures and you're gone. **And any ally who reaches you can stop all of it** by getting you back to even 1 hit point.

So going down is bad, but it is not the end, and the rest of the party will come for you.

---

## You can try things that aren't on your sheet

This is the part new players don't expect, and it's the best part.

**Your sheet is a list of things you can definitely do. It is not a list of everything you're allowed to do.** You can shove someone into a fire. Kick a brazier over. Cut a rope. Grab a torch and jam it in a man's face. Climb the fence and jump on somebody.

**The way you do it is to just say it out loud.** Michael will tell you what to roll. If he says a thing won't work, he'll say so before you commit — you'll never waste a turn by accident.

---

## Things nobody expects you to know

- **"What can I do right now?"** is a completely normal question. Ask it every turn if you want. Everyone at that table has asked it.
- **You will not break anything.** If a rule gets used wrong, Michael fixes it and the game keeps going. Nobody re-rolls the last ten minutes.
- **You don't have to be optimal.** Doing the interesting thing is worth more at this table than doing the mathematically best thing.
- **The other players will help you** and they will enjoy helping you. Let them.

---

# ⭐ The only thing you need to remember

> ## On your turn:
>
> ### 1. Move if you want to.
> ### 2. Say what you're doing.
> ### 3. Roll a d20, add the number next to it on your sheet.
> ### 4. If you hit, roll the damage dice.
>
> **Everything else, just ask.**
