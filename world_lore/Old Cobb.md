# Old Cobb

**Type:** NPC — enslaved former slaver's clerk at **Bramblefen**; rescue objective and the emotional payload of the slaver arc.
**Function:** the man who wrote down every sale — including [[JonLief]]'s wife and son — and the walking evidence against **Lord Leofard Ironwake**. Vaskar (see [[Vaskar the Brand]]) is torturing him when the party arrives.

> 📍 **STATUS AFTER CHAPTER 7** *(see `sessions/Chapter 7 - Record.md`)* — **DEAD.** Vaskar tortured him for days under a tarp in the Hall and, at sundown in Session 2, threw his body into the yard: *"This is what happens to people that betray me!"* **The party never spoke to him.** Nobody at the table said they buried him. His book lives on: **Cobb's Ledger is with Ysabel**, and the Working Ledger he was copying from is with the party.
>

## The Man Who Kept the Books

Cobb was never a whip-hand. He'll tell you that, quickly, if you let him — *he never once laid a chain on anyone.* He was a clerk. A factor. A tidy man with a tidy hand who, for the better part of forty years, sat at a high desk in the corner of a slaver's yard and **wrote the names down.** Who was bought, who was sold, for how much, to whom. He told himself the ledger was a neutral thing — that a man who merely records a sin does not commit it. It is a lie he believed for a very long time, because believing it let him sleep.

He was good at it. Too good. A clerk who has recorded every buyer, every price, every quiet "household purchase" a great lord would rather no one remember, is a clerk who knows far too much. When Cobb grew old and slow and his eyes began to fail, the masters did the arithmetic he'd taught them: a man who knows your secrets is cheaper to collar than to pension. So the bookkeeper of the trade was written, at last, into his own ledger — a line item, sold to Bramblefen, where Vaskar keeps him because Vaskar keeps everything that might one day be useful.

## Why Vaskar Is Hurting Him

Someone has been using Cobb's old records against Ironwake — a name here, a date there, surfacing where it shouldn't. Vaskar suspects a leak, and Cobb is the likeliest source. He is not wrong. In his last years of freedom, sick with a conscience that arrived decades too late, **Cobb quietly kept Cobb's ledger** — a true copy of the sales the masters most wanted forgotten — and hid it, meaning to do *one* right thing before he died.

**He does not have it any more.** He gave it to **Erwana** the night she ran, told her about the drain, and asked her to get it out of the fen. He does not know where she is, whether she lived, or where the book is now. It is the only right thing he has ever done and he did not expect to survive it.

So when Vaskar asks him where the ledger is and who has seen it, **Cobb genuinely cannot answer** — and Vaskar cannot tell the difference between a man protecting a secret and a man who no longer has one. That is the tragedy sitting in the chair. No amount of pressure will produce anything, because there is nothing left in him to produce.

**The clock — and it is earlier than it looks.** Vaskar kills Cobb **shortly before the sundown match**, on his way out of the office to go and watch it. He does it himself, without heat, the way a man closes a book he has finished: Cobb has stopped being an asset, and Vaskar does not leave loose property tied up in a room he is walking away from.

The deadline is **not** "after dark" — it is *Vaskar's walk to the arena*. He cannot kill Cobb once he is standing at the rail, which means **anything that keeps him in his office keeps the old man breathing**: a buyer to receive, an alarm to answer, a noblewoman he has to be courteous to. And the party cannot be in the Hall and at the match at the same time. That is the choice the evening is built around.

## The two ledgers — keep these straight

The single most confusable thing in the session. They are different books, in different places, doing different jobs:

| | **Cobb's Ledger** | **The working ledger** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Already in the party's hands, via Erwana | Vaskar's office in the Chainmaster's Hall — current business, never leaves the Hall |
| Covers | Decades of old sales the masters wanted forgotten | The last months, including this one |
| Worth | **Ironwake.** Names, prices, buyers, the shape of the whole operation | **Rowan.** The name of the man who bought him: **Barney Soot**, chimney sweep, Solteres. Also the lines about women sold to buyers who refused to identify themselves and "paid well" |
| Useless for | Finding Rowan — it predates him by years | Leverage. It's a business record, not evidence against a lord |

**Optional, recommended:** Cobb's ledger holds a *near miss* — an old entry for a half-elf woman and a boy child, "clan business," wrong date, wrong price, wrong buyer. Not them. It proves Cobb writes down exactly the kind of thing JonLief needs, so the hope transfers to *reach Cobb* instead of dying on an empty page.

## What He Knew — and took with him

Everything, going back decades. Cobb remembers every transaction he ever recorded, the way some old men remember every hymn from childhood. Names, faces, buyers, prices. He can name, buyer by buyer, the full shape of Ironwake's slaving — the single most dangerous document in the fens, carried in a dying clerk's skull.

**He remembers a half-elf woman and a small boy, brought in about two months ago by men who called it "clan business." The woman died of sickness within a fortnight and her body was burned outside the wall. The boy was held apart for some weeks and then sold off quickly and cheaply — and that sale is the one line Cobb cannot produce from memory.**

**What he cannot give from memory: the boy's buyer.** He can tell JonLief everything about **Elizabeth** — that she came in starved and beaten and already sick, that she would not let go of the boy, that she was dead inside a fortnight and they burned her because of what was on her. That is the part that lives in him.

> ### ⚠️ What Cobb actually saw — read `Elizabeth and the Green.md` before running this
>
> He will call it **fever**, because that is the word everyone at Bramblefen used. It was not fever.
>
> **First she stopped caring about the boy.** Not cruelly — she simply stopped answering him, stopped looking when he cried, and everyone read that as a woman breaking down. **Then, after about a week, the green came:** patches across the arms and throat, then small nodules pushing up out of them, like moles. They did not hurt.
>
> Vaskar had her killed and burned, and isolated the boy, and sold him weeks later for far less than a healthy half-elf child is worth — **because he wanted him gone.**
>
> **Cobb died before he told anyone any of this.** The party pieced together the outline in Session 2 from an elf in the Deeps and a guard Luthien charmed — the apathy, the green patches, the burning, the boy isolated in the tower and sold. **What nobody living can tell them is the order and the meaning of it** (`Elizabeth and the Green.md`).

## What his death leaves

- **His ledger is doing the work he wanted, in the hands he would least have chosen.** He told Erwana to take it to a Church in another city. It went to Ysabel, who is using it for blackmail in Solteres *(Chapter 8 §3)*.
- **Dov Brandt remembered him** — the clerk who asked how to spell *Brandt* and was decent about it. *(`Ysabel's Household.md`.)*
- **Erwana is the only living person who knew him as anything but Vaskar's clerk.** She is at Ald's Star.
- **His body.** Unburied, as far as the table knows. Leave it, or let someone remember.
