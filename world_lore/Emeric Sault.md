# Provost Emeric Sault
### Office of the Lost Flock — *Keeper of the Open Files*

> **He is not the third hunter. He is the man who decides there will be a hunt.**
>
> Baltus Thole answers to him. Hedda Merrow answers to him. Neither of them knows what he knows about the other, and he is in no hurry to tell them.
>
> See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md` for the brand, the pull, and the cloaks. `Baltus Thole.md` and `Hedda Merrow.md` for the two Shepherds already in play.

---

## First sight

Sixty-eight. Thin, upright, unhurried in the way of a man who has never once in his life been late. A soft brown hat he removes indoors, every time, without thinking about it. Reading spectacles he does not need for distance.

The violet cloak, clean and very expensive and — like nearly every Shepherd's — bare. That is not the interesting part, and if the party has met Baltus first they will read it wrong.

**The interesting part is that he does not own any.**

Most Shepherds have a box of lambs they never open. Baltus wears his. Hedda carries hers in a pouch. **Sault has never been issued one, not once in forty years**, because a lamb is struck when a file closes badly and **a lamb is struck for the Shepherd who was assigned it.** He has never been assigned anything.

> **He has never been given a file. He is the man files are given *from*.** The Office does not strike a lamb for the clerk, no matter how many of them close.
>
> Baltus will say that flatly, with no expression, and the party can make of it what they like.

### The satchel

Brown leather, softened by decades, worn across the body and never set down. It is not a pack. **It is the drawer he could not leave in the building.**

Inside are files. Penitent files — units filed overdue, cases raised by a Redeemer or a harbourmaster or a clerk who noticed a chartered boat never came back — **and not one of them has been assigned to a Shepherd.**

The bond in the brand is inert until a Shepherd is assigned. That is canon and the party may already know it. What they do not know is that assignment is not a process. **It is a man, and he is carrying the alternative on his hip.**

### The mace

Two-handed, black iron, and the head is a horror — a knot of ornate barbs cast in a pattern that is clearly meant, clearly *designed*, and clearly ecclesiastical. There is scripture worked into the haft. It has a name in the inventory at Hallowmere and he uses that name when he refers to it, which is not often.

**It is not a weapon. It is a liturgical implement, and that is far worse.**

The barbs are a die. A strike from it leaves the **Mark of Atonement** in the wound.

## ⚙️ MAJOR CANON — The Rite of Reaffirmation

**Read this before you use him. It reaches backwards into everything the campaign has said about the brand, and it reaches directly at Hedda.**

There is exactly one thing in the world that severs the Mark of Atonement: **the Shepherd's ceremony.** Not cutting, not burning, not taking the arm off — the rite, and only the rite, and only a Shepherd has it. *(See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md`.)*

The Office does not advertise the second half of that. **The rite has an inverse, the Church calls it the Rite of Reaffirmation, and Provost Emeric Sault is the only living man ordained to perform it.**

A bond severed under the ceremony can be put back.

**How it works:**

- The subject must be **living, present, and held.** No proxy, no relic, no name on a page.
- **He strikes them with the mace.** Once, deliberately, over the old scar. It is not a combat action and he does not hurry it.
- The wound heals into the Mark. **The bond is re-consecrated and the file reopens** — same file, same debt, and the years spent dead on paper do not count.
- He speaks the rite while he does it, and he means every word of it, and it takes about two minutes.

**How rare:** he has done it **nine times in forty years.** The Church regards it as a mercy — a soul restored to the road of penitence that it had thrown away — and Sault regards it as a mercy too, without a flicker of doubt. *Run him in the same register as Ysabel and the Church: bewilderment first, then the category argument, then cold.*

### What this does to the campaign

| It threatens | How |
|---|---|
| **Hedda's eleven** | She frees people **using the Office's own ceremony** — which means every one of them is precisely, exclusively, the kind of severance Sault can undo. Her secret is not merely career-ending. It is eleven people put back on the leash by one old man with a hammer. |
| **Gimble** | Nobody can take Dave's brand off him except a Shepherd. **And if a Shepherd ever does, the escape has a door in the back of it, and the door has a name.** |
| **Any plan the party makes** | The party's instinct will be to cut the brands out themselves. **That does nothing** — and the only people who can do it properly are the people hunting them. There is exactly one route out of the leash and it runs straight through the Office. |

> **Do not let a player discover this by having it done to them.** Let them hear it described — courteously, by the man who does it — while somebody they care about is standing in the room with a scar on their shoulder.

## ⚔️ What he actually is

**The Office's ignorance is not an accident. It is an administration, and he is the administrator.**

The Church has thousands of Penitents and perhaps forty Shepherds. Sault has spent forty years deciding which of the overdue are worth a coat, and he has been right often enough that nobody above him has ever asked how he does it.

**He does it by triage.** A file gets opened when opening it serves the Church. Not when it serves the Penitent.

- A unit lost with nothing of value attached: **stays in the satchel.** A clerk eventually writes *presumed lost in service.* Nobody ever goes anywhere. This is most of them.
- A unit lost somewhere the Church wants a reason to send a man: **opened immediately.**
- A Penitent whose sentence someone powerful paid for: **opened when that person asks, and Sault knows exactly what he is being used for, and does it anyway,** because refusing costs the Office more than it costs the prisoner.

He is not corrupt. He has never taken a coin. He is something considerably harder to fight: **a man who has decided, on the merits, that the Church is better served by an Office that is small, quiet, and selective — and who is not entirely wrong.**

### On Baltus

Sault has assigned Baltus Thole thirty-one files in twenty-two years and has watched him break under every one of them.

He respects him enormously. He also considers him **the Office's finest instrument and its worst possible advertisement**, and he keeps him in the Southern Diocese, away from anyone who matters, because a man who genuinely believes the door at the end is real is difficult to have in a room with a bishop.

**Baltus does not know he is being managed.** He thinks the Provost is a good man who gives him hard work because he can carry it.

### On Hedda

**He has done the arithmetic.**

Forty-seven marks in sixteen years is not a career, it is a rate — and Sault reads the files, all of them, and eleven times a case has closed with a mark brought in and something in the account that did not sit flat. Not proof. A shape.

**He has never opened an inquiry, and he is not going to.**

Because an Office whose reputation includes a woman who brings back skin is an Office that gets fewer runners, fewer standoffs, and fewer dead Shepherds. **Whatever Hedda Merrow is actually doing, the fear of her is worth more to him than the truth about her.**

> He holds it the way he holds everything: filed, unassigned, and available.
>
> **This is the lever.** If the party ever needs Hedda to do something she will not do, Sault is the reason she cannot refuse — and she does not yet know he knows.

## 📌 This party

**Their file was in his satchel.**

They went into the water on 13 August. Somebody eventually noticed a chartered boat had not come back, and the file reached Hallowmere, and Provost Sault read it and did not assign it, and it rode on his hip for weeks while the party walked across half a diocese assuming nobody was looking for them. **They were right. That was him.**

**Two weeks ago he took it out and gave it to Baltus Thole**, and Baltus prayed over it and felt four pulls and has been walking ever since.

> **The question the party should eventually get to ask him is: *why now?*** He will answer it. He answers most things. *(See Open Decisions — pick the reason before he's on screen.)*

**What he wants from them, if he meets them:** nothing dramatic. He wants them to go in. Four living Penitents who walk into a Church office of their own accord is an outcome he can write down, and it costs the Office nothing, and he will say so in exactly those terms.

**What he will do if they refuse:** file it. Politely. And the next time their names cross his desk they will have a history, and a history is what decides which pile you go in.

## ⚔️ Stat block — Provost Emeric Sault

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful neutral — CR 4 (1,100 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 15 (consecrated vestments, *see below*)
- **Hit Points** 66 (12d8 + 12)
- **Speed** 25 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 (+2) | 10 (+0) | 13 (+1) | 17 (+3) | 18 (+4) | 16 (+3) |

- **Saving Throws** WIS +7, CHA +6
- **Skills** Insight +7, Persuasion +6, History +6, Investigation +6, Religion +9
- **Senses** passive Perception 14
- **Proficiency Bonus** +3

### Traits

- **Vestments of Office.** Consecrated cloth. **Any creature bearing the Mark of Atonement has disadvantage on attack rolls against him.** They do not know why. It feels, from the inside, exactly like reluctance. *(Have the player roll and then ask what stopped them.)*
- **Unassigned.** He does not receive the pull, because he has never been given a file. He cannot track anyone. **He does not need to — he decides who is tracked.**
- **The Satchel.** Contains the open files. If it is taken from him, the party is holding the names of every Penitent the Church has not bothered to look for — **and every one of those files can be assigned by whoever ends up with them.** Losing it would be the worst thing that has happened to the Office in a century.
- **Forty Years of Files.** Advantage on Insight and on any check to recall a person, a case, a date, or a sentence. He has read about the party. He has read about most people.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** One mace attack. He does not multiattack; he is sixty-eight.
- **Barbed Mace.** *Melee:* **+5** to hit. *Hit:* **2d6 + 2 bludgeoning and 1d6 piercing.** On a hit against a creature at 0 hit points or restrained, he may instead begin the Rite.
- **★ The Rite of Reaffirmation (special).** Against a **living, held** creature that once bore the Mark of Atonement and no longer does: **2d6 damage, and the Mark is restored.** The bond re-consecrates, the file reopens, and every year the subject spent free is struck from the ledger. *Not a combat action. It takes two minutes and he will let you watch.*
- **Sentence (1/day, action).** He speaks the finding of the Office. Any creature that can hear him and bears the Mark: **DC 15 Wisdom save** or be **frightened of him for 1 minute** and unable to willingly move closer. On a success, the creature is immune for 24 hours. **It is not magic. It is forty years of the Church's authority in one flat sentence,** and it does not work at all on someone who has stopped believing the Church has any.

### Reactions

- **Point of Order.** When a creature within 30 ft. makes an attack against an ally of his, he names them — full name, sentence, and diocese, off the file. The attacker must succeed on a **DC 14 Wisdom save** or the attack has disadvantage.

### If it comes to a fight

**He does not fight and he does not run, and both of those are choices he made a long time ago.**

He is old, slow, and armed with a ceremonial object. He will stand exactly where he is, keep talking, and let whoever is with him do the work — **and he is never alone.** He travels with an escort of two or three Office men, or, far worse, with a Shepherd on assignment.

> **Killing him is not hard and it is the single worst thing the party could do.** The satchel becomes a bonfire's worth of unassigned files that someone else will now have to assign — and his successor will assign all of them, because that is what a new man does.

## Voice

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | Dry, courteous, precise. The voice of a man reading you the relevant paragraph. |
| **Register** | He never threatens, because he has never had to, and he would consider it unprofessional. Everything is procedure, timing, and what will happen next. |
| **Habit** | Removes his hat. Uses full names, always, including middle ones. Says *"I'm sorry"* and means it, and does the thing anyway. |
| **Never says** | *Come home.* That is Baltus's line and Sault finds it sentimental. |
| **Tell** | When he opens the satchel, the conversational part is over. **He does not open it to look. He opens it to file.** |

**Four lines to have ready:**

> *"No one came for you because I did not send anyone. I want to be clear that this was not an oversight. It was a decision, and I made it, and for eleven weeks it was the best thing that ever happened to you."*

> *"Thole will have told you there's a door at the end of it. There is. I have signed three of them in forty years and I keep the copies, and I would show them to you if I thought it would help, and it will not, because three is the number you'd take away from it."*

> *"You cut it out. Yes. People do." (a pause, entirely without malice) "You understand that the scar is a place I can put it back."*

> *"I don't want anything from you that you weren't already sentenced to. That is rather the point of me."*

## Hooks

- **He is the reason Chapter 1 didn't end in Chapter 2.** The party's freedom to date has a cause, and the cause is a filing decision by a man they've never met. Delivering that fact is worth a whole scene.
- **The satchel is a heist.** Not a fight — a theft. And the party would then be holding several hundred people's leashes with no idea what to do with them, which is a far better problem than a treasure.
- **He is Hedda's blackmail and she doesn't know it yet.** If she and the party are ever on the same side of something, Sault is the thing that can turn her.
- **He is Baltus's handler, and Baltus has no idea.** If the party can make Baltus see how he has been used, they don't get an ally — they get a very large man having the worst week of his life in a purple coat.
- **He can be argued with, and that is the trap.** He is reasonable, informed, and will concede points. He will concede almost anything except the file.
- **Ysabel would like him enormously** and the party should have to watch that happen.

## Open decisions

1. **Why did he open the party's file two weeks ago?** *Recommendation: the first Dol team.* Five Penitents went to Dol Island and never came back; he never assigned that file either. When a second unit went to the same island and also stopped reporting, it stopped being an ordinary loss and became **a pattern on an island**, and patterns are the one thing that gets a coat sent. **Note what this means: he is not hunting the party. He is investigating Dol, and they are the thread.** *(Alternative: someone paid. Cleaner, meaner, less interesting.)*
2. **Does he already know about Gimble?** *Recommendation: no.* Gimble is the only survivor of a file Sault never opened, and the Provost learning that a man he wrote off is alive and collared in a Solteres fen is a **Chapter 8** event, not a background fact.
3. **Where is the rest of the Office?** He implies a building at Hallowmere with clerks in it. Decide whether that place can be visited, because the party will want to.
4. **Is the Rite reversible a second time?** *Recommendation: leave it unanswered and let him refuse to say.* His refusing to answer is more frightening than either answer.
5. **Does he outrank Vozruk?** They are different arms of the same Church. *Recommendation: neither outranks the other, they loathe each other's paperwork, and each considers the other the reason the system doesn't work.*
