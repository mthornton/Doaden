# Durgan Half-Ear

**Type:** NPC — enslaved dwarf; handler and keeper of **The Juggernaut ("Mott")**. Owned by **Lord Leofard Ironwake.**
**Role in play:** the leash and the heart of the pair — the only one who can calm Mott, and a quiet goldmine of intel on Ironwake's pits (see [[The Juggernaut]]).

> Names and clan are placeholders — rename freely.

## A Dwarf of the Long Roads

Durgan was not born to the mines like most of his kin. His people, **Clan Brannock**, were drovers and beast-handlers — a trade-caravan clan that moved goods, draft-oxen, and prize animals along the hard roads between the free towns and the Church's cities. Durgan grew up with his hands in manes and muzzles, learning the old family craft: how to read a frightened creature, how to gentle a beast three times his weight with nothing but a low voice and a steady presence. His father used to say the Brannocks didn't *break* animals, they *listened* to them — and that a drover who couldn't tell fear from meanness had no business on the road.

It was a good life, if a small one. Durgan expected to inherit a wagon, take a wife from a cousin-clan, and die old with a pipe in his teeth and a hound at his feet.

## The Road Ends in Chains

The Church of Azerai's peace never reached the fen-roads south of Solteres, and the men who worked those roads knew it. Dwarves are not human, and in Doaden that is enough. A caravan of "non-folk" hauling valuables was not a crime to be reported but a cargo to be taken.

Durgan's caravan was betrayed by a guide who'd been paid twice — once by the Brannocks, once by the slavers who were waiting. He remembers very little of the night itself: torches in the reeds, the oxen screaming, his uncle's axe, and then the mud. When he woke, he was collared. Most of his clan were dead. The rest were split up and sold within a week, and he never saw a single one of them again.

He did not go quietly. He ran twice. The first time they caught him they laughed and dragged him back. The second time, a slaver pinned his head to a stump and **took the top half of his left ear with a skinning knife** — "so we'll know you next time, little digger." He has been **Durgan Half-Ear** ever since. He stopped running after that. Not because he was broken — because he learned that a man who wants to survive slavery doesn't fight the wall. He finds the crack in it, and waits.

## The Monster in the Pit

Ironwake bought the giant they'd come to call the Juggernaut for a pittance, because the giant kept killing his handlers. Three men in as many months — some by rage, one, it was said, simply because he cried too loudly and the giant didn't understand and lashed out. The pit-masters were ready to put the thing down as a bad investment.

Durgan was sent in to handle him because Durgan was expendable. Everyone expected the dwarf to die, and no one would miss a maimed non-folk drover if he did.

But Durgan didn't see a monster. He'd spent his whole life in pens with terrified giants — he'd just never met one that could talk. He walked in slow, kept his hands open and his voice low, the way his father taught him, and he *listened*. And what he heard under the roaring wasn't malice. It was a child, in the dark, in pain, who had never once in his life been shown a moment of kindness. So Durgan gave him one. He gave him a name — **Mott** — and a piece of bread, and he sat with him until the giant stopped shaking.

They have not been parted since.

## Who He Is Now

Durgan is quiet, watchful, and dry as old timber. He speaks little to strangers and sizes them up the way he'd size up a strange dog — where are its hands, where are its eyes, is it afraid, is it cruel. He has a slow, deadpan humor that surfaces once he decides you're not a threat. He is patient past the point most men would break, because patience is the only weapon a slave is allowed to keep.

And he carries a great deal of guilt. Ironwake makes him **rouse Mott into a killing rage** before the pit-fights, and then makes him stand at the rail and watch the giant burn himself hollow for a crowd's amusement. Durgan does it. He does it because if Mott stops earning, Ironwake will have him butchered, and Durgan would rather damn his own soul a hundred times than let that happen. Afterward he cleans Mott's wounds, tells him he did well, tells him it was only a game, and lies to that big gentle face about how proud everyone is — while hating himself, and Ironwake, and the whole rotten city, in equal measure.

He protects Mott like a son. It is the one thing left that is entirely his.

## What He Wants — and What He Knows

What Durgan wants is simple and impossible: **out.** A quiet holding, a warm room, a stretch of road where Mott can be big and slow and safe, and no one ever makes either of them fight again. He has stopped believing anyone will give him this, which is exactly why the party can win him — not with gold, and never with threats, but by **showing, not saying, that they'll protect Mott.** Be kind to the giant where Durgan can see it. Free him. Take a hit for him. Do that, and the dwarf who trusts no one will hand you his life.

Threaten or harm Mott, and you make an enemy who can't beat you in a fight and won't try — he'll simply make sure you never sleep easy in Solteres again.

And Durgan knows things. Years at the rail of Ironwake's pits have made him a quiet ledger of the man's whole rotten operation: the bribed magistrates, the fixed odds, the schedule of the fights, **which "champion" is meant to win the trial by combat and why.** Won over, he is the single best source of intel the party could have against Lord Ironwake.

> **Optional connective hook (recommended):** Durgan has watched a great deal of "stock" pass through Ironwake's holdings. If it suits your table, he *remembers a half-elf woman and a young boy* — quiet, the boy always at her skirts — brought in years apart and kept apart. He can put a name and a fate to **JonLief's wife and son** when no one else can. This makes Durgan the human (dwarven) heart of the whole slaver arc, and gives JonLief a reason to move the world to keep this dwarf alive.

## Roleplay & DM notes

- **Voice:** low, unhurried, economical. He gentles people the way he gentles beasts. Rare dry jokes signal trust.
- **The bond mechanic** lives in [[The Juggernaut]]: while Mott can see and hear Durgan, Durgan can end his rage as an action. Kill or silence Durgan and you lose the off-switch — see the **Grief-Rage** rules.
- **He is not a fighter.** Use a Commoner or Scout stat block. His power is knowledge, loyalty, and the leash on a living siege engine — never his axe.
- **Winning him over** is a roleplay arc, not a Persuasion check. Kindness to Mott is worth more than any speech. One genuine act of protection cracks him open; a betrayal seals him forever.
- **Freedom payoff:** free the pair and Durgan becomes a fiercely loyal ally and quest-giver, a fount of anti-Ironwake intel, and — if you use the optional hook — the man who can finally tell JonLief the truth about his family.
