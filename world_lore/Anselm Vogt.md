# Anselm Vogt

**Type:** NPC — enslaved Westfield man; leader of the coming revolt at **Bramblefen**.
**Function:** the party's honest source inside the compound, the hand on the revolt's trigger, and the man who decides what [[Wolfram Eisenherz]] is worth. The deliberate opposite of [[Denrick]] — same collar, same pens, opposite answer.

> The other two Westfield men with him are unnamed for now.

## The Sheriff of a Dying Barony

Anselm was a sheriff of Westfield for twenty-six years, which in that country means he was an Eisenherz man. Old Baron Dietrich bought the first sheriffs out of his own emptying coffers and rode patrol beside them; his son Konrad commanded them by the time Anselm took the oath. It was never a grand office. It was a horse, a spear, a stretch of bad road, and a barony too poor to pay you on time. Anselm did it anyway, for most of his life, and he was good at it — not fast, not clever, but *steady*, which on a bad road is worth more.

He knew the family. He stood in the yard at the manor with rain running off his helmet more times than he could count. He remembers Wolfram as a serious boy who trained too hard and asked the sheriffs questions no one else bothered to ask.

## What the Rot Cost Him

When Bishop Thurstan came to Westfield, the sheriffs went the way of everything else. Church coin dried up. Elders who complained found their farms off the patrol route. Anselm was told his stretch of road was no longer a priority, and then he was told nothing at all.

When word came that Wolfram had killed the Bishop, Anselm did not believe the Citadel's version for a moment — he had seen what Thurstan was, and so had every man who rode that road. He went to the Sunlit Citadel to say so. He was not permitted past the outer courts. He waited eleven days, gave his statement to a clerk who did not write it down, and was told the matter was closed.

He did not go home. He went looking for Wolfram instead — to find out where the Penitents had sent the boy, and to say to his face that somebody had believed him. A former sheriff with no office and no coin, asking after a condemned heretic, in city after city, working his way east and south for the better part of a year.

## How a Free Man Ends Up in a Cage

He was never kidnapped off a road. **He was sentenced**, and that is the part he cannot get past.

Somewhere south of the Diocese he was picked up for vagrancy — no patron, no papers, no coin, and a name that meant nothing outside Westfield. In the courts that serve Solteres, that is not a nuisance charge; it is an inventory problem with a legal remedy. A magistrate handed down a term of labor to settle the debt of his own keeping. Within the week the term had been bought, and the man attached to it went with it.

That is how Bramblefen takes humans. Erwana and the others in the pens are *stock* — non-folk, bought and sold outright. Anselm is **a sentence somebody purchased**, which is legal, documented, and in Old Cobb's ledger under a line as tidy as any other. Slavery is illegal by the Church's own ruling. This is not slavery. This is a term of service, lawfully transferred. Everyone involved says so.

Whether the charge found him by accident is a question he has turned over for four months without an answer. He had been asking loud questions about a dead bishop in front of anyone who would listen. He has decided it does not matter. Either the Church put him here or the world did, and both of them wear the same face from inside a cage.

**What that makes him:** a witness the Citadel disposed of without ever having to do anything as vulgar as murder. He can testify that a man who tried to speak for Wolfram Eisenherz was written into a ledger and shipped four hundred miles. That is a dangerous thing to be carrying, and he has no idea yet that he is carrying it.

**The thing to land at the table:** Anselm and Wolfram are both serving sentences handed down for the same dead bishop. One wears the Mark of Atonement. One wears a collar. Neither of them chose it, and the same institution wrote both.

## In the Pens

Anselm has been at Bramblefen four months and he has spent every day of it doing the only thing he knows how to do: **keeping order and keeping records.** He settles fights in the pens. He makes sure the sick get water. He has scratched a tally into the stone at the back of the Pens — one mark for every man carried out — and he can tell you the number without looking. He does not know that a hundred feet away [[Old Cobb]] has spent forty years writing the same names down for the other side. If those two ever compare their lists, it will be the quietest and worst scene of the session.

He has been ready to rise for weeks. He has not, because he is not a hothead: **an uprising that fails kills everyone he is responsible for.** He is waiting for the moment every eye in the compound is somewhere else. The sundown match is that moment. He gives the signal, and not before — unless somebody else forces his hand.

## What He Knows (and will tell, accurately)

Anselm has counted everything, because counting is what he is. Freed, trusted, or simply *asked like a man*:

- **Twenty men — fourteen of Vaskar's and six of Ironwake's** — where every one of them stands, and which of them drink. He will give this slowly and exactly, post by post, because that is how he holds it in his head.
- That it was **four or five** until about a month ago, and that everything above that number arrived with the giant. If the party came in believing the old count, Anselm is where they find out why it was ever true.
- Four of Ironwake's asleep in the barracks and two standing on the big one's cell day and night — and that those six are why he has not moved yet. He can beat Vaskar's men. He cannot beat Ironwake's.
- Where the armory keys hang.
- That the big one in the Deep Cages is gentle until the dwarf makes him otherwise ([[Durgan Half-Ear]]).
- That the masked man will not survive the match.
- That [[Denrick]] talks to the overseers at night and should be trusted with nothing.

**He does not lie and he does not embellish.** If the party runs an Insight check on him, tell them straight: this man is telling the truth. Earning good information honestly, right after Denrick offers them a poisoned version of the same thing, is the whole point of him.

## The Wolfram Scene

The moment Wolfram is recognizable — the crest, the name, the face — Anselm knows him. Play it slow.

> *"The old man at the back of the cage has not moved since you came in. Now he stands, and he takes his time about it, and he looks at you the way a man looks at a debt he never expected to collect. 'Eisenherz,' he says. Not a question. 'I gave a statement about you to a clerk in the Citadel. He never wrote it down.'"*

What happens next is Wolfram's to decide, and Anselm's judgment of it is the session's verdict on him:

- **Wolfram frees them and fights beside them** → Anselm hands him the revolt without being asked. The men follow the crest because Anselm does.
- **Wolfram arms them and goes about his own business** → Anselm takes it, uses it, and does not forgive it. Cordial afterward. Never warm.
- **Wolfram stays out** → Anselm says nothing at all, which is worse than anything he could say. This is the source of the "fraud" verdict; the men take their cue from him.

He will also, if asked, tell Wolfram the one thing nobody has: **that people back home did not believe the Church's story either.** Whether that is a mercy or a fresh wound is up to the table.

## Personality & how to run him

- **Old lawman, not old soldier.** He thinks in terms of order, custody, and who is responsible for whom. He asks the party who is in charge, and means it.
- **Slow, level, unhurried.** He does not plead and he does not perform. When he wants something he says it once, plainly, and lets the silence do the rest.
- **He counts.** Guards, days, the dead. Let him produce a number in the middle of a scene; it is his tell.
- **His flaw is patience.** He has waited so long for the perfect moment that he may be waiting still when the moment is gone. If the party gives him a reason, he moves. If they give him none, he watches eighteen men die on principle and knows it.

## Stat block — Anselm Vogt

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful good — CR 1/2 (100 XP) as written*

- **AC** 11 · **HP** 26 (4d8 + 8) · **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |

| 14 (+2) | 10 (+0) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) | 15 (+2) | 13 (+1) |

- **Skills** Athletics +4, Insight +4, Perception +4, Survival +4
- **Senses** passive Perception 14
- **Languages** Common
- **Condition** begins with **1 level of exhaustion** (four months of short rations and the lash)

**Traits**

- **Kept the Count.** Anselm knows the compound's guard positions, rotations, and numbers exactly. Treat as fact, not a check.
- **Steady Hand.** Allied freed captives within 30 ft. that can see and hear him have advantage on saving throws against being frightened. If he falls, they lose it immediately — and morale with it.
- **Twenty-Six Years on a Bad Road.** Advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks. He read [[Denrick]] within a week of arriving.

**Actions**

- **Improvised Weapon** (chain, bar, cudgel). *Melee:* +4 to hit. *Hit:* 1d6 + 2 bludgeoning.
- **Spear** (if armed from the Armory). *Melee or Ranged:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft. or range 20/60. *Hit:* 1d6 + 2 piercing (1d8 + 2 two-handed). Give him a spear and he is a sheriff again; play the difference.

**Reaction**

- **Pull Him Back.** When an ally within 5 ft. is hit by an attack, Anselm can move them 5 ft. and grant +2 AC against that attack. He has been pulling people out of the way his whole life.

## Hooks & aftermath

- **The signal is his.** Any disruption may trigger the revolt, but *he* chooses the instant. A party that talks to him first can time it deliberately — the single biggest lever they have on the sundown fight.
- **He is the reason freed men stay a group** instead of scattering into the fen. If he lives, the eighteen have a leader and a destination. If he dies, they are a mob and someone else's problem.
- **He will not go quietly to Solteres.** A city where slavery is ordinary is not a refuge to him; he will argue for going inland and taking the men with him.
- **He is evidence.** Anselm's conviction is a paper trail leading back to the Citadel's handling of Wolfram's trial. Nothing in this session forces the party to notice — but a party that does has leverage on the Church itself, which is a very different weapon than leverage on a lord.
- **He wants Westfield back.** Not the barony — the road. If the party ever moves against the Church's rot or restores anything of the Eisenherz name, Anselm is the man who raises the sheriffs again.
- **Cobb and Anselm.** Two record-keepers, two ledgers, opposite reasons. Put them in the same room before the session ends if you can.
