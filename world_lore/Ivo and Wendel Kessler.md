# Ivo and Wendel Kessler
### Office of the Lost Flock — the pair the Office does not write down

> **They are not sent to find Penitents. They are sent when the Office has already decided the Penitents are lost, and the only remaining question is what has them.**
>
> Their arrival is not a hunt. **It is a verdict**, and the party should understand that the moment they hear two hats are on the road.
>
> See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md` for the brand, the pull, and the cloaks. `Emeric Sault.md` for the man who releases them — verbally, and never on paper.

---

## First sight

**Describe the hats first.** Two of them, absurdly wide, deep violet, visible from a long way off across flat ground. Nothing else in the Church looks like that. People who know what the hats mean do not come outside.

**Ivo** — the elder. Very tall and very thin, the thinness of a man who eats when reminded. His robe is violet and it is in ruins: torn at the hem, burnt through in two places, and **studded with small holes in neat rows where lambs used to be.** He carries a broadsword nearly as long as he is tall, most often at the trail, point down, so that it drags and you hear him before you see him.

He does not look at people. He looks slightly past them, at where they are standing.

**Wendel** — the younger. Ordinary build, ordinary bearing, a violet cloak in perfect repair and **entirely bare**, like nearly every Shepherd's. His twelve lambs are in a tin in his pack, along with a needle and a great deal of violet thread, and the tin is not for his. A hand crossbow on each hip, both half-cocked, always. He looks at people a great deal. He is looking at their hands.

> **The hats are Wendel's doing.** Ivo used to stop in open country and stare upward for hours at a time and could not be moved. A brim wide enough solved it. Wendel wears the same hat, in the same size, so that his brother is not the only man in the world wearing one.

### The holes in Ivo's coat

**Almost no Shepherd wears his lambs.** They go in a box. Two men in the Office wear them, and one of them is Ivo Kessler, and the other is Baltus Thole — *which is the thing nobody has ever said to Baltus's face, and should be said to the party's, by somebody, at the wrong moment.*

Ivo's coat carried **fifty-eight.** There are **nineteen holes.**

Wendel has sewn nineteen lambs back onto that coat more times than he will say, and they keep going, and Ivo does not remember taking them off and Wendel has never once seen him do it. He sews at night. In the morning there are fewer. **Twelve of the lambs in his tin are his own, and he has never put one of those on anything.**

**Do not explain this.** It is the single best thing about the pair.

## ⚙️ What the Office actually does with them

**There is no order. There is no file. There is no signature.**

Provost Sault does not put these two on paper. He finds Wendel, tells him a place and a direction out loud, and goes back to Hallowmere, and if anyone ever asks, the Office of the Lost Flock has no record of the Kessler brothers being anywhere.

That is the deal, and everyone involved understands it, and it exists for one reason: **whatever they are sent at, the Church would rather not be documented as having known about it.**

So the brothers are released when there is **strong evidence that a Penitent unit has gone in too deep with something of enormous evil** — not a slaver, not a cult of six men in a barn, but the kind of thing that has a name older than the Church and gets left out of sermons.

They are given nothing. They take nothing. **Nobody is coming after them and nobody will collect them**, and both brothers know it, and Wendel has made his peace with it and Ivo does not appear to have noticed.

> **⚠️ What their arrival tells the party.** If Ivo and Wendel Kessler come over the hill, then somewhere in an office in Hallowmere, a decision has already been made: *these four are not recoverable, and the thing they are tangled in matters more than they do.* Nobody says this to their faces. Wendel is perfectly polite. **The party should work it out from the fact that no one asked them to come home.**

## ⚔️ Ivo — what is wrong with him

Nothing specific, and that is the point. **It was an accumulation.** Thirty years of walking into places where Penitents had ended badly, and seeing every one of them, and going out again the next week. No single cause, no incident to find, no cure to chase. *(Leave this open — if a thread later needs a hook, one of the nineteen is a very good place to put it.)*

He is not stupid and he is not gone. He is **somewhere adjacent**, and he talks, and what he says is often true.

### The dosage

**Being near Ivo for any length of time is bad for you, and it is not magic.**

He says things that are accurate and specific and that you were not ready to hear, in an even voice, about you. Not threats. **Observations.** And they cannot be unheard, and they sit in a person and change the shape of them, and that is all the mechanism there is.

**Wendel measures it in minutes.** He has been managing his brother's dosage on other people for years and he is very good at it. He will physically end a conversation — a hand on Ivo's arm, *"that's enough"* — and he does it for the party's sake, not his brother's, and the party will not realise that until later.

> ### 🎲 How to run it
>
> A creature that talks *with* Ivo — not near him, *with* him — for more than a few minutes makes a **DC 14 Wisdom save.**
>
> On a failure it takes **a splinter**: one true thing about itself it now cannot put down. Mechanically, **disadvantage on saving throws against fear and despair effects** until the character does something about the thing he said. Not a curse. No dispel. **A player deals with it by dealing with it.**
>
> **You only need this rule if a player leans in.** They will.

### Things Ivo says

Pull one, deliver it flat, do not explain it, and let Wendel cut it off one sentence too late.

> *"You've been walking east because east is where the road went. Nobody told you to. You just haven't stopped."*

> *"There were five before you. I know how many because I know how many barrels."*

> *"You still think there's a version of this where you go home and it's the same."*

> *"She's not saving you. She's not even lying. That's the part that gets people."*

> *"Don't take it off. It's the only part of you they've written down."*

> *"I met the man who branded you. He was kind to me. That's the whole trouble with him."*

## Wendel — the keeper

**He stays in the Office for exactly one reason: if he leaves, they send Ivo out alone.**

He is forty-four. He was going to be a joiner. He has been holding his brother's collar for nineteen years and he is not bitter about it in any way a party could use, because bitterness would be a kind of resistance and he stopped resisting a long time ago. **Every file is rent.**

He is competent, courteous, funny in a dry way when he is not working, and completely unmoved by appeals of any kind. He has heard them. He has made most of them himself, upward, to men who said no.

**The two crossbows are not a flourish.** They are loaded differently, always, and he never confuses them:

- **Left hand — steel.** For the thing they were sent at.
- **Right hand — grace.** Blunt bolts and a soporific he mixes himself. For Penitents, for bystanders, for anyone he has decided is going to survive the evening whether they want to or not.

And for Ivo. He has shot his brother eleven times. Ivo has never mentioned it.

## 📌 This party

**They are a late arrival, and they should not be spent early.**

The brothers do not come for Bramblefen. Vaskar is a slaver with a collar and a coliseum and the Office has men for that. They come when the party is far enough into **Saraswati, Adephagia, and what is under Dol Island** that a report crosses a desk and stops being about four missing lambs.

**Which means their arrival is the campaign telling the players what they've become.** Not *you are in trouble* — the party has been in trouble since Chapter 1. **You are now the kind of trouble the Church sends the unwritten men at.**

- **Gimble** should recognise something. He is the only survivor of the first unit, and the first unit is what makes this a pattern rather than a loss.
- **Wolfram** will want to help them, and Wendel will let him, and that will go badly for Wolfram in a way that has nothing to do with combat.
- **Ivo will say the true thing to whichever player is least ready for it.** Choose that on purpose, in advance, before the session.

**They can be allies.** That is the trap and it is a good one. Their objective genuinely overlaps with the party's, they are effective, and Wendel will cooperate frankly and in good faith right up until the moment the party becomes the exposure — **and there was never an order saying otherwise, because there was never an order.**

## ⚔️ Stat block — Ivo Kessler

*Medium humanoid (human), chaotic neutral — CR 8 (3,900 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 14 (unarmoured; nothing lands cleanly on him)
- **Hit Points** 133 (14d8 + 70)
- **Speed** 35 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 (+4) | 14 (+2) | 20 (+5) | 11 (+0) | 15 (+2) | 8 (−1) |

- **Saving Throws** STR +8, CON +9, WIS +6
- **Skills** Perception +6, Survival +6, Religion +4
- **Damage Resistances** psychic
- **Condition Immunities** frightened, charmed
- **Senses** passive Perception 16
- **Proficiency Bonus** +4

### Traits

- **Nothing Left to Take.** He cannot be frightened, charmed, taunted, bargained with, or read. Insight against him returns nothing usable. *(Tell the player the roll succeeded and that it did not help.)*
- **He Knows What It Is.** Once per encounter, as a free action, he names a creature's true nature aloud — species, origin, the thing it is actually serving. **This is always correct**, and it grants him and every ally who heard it **advantage on attacks against that creature for one minute.**
- **Does Not Stop.** The first time he would drop to 0 hit points, he drops to **1** instead and takes his turn.
- **Reckless.** He may attack with advantage; attacks against him have advantage until his next turn. **He does this nearly every round.**
- **The Dosage** *(non-combat)*. See above. This is the trait that matters.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** Two broadsword attacks.
- **Broadsword.** *Melee, reach 10 ft.:* **+8** to hit. *Hit:* **2d6 + 4 slashing.** Against a creature he has named with *He Knows What It Is*, add **2d6.**
- **The Long Cut (recharge 5–6).** He steps through a line 20 ft. long and 5 ft. wide. Every creature in it: **DC 16 Dexterity** save, **4d6 + 4 slashing**, half on a success. **He does not check who is in the line.**

## ⚔️ Stat block — Wendel Kessler

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful neutral — CR 5 (1,800 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 16 (studded leather)
- **Hit Points** 75 (10d8 + 30)
- **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 (+1) | 18 (+4) | 16 (+3) | 14 (+2) | 16 (+3) | 12 (+1) |

- **Saving Throws** DEX +7, WIS +6
- **Skills** Perception +6, Insight +6, Medicine +6, Sleight of Hand +7
- **Senses** passive Perception 16
- **Proficiency Bonus** +3

### Traits

- **Nineteen Years of Practice.** He is never surprised while his brother is within sight, and he always acts on the first round.
- **Sighting.** Bonus action: he names a target. The next ally to attack it this round has **advantage.** He mostly names things for Ivo.
- **The Dosage** *(non-combat)*. Reaction, any time: he ends a conversation. A hand on the arm, *"that's enough."* **The save does not happen.** He decides who gets protected and the party does not get a vote.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** Three bolts, in any combination of steel and grace.
- **Steel Bolt.** *Ranged (30/120):* **+7** to hit. *Hit:* **1d6 + 4 piercing.**
- **Grace Bolt.** *Ranged (30/120):* **+7** to hit. *Hit:* **1d6 + 4 bludgeoning**, and the target must make a **DC 15 Constitution** save or be **poisoned for 1 minute.** A creature that fails by 5 or more falls **unconscious** for the duration or until damaged. *He carries a great many of these.*
- **★ Brother, Stop.** He shoots Ivo. One grace bolt, no attack roll, automatic hit, **DC 15 Constitution** save as above. **He will do it to save a Penitent, and he will do it to save one of the party, and it costs him nothing to decide and everything else.**

### Reactions

- **Between Them.** When a creature moves to attack Ivo, Wendel makes one bolt attack against it.

### If it comes to a fight

**Wendel opens the conversation and Ivo ends it.** Wendel will talk for as long as talking is working, and it is usually working, because he is good at it and he is not lying.

If it goes to violence, Ivo goes forward and does not come back, and Wendel works the edges — grace bolts on anyone he has decided is not the problem, steel on whatever is. **The party may well find that Wendel is shooting them with the soft ones on purpose.** That is not mercy exactly. It is bookkeeping.

**They do not pursue.** If the party breaks off, the brothers finish what they came for and leave, and nobody writes any of it down.

## Voice

| | Ivo | Wendel |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound** | Even, unhurried, slightly too loud for the distance. No emphasis anywhere. | Warm, plain, a little tired. A tradesman's voice. |
| **Habit** | Answers the question you did not ask. Never repeats himself. | Says people's names. Asks after their feet, their food, their sleep. Means it. |
| **Never says** | Anything about himself. | *No.* He says *"that's enough,"* which is not the same and is not negotiable. |
| **Tell** | When he sets the sword point down and lets go of it, he is about to say the worst thing in the scene. | When he thumbs the right-hand crossbow, someone in the room is going to sleep. |

**Wendel, three lines:**

> *"Nobody sent us for you. I want that clear, because you'll hear it wrong otherwise. We were sent for the thing you're standing next to."*

> *"He'll say something to one of you tonight. I can't stop it and I won't apologise for him. I'll tell you now so you know it isn't personal, and later you'll think it was, and it wasn't."*

> *"There's no paper on this. That means nobody's coming to check on you, and nobody's coming to check on us either. Take what comfort's in that."*

## Hooks

- **They are the campaign's altimeter.** The single clearest way to tell the players how deep they now are, without a word of exposition, is that these two showed up.
- **The nineteen holes.** A player who counts them and asks is opening a door. *(Recommended: neither brother knows. Wendel has a theory he has never said aloud.)*
- **Ivo and Baltus have never met on screen and both wear their lambs.** Every other Shepherd has quietly drawn the line between those two facts. **Baltus has not**, and watching him work it out is worth an entire scene.
- **Ivo is a source and cannot be interrogated.** He knows things about Dol, about Saraswati, about the first unit. He will say them at random, unprompted, in the wrong order. **The party's problem is not getting him to talk. It is being there when he does.**
- **Wendel is recruitable — for one thing, once.** Not for a cause. For a specific job that gets his brother a quiet year. He will consider that offer seriously and the party will have to actually be able to deliver it.
- **If Ivo dies, Wendel is free.** He has never once let himself want it. **Watch what a player does with that, if they work it out.**
- **Sault's exposure.** If it ever became provable that the Provost has been releasing two unaccountable men at things the Church denies exist, it would end him. **Wendel is the only living witness, and Wendel would not testify, and Sault has never had to ask.**

## Open decisions

1. **What broke Ivo?** *Deliberately unspecified.* Keep it that way until a thread needs somewhere to land — and when one does, put it in one of the nineteen.
2. **Who takes the lambs off?** *Recommendation: never answer.* If pressed for a table ruling: it is Ivo, at night, and he does not remember, and it is not important that it be more than that.
3. **Have they been sent at Dol before?** *Recommendation: no — and the party should be the reason they finally are.* That makes the party the cause of the Office finally looking at the island.
4. **Does Wendel know about Hedda?** *Recommendation: no.* He is the one Shepherd who would understand her instantly, and keeping them apart until it is useful is worth more than the scene.
5. **Do they survive the campaign?** *One of them, probably.* Decide which before you put them in a room with the party, because it changes how Wendel talks.
