# Turok of the Ash and Pines 
 
## The World Through a Hunter's Eyes 
 
For Turok, the world has always been a simple thing, governed by the ancient trinity of the forest: the belly, the blade, and the tribe. A man is born, he learns to hunt, he finds a mate, he protects his own, and he dies. At thirty years old, Turok was a master of this world. His home was the vast Mistwood forest in the Western Diocese, a land of colossal pines that clawed at the sky and whose floors were a permanent twilight of shadow and fallen needles. He was a hard man, his body a roadmap of scars and muscle carved by a life of wrestling the wilds for every meal and every breath. Tracking was not a skill to him; it was an instinct. He could read a broken twig like a sentence and the scent of rain on the wind like a prophecy. 
 
## Leader of the Grey Wolf Tribe 
 
Turok was the chieftain of the Grey Wolf Tribe, a small, hardy clan of about fifty souls. Like the other human tribes of the Mistwood, they were a people of grit and tradition. They traded smoked meat with the Bear Clan for iron axe heads and gathered with the River Tribe for the annual Salmon Run. Life was a constant, grueling struggle against brutal winters, ancient beasts that slumbered in the hills, and the fickle spirits of the forest. But it was their life. It was balanced. In this harsh world, Turok had found contentment, a measure of peace in the arms of his mate, Ylva, and a sense of purpose in the trust of his people. 
 
This fragile balance was maintained by an unspoken truce with the forest's other inhabitants: the Elves of the Deeps. For centuries, the reclusive elves kept to the oldest, most mystical parts of the forest, living in communion with nature. Encounters were rare, marked by a silent, mutual respect between hunter and guardian. The humans did not disturb their sacred groves, and the elves did not interfere with their hunting grounds. 
 
## The Green Rot 
 
The balance shattered with the rise of Zelryn. A young, charismatic elf, Zelryn was filled with a venomous resentment for the "human infestation" in what he deemed a purely elven domain. While all Mistwood elves possessed a talent for the subtle magic of wood and leaf, Zelryn's power was something else entirely—something aggressive, corrupting, and terrifyingly potent. It was rumored he had found a 'Heartwood Shard,' a splinter from a long-dead, malevolent nature primordial, and it had whispered secrets of power into his mind. 
 
Overnight, his simple charms for coaxing fruit from branches became the power to command roots to grow like lances and animate trees into lumbering sentinels. He used this fearsome new magic to bend the three great elven factions to his will. For the first time in centuries, the elves of the Mistwood were united, not in defense, but in conquest. 
 
Zelryn's war began with choking vines overrunning human hunting trails and sacred streams turning brackish and foul. The human tribes, isolated and proud, were picked off one by one, pushed back from the deep woods into the harsher, more barren foothills. 
 
## A Call to Unity, A Whisper of Doom 
 
Known for his calm judgment and unmatched skill as a warrior, Turok was respected beyond his own tribe. He saw the only path to survival: the tribes of man had to unite as the elves had. He sent out runners and met with the chieftains of the Bear and River tribes, urging them to form a war council. He argued passionately that their traditions of independence would be their doom. While some were hesitant, Turok’s words began to kindle a spark of defiance. 
 
But the forest has ears, and Zelryn’s spies were everywhere. Learning of Turok’s plan to forge an alliance, he decided to make an example of the man who dared to resist. 
 
## The Silent Village 
 
Turok was leading a three-day hunt for a great elk, vital for the coming winter stores. Returning at dusk, his party crested the final ridge overlooking their village. There was no welcoming smoke from the longhouse fire. No laughter from the children. There was only an unnatural, profound silence. 
 
A cold dread seized Turok. He broke into a run, crashing through the underbrush. The scene in the village was one of calculated slaughter. The Grey Wolf Tribe had been ambushed, caught completely unaware. But this was no simple raid; it was a display of Zelryn's power. Thorny, grasping vines, thick as a man’s arm and unnaturally dark, had erupted from the ground, entangling homes and people alike. Elven arrows protruded from every body. 
 
Turok's world ended when he found the chieftain's longhouse. He found Ylva near the hearth, a half-finished child's moccasin in her lap, her defiant spirit silenced by a single, black-fletched arrow. The spark of hope Turok had been trying to ignite in the other tribes died within him, replaced by a cold, all-consuming void. 
 
## The Outcast 
 
Grief-stricken but driven by a grim duty, Turok went to the other villages, not to rally them, but to warn them. He found no allies. He found only terror. Zelryn's message had been received. The other chieftains, their faces pale with fear, refused him entry. They saw him as a ghost, a man marked by a power they could not fight. To help him, even to speak with him, would be to invite the same fate upon their own families. He was no longer Turok, the respected leader; he was Turok, the harbinger of destruction. 
 
He was utterly alone, cast out by the very people he had sworn to protect. 
 
Leaving the Mistwood was not an act of fear, for death now held little meaning for him. It was an act of profound disillusionment. He would not stay to fight for those who had surrendered their will to live. He would not haunt the forest that had become his family’s tomb. With nothing but his father's axe on his back, a braid of Ylva’s hair tied around his wrist, and a despair as cold and deep as a mountain lake, Turok turned his back on the only home he had ever known and began to walk, a man with no destination and nothing left to lose.
