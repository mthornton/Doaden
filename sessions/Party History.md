# Party History

*A recap of the Penitents' journey so far, for reference at the table. Covers only what the party themselves witnessed or learned — no DM secrets.*

---

## Chapter 1: Welcome to Doaden
**Session 1 — October 20, 2025**

Four strangers — JonLief, Virgil, Luthien, and Wolfram — woke with no memory of how they'd arrived, seated together at a rough table in a stone dining hall crowded with other broken, hollow-eyed prisoners. Over a bowl of stew they traded a few words before Church of Azerai guards arrived, called all four names, and marched them to a blacksmith's forge.

There, one by one, each was held down and branded on the shoulder with the Mark of Atonement — a stylized sun with a single falling tear. Luthien was seized and branded first, without warning. JonLief, recognizing the futility of resistance, stepped forward next rather than be dragged to it. Virgil and Wolfram followed. One of the guards bore the same mark himself, faded with age, and told them plainly: every Penitent leaves the smithy branded, on their feet or as a corpse.

They were brought before **Vozruk the Redeemer**, who laid out the terms of their new lives: each owed five hundred years of service to the Church, a debt reduced only by completing missions successfully. He assigned the four of them together as a unit, gave them the night to get equipped and settled, and told them their first mission would begin at dawn.

At the armory, an ancient halfling quartermaster named **Finnian Quickstep** outfitted them: Wolfram took full armor, a shield, a longsword, and javelins; Virgil asked only for a cloak and staff; JonLief was given a bow, a sword, light armor, and a bear-fur cloak that fit him perfectly. Luthien, distracted by a bone guitar sitting atop a precarious pile of gear, grabbed it without asking and triggered a spectacular avalanche of stacked equipment across the armory floor, cutting a guard's arm in the process. Finnian banned him from the armory for life and sent him off with barely-functional castoffs — a cracked dagger, a broken short sword, torn leather armor — after keeping him working off the mess long into the night.

The other three ate dinner without him. Luthien rejoined the table much later, good-humored about the whole disaster despite a minor cut on his finger. Virgil, sympathetic, shared a piece of the edible fungus that grows from his own body.

The next morning, Vozruk laid out their first mission: a small island called **Dol**, off the southern coast in the Shattered Coast region, where suspicious activity and disappearances had been reported. A previous team of Penitents sent to investigate never returned. The party's orders were to reach the island, find out what happened to that team, and eliminate whoever was operating there.

Vozruk introduced **Fid**, the camp's wild-haired gnome inventor, who arrived on a bouncing, malfunctioning contraption he called the Puff-a-loco. Fid showed off two of his inventions: the **Ball of Undeath**, a copper sphere that — thrown to exactly the right height — bursts into shards that harm only undead creatures (thrown wrong, it hurts everyone nearby), and a **knitting spider**, a one-use clockwork device that leaps to fresh wounds and stitches them closed with a touch of magic. Vozruk judged the party too inexperienced to be trusted with the Ball of Undeath, and to prove his point, threw a knife that grazed JonLief's cheek — instantly healed by one of Fid's knitting spiders as a demonstration. Fid officially issued the party one knitting spider, entrusted to Wolfram — but quietly slipped Wolfram a second one as well, out of Vozruk's sight. Wolfram let the rest of the party know he was holding two. Afterward, Fid also quietly gave Luthien the Ball of Undeath, telling him not to mention where he got it.

The party set out from the military camp on the Castur River aboard a modest 30-foot ship, the ***Hreowan***, more suited to fishing than a military voyage. The trip to Dol Island took three uneventful weeks — until the night before they were due to arrive, when a violent storm drove the ship onto submerged reefs. The *Hreowan* was wrecked, and the party woke washed up on an unfamiliar beach.

## Chapter 2: Dol Island

The party woke scattered on an unfamiliar beach after the *Hreowan* wrecked, with no sign of the crew. Exploring, they came across puddles of blood on the ground but didn't investigate further, and disturbed a pile of old bones — two skeletons rose up to attack, and the party made short work of them.

A path through the vegetation led to stairs descending into the cliffside, their walls covered in old glass mosaics the party paused to admire. The stairs opened into a great sunlit cave built around a small island. Scattered across it were more bones and old, homemade jewelry — the remains looked to be only about two years old rather than ancient. A large apple tree on the island caught the party's attention, and they picked as many apples as their packs could hold.

At a stone door blocking the far side of the island, the party puzzled out a way through by wedging an apple into place to trigger it open. Suspicious of a ring of runes carved into the floor beyond, they used *mage hand* to tap each stone from a distance rather than risk it themselves, which revealed and let them avoid a hidden trap. Wary of a second trap on the bridge ahead, they had Wolfram cross first with a rope tied around his waist as a precaution.

In the first chamber, they fought and defeated two mummified guardian warriors. JonLief recognized the mirrored statue in the room as imagery from the Vasant Panchami festival. In the second chamber, they managed to capture a fleeing cultist and tried to question him — he answered only with a cry of "Hail Adephagia!" before biting off his own tongue and choking to death rather than talk. In that same room, Wolfram pushed through a wave of unnatural fear and dread to approach a cracked, bleeding tomb, and saw a bound woman inside look back at him with sorrow and terror in her eyes. When he brought the others over to look, she — and the sense of dread — had vanished.

In the third chamber, the party fought and defeated a monstrous creature made of coagulated blood. They emerged from the ruins onto a different beach, where the last remaining cultist stood and fought them alone while the rest of his group escaped by boat with barrels of blood already loaded aboard. Among the dead cultists' belongings, the party found a map and an amulet. Examining the amulet, JonLief touched one of its crystals and was instantly teleported back into the third chamber underground. The party quickly reasoned that touching the amulet's third crystal would trigger the same effect again.

With the fighting done, the party took a long rest on the beach, and the chapter ended.

## Chapter 3: Ald's Star

Stranded on the beach after clearing Saraswati's crypt, the party watched the cultists' ship recede into the distance with its cargo of barrels, the mainland's mountains a faint shadow across roughly three miles of open water. Too exhausted to give chase, and with night falling, they settled in to make camp instead. Wolfram suggested sheltering in the crypt below, but Virgil and Luthien refused — neither had any interest in bedding down among the cultists' corpses, or risking the guardian statues stirring again. They built a fire on the beach instead, ate rations supplemented by berries Virgil gathered, and JonLief took the night watch, resting through meditation rather than sleep.

The next morning was clear and calm. Studying the map they'd taken off one of the dead cultists, the party recognized it as the stretch of coastline across from Dol Island, with a location marked "St. Pyotr." Reasoning that this was their only real lead — and that walking back to Vozruk's camp would take months even if they knew the way — they rowed to the mainland to try to find it.

The crossing was uneventful. They landed on a rocky beach, found a road matching the one on the map, and followed it east toward St. Pyotr's, passing through a small village along the way.

As they approached, a halfling working a nearby field spotted them, dropped his tools, and ran for the village shouting "invaders!" JonLief called for the party to sheath their weapons and look friendly. They were met at the village gate by an older halfling named **Ald**, pitchfork in hand and furious, flanked by a red-haired halfling named **Red** — both with unmistakably green skin. Ald mistook them for another visit from the Alchemical Order, pressuring him to sell cabbages he wasn't ready to part with. When Virgil tried to explain he'd left the Order, Ald didn't buy it and moved to run them off. Wolfram stepped between the pitchforks and the party, explaining they were Penitents of the Church chasing heretics, not Alchemists — and Ald stood down.

Ald introduced the village as **Ald's Star**. When Luthien bluntly asked why he was green, Ald explained — with some pride, and a little offense at being asked — that his family had cultivated a strain of cabbage generations back that turns the whole village green if eaten daily; no magic involved, just farming.

Ald mentioned he was gathering a posse — not to chase the party off, but to hunt down whatever had been savagely killing the village's livestock at night. Wolfram, recognizing an opportunity, offered to help hunt it down free of charge, as a servant of the Church. Luthien objected — they had no money at all — so Ald offered a trade instead: help with the hunt, and he'd tell them what he knew about St. Pyotr's, which he flatly called "no church" but "a loony bin."

The party was brought into the village hall to eat with the community — about two dozen green-skinned halflings going about their evening. Virgil, still suspicious of the villagers, slipped away mid-meal and shapeshifted into a spider to scout the village while Luthien provided a distraction, performing for the room. His first song, a traditional harvest tune, was a hit — but his second, an original piece called "The Nuns of Nantucket," grew steadily more sacrilegious as it went, scandalizing the devoutly Emperor-fearing crowd. Ald cut the performance short and scolded Luthien, who snapped back that he served the Church only because he'd been forced to, and it had never done him any good. JonLief smoothed things over, framing Luthien as a renowned musician from cities where piety mattered less. Virgil's scouting, meanwhile, turned up nothing — the village really was just what it appeared to be, a simple farming community.

After dinner, the hunting party set out for the field where the last livestock had been killed: JonLief, Luthien, Wolfram, and Virgil, joined by Red, Ald's son **Bart**, Ald himself, and — over his objection — the village's sharp-tongued housekeeper **Alice**, who refused to be left behind.

They kept watch for about an hour before a massive, mutilated hound came out of the darkness — flaps of torn skin, open wounds, and iron bars and spikes worked into its body. It killed Alice almost instantly, tearing out her throat before anyone could react. As Bart and Wolfram engaged it, a second, unseen hound attacked from behind and killed Red just as violently. Wolfram eventually found a gap in the first hound's iron plating and cut it down with a single blow; the second hound turned on Luthien and Virgil, and after Luthien's rapier proved useless against it, he immobilized it with a spell long enough for Virgil to finish it off.

The party carried Alice's and Red's bodies back to the village, along with the corpse of one hound. Ald, grieving, asked what the creatures were and whether more would come. Wolfram told him he'd sensed the hounds weren't of this world, and that his senses were now attuned enough to detect their presence from miles off — he was fairly confident there were no more nearby, but urged the village to be cautious after dark regardless. Virgil examined the hound corpse he'd brought back and confirmed it matched nothing he'd ever studied; he disposed of the remains in the village's fire pit. The party spent the night in Red's now-empty house, where they found and took six jars of his pickled cabbage, having no coin or supplies of their own.

The next morning, before moving on, the party attended a wake for Alice and Red alongside the rest of the village, and spent the morning getting to know most of Ald's Star — a small enough community that they met nearly everyone, including people like William, who tended the village's horses and livestock, and Walter and Edith, whose young daughter Matilda kept the party entertained with constant chatter. Afterward, Ald made good on his promise: he told the party what he knew about St. Pyotr's — repeating his warning that it wasn't a real church — and gave them directions to find it.

## Chapter 4: St. Pyotr's

Following Ald's directions, the party made their way to St. Pyotr's — and even from a distance, something was clearly wrong. Dark clouds had gathered over the building, with tendrils of black fog visibly reaching down toward it. Before going any closer, someone conjured a bird to fly ahead and scout — but it didn't survive contact with the fog, dying the moment it flew into the haze.

The front doors were unlocked and unanswered. Inside the entry hall stood a bronze statue of Saint Pyotr; Virgil tried to push it over on a whim, but it was far too heavy to budge. Before they got much further, they heard cultists talking in the garden ahead and chose to strike first rather than wait to be found. The fight turned into a chaotic scrum as everyone tried to funnel through the same doorway at once, but the party made short work of the cultists guarding the garden regardless. A locked trapdoor in the garden resisted their attempts to get it open, and they moved on without finding out what was underneath it.

In the kitchen, they found the gruesome remains of a priest who'd been killed and split open by whatever had been living inside him — an unsettling scene, though nobody was shaken badly enough by it to lose their composure. In the library, a search turned up a book titled *Myths of the Old Gods*.

From a hidden vantage in the south bell tower, the party silently witnessed cultists execute a priest — hanging him from the bell's crossbeam and cutting him open as he died — before the cultists slipped away unseen.

Deeper into the sanitorium, they found a locked cell holding a prisoner who begged to be freed. Something about him didn't sit right with the party, and they chose to leave him locked away rather than let him out.

In the torture chamber, they fought and killed one guard and used magic to charm the other — then had the charmed guard walk back into the chamber before locking him inside and leaving him there. Among the room's clutter they found a stock of healing potions. They also found a second torture victim, a woman, still alive but barely sane after what had clearly been a prolonged ordeal. The party healed her wounds, but given her fractured mental state and their focus on the Church's mission, they made the difficult call not to bring her with them. They left her behind in an unlocked cell with a few days' worth of rations rather than risk taking her along — a decision made with real hesitation, weighing what little they could offer her against what the Church still expected of them.

In the sanitorium's upper office, they found the caretaker Father Ignatius dead, nailed to the wall, alongside his personal journal. It chronicled his long decline: what began as harsh but ordinary treatment of his patients grew steadily crueler over the years as he became convinced they were possessed by spirits from another realm, and that enough pain could drive the possession out. Near the end, his handwriting nearly illegible, he wrote of sensing the barrier between this world and that other realm growing thin — and believing that if he wore it down far enough, the spirits infesting his patients would be drawn back through it. A ring of keys was found on his body.

The chapel held the worst of it. The party fought through a group of cultists guarding the room first — an intense fight that saw JonLief dropped to 0 hit points, clinging to life through his death saving throws before the party turned the tide. Beyond them, a herald in an ornate headdress stood chanting over a black-mist ritual circle, with a bound figure laid across the altar nearby — already dead by the time the party reached them. Before the fighting reached him, the herald's followers taunted the party, accusing them of chasing revenge for Saraswati being "outplayed" by Adephagia, and promising that Brudda Death would "tutor" them in suffering to come — the first time the party had heard that name. The party struck the herald hard enough to break the ritual, but he proved strong enough to escape out the back of the chapel before they could finish him off.

## Chapter 5: The Inn

The party arrived at a busy roadside inn, and Luthien struck a deal to play music in the common room in exchange for food and lodging for the night — a performance that would end up mattering more than anyone expected.

The common room had its share of notable company. A table of slave merchants sat trading business nearby — openly dealing in people, and dealing only with humans as buyers or sellers. The party wanted nothing to do with them and kept their distance. Also staying the night were Master Thaddeus and his wife Marion of the Alchemical Order, waiting on a delayed shipment; when the party asked about Thaddeus's strange, fungal transformation, he gave them an explanation — later proven untrue — that it was the result of a vanity experiment gone wrong. The real story would come out later. Also present was Lady Ysabel Rochefort, traveling with her champion Ulfric Dreadmarsh, her lawyer, an assistant, and several guards; she was facing an accusation from her uncle, Lord Leofard Ironwake, of poisoning her niece, and had called for trial by combat to clear her name, with Ulfric set to fight as her champion in a few days' time.

That evening, a commotion broke out downstairs — a staged confrontation and brawl centered on a young Baron and his lover, engineered as a distraction. The party watched it unfold but chose to stay out of it; Luthien kept playing through the chaos.

It was only after things settled down that Ulfric was found murdered upstairs — and the knife left in his body was recognized as Luthien's own, stolen from him at some point during the night's chaos. Luthien was accused on the spot. When the local Sheriff, Eastwood, arrived to investigate, he quickly cleared Luthien — half the room had watched him playing music on stage at the time of the murder, an alibi nobody could dispute. Eastwood went on to crack the case himself: a waitress had drugged Ulfric with a sleeping tonic earlier in the evening and had also lifted Luthien's knife, and Baron Emeric Blackholt was identified as the one behind the whole scheme. Emeric was taken into custody. What became of him, the waitress, or the young Baron and his lover afterward, the party never learned.

While the inn sat locked down waiting on Eastwood, a very old man walked into the common room and sat down with the party — and strangely, nobody else in the room seemed to notice him at all, as though he simply couldn't be perceived by anyone but them. He thanked them for stopping the summoning of Brudda Death back at St. Pyotr's, offered nothing else about who he was or what he wanted, and simply encouraged them to keep up the good work before the moment passed.

The next morning, the party agreed to escort Lady Ysabel on the road to Solteres. Master Thaddeus and Marion joined them for the first leg of the journey — they were due to meet an acquaintance along the way in a few days' time before turning back toward home.

## Chapter 6: Swine Raid

The party set out escorting Lady Ysabel Rochefort toward Solteres, along with her retinue of servants and guards. Master Thaddeus and Marion traveled with them for the first leg of the journey, planning to meet an acquaintance on the road before turning back.

A few nights into the trip, Thaddeus took Virgil aside and finally told him the truth about his transformation — the story he'd given at the Inn had been a lie out of embarrassment. In reality, Thaddeus came from a long line of alchemists and had spent years obsessed with finding a way to extend human life, inspired partly by his mother's early death. His research into the disease-resistant biology of giant squids led him to test an experimental treatment on himself and Marion. The results weren't what either of them hoped for: Marion gained the ability to communicate telepathically but lost her voice entirely, her mouth and throat reshaped into something like a squid's beak, while Thaddeus's whole body transformed into what looks, to everyone else, like a giant worm.

Thaddeus also confided in Virgil about the real reason he and Marion were on the road: a farming village called Underwood, near Haleyon, has been suffering for two years from a strange affliction — green skin patches that progress into spore-bearing pods, eventually consuming and killing those infected. The Church had denied the village aid due to the ongoing war in the north, so the Alchemical Order sent Thaddeus and Marion instead. They were on their way to meet a colleague, Willowbread, who claimed to have found something similar growing farther east and was bringing samples and notes. Thaddeus asked Virgil to come help directly, dangling the chance to earn back his standing with the Order — but Virgil turned him down, telling Thaddeus he needed to stay with the party, who were still under standing orders from the Church.

The next day, the road ahead erupted into screaming: a band of Porcine beastmen was in the middle of raiding a wagon of produce. The party threw a bottle of oil into the fray and JonLief followed it with a fireball, and between the two the fight went far more easily than expected. In the chaos, though, several of the beastmen broke through to Marion and nearly killed her before the party could drive them off.

The wagon turned out to belong to Willowbread's party — the very colleague Thaddeus was meant to meet. Willowbread himself was dying from wounds sustained in the raid; with his last words, he gave Thaddeus a rough location for where the strange pumpkins had been found and warned him that a coven of witches was growing them — his group had avoided confronting the witches directly and had instead used a distraction to steal what they needed. Most of the cart's pumpkins had been ruined in the fighting, but Thaddeus salvaged what scraps he could.

The party did what they could for Marion, stabilizing her before she and Thaddeus went their own way. Afterward, Virgil examined the surviving pumpkin material and found that the magic wasn't in the flesh at all — it was concentrated entirely in the seeds, a blend of Enchantment, Necromancy, and Transmutation magic. With that information in hand, and Marion stable, Thaddeus gathered his notes and the pumpkin remains and set off back toward Underwood.

With the fight over, the party took a long rest before continuing on toward Solteres with Lady Ysabel. The following two weeks of travel passed without incident, leaving them still two days out from the city when the next leg of their journey began.
