# Baltus Thole
### Shepherd of the Office of the Lost Flock

> **He is not a villain and he is not a fight.** He is a large, devout, exhausted man who has been walking toward this party for weeks, and who wants exactly one thing: to bring all four of them home alive. That is the problem.
>
> See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md` for how the brand works and what the cloak means.

---

## First sight

You hear him before you see him — a soft irregular clinking, like a purse of coins carried by a man with a limp.

He is big. Not the Juggernaut's kind of big; the ordinary human kind, run to heavy through the shoulders and the gut, a man built like a door in a coat. Late fifties. Grey stubble, a wide flat face, hands like gardening tools.

The cloak is deep violet and it goes to his heels, and it is **covered** — collar to hem, front and back, sewn in careful rows — in **small silver lambs.** Thirty-one of them, if anyone counts, and they chime softly against one another whenever he moves.

> **⚠️ This is not the uniform.** The Office issues a lamb for every Penitent a Shepherd loses; almost every Shepherd puts them in a box and does not look at them again. **Baltus is one of two men in the Office who wears his.** *(The other is Ivo Kessler, and nobody has ever said that to Baltus's face.)* His colleagues think the coat is zeal — a private weight made public — and they are polite about it and they mean it as a criticism. **A purple cloak means Shepherd. A purple cloak that chimes means him.**

It is a great deal heavier than a cloak should be. He has never once adjusted it.

Across his back, a two-handed maul with a head the size of a loaf of bread. The haft is worn pale in two places where his hands go.

He is unhurried in a way that is difficult to be around.

---

## Who he is

Baltus Thole has been a Shepherd for twenty-two years and has brought home nine people.

He does not consider this a poor record. He considers it nine. He can give you all nine names, where he found them, what condition they were in, and what became of them afterward — and he checks. He writes to them. Two write back.

He is genuinely, unshowily devout. He prays at dawn because the rite requires it and then keeps praying afterward because he wants to. He does not preach, he does not threaten, and he has never in his life raised his voice to a Penitent.

**What he believes**, and he will say it plainly if asked:

The Church did not take these people's lives away. Their own choices did that. What the Church did was refuse to let the story end there — a debt, a road, and at the far end of it a way back that no gallows ever offered anyone. He has seen a woman walk out of the Office free at the end of her service. Once. He was thirty-one. He has been chasing that afternoon ever since.

**What he cannot hear:** that this is slavery. Not "will not" — *cannot*. Ask him and you get bafflement first, the way Ysabel goes baffled over breakfast. *You are not owned. You are in service, and there is a door at the end of it, and I am the man who has spent twenty-two years trying to get people to that door.* Press harder and he goes quiet and polite and slightly colder, and something in him files you as a man he will have to be careful with.

---

## The weight

**This is the whole character and it should be visible before he opens his mouth.**

**Every lamb on that cloak is somebody he lost.**

The ones he brought home get nothing. Nine people in twenty-two years walked back into the Office alive on his account, and there is not one mark anywhere on him to show it — because bringing them home was the job, and the job does not get a monument. What gets a monument is failure, and he wears thirty-one of them, and he will wear them until he dies.

Each lamb is struck by a silversmith in the Sunlit Citadel who has known him twenty years and long ago stopped asking what the visits mean. Each is stamped on the flank with **what took them**, and it is the repetition that does the work — **his cloak is a census of the evils of this land**, and he reads it every morning while he dresses:

| Stamp | On his cloak |
|---|---|
| **Unknown** — he walked the bearing all the way in and found nothing at the end of it, or found a body with nothing on it to say what had done it | **14** |
| Beastmen | 5 |
| Fever, wound, exposure | 4 |
| Slavers | 3 |
| Water | 2 |
| Heretics | 2 |
| Ran, and was never found | 1 |

**Fourteen unknown.** That is the number that has quietly ruined him — fourteen people the world simply closed over, and he does not know what did it, and so he cannot even hate the right thing.

> **And the thing that has ruined him more, which is not on the coat at all:** the dead pull exactly as strong as the living, right up until the body goes *(see `The Office of the Lost Flock.md`)*. **Every single morning of his career, the bearing he woke to might have been a corpse, and there has never been a way to tell.** He has hurried for weeks toward people who were already three weeks gone. He has done that more times than he has lambs.

**One runner.** In twenty-two years. Ask him about that one and he will change the subject, which he does not otherwise ever do.

He does not remove a lamb if the case is later mended. He was asked once. He said the failure happened whether or not it was afterward put right.

**And then, two weeks ago, he prayed over a new file — four names, out of contact since summer — and felt four pulls.**

Four. Alive. All of them.

He has not had a file pull like that in six years. **He set out the same morning.**

That is why he is coming, and it is why he is going to be so hard to hate: he is the happiest he has been in years, and the reason is them.

---

## How he hunts

- **Dawn prayer, once a day, a bearing for each living name.** No distance. He walks toward it and re-checks tomorrow.
- **He walks.** He does not ride and does not run. He is never faster than the party and he is never *not coming.* If they outpace him today he arrives the day after tomorrow, and the day after that, and this is far more frightening than a chase.
- **He asks.** Villages, tollhouses, inns. He is polite, he pays for information, and people remember him, which means the party can hear he is coming before they see him — *a big fellow in a purple cloak was asking after four men, three weeks back.*
- **He does not set traps or ambushes.** He walks up to your fire and sits down.

**When the pull goes dark** — because they've crossed into another diocese, or gone underground somewhere the divine can't reach, or died — he stops, and prays again the next dawn, and if it stays dark he goes to the last place it pointed and starts asking questions like an ordinary man.

---

## What he wants from this party

**He wants to take them in, and he genuinely believes this is the best thing that could happen to them.** He is not entirely wrong.

The party's file says out of contact since summer. What he will find is four Penitents who — depending on how Chapter 7 goes — are carrying:

- **A survivor of the first Dol Island team**, which is the one order out of Chapter 1 they can actually claim to have completed.
- **Evidence that a lord's chainmaster collared a branded Penitent.**
- **A ledger implicating half of Solteres in something the Church executes people for.**

**Coming in quietly might genuinely be their best move**, and Wolfram of all people may be the one to say so. That is what makes Baltus a *decision* rather than an encounter.

**And what he will actually do about Gimble is the thing to hold onto.** Vaskar's nightmare — the wrath even Ironwake cannot stand in front of — is not an army. It is this man, arriving on the fen road, being very quiet, and asking to see the ledger.

## ⚔️ Stat block — Baltus Thole

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful good — CR 6 (2,300 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 18 (breastplate and mail beneath the cloak)
- **Hit Points** 114 (12d8 + 60)
- **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 (+4) | 10 (+0) | 20 (+5) | 11 (+0) | 16 (+3) | 14 (+2) |

- **Saving Throws** STR +7, CON +8, WIS +6
- **Skills** Insight +6, Perception +6, Religion +3, Survival +6
- **Condition Immunities** frightened
- **Senses** passive Perception 16
- **Proficiency Bonus** +3

### Traits

- **★ Take Them Alive.** Baltus **never deals lethal damage to a Penitent.** A Penitent reduced to 0 hit points by him is **unconscious and stable**, not dying. He will break arms, legs and ribs to end a fight and he considers every one of those a mercy. *(Against anyone who is not a Penitent, he is under no such restraint — and the party should get to watch that distinction the first time someone else attacks him.)*
- **The Pull.** At dawn he knows the compass bearing to each living Penitent on his file, anywhere in the diocese.
- **Shepherd's Patience.** Immune to frightened. Advantage on saves against being charmed. **He cannot be provoked, taunted, or goaded into killing.**
- **Unhurried.** He never takes the Dash action and never flees. He does not need to.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** Two attacks with the maul.
- **The Maul.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* **+7** to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* **2d6 + 4 bludgeoning**, and the target must make a **DC 15 Strength save** or have its **speed halved until the end of its next turn** and be knocked **prone**. *He aims low. He is trying to stop you walking, not stop your heart.*
- **Ring the Bell (recharge 5–6).** One creature within 5 ft. makes a **DC 15 Constitution save** or is **stunned until the end of its next turn.** *A flat blow to the helm, or the head.*
- **Come Home (1/day, action).** Baltus speaks a Penitent's full name and file number. That creature makes a **DC 14 Wisdom save** or is **incapacitated until the end of its next turn** as the brand answers him — a pull in the shoulder like a hand on a collar. **Being brand-marked is the only reason this works; nobody else in the world can do it to them.**

### Reactions

- **★ Interpose.** When an attack he can see would hit a creature within 5 ft. of him, Baltus can step in and **take the hit himself.** He will do this for a Penitent he is hunting without hesitation.

> **That reaction is a deliberate three-way rhyme, and it is worth pointing at.** Vaskar makes other people absorb his blows. Wolfram absorbs blows for other people. **Baltus absorbs blows for the people he has come to take away**, which is the most confusing possible thing for the party to watch.

## Running him

- **Do not open with a threat.** He walks into their fire, sits down without being invited, accepts food if offered, and says their four names in full, slowly, as though checking them off. Then: *"You've been very hard to find, and you're all still breathing. Do you know how rare that is?"*
- **He is relieved to see them.** Play the relief. It is the most disarming thing he could possibly do.
- **He does not lie.** If asked what happens next, he tells them exactly: back to the Office, an account of their movements, a judgement. He does not know what the judgement will be and says so.
- **Never raise his voice.** Not once, not even in the fight.
- **If they run, he does not chase.** He watches them go, and he is there again in three days. Let them feel that.
- **If they fight him, he wins**, and nobody dies, and they wake up in a wagon with their weapons in a sack. **Do not make this a TPK — make it a humiliation with no blood in it**, which is far worse and leaves the whole plot intact.

### His weakness is not a stat

**He can be argued with, but only about the mission.** He is a servant of the Church who has spent twenty-two years believing the Church saves people. Show him a Penitent that the Church's own system enslaved, or a ledger proving the faithful of Solteres finance heretics, and you are not attacking him — **you are handing him a problem he cannot file.**

He will not switch sides at the table. He will go very quiet, and ask to see the book, and start walking somewhere else.

## Voice

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | Slow, low, formal. Long pauses that are thinking, not menace. |
| **Habit** | Uses full names and file numbers. Says *"come home"*, never *"surrender"*. |
| **Tell** | Turns one of the lambs over between finger and thumb while he listens — reading the stamp without looking at it. Does it without knowing. |
| **Never** | Never shouts. Never threatens. Never calls a Penitent a prisoner, a runaway, or a thing. |

**Three lines to have ready:**

> *"Thirty-one. Everyone counts them, and everyone asks, and I always answer, because it seems to me the number ought to be said out loud by somebody."* — and then, if they ask how many he saved: *"Nine. There is nothing on the coat for those. That was only the work."*

> *"I felt four of you. Four, and loud — you have hardly paid anything yet, and it comes off you like a lamp. I have prayed over forty of these files and got back nothing at all more mornings than I can stand to count, and I want you to understand that I was glad, that morning, in a way I have not been glad in some years."*

> *"No. I did not know you were alive. I have never once known that. I knew which way to walk."*

> *"You are not owned. There is a door at the end of it. I have watched one person walk through that door and I have spent the rest of my life trying to get somebody else there."*

## Hooks

- **He is the answer to "what are the consequences?"** — the standing question in `CLAUDE.md` that nothing has ever forced. He is that question with a hammer.
- **He can be pointed at Ironwake.** A collared Penitent is a Church matter. Gimble is a Church matter. **Baltus is the wrath Vaskar has been afraid of this whole time**, and the party is holding the evidence.
- **He is a way to reach Vozruk** — or to go around him. Baltus's file did not come from Vozruk *(see the Office file, decision 1)*, and a Shepherd who has learned the party completed the Dol Island order can say so to people well above a Redeemer's head.
- **Second meeting is where it bites.** First contact should end unresolved. Let them dodge him, or talk him into a delay, or hand him something to think about. Then have him turn up again — same pace, same cloak, one more thing he now knows.

---

## Open decisions

1. **When does he first appear?** *Recommendation: the road out of Bramblefen, or on the approach to Solteres in Chapter 8.* He is much stronger arriving after the party has done something defensible than before.
2. **Does he learn about Gimble immediately?** A Penitent in a slaver's cage changes his whole posture — and possibly his target.
3. **The fourteen unknowns.** He can put a thumb on each one and tell you the last bearing the pull ever gave him, and what he found when he walked there, which was nothing. **Worth writing two or three** — and having one of them go dark somewhere the party has been (the Shattered Coast, Dol, the fen) costs you nothing and pays enormously.
   > **And there is a version of this that is very hard to walk back from:** the First Penitents were five, and if that file was ever Baltus's, then five of the lambs on his coat are Ottilie Marsk, Aldous Penn, Sabin Ferrow, Grigor Tell — and a gnome. **Gimble Beren is on that cloak, stamped *unknown*, and he is standing right there.** Decide before their first meeting whether you want that, because it is the single strongest card in this NPC's hand.
4. **Does he already know Wolfram's name?** A baron's son who killed a bishop is a famous file. If Baltus recognises him on sight, their first conversation starts somewhere much more interesting.
