# Doaden campaign review

**DM ONLY — major future spoilers.** Written 7 September 2026, at the DM's stated point: after Chapter 7, Session 2, with one session expected to remain.

## Scope and confidence

This review draws on the chapter notes, Session 1 play log, campaign history and timelines, PC files, the principal world-lore files, the four NPC background PDFs, and the processed summaries of the relevant source adventures. It uses those summaries rather than rereading the entire raw adventure archive. It is a narrative and continuity review, not a comprehensive rules or stat-block audit.

The latest clearly identified play log I found is Chapter 7, Session 1. The Session 2 script contains the Anselm conversation and then breaks off during possible outcomes for Vaskar's reception. It does not establish the session's final state. Consequently, recommendations for the remaining session are conditional: this review does **not** assume that Gimble has been freed, Cobb has died, the revolt has begun, or Elizabeth's fate has been revealed.

For conflicts, use this order: what the DM and players actually established; explicit subsequent corrections; current NPC/world rules; current session prep; older prep; proposed future arc; source-module defaults. The documents sometimes mix these categories within a single paragraph.

## Overall assessment

The campaign is becoming a story about people treated as property learning what freedom requires. The progression from Penitents, to a captive goddess, to institutional torture, to Bramblefen is coherent. The most promising larger structure is:

**Rescue particular people → expose the institutions trading them → trace the cult's supplies → decide what to do about a captive goddess and the realm that holds her.**

The strongest connective device is the mundane commerce surrounding the supernatural horror. A ledger, a tariff stamp, a ship, a buyer, and a shipment can carry the party from JonLief's personal objective to Brudda Death without another mysterious stranger delivering a quest.

The biggest risk is that the campaign's outcome becomes more predetermined as the prep becomes more elaborate. Many passages prescribe how characters must feel, which rescues must fail, what the party must overlook, and how acts of kindness must later become tragedies. Those directions would be effective in a novel. At a table, they can weaken the relationship between player decisions and results.

The campaign has enough secrets. Its next improvement should be visible progress, consistent rules, and room for the players to change the intended ending.

## What deserves preservation

- **Bramblefen joins several PCs' interests naturally.** JonLief has his family; Wolfram has Anselm and the revolt; Gimble is the missing unit's survivor. Luthien's music and Virgil's research can connect the aftermath to the larger story without competing for a separate dungeon each.
- **The institutions have recognizable incentives.** Ironwake profits, Vaskar survives, Ysabel protects her position, and the Church protects its authority. Their interests overlap without needing every antagonist to be a knowing cultist.
- **The NPCs can reward particular kinds of interaction.** Durgan responds to protection of Mott, Cobb to kindness, Anselm to practical solidarity, and Corvant to evidence that this attempt will differ from his failed one. That is more useful than a succession of interchangeable persuasion checks.
- **The objects have history.** The guitar, amulet, knitting spiders, book, and even cabbage are memorable because the players acquired them through specific incidents. They do not all need to become indispensable finale keys; their remembered origins already do useful work.
- **Ald's Star provides emotional contrast.** Hospitality, music, food, and people with ordinary lives made the hound attack matter. More such scenes will make later horror stronger.
- **The Shepherds offer differing responses to the same institution.** Baltus's commitment to recovery, Hedda's illicit releases, and Sault's selective administration can produce substantive decisions. They work best as people the party can negotiate with, not a scheduled sequence of increasingly powerful pursuers.

## The earlier diagnosis of a “passive party” is too harsh

The existing Arc Plan calls the party passive and proposes collecting consequences on their inaction. The play record supports a more useful interpretation: **they are cautious, loyal to each other, and uncertain about their authority.**

They pursued the cult's map after the shipwreck, fought the hounds, interrupted a summoning, saved Marion, helped Erwana, and committed to Bramblefen when JonLief found his family's names. Virgil explicitly declined Thaddeus to remain with the party. Wolfram's reluctance to champion Ysabel is explained as duty, and the party discussed the practical danger of the duel. After learning that Ysabel owns slaves, refusing to risk their lives for her is especially understandable.

Some supposed failures were not meaningful choices in the first place. The ship was already pulling away from exhausted characters with a rowboat. The garden grate resisted their attempts to open it. The prisoner they left locked up actually was dangerous. They healed the woman at St. Pyotr's, left food, and unlocked her cell. These distinctions matter when assigning consequences.

**Recommendation:** give them a clear near-term objective and a comprehensible choice about authority. If they report to the Church in Solteres, as they already intend, let that happen promptly. Establish what their mission permits, what counts as success, and what they can refuse. Their later decision to obey or reject those terms will carry more weight than punishment for a reporting procedure nobody explained.

Avoid treating Wolfram's reassurance to Ald as a promise of permanent safety. The recorded statement concerned the immediate area at that time, and he advised continued caution. A later attack can be a new danger without becoming proof that he betrayed his word.

Sources: [Arc Plan §3.4](../world_lore/Campaign%20Arc%20Plan.md), [Chapter 3, Story section](../sessions/Chapter%203%20Alds%20Star.md), [Party History](../sessions/Party%20History.md), [Session 1 play log](../sessions/Chapter%207%20-%20Session%201%20-%20Game%20Play%20Notes.md).

## Highest-priority continuity and design repairs

### 1. The green needs one consistent set of rules

There are three separate issues here.

**Symptoms and dormancy conflict.** The containment rule says an infection on thin ground does nothing until the rift opens. Underwood already has years of visible progression and deaths. Elizabeth erupts and dies at Bramblefen, which is explicitly not thin ground, and her ashes support vigorous abnormal growth there. The text also calls her strain permanently invisible before describing a terminal visible eruption.

**The vaccine's stated effect does not match its victory claim.** It protects the uninfected and explicitly does not treat existing infection. The dormant infected are then described as the weapon it will defuse. Those people are already infected. Prevention of further transmission can still be a major victory, but it does not by itself neutralize the existing cases. Nor does a discovery in one laboratory establish that protection reaches the threatened population before the finale.

**Wolfram already has relevant powers.** His local character sheet expressly provides disease immunity and a Lay on Hands option that cures disease. That conflicts with “Rowan is the only immune specimen in the world.” These are abilities already written for this player, not an obscure future loophole.

**Suggested repair, subject to your intended homebrew:** distinguish ordinary transmission, local active corruption, and Elizabeth's directly imposed condition. Underwood could be beside an already active local breach; the finale would expand that breach's reach. Elizabeth's condition could be self-sustaining in her body and remains, without establishing transmissible infection in ordinary terrain. Describe her as invisible *until the terminal phase*.

Then decide openly how established healing interacts with it. One rewarding option is that Wolfram can cure individual infections and remains protected, while continued exposure can reinfect other people. Virgil develops durable, reproducible protection that can be distributed without a paladin present. Rowan can carry a uniquely transferable protection without being the only immune person. If the green instead bypasses normal disease rules, establish that distinction before a player spends an ability and discovers it has been disallowed to preserve the plot.

Define whether the research prevents infection, suppresses dormant infection, or cures active disease, and give the corresponding achievement its actual scope. The Order could provide manufacturing and distribution as an earned alliance. A successful vaccine might save thousands even if the portal battle goes badly; it does not automatically solve everything a manifested Jailer threatens.

Also, “a spore is a seed” should be an analogy, not a required scientific answer. They are different reproductive structures. The useful inference is that both carry the same magical agent or signature.

Sources: [Elizabeth and the Green §§3–4, 7, 10](../world_lore/Elizabeth%20and%20the%20Green.md), [Underwood](../world_lore/Underwood.md), [Wolfram's sheet](../players/Wolfram%20Eisenherz.md), [burn-ring prep](../sessions/Chapter%207%20DM%20Notes%20-%20Session%202%20Onward.md).

### 2. Fix Saraswati's location and the blood-harvest calendar

- Chapter 2 has her captured during Vasant Panchami, left for **666 days**, and then harvested.
- The First Penitents places that interval's end in **late June 1126**. On the apparent seasonal calendar, 666 days after an early-spring festival falls around winter, not late June. The roughly two-year-old remains are flexible enough; the precise number needs an anchor.
- The Blood of Saraswati describes a dockworker opening one of these shipments **four or five years ago**, although this harvest appears to begin only in summer 1126.
- Chapter 2 shows her body disappearing. Other lore places her in the Prison Realm. The Blood of Saraswati nevertheless states in present tense that she remains chained and being harvested beneath Dol.
- Corbin's contact with an imprisoned Saraswati is described as beginning years ago, another date to align with the capture and transfer.

**Suggested repair:** write six dates or intervals: capture, first-team arrival, first harvest, transfer to the Prison Realm, current party's arrival, next festival. Decide whether current freight consists of stored blood or an ongoing supply from another location. Move the dockworker incident into the current harvest window, or explicitly make it earlier cult cargo rather than Saraswati's blood. Do not create an earlier divine imprisonment merely to save an incidental anecdote.

This determines whether returning to Dol could still interrupt the supply, what the amulet can accomplish now, and why the final rescue must happen elsewhere.

Sources: [Chapter 2 DM Notes](../sessions/Chapter%202%20Dol%20Island.md), [The First Penitents](../world_lore/The%20First%20Penitents.md), [The Blood of Saraswati](../world_lore/The%20Blood%20of%20Saraswati.md), [Corbin and Orla](../world_lore/Corbin%20Wainwright%20and%20Orla%20Miller.md).

### 3. Adephagia needs a reason for the summoning she later opposes

Her cult captures Saraswati and harvests her blood explicitly to bring the Jailer to Doaden. The proposed finale says a Jailer loose in Doaden would destroy Adephagia's food supply, so she wants the party to stop him.

This is an excellent potential reversal, but the motive connecting its two halves is not established. Pick one: she intended a limited Aspect and the herald expanded the plan; the Tormentor faction diverted her supplies; or she knowingly made a bargain that proved worse than expected. Give the party evidence of the disagreement before she offers help.

Corbin's bargain fits naturally into a faction split: he wants a rescue, the Tormentors want manifestation, and Adephagia wants Saraswati contained. They can collaborate temporarily without wanting the same ending.

Sources: [Chapter 2](../sessions/Chapter%202%20Dol%20Island.md), [Arc Plan §8.3](../world_lore/Campaign%20Arc%20Plan.md), [Corbin and Orla](../world_lore/Corbin%20Wainwright%20and%20Orla%20Miller.md).

### 4. “Ashgate” cannot independently appear on freight if Cobb invented it

The Second Ledger says Ashgate is Cobb's private filing label, absent from outside registers. The blood-barrel prep proposes putting the same name on the cask to connect the records.

Either make Ashgate an alias supplied by the buyer, or preserve Cobb's invention and connect the barrel through a carter, tariff code, guarantor, or dated consignment. The latter preserves a small, satisfying piece of investigative work.

Also distinguish the cult's **financiers** from all the ledger's **purchasers**. “Ironwake is not among the guarantors” is compatible with the book implicating his operation; “Ironwake is not in the book” can conflict with the earlier description of decades of entries involving him. The ordinary working ledger should not be declared inherently worthless as evidence if it records a branded Penitent's captivity and suspect sales.

Sources: [The Second Ledger §§2–3, 7](../world_lore/The%20Second%20Ledger.md), [The Blood of Saraswati §4 and open decisions](../world_lore/The%20Blood%20of%20Saraswati.md), [Chapter 7 evidence notes](../sessions/Chapter%207%20DM%20Notes.md).

### 5. Chapter 7's clock must follow events, including cancellation

The present logic says:

1. Cobb dies when Vaskar leaves to watch the match.
2. Freeing Gimble cancels the match.
3. Time spent freeing Gimble nevertheless guarantees Cobb's death before the party returns.

Those statements do not automatically coexist. If the match disappears, Vaskar's scheduled departure changes. Saving Gimble may therefore buy Cobb time. That is a valid player-created outcome.

The same file says no substitute opponent exists and later suggests Yarel as a substitute. Decide Vaskar's actual policy beforehand. Do not replace the opponent only because the players removed the scene you expected to run.

Likewise, cancellation does not by itself explain why Vaskar would abandon a profitable detention of Ysabel. Give him a practical constraint: lack of Ironwake's authorization, unwillingness to attack an armed retinue without crowd cover, or a higher-priority emergency. Then adjudicate that constraint.

Sources: [Session 2 onward §§2, 6, 9–10](../sessions/Chapter%207%20DM%20Notes%20-%20Session%202%20Onward.md), [Run Sheet](../sessions/Chapter%207%20Run%20Sheet.md).

### 6. The Drain is still a possible route

The Session 2 update removes it because Erwana has left. But Cobb knows it, Denrick knows and trapped it, the party has been told where its entrance is, and it remains a physical feature of the compound. The same update later suggests exiting through it to reach Elizabeth's burn ring.

Remove Erwana as an available guide; retain the route as discoverable. A party that finds its own way should benefit from doing so.

Sources: [Session 2 onward §§1, 3, 5b](../sessions/Chapter%207%20DM%20Notes%20-%20Session%202%20Onward.md), [Denrick](../world_lore/Denrick.md), [Old Cobb](../world_lore/Old%20Cobb.md).

### 7. Resolve the trial's dates and procedures before Solteres

Chapter 5 puts the duel two days away, followed by weeks of travel, and Chapter 7 again puts it two days away. An explicit postponement after Ulfric's murder is a simple possible repair, if it fits what players heard.

Also decide: who has jurisdiction; whether either side can replace a champion; what happens if one fails to appear; and what evidence can suspend or supersede the duel. The writ proves Ironwake's connection to Bramblefen, not directly that Ysabel did not poison her niece. It creates leverage, but acquittal still needs a legal or negotiated mechanism.

Ironwake being unable to hire anyone else in two days is doubtful for a wealthy crime lord unless champion registration is already closed. Mott's temporary burnout is also not equivalent to permanent unavailability: his own rules allow recovery, and the trial is two days later.

Ysabel's means also need updating. Her older file describes a ruined mid-level merchant; Session 1 establishes a much larger fortune and multiple businesses. Financial distress is compatible with valuable assets, but distinguish ready cash, frozen or seized assets, debts, and saleable property. This changes both her help at Bramblefen and her options in court.

Sources: [Chapter 5](../sessions/Chapter%205%20The%20Inn.md), [Chapter 7 §12a and aftermath](../sessions/Chapter%207%20DM%20Notes.md), [Solteres](../world_lore/Solteres.md), [Session 1](../sessions/Chapter%207%20-%20Session%201%20-%20Game%20Play%20Notes.md), [Mott's recovery rules](../world_lore/The%20Juggernaut.md).

### 8. The brand system needs a short consistency pass

The Office says death does not stop tracking until the remains decay. Baltus's file still treats four pulls as confirmation of four living people and lists death as an immediate cause of silence. Vaskar plans to conceal Gimble in an unmarked grave, which does not solve magical tracking under the current rules.

Vaskar can plausibly be ignorant of postmortem tracking; that ignorance should be specified. Baltus can be hopeful rather than certain. The first team's catacombs cannot simultaneously suppress their brands and yield island bearings without a change in shielding or the location of the remains.

The proposed blood-tracer also needs a rule before it becomes operational. If an amputated shoulder does not carry the bond away from its owner, why do separated drops carry a trackable signature? A prepared sympathetic tracer could be different from the Shepherd's person-bond, but that is a new mechanism, not an automatic consequence of the old one.

There is minor career-calendar drift too: Baltus's 22 years, Hedda entering apprenticeship 16 years ago, and a requirement of ten solo years before taking apprentices do not fit as presently labeled. Specify whether each duration counts apprenticeship, years since branding, or total service.

## Narrative risks worth addressing

### Let rescues remain rescues

Elizabeth is already dead; the abandoned woman is scheduled to return possessed; Ald's Star may be persecuted for its skin color; its rescued inhabitants are proposed as murder victims; using the ledger may produce mass conscription; and several source adventures reveal that everyone supposedly being rescued is already dead.

Any one of those can work. Together, they risk teaching the party that attachment only gives the DM something to destroy.

Keep some durable successes: Erwana establishes a life, a freed captive becomes useful by choice, Mott gets an evening without violence, Rowan gets a secure home, the rescued community survives a later threat because the party prepared it. A dark world can contain improvements that stay improved.

The revolt table is especially worth revisiting. It awards battlefield leadership, but says a quiet rescue leaves Wolfram with nothing. Organizing a successful evacuation can earn trust as surely as fighting. Let casualties reflect the plan and interventions rather than a mandatory death quota or a fixed list of vulnerable people.

### Elizabeth's truth should be discoverable, not a compulsory misunderstanding

Her bargain is emotionally strong: the apparent loss of love was a consequence of saving Rowan. However, the notes repeatedly prescribe that JonLief must spend much of the campaign believing she abandoned their son.

Cobb can report that she stopped responding. He cannot establish what she felt. A player may correctly interpret it as illness or coercion immediately; let that interpretation stand. Rowan can retain memories of affection from before the terminal decline. Elizabeth should be present through something she chose, made, taught, or protected as well as through what was done to her.

Keep a reachable route to the bargain's truth. A Black Scroll is a strong option, but do not make the resolution depend on an unrelated late-game expedition inside Jormungand. The surviving bargain record could be a limited fragment attached to the cult's own operations.

Similarly, give the woman left at St. Pyotr's motives beyond punishing the party. Adephagia can exploit her distress without the narration declaring the party responsible for the abuse committed by her captors. Rescue, resistance to possession, and negotiation should remain possible if the players pursue them.

### Preserve ownership of the PCs

- **JonLief:** after Elizabeth's revelation, provide a concrete next action toward Rowan. His clan and the opponent he spared are also still available personal threads; neither must secretly belong to the cult.
- **Wolfram:** make leadership an invitation, with Anselm and the freed people retaining judgment and useful skills. Let faith and opposition to Church officials coexist.
- **Luthien:** give the guitar a noticeable response soon, but keep his existing talent and choices central. Do not imply that everything impressive about his music belongs to the instrument. A meaningful performance is a good trigger if he chooses to play.
- **Virgil:** offer observations and research opportunities. Do not prescribe that he treats Rowan callously or that conflict with JonLief is inevitable. They might invent a humane research arrangement, and that should succeed if it addresses the problem.
- **Gimble:** let Dave control the timing and tone of his testimony. Give him usable equipment and a near-term goal beyond reciting the first team's tragedy. He should be able to act at the start of the final session if already rescued.

### Avoid making every clue depend on a guessed connection

The barrel, artists, guitar, seeds, scrolls, and brands make a dense network. The players have much less access to it than the DM does. The claim that the cosmic plot has vanished is now outdated: the old man's visit, Underwood, the book being read, and the scarred buyers all keep it present. The current need is an actionable connection, not another cosmology lecture.

Give each essential conclusion several independently accessible routes. Rowan's buyer might be found in the working ledger, recovered from someone who actually read the entry, or traced through the city's sweep trade. Talwyn cannot reproduce a page he never saw; establish his access before using him as a backup.

The prisoners' suffering and a blood barrel also make Bramblefen look like a ritual site for sound reasons. If it is only a transport stop, give affirmative evidence of that distinction. Do not treat the players as foolish for combining clues the prep deliberately places together.

## Finishing Chapter 7 in one more session

**Start from a written state snapshot:** current fictional time; each PC's location and resources; Gimble's freedom and equipment; Cobb's condition; Vaskar's knowledge and location; Mott and Durgan; arms distributed; guards defeated or displaced; revolt status; and who holds each document. Include what the party actually learned about Elizabeth and Rowan.

For a remaining session of roughly 2.5–3 hours, budget approximately:

| Table time | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 10 minutes | Recap the situation and immediate threats; let the party choose its plan. |
| 25–35 minutes | Resolve outstanding access, arming, rescue, or positioning that makes the climax possible. |
| 60–75 minutes | One principal confrontation or coordinated escape. Run the wider revolt mostly through consequences and brief decisions. |
| 25–35 minutes | Secure people and documents; resolve the immediate Bramblefen outcome. |
| 15–25 minutes | One emotional aftermath scene and the next destination. |

This is a budget, not a scene order. If a major fight has already finished, spend more time on aftermath. If both Cobb and Gimble remain inaccessible and several major confrontations remain, a single session requires cutting encounters or finding a common resolution; speeding through every prepared scene will dilute the important ones.

**Keep:** a usable lead to Rowan; Gimble's participation; the consequence of the party's choices for the captives; disposition of the ledgers and the bargain with Ysabel.

**Compress or omit if unplayed:** repeated fruitless questions in the Pens, unused entrance branches, a separate fight for each guard post, eighteen full introductions, and multiple last-minute lore revelations. The burn ring, guitar response, and Gimble's full history do not all need to happen before the same curtain.

**Choose one main ending:** Vaskar confronted, the captives evacuated, or control of Bramblefen decided. Mott can be negotiated with or function as a complication; he does not require a separate boss fight. Vaskar escaping or bargaining can still close this chapter if the rescue objective resolves.

**Check the combat assumptions.** The Run Sheet estimates the tuned Vaskar plus two thugs at roughly 1,300 adjusted XP without establishing a new CR. Reducing his HP and removing extra actions does not validate that number by itself. His original file also calls a CR 5 solo opponent “hard,” while the Run Sheet's own four-PC thresholds place 1,800 above its 1,600 deadly threshold. Treat the custom encounter as unverified rather than trusting its label.

Mott's two raging slams average 32 damage if both hit; Wolfram's sheet lists 28 HP. That is a practical warning about how quickly the encounter can turn. Use actual remaining resources and the now-possible fifth PC. Keep escape, surrender, cover, and Durgan's intervention meaningful. If Vaskar redirects hits onto Cobb, telegraph the shielding before an apparently ordinary attack unexpectedly kills him.

The contradictions in the old arrival rosters also matter operationally: account for the same twenty guards across scenes rather than replenishing posts. The two Hall servants and Ysabel's household need places in an evacuation alongside the named eighteen.

## Direction after Bramblefen

**Resolve Rowan early in Solteres.** Once JonLief learns the buyer and city, three prescribed chapters of delay will be difficult to justify. Let Rowan's rescue intersect the trial and investigation if convenient, but do not make it wait for Chapter 9 simply because the older map says so.

**Let the planned Church report happen.** A local office or Baltus can give the party an immediate institutional response. The fuller reckoning with Vozruk can come later. Gimble provides a concrete mission achievement; record it as such, with a tangible change in standing or debt if the Church credits it.

**Use Solteres to reveal the supply chain.** The working ledger, Cobb's hidden record, the writ, and tariff records can answer different questions. Let Ironwake be arrested, ruined, killed, negotiated with, or bypassed if the party finds another route. Destroying a shipment, exposing a financier, or freeing artists should measurably reduce the cult's capabilities.

**Introduce Corbin as a useful artist with an agenda.** His connection to Luthien is promising, but he should be discoverable as compromised before the finale. Decide what he knows about the ritual and why Saraswati cannot clearly warn him. He currently destroys his best paintings to conceal the brush, which deprives her of the appreciation he claims to seek; this can be deliberate selfishness or a mistaken strategy, rather than an overlooked contradiction.

**Underwood should reward Virgil with real work.** Let diagnosis, a small treatment success, a protected evacuation, or research collaboration change lives before the eventual vaccine. A single reveal that his thesis was right is less satisfying than watching him solve a difficult problem over time.

## Keep the finale flexible

The proposed rescue objective is strong: the party frees Saraswati while holding off the Jailer. Three restrictions in the Arc Plan need reconsideration.

1. **Ald's Star does not have to become a 350-person town.** The plan assumes eighteen months of growth, while the next Vasant Panchami is roughly five months away. Real-world campaign duration is not fictional elapsed time. A smaller investigation, a temporary refuge or festival gathering, or a different town can support the finale. If the freed people choose another home, follow that consequence.
2. **The amulet is a volume, not a five-person quota.** Its established effect takes everything within a ten-foot-diameter, eight-foot-high cylinder. The plan assumes only four or five people fit and somebody must be abandoned. Actual packing, bodies of different sizes, and player ingenuity may change that. Establish any additional limit beforehand or accept a plan that gets everyone out. It returns to Room 3; the tomb was in Room 2, so the closing image needs a short walk rather than a destination retcon.
3. **The investigation should be able to prevent the summoning.** The processed Return of the Jailer summary describes an unavoidable fifth murder, and the proposed conversion assumes a confrontation regardless. Let catching the killer early, saving a victim, or interrupting supplies avert or weaken manifestation. If the party prevents the planned boss fight, that is a campaign victory.

For an objective-based last battle, write the actionable rules in advance: how to reach the prisoner, what opens the bonds, what the blood anchor does, what happens when it is disrupted, and how to escape. Artifacts and allies should improve the odds without making one lost object or one rejected NPC alliance an automatic failure.

The Emperor is worth keeping mysterious, but his limits need to be clear to the DM: why can he perceive and privately visit the party, why can he not perform the rescue himself, and what does he want? A revelation that he is the old man should explain something already experienced. It should not give him authority to dictate the ending. Also distinguish the ancient God Emperor from Fredric enthroned in 707 PA; the recurring title currently obscures the chronology.
