# The Second Ledger

> **DM-facing design document.** What is actually written in Old Cobb's hidden book, what it reveals at each level of scrutiny, and what it does to everyone who touches it.
>
> **Status:** built on established canon (`Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`, `Old Cobb.md`, `Erwana.md`, `Solteres.md`, the Session 1 play notes). **Names of the Solteres figures in §7 are new and yours to change.** Open decisions at the end.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

For thirty-eight years Old Cobb kept a true copy of the Bramblefen sales, and buried in it — unremarkable, one line at a time, years apart — is a **procurement record.** A buyer with no name has been purchasing people from that holdfast since before most of Solteres was born: a steady trickle for decades, and then a surge in the last two years. Predominantly women. Disproportionately artists. Paid for with drafts guaranteed by real, respectable, named counting houses in Solteres. **Cobb thinks he is holding evidence of a trade in people. He is holding evidence of heresy** — and heresy is the one thing the Church of Azerai will actually burn a city over.

---

## 2. What the book physically is

A bound quarto in cheap calf, about four hundred pages, roughly two-thirds full. It has been rained on at least once and buried in a hole under a flagstone for four weeks.

The hand is small, careful, and clerkish, and it degrades across the volume as Cobb's eyes go — the last fifty pages are noticeably larger and less even.

**It runs thirty-eight years and stops about six weeks ago, mid-page.**

**Why it stops there:** Vaskar had begun to suspect information was leaking out of his operation. Cobb stopped writing that same week and hid the book. He gave it to Erwana a fortnight later, on the night she ran.

**⚠️ Worth deciding now (and recommended: yes):** the last complete entry in the book is a **cult sale.** Cobb didn't merely stop because he was frightened. He stopped because he had finally written down the thing he had spent four decades circling, and understood that the book now had to leave the building or be burned. That makes his final act a decision rather than a reflex, and it means the most important entry in the ledger is the easiest one to find.

## 3. What it reveals, at four levels

Give these out as separate moments. Do not collapse them.

### Level 1 — a glance *(this is Ysabel's read, and JonLief's at the campfire)*
Names, prices, dates, buyers, going back decades. **Politicians, noblemen and merchants of Solteres who have bought people.** Nobody in that city greatly minds a man buying people — but a *second* set of books means somebody was hiding something, and men do not hide a thing they wouldn't be damaged by.

**Conclusion a reasonable person draws: this is ordinary corruption, and it is worth a fortune in leverage.** Ysabel is holding this conclusion right now, and it is wrong.

### Level 2 — an hour with it, reading properly
A buyer recurs. Not often — every year or two, sometimes with five years between — but across the entire length of the book.

Cobb had to file them somehow, and men who will not give a name cannot be filed. So he invented one for his own books, the way a clerk does when the alternative is an unbalanced page.

He wrote them down as **"Ashgate."**

There is no house called Ashgate in Solteres. There never has been. **The party will carry that word into the city, and it will not appear in any register, guild roll, or tax list they can find.**

**And the payments are the crack in it.** Men who will not give a name still have to pay, and coin in that quantity does not travel in saddlebags. The Ashgate purchases were settled by **letters of credit guaranteed by real, named Solteres counting houses** — because Cobb, being a clerk, recorded the instrument as a matter of course. He never once thought about what he was recording.

### Level 3 — the whole book, and arithmetic
This needs a day, or someone very fast. *(See §8 — Talwyn Ceth.)*

**The pattern:**

- **Thirty-eight years of Ashgate purchases**, running in an unbroken thread through a book that is otherwise ordinary commerce.
- **Predominantly women.** Not exclusively, but overwhelmingly.
- **Disproportionately artists.** Where Cobb noted a trade — and he often did, because a skill affects a price — the Ashgate lots are thick with them. *A lutenist. A woman who paints. A singer out of a temple choir. A girl who carves.* Nobody buying mill labour asks for a girl who carves.
- **And then the last two years.**

**The count is the horror, and it is just addition.** Across the first thirty-six years, three or four people a year — call it **a hundred and forty**. In the last two years alone, **sixty more.** Two hundred people, and nearly a third of them since the summer before last.

**Say the number out loud at the table. Let a player do the adding.**

### Level 4 — cross-referenced with what the party already knows
Nobody has to explain this to them if they've been paying attention:

- The surge begins **about two years ago** — the same season as the two dozen unarmed dead in the sunlit cave on Dol Island.
- Erwana described the buyers: hooded, and covered in layered scars, old and new, more than any beating accounts for.
- The party stood on a beach on Dol and watched barrels go aboard a ship.
- There is a woman with four arms chained in a tomb with a wound in her side that has been open a very long time.

**This is not a trade in labour. It is a supply line.** The ledger is a procurement record for something that consumes people, it has been running since before anyone alive can remember, and **two years ago it started needing considerably more.**

## 4. Why "artists" matters

Saraswati made her art-items to keep herself alive on the mana of people appreciating great work (`Saraswati and Adephagia.md`). Adephagia has been buying artists off a slave block for decades.

You do not have to state the mechanism. The party can hold two facts — *she is fed by art* and *her enemy has been quietly buying up artists for forty years* — and draw the conclusion themselves.

**And Luthien is carrying one of her items.** If the guitar's nature ever surfaces, the ledger is the document that makes it frightening rather than flattering: he is holding something the people in this book were bought to prevent.

**Kesh Vanner** — Stock, half-elf, sixty-odd, forty years a ship's navigator, sold twice already, currently in the Auction Yard — is your living confirmation. The Arc Plan already asks whether he remembers the black-robed buyers asking after artists. **He does.** He was passed over twice because he could only navigate.

## 5. Old Cobb — what he did, and why he's wrong

Cobb kept the book because a man who spends forty years writing down what happens to people eventually cannot bear that no one else will ever read it. He does not think of himself as brave. He thinks of himself as accurate.

**His instruction to Erwana:** run as far from Solteres as your legs will carry you, find a Church — any Church, in any city but this one — and put the book in their hands. The Solteres church looks the other way about slavery and has for years. But the Church is *good* underneath. Somebody in it will do something.

**He is right that they will act. He is completely wrong about why.**

The Church of Azerai has ruled slavery illegal and does nothing about it anywhere, in any diocese, ever. A ledger full of slave sales will be received politely and filed.

But **Crimen Grave Hereses** — High Bishop Silas I's first act, 693 PA, worship of any god but the true God, punishable by death — has never been repealed. It is the law the Great Heresy Purge was run under. It is the Church's one live nerve, and this book touches it directly: **respectable men of Solteres have been financing the servants of an old god for thirty-eight years.**

**So the Church will move. Hard. And not one line of it will be about slavery.**

Cobb spent forty years quietly building an instrument he does not understand, to end a thing it will not end. **Do not let him find out.** If the party reaches him alive, let him ask whether they'll take it to a Church, and let them say yes, and let him be glad.

## 6. What the Church will actually do

Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters.

**One: it will burn people.** Accusation was enough in 694 and it is enough now. The names in that book are men who guaranteed drafts, and some of them knew exactly what they were funding, and some of them were laundering, and at least one of them will be a chandler who was told where to send the paper and never asked. All of them burn the same.

**Two: it will change nothing about slavery.** The trade continues. Bramblefen has a successor within a season.

**Three — and this is the real cost — the Church seizes the property of convicted heretics.**

Mills. Warehouses. Ships. **And the people working them.**

Seized property belongs to the Church. People belonging to the Church who came to it through crime are not slaves — the Church does not hold slaves. **They are sinners, being offered a road back. They are Penitents.**

**Hundreds of collars come off, and hundreds go on, and the Church counts it as a rescue.** A sun with a single tear, burned into the shoulder, and five hundred years.

**That is what the party is buying if they use this book, and they should be able to work it out before they decide.** Wolfram is the one who'll see it first. Let him.

## 7. Who is in the book

Five names, escalating. **All new — change any of them.**

| Name | What they are | How guilty |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldric Vantour** | Salt and spice importer. Dead twenty years; his house trades on. Guarantor on the earliest Ashgate drafts in the volume. | Long dead. **His grandsons run the house and know nothing.** Proves the depth — this predates everyone. |
| **Symeon Tallow** | Chandler. Small, careful, sixty. His house guaranteed four drafts. | **Told where to send the paper and never asked.** The one who is horrified. The one who burns anyway. |
| **Lady Hesper Dunmoor** | Ysabel's chief rival in the dress trade. Guarantor eleven times, most recently last spring. | **Knew.** Not a believer — she was paid handsomely to be a respectable name on a draft. |
| **Magistrate Belden Roke** | Sits in Solteres. **Signs the sentences that make humans into Terms**, and guaranteed six Ashgate drafts. | Guilty twice, in a closed loop: he manufactures the stock and finances the buyer. **The clearest single indictment in the book.** |
| **Bishop Aurelian Crest** | Of the Solteres diocese. One draft, nineteen years ago. | **Optional — see §11.** The only Church name in the volume, and the reason Cobb's plan curdles completely if you use him. |

**Lord Leofard Ironwake is not among them**, and that's deliberate. He sold to Ashgate; he did not finance them. The writ in Vaskar's strongbox is what ties Ironwake to Bramblefen. Keep the documents doing separate jobs.

**Ysabel is not in it either** — she bought labour, she never guaranteed a draft. She will check. She will be relieved. Then she will realise that the seizures in §6 do not require her to be in the book at all.

## 8. Who can actually read it

- **JonLief** read enough at the campfire to find Elizabeth and Rowan. That's Level 1.
- **Erwana cannot read.** She carried it four weeks without knowing anything but that it was old.
- **Level 3 needs a day** with the whole volume and someone who can hold columns in their head.
- **Talwyn Ceth** — Stock, half-elf, 30s, a merchant's scribe before the collar, in the Pens — **is literate, fast, and can reproduce a ledger page from memory.** He is the natural analyst and he is sitting in a cage at Bramblefen. Freeing him is how the party gets Level 3 without a montage.
- **He is also the backup copy.** If the book burns, is seized, or is handed to Ysabel, Talwyn can reconstruct the Ashgate entries. **Do not tell the party this** — let it be the thing that saves the arc if the prop is lost.

## 9. Ysabel — she is wrong, and finding out should frighten her

She has glanced at it and concluded Level 1: names, prices, embarrassment, leverage. Her plan is blackmail — squeeze the men in the book, have them squeeze Ironwake, get the charges dropped. **Within the world she understands, this is a sound plan.**

**Blackmailing a man over a bribe is business in Solteres. Blackmailing a man over heresy means he has no option but to kill you**, because the alternative is the fire. The moment she reads deeply enough to understand what Ashgate is, her leverage becomes a death sentence she is carrying in a satchel.

**Play the flip as a scene.** She goes quiet. She reads a page again. She asks — carefully, not accusingly — whether the party knows what this is.

**What it buys you:** a reason for her to stay close to the party rather than vanish with the book, which is the outcome your original notes were trying to engineer by keeping it from her. **Now she wants their protection**, and possibly wants them to take the thing off her hands.

**And she is exposed twice over.** Her mills run on bought labour. If the Church starts seizing heretics' property in Solteres, she is not a spectator — she is a candidate. The party will be holding the document that decides.

## 10. Three doors, one sentence — how to run all of it

This is the spine, and the ledger is where it becomes legible.

| | The system | The justification |
|---|---|---|
| **Stock** | Non-humans, owned outright, permanent | *They cannot make meaningful lives alone. Fed, housed, safe, given purpose.* |
| **Terms** | Humans, a purchased sentence, finite | *He chose it. It's lawful. He walks away clear.* |
| **Penitents** | Sinners, five hundred years, branded | *Their worthless lives now have a purpose. A chance at redemption most never get.* |

**All three say: they are better off held, and we are doing them a kindness.**

**Run every defender of all three in the same register** — the one already written for Ysabel in `Solteres.md`:

1. **Bewilderment first.** Not defensiveness. They assume you've misunderstood the arrangement and explain it again, more kindly.
2. **Then the category argument.** *The ruling concerns people.* / *It's a term of service.* / *A Penitent is not a slave, it's mercy — and frankly an ungrateful way to describe it.*
3. **Then cold**, once they know they're being judged. Never cruel. Never loud.
4. **Never converted by one good roll.**

**Three specific consequences:**

- **A churchman told "we are slaves" reacts exactly as Ysabel did at breakfast.** Blank incomprehension, then the category argument, then chill. Same beats, different costume. A table that has met both will feel it without you naming it.
- **Vaskar's fear of the Church is a property claim, not a moral one.** He is terrified of holding a collared Penitent because **that one belongs to somebody else.** Not because it's wrong.
- **Anselm's question to Wolfram — *"Who's in charge of you?"* — is the whole theme in four words**, asked by a man in a collar of a man wearing a brand, and neither of them means it as a trap.

## 11. Open decisions

1. **Is the last entry in the book a cult sale?** *Recommended yes* (§2). Makes Cobb's stopping a decision.
2. **Is Bishop Aurelian Crest real?** Including a Church name makes the betrayal total and gives the Solteres diocese a concrete reason to look away — but it removes the party's last clean institution. *Recommend holding him in reserve and deciding after you see how the table reacts to §6.*
3. **Why predominantly women?** You do not need an answer to run it, and the not-knowing is good. Options if you want one: the ritual requires it; Adephagia's spite toward Saraswati; or simple economics — women were cheaper and less likely to be missed.
4. **Does the party ever learn the seizure consequence *before* they hand the book over?** *Strongly recommend yes.* A choice made in ignorance is not a choice. Vozruk, a Solteres churchman, or Ysabel's lawyer can all say it out loud without meaning to be cruel.
5. **What is "Ashgate"?** Currently: a filing name Cobb invented. Alternative — the buyers gave it themselves, which means somebody chose it, which means it *means* something. Slightly better hook, slightly more work.
6. **Does Cobb ever learn what his book actually does?** *Recommend no.*
