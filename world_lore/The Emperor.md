# The Emperor
### The God Emperor — an Old God, nearly starved, trying to come back. The old man at the Inn, and Cassian's patron.

> ⚠️ **DM ONLY.** Nothing on this page goes into a player-facing file or into `players/`. **The party has met him once and does not know who he is. Cassian has met him as "Bishop Osric" and believes the cover story. Channing doesn't know either, and won't be told** — he may work it out himself, which is the point.
>
> **Source:** the DM, 20 Oct 2026, unless marked otherwise. This replaces the proposal in `Campaign Arc Plan.md` §7 wherever they differ.

---

## 1. What he is

**The Emperor is an Old God** — and the Old Gods are not gods. They are **ancient creatures of a single race, whose name is lost to time**, that **feed on the worship of others**. The more they feed, the stronger they grow, until next to people they seem to be gods. **As long as they have a flow of mana, they live.** They were never numerous. *(See `Old Gods.md`.)*

## 2. How he became the only god

- **His twisted logic:** he decided his fellow Old Gods were **cruel to their worshippers**, and set out to consolidate power and **starve them**.
- **His advantage:** he was **the only Old God who studied human nature.** He was the first to work *directly* with people — to **codify the rituals**, and to **build intolerance of any other god into the religion itself.**
- **By the time the others understood what was happening, it was too late.** He was too strong, and **his followers forced the followers of the other gods to convert or die.**
- *(This is the Ascension the party read about in `Myths of the Old Gods` — the shuttered temples, the burned Black Scrolls, the rewritten history, the "Sole Light of the World." See `What the Party Knows - The Book.md`. **The book is the Church's account of it; this page is what it actually was.**)*
- That was the world for a long time.

## 3. The Apocalypse, and the long starvation

- **The Apocalypse (Year 0 PA) cost him almost all of his stored mana. He nearly died, and he disappeared.** *(What the Apocalypse was is deliberately undefined for now.)*
- **It also shook the faith.** In the vacuum, **humans took over and ran the religion in his name — but were not really loyal to him.** *(The Church is "re-founded" in 147 PA — `world_lore/Timeline.md`.)*
- **Within a generation the Emperor was a memory**, and most people came to believe the stories of him were allegory.
- **Worship no longer reached him** — it was aimed at an idea, not at him — so the flow of mana became **a trickle.**
- **It took hundreds of years before he was strong enough even to see the world, and hundreds more before he could act on it.** **He is still very weak today.**
- **Then the Fredrics.** In **707 PA**, after the War of the Five Kingdoms, a mortal, **Fredric I**, took the title of **Emperor** and made it **hereditary**. **The current Emperor is Fredric IX.**
- **The doctrine** *(DM, 21 Oct 2026)*: the Fredric line is **the mortal representation of the true Emperor**, a bloodline **chosen by the Emperor to carry on his will until his return.** When he returns, **he will be born into the Fredric line**, and the sign will be that **that Emperor Fredric is immortal.** So **every reigning Fredric can be held up as possibly the true Emperor** — disproven only by his death, at which point the heir becomes the new possibility.
- **All of it is untrue.** The line was never chosen; **Fredric I simply saw an opportunity and took power.**
- **The Fredrics believe it anyway.** The modern heirs believe the lie and **their own divinity**, and **secretly pour a great deal of the Church's resources into researching magic to extend life.** No major breakthroughs — but it is a hope they cling to.
- **Below the throne, faith has thinned.** Almost nobody in the Church believes there ever really was an immortal Emperor; the doctrine is kept because it holds the Church and the throne together.
- **That doctrine set the true Emperor back badly.** Worship that was already aimed at an idea is now aimed at a man on a throne, and **the mana reaching him narrowed even further.**
- Meanwhile, **the Old Gods who survived his reign have been gathering strength too.**

## 4. What he wants

> 🔑 **He wants to come back to power.** And **someday he means to destroy the false Emperor, Fredric** — though he is nowhere near strong enough to try.
>
> ⚖️ **The irony of "may he rise again."** The prayer is said in every village — and **it is a prayer for Emperor Fredric** to *rise again* as the true Emperor, proven by becoming immortal. Ordinary people say it more simply — *"we pray for his return"* (Ald, Ch3) — which is the doctrine in a farmer's words. **It describes the true Emperor's goal exactly, it is said by his own faithful, and it is addressed to his usurper.** It feeds him nothing.

**How he works:** he **watches**, and occasionally spends what little strength he has **to influence the Church**. Sometimes he acts **to strengthen the faith**; sometimes he must act **to weaken another Old God.** He can never afford much at once.

### ⚠️ Hooks in the Fredrics' life-extension research *(proposals — none is canon)*
- **The Alchemical Order.** A Church paying for longevity magic is the Order's best customer — and **Virgil was sentenced as a blasphemer for fungal symbiosis research that is, in effect, a theory of living longer.** The Church may be quietly funding the same work it punished him for.
- **Saraswati's blood.** A god's blood is the most concentrated life in the world. If anyone near the throne learned what is in the forty-eight casks, **the Tormentors would have a second buyer** — and the throne a reason to protect the trade.
- **The true Emperor's view.** He wants the line destroyed; **its own longing for immortality is the obvious lever.** A Fredric who reached for the wrong source of life would be the kind of opportunity he waits centuries for.

## 5. Why he wants Saraswati free

**Adephagia is getting stronger** — she needs no worship, she feeds on vice itself, and she is the strongest surviving Old God *(`Adephagia.md`, `The Three Faiths.md`)*. **With Saraswati imprisoned, Adephagia grows even faster. If she gets too strong, the Emperor will never regain power.**

So **freeing Saraswati is a move against Adephagia**, not a kindness.

> ⚠️ **Implications worth holding onto** *(proposals, not rulings)*:
> - **The irony is total.** He is the reason Saraswati starved in the first place — he shut her temples. Now he needs her standing up again.
> - **His help has a ceiling.** He wants Saraswati *free and pushing back against Adephagia* — not thriving enough to become a rival herself. A party that gathers every vessel and makes Saraswati strong may find him less helpful near the end.
> - **Brudda Death** is not an Old God, but a convergence that hands the world to the Jailer ends any hope of his return just as surely. That is why he thanked the party for stopping the summoning at St. Pyotr's.
> - **He is not a good man with a bad past.** He is still the being who decided he knew better than every other god and made convert-or-die a doctrine. His goals happen to line up with the party's for now.

## 6. Cassian Zev — his patron, in disguise

- ✅ **He is Cassian's Hexblade warlock patron.** Cassian's magic really is the Emperor's gift. **Cassian does not know that.**
- ✅ **Alias: Bishop Osric** — Old English for **"god-ruler"** (*os* = god, *ric* = ruler). Cassian may call him "His Grace" or "the Old Bishop."
- ✅ **Cover story:** he is **the Emperor's top bishop**, granted his very long life **as a reward for his faith and devotion.** Cassian believes it completely.
- He has **appeared to Cassian in the same form the party saw at the Inn**, so **Cassian will recognise him on sight** — and has no idea the party has met him too.
- ✅ **First meeting:** during Cassian's training, **alone in a chapel late at night, praying.** *(DM, 20 Oct 2026.)*
- ✅ **The prayer beads.** At that first meeting he gave Cassian **a string of prayer beads.** When Cassian needs guidance he prays with them, and **sometimes he is given guidance** — the Emperor answering when he can afford to.
- ✅ **He has appeared to Cassian a couple of times since, and each time nobody else around noticed him** — exactly as at the Inn.
- **Player handout:** `players/Cassian Zev - The Bishop in the Chapel.md` tells it from Cassian's side. ⚠️ **Details it invents** (yours to keep or cut): the beads are dark wood, 53 of them, with a small iron sun that goes warm; his blade-magic first manifests the morning after; the two later visits are a feast day in a crowded refectory, and the night before his posting to the Office, when Osric tells him to go where he's sent and "be kind to the people they put in front of you"; a training-house rival, **Brother Hale**, whom Osric says will be "useful to him one day."
- ✅ **Why he hides his true name — even from Cassian: he is not strong enough.** *(DM, 20 Oct 2026.)*
- ✅ **Why Cassian:** **the Emperor sees the potential for greatness in him. It is as simple as that.** The pact is **an investment of power** — a large one, for a god this weak — and **he appears to Cassian and guides him when he needs something done.** *(DM, 20 Oct 2026.)*
- **The cover story is very nearly true.** He *is* the Emperor's closest servant, in the sense that he is the Emperor. A being who has outlived every record would naturally claim to be a churchman from before the records — and the Church's chronology has a 300-year and a 200-year gap with no High Bishops named *(`sessions/Timeline.md`)*.

## 7. Canon — what has happened at the table

- **Chapter 5, the Calf and Ass:** a very old man sat down at the party's table in a crowded room where **nobody else could see him**, thanked them for stopping the summoning at St. Pyotr's — which they had told nobody — and left.
- **"May he rise again"** is said by villagers everywhere — a prayer for Emperor Fredric to become immortal *(see §4)*.

## 8. Scenes this sets up

- **Chapter 8, §10a — the Emperor at the turn.** He comes because Maelis's shears are about to pass into Ironwake's trade and east — **a vessel lost is mana lost to Saraswati, and a stronger Adephagia.** Cassian is at the table: **he recognises "the bishop"; the party recognises the man from the Inn; neither knows the other has met him** *(Decision 76)*.
- **Whenever the party describes the Inn encounter in front of Cassian.** An old man nobody else could see, who knew things he shouldn't — Cassian will hear "Bishop Osric" in it.
- **The day the cover story cracks.** A bishop invisible to everyone else in a room, whom no Church record names. **Let the party do the arithmetic; Cassian will be the last to accept it.**
- **Arc Plan §7's staging still applies** *(proposal)*: he appears two or three more times before the finale, each time slightly more solid; he never solves a problem; if he acts at the end, it costs him nearly everything.
