# Saraswati and Adephagia

Adephagia was jealous of Saraswati.  All the other old gods had fallen into waste.  Barely clinging on or unheard of for a century.  Adephagia was now the strongest, but Sara held on.  People still sang her name.  Adephagia wanted that praise for herself. 
 
She decided to move against Sara by first influencing the Church to take a harder stance on Vasant Panchami.  Adephagia stared to manifest to members of the Church that were not the most pious.  Clergy that were more interested in gaining power and wealth than spreading the faith.  Adephagia did this by appearing as a whisp of light.  Praising them clergy for their wisdom and strong faith.  This flattery gained their confidence.  Next she told them how Vasant Panshami was an opportunity to further the cause of the church.  Have all local clergy encourage a "donation of renewal" in coin to the church to celebrate the renewal of the season.  Little encouragement was needed as the greedy clergy Adephagia targeted immediately saw how they could pocket some of the donations for themselves.  This plan took place over a few decades until the practice of a "donation of renewal" became normal and everybody thought it was always the case. 
 
Adephagia then moved to the next step.  The main focus of Vasant Panchami should be the donation to the church and all the good they will do with the donations.  About renewing vowels and rebirth.  This would be the best way to bring the Church front and center of the celebration as it should be.  But Adephagia explained, all the idols paraded around the festival of Sara diverts attention away from the message of the Church.  The clerics started a campaign to ban the idols.  Over another ten years this effort was largely successful.  It is still common to see images of Sara but the large statues in the parades are almost non existent.  As she becomes less and less a part of the celebration, her power declines. 
 
She became weak enough that Adephagia was able to attack her directly.  This happened over several fights.  Each fight Adephagia was the victor with Sara successfully fleeing each time.  Each fight was easier for Adephagia since her mana pool was getting fed at a higher rate. Eventually Adephagia was able to capture Sara and imprison her. 
 
Before the final battle, Sara knew she was doomed and hatched a plan.  She took almost all her remaining power and created a set of magical items.  These items would give the wielder great technical skill and inspiration enabling them to create great works of art.  The "worshiping" the works created by these items would also feed Sara's mana pool.  The amount would be very small.  Her hope is there will be enough great works made and appreciated that it will be enough to make her strong again.  Great works of art also last centuries, so the accumulation will continue.  Someday she will have enough power to rise again. 
 
What she did not know is a deal was made to have her imprisoned in the Prison Realm.  Her mana is growing from her plan, but it would take centuries to gain enough power to escape that place. 
 
She is not without hope.  There are still a few true believers.  She is able to send them brief flashes of information in their dreams.  These flashes are weak and thus unclear. Most go unnoticed by the worshiper.  But occasionally they get through and are recognized.  There are a few that are working to free Sara from the Prison Realm. 
# The Art-Items (and Luthien's guitar)

Before the final battle, Sara spent nearly all of her remaining power making a set of tools for artists — brushes, chisels, instruments, pens. Each one takes a person who is already good and makes them legendary. Not a shortcut for the talentless; an amplifier for the gifted. The wielder finds technique and inspiration arriving faster than practice can account for, and produces work far beyond what they should be capable of.

That is the whole design. Every time somebody genuinely appreciates a great work of art, the act functions as a small unwitting act of worship, and a trickle of mana flows back to Sara. The amount per person is negligible. But masterpieces last centuries and are admired by thousands, so the trickle never stops, and it compounds. It is the only strategy available to a god who cannot be seen, cannot be named, and cannot ask anyone for anything — a way to stay relevant in the modern age while staying entirely off the Church's radar.

It is also, from her side of the Prison Realm, agonizingly slow. Centuries slow.

## Luthien's bone guitar — DM only

**The bone guitar Luthien stole out of Finnian's armory in Chapter 1 is one of Sara's items.**

Nobody in the world knows this, including Luthien, including Finnian, who has had it sitting on a seven-foot pile of surplus junk for an unknown number of years.

How to play it:

- **It is already working.** Luthien has been getting better since the voyage to Dol. Verses arrive whole. His hands find shapes he never practiced, cleanly, first try. He notices in an idle way and does not examine it, because it feels good and questioning good feelings is not among his talents.
- **It keeps escalating.** Do not plateau it. Each chapter he should be visibly, describably better than the last, until at some point somebody who knows music hears him and reacts to it as an event rather than a nice evening.
- **The reveal is earned, not given.** He learns what it is when the fiction forces it — a member of the Cult of Saraswati recognising the instrument, a Sara dream-flash getting through, an Adephagia cultist smelling her on him the way the demagogue at St. Pyotr's already did once (Ch4, unprompted, before anyone in the party had heard her name).
- **Cross-reference:** `Cult of Saraswati.md` — Corbin Wainwright learned about the items from Sara's dream messages and is actively looking for them. That is the natural collision.

The Chronicle (`sessions/The Penitents Chronicle.md`) seeds this from Chapter 1 onward and never confirms it. Keep it that way in player-facing material.
