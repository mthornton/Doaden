# Barney Soot
### Master sweep, Solteres — the man who bought Rowan

> ⚠️ **DM ONLY.** Chapter 8. The party has not read his name yet — **JonLief reads the Working Ledger at the start of the next session**, and this is who that name belongs to.
>
> See `sessions/Chapter 8 DM Notes - Planning.md` §5, `What To Do With Rowan.md` §3E, `Elizabeth and the Green.md` §1.

---

## 0. What is settled *(DM, 15 Oct 2026)*

- **Not tied to Ironwake.** No syndicate, no patron, no part in anybody's plot. **A poor man making a living as a chimney sweep.**
- **The trade needs children.** Flues are narrow; only a small boy fits. It is dangerous, and it has always been looked down on as low-class work.
- **Not a bad man. Pragmatic, and resigned to his lot.** That is why he is **stern** with the boys he buys: softness gets a boy killed in a flue.
- **Many of his boys do not reach adulthood.** Some die in accidents. Some die of the sickness that comes from breathing ash.
- **When a boy gets too big for a chimney, Barney puts him out on the street.** He gets his freedom and nothing else.
- From the Chapter 8 plan: **he liked Rowan**, he was forced to sell him back for less than he paid, and he is **bitter, frightened and alone in the shop** when the party arrives.

**Everything below is settled (DM, 15 Oct 2026).**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | About fifty. Looks sixty-five. |
| **Build** | **Small**, the way sweeps are. ⚠️ **This is the guards' "sooty little fella."** He stopped growing early, the way climbing boys do, and he is the size of a boy of fourteen with a man's face on top. |
| **Tells** | Soot so deep in the creases of his hands and neck that washing does nothing. **Thick grey calluses on both knees and elbows** — a climbing boy's marks, made by shinning up brick, which do not fade. A **wet cough** he no longer notices. Red-rimmed eyes. |
| **Trade** | **Licensed master sweep.** Works the wealthy quarter, where the houses have the most chimneys and the narrowest flues. |
| **Shop** | A narrow two-room place on a lane behind the wealthy quarter, with a cellar. Sacks of soot stacked to the ceiling, waiting to be sold to farmers as fertiliser. **The boys sleep on the sacks in the cellar.** |
| **Stats** | **Commoner.** He will not fight. He has never won a fight in his life. |

## 2. Backstory — he was one of the boys

> 🔑 **Barney Soot is not his name. It is what his master called him.**

He came into the trade the way every one of his boys did: **bought small, put up a flue, and told to climb.** He does not remember a name before *Soot*, and he does not miss one. His master gave him rules, a sack to sleep on, two meals a day, and a beating when he froze in a chimney. **He survived it**, which most of the boys he climbed beside did not.

**Then he got too big, and his master put him out on the street**, with nothing, exactly as Barney now does to his own boys. He was about fifteen.

**He did not die, which was what was expected of him.** He did the only thing he knew how to do. He swept the chimneys of the poor for coppers, with a borrowed brush and no boy, and climbed the ones he still fitted, until he had saved enough to buy his old master's licence and brushes when the man coughed himself to death. **He has run that shop for about twenty-five years.**

### ⚙️ What that does to him

**He does not think he is doing anything wrong, and he does not think of it as cruelty. He thinks of it as the way he survived.**

- **The rules he enforces are the rules that kept him alive.** *Don't freeze. Don't look down. Don't cry in a flue, because it fills your mouth with soot.* He is stern because the master who was stern with him is the reason he is breathing.
- **Putting them out is not spite.** He was put out at fifteen with nothing, and he made a life. **He believes, sincerely, that this is enough.** *"I got the same. It was enough for me."*
- **He has never once asked whether it was enough for the ones who didn't come back.**

> ⚠️ **Run him in the campaign's institutional register.** Like Ysabel and the Church, he is not a monster who enjoys it. He is a man administering a system he came out of, and **he thinks of the system as the thing that saved him.** The method was taught to him by being used on him. *(Same shape as Corbin, `Corbin Wainwright and Orla Miller.md` — a small, poor, honest version of it.)*

## 3. How the business runs

| | |
|---|---|
| **The boys** | **Three at a time, sometimes four.** Small, thin and young: six to ten is the useful range. |
| **Where they come from** | **Non-human boys from Bramblefen**, because they are **Stock**, which makes them cheap, and nobody comes looking. **Human boys come apprenticed from the Church**, which places the children in its care *(§8)*. |
| **Feeding** | **Enough, and no more.** He is precise about this and proud of it: a starved boy cannot climb, and a fed one gets stuck. He weighs them by eye every week. |
| **Discipline** | Stern, not sadistic. Rules, a strap, no second chances in a flue. **He has never had to use a fire to make a boy climb**, and he will tell you so as though it were a credential, because other masters do. |
| **When a boy dies** | He pays for a pauper's burial, out of his own pocket, every time. **He is the only person who goes.** |
| **When a boy ages out** | Out the door, with the clothes he is wearing. No coin, no reference, no goodbye. |

### 🪵 The beam *(optional, strong)*

There is a beam over the cellar stairs. **For every boy who aged out, he cut one straight line into it. For every boy who died, a cross.** Twenty-five years. ⚠️ **About forty marks, and eleven of them are crosses.**

He does not keep their names. **He keeps the count**, and he could not tell you why.

> **Do not point at it.** Describe the beam when someone goes down to the cellar. **Let a player count.**

## 4. Rowan, from Barney's side

### Why he bought him

**He was cheap, he was small, and he was half-elf**, which in Solteres means slight, long-boned, and nobody's business. **Barney noticed the price was strange and did not ask why**, because a man in his position does not ask why something is cheap. *(The reason is Vaskar's fear of the green, `Elizabeth and the Green.md`. Barney knows none of it.)*

### What Rowan was like

**The best climber he had ever bought.** Light, quick, did as he was told, and **never cried in a flue.** Not once, from the first day, which Barney has never seen in twenty-five years and found slightly unsettling.

### 🌿 The name

**The boy told him his name was Rowan.** Barney told him, *"Not in here it isn't,"* the same way it was said to him, **and called him Sprig.**

> ⚠️ **That is what JonLief hears first: someone else's name for his son.** Barney uses it without thinking. *"Sprig. Your boy. Good little climber."* He does not know what a rowan is, and he did not mean anything by it, **but it was the nearest he came to kindness, and he knows that.**

### 🌙 What Rowan said in his sleep

**Barney never asked the boy one question about himself.** That is the rule. You do not learn their names, because they die.

**But the boys sleep in the cellar, and you hear things.** Rowan talked in his sleep, most nights, one sentence:

> *"Mama, look at me."*

⚠️ **This is the cruellest thing in the file, and it is entirely true.** It is the boy's version of Elizabeth's bargain — her apathy, which Rowan has been carrying as *she stopped looking at me* — and Barney does not understand it at all. **He tells it to JonLief as a kindness**, to prove the boy was all right in his sleep. *(`Elizabeth and the Green.md` §1. Do not explain it. Nobody alive can.)*

> **Timing:** this belongs **after** Barney has described the men who took him, so JonLief has a direction to run in before the line lands. It should come almost as an afterthought, while they are leaving.

### Why he misses him

**He will not say it. You can see it.** He grumbles about the money first and loudest, because that is the thing he is allowed to be angry about. **The reverse is true.** He kept the boy's sack in the corner of the cellar and has not put another boy on it. He has not bought a replacement, and with three boys left he is short-handed in the busiest season of the year, which a pragmatic man would never allow.

## 5. The day Vaskar's men came

**What Barney tells the party, roughly in this order:**

1. **They came in the morning**, three of them and one older man. They knew the boy's description before they walked in. *(They had the Working Ledger's entry from Vaskar's memory.)*
2. **The older man did all the talking.** Heavy, grey, quiet. **Never raised his voice.** Put coin on the table and said *"for the boy"* as though the sale were already done. ⚠️ **The party will know it is Vaskar.**
3. **It was less than Barney paid.** Barney said so. The man did not answer, and one of the others put a hand on the back of Barney's neck, quite gently. **Barney took the coin.**
4. **One of them was not like the others** — a lean man who looked at the shop the way a man looks at a room he is going to describe to someone later. *(The tracker, Ch8 §6. Give him a detail here — a missing ear tip, a limp — so the party can recognise him.)*
5. **The boy did not cry.** He went with them the way he went up a flue.

**Barney is angry, and it is a small, impotent, resigned kind of anger**: he was robbed by men he could not refuse, which has happened to him all his life, and it is what he expects of the world.

## 6. 🪜 What Barney is actually worth to the party

**He is not a dead end, and that is the best reason to build him properly.**

> ### 🔑 Sweeps go into every house in the wealthy quarter.
>
> **His boys have been up the chimneys of half the great houses in Solteres.** They know which flues connect, which roofs join, which houses have a cold chimney that should be lit, and which have a lit chimney in a house that is meant to be empty.

**If Vaskar is holding Rowan in a house** — and a man who needs a child alive and hidden in a city he does not live in will rent somewhere quiet — **then a sweep's boy will notice a fire in the wrong grate before anybody else in Solteres.**

- **Barney can be hired**, or shamed, or paid back what he lost. **He will help for money.** He will help faster for the boy, and he will say it is for the money.
- **His boys are the party's eyes on the rooftops.** Three small, filthy, invisible children who can go anywhere in the wealthy quarter carrying a brush.
- ⚠️ **And the chimney is a way in.** Barney knows the flue of every house he has swept. **Gimble is a gnome.** Bramblefen's forge chimney was already "a gift to Dave" *(Chapter 7 prep, now in `_archive/`)*; this is the second, and **Barney can teach him how to climb one** in about ten minutes, with his knees and elbows, the way he was taught.

**This gives Chapter 8 a non-combat route to the boy, which suits a deliberately unfair fight with Vaskar.**

>
> - **Pip** is the obvious one: a boy with days left who needs somewhere to go. **Ald's Star is right there**, with Anselm and thirty people who know what it is to be put out with nothing. *(This also gives JonLief a practice run at the Rowan decision.)*
> - **Buying them** is possible. Barney will sell. ⚠️ **Note what that makes the party:** buyers of Stock, at Bramblefen prices, from a licensed trade. It is still the right thing and it should still feel wrong.

## 8. Where the human boys come from — the Church

**The Church puts the children in its care into apprenticeships.** That is ordinary, and nobody in Doaden thinks it sinister: an indenture, a fee to the house, a blessing on the paperwork. *(See `Doaden - World Rules.md`.)* A sweep is a licensed trade, so a sweep can take a Church apprentice like anyone else. **Barney has taken three or four that way over the years.** Same flue, same beam.

**The indenture runs until the boy is fifteen**, when he is no longer a child and is on his own — exactly as Barney was. In practice a climbing boy is too big for a flue well before that, and Barney puts him out early, Church boy or Bramblefen boy alike.

> 🔑 **This gives the Rowan warning its teeth.** The apprentice relays Baltus's stories: *a child with no guardian but a Penitent is an orphan, and orphans are raised by the Church.* **"Raised by the Church" means apprenticed.** If the party has been in Barney's cellar, they have seen one of the places that leads.

## 9. Voice card

**Short sentences. Coughs instead of pausing. Calls every man "sir" and means nothing by it.** Never asks a question about you. Answers questions about himself with facts and no feelings.

> *"Bought him fair. Paid more'n he was worth, and less'n he was worth, if you follow. Small, see. Small's the thing."*

> *"They don't cry in a flue, sir. Not twice. Fills your mouth."*

> *"Out the door at fifteen, me. Same as them. Nothing on me but my shirt. And look." (He means the shop. He is not joking.)*

> *"I don't keep names. You don't, in this. You keep the count."*

> *"He'd say a thing in his sleep. 'Mama, look at me.' Every night, near enough. So he was all right, see. In his sleep."*

## 10. Branches

**IF the party is hostile** — he does not resist, and tells them everything, faster, because he has been frightened of bigger men his whole life. **Nothing is gained by threatening him except the cost of having done it.**

**IF the party pays him back what Vaskar's men cheated him of** — he is wrong-footed. Nobody has ever done that. ⚠️ **This is the cheapest way to get his boys on the rooftops**, and the only way to get them there willingly.

**IF JonLief tells him who he is** — Barney goes very still and takes his cap off. He does not apologise, because he does not think he did anything to apologise for. He says *"He was a good climber,"* which is the highest praise he has.

**IF the party offers to take the boys** — he sells. He haggles. **He is relieved about Tam and does not say so.**

**⭐ Tam is missing** *(DM, 21 Oct 2026)*. **By day the shop is empty** — Barney and the boys are out working. **When the party comes back in the evening**, Barney tells them everything above, and that **Tam went up a chimney at the empty Marlowe house today and never came back down.** He doesn't know what's in there. He is heartbroken, and he's already counting: **another cross on the beam.** He won't ask for help. **IF the party goes after Tam and brings him back — Barney and all his boys join the search for Rowan**, for the boy they liked and for the debt. Full encounter: `Solteres - Places.md`, **Side fight C** (an otyugh in the flooded cellar; Tam is rescuable).

**IF they do nothing** — Pip is on the street before the chapter ends. Let somebody see him there.

**After the rescue** — he offers to take Rowan back, as in `What To Do With Rowan.md` §3E. **He means it as a kindness.** It is the best thing he has to offer, because it is the only life he knows.
