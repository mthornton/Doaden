# Erwana

**Type:** NPC — escaped slave from **Bramblefen**; opens Chapter 7 at the campfire and, if the party takes the Drain, guides them inside.
**Function:** the one who recognizes [[JonLief]]'s pendant, the courier of [[Old Cobb]]'s second ledger, and the human center of the "Stock, not Terms" injustice the whole chapter is built on — a woman who is half human and has never once been treated as one.

> First appears at the campfire the night before Bramblefen (`Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`, "Campfire revelations"). See `Chapter 7 Run Sheet.md` for her voice card and Branch B (the Drain).

## The Wheel

Erwana was born under canvas. Her mother, Nessa, was human — a fortune-teller who worked the tent at *Voss's Wandering Wheel*, a threadbare circus that made its living on the market-day circuit through the Southern Diocese, never far from Solteres for long. Her father, Sask, was Tabaxi, an acrobat from a small wandering troupe of his own kind that fell in with the Wheel for a season and, because of Nessa, never quite left. Erwana never knew whether her mother's readings were a true gift or a very good act — Nessa never told her, and by the time Erwana was old enough to ask properly, it was too late to matter. What she does know is that her father taught her to fall before he taught her to stand up straight, the way acrobats teach their children, and that between the cards and the tumbling she spent the first thirteen years of her life certain the world was basically kind. The Wheel billed her, briefly and fondly, as *"Little Erwana, the Tumbling Fortune's Daughter"* — a child's act, mostly cartwheels and a deck of cards she couldn't really read yet. It is the only thing anyone ever called her that she chose to keep.

## What the Circuit Cost Them

A local zealot — never named, never punished, just a man with a little brief authority in a little town — decided Nessa's tent was a step short of witchcraft and said so loudly enough that it became true. It did not take long. A crowd that had paid a copper each to have their palms read that afternoon became, by evening, a crowd that dragged her mother out of the tent by her hair. Sask went in after her and did not come back out. Erwana, thirteen years old, watched both of it happen from behind a wagon wheel because a stagehand held her there and would not let her go, and she has never decided whether that was mercy or cowardice on his part.

Voss, who owned the Wheel and had a scandal to make disappear before the next town heard about it, did not bury his acrobat and his fortune-teller with any ceremony worth the name. He sold their daughter to a passing slaver inside the week — quietly, quickly, the way you get rid of the last piece of evidence that anything happened at all. Erwana does not know if he felt bad about it. She has had thirty years to decide it doesn't matter either way.

## A Life in Other People's Houses

For most of three decades Erwana was a household servant — competent, quiet, good with her hands, the kind of slave a house doesn't notice until she's gone. She would very likely have died old in someone's kitchen if not for a rumor: that Tabaxi relieve themselves indoors when no one's watching. It isn't true. It has never been true of her or of anyone she's known. What is true, and what she has never once been ashamed of, is that Tabaxi carry a musky scent that some unkind nose mistook for the other thing and turned into a story — and once a household decides it believes the story, no amount of being clean and careful and perfect at her work put it back in the bottle. One mistress after another decided she'd rather not risk the carpets. She was reassigned, house by house, out to the fields and the stock pens, where the smell of animals covered her own and nobody minded either way.

It is, she has noticed, the second time in her life that being half of something has cost her the whole of something else. She is exactly as human as she is Tabaxi and has never once been treated as the human half. The law agrees with the households: **Bramblefen holds her as Stock** — non-folk, bought and sold outright, no paperwork beyond a line in Cobb's ledger — while the Church's own ruling against slavery is written for people it has quietly decided she is not one of. She does not argue about it out loud. She has learned that arguing about it is a good way to get sold again.

## The Cordwin Steading

Her last posting before Bramblefen was fifteen years on the Cordwin steading, a middling cattle-and-goat estate a hard day's ride from Solteres, where she mucked stalls, birthed calves, and — she'll say this plainly, it's the one thing left she's proud of — was better with a frightened animal than anyone else on the property, human or otherwise. Old Cordwin's son gambled the estate into the ground inside three bad years. When the bailiffs came to settle the debts, the herds and the "stock" that tended them went under the auctioneer's hammer in the same afternoon, sold off in lots to whoever would clear the most coin fastest. Erwana went to a broker who supplied Lord Ironwake's holdings, and the broker sent her to Bramblefen, where she has sat unsold for a couple of months now — too old, too plain, and (see above) not a house anyone wants, no matter how the market's running.

## The Escape

Old Cobb told her about the drain. He'd known it for years and never used it himself — an old man with failing eyes and forty years of habit doesn't run — but he gave it to her along with the second ledger, the one true thing he'd done in decades of writing down everyone else's sins, and asked her to get it out of the fen. She did not know him well. She knew him the way everyone in the Pens knew him: the clerk, harmless, kind in the only way the compound allowed anyone to be kind. She has thought about him every day since.

That was four weeks ago. She did not run far. She tells herself it was because the ledger needed somewhere safer than a hole in the mud, and that's true as far as it goes — but the truer answer, the one she doesn't say, is that thirty years of being owned does not leave a person knowing where "far" is, or what to do with it once she's there. She has spent the month living on berries and nerve within a few miles of the one place she has any reason to fear, because it is also, God help her, the only place she has any memory of anyone being kind to her in years. She was circling back toward where Ysabel's party made camp hoping for scraps left over from breakfast when the guard's rope found her.

## What She Knows

- **The tunnel and the ledger.** Cobb told her about the drain and gave her the second ledger the night she ran (see `Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`, items 2–3). She does not know what's in it beyond that it's old — she's never read it herself.
- **The pendant.** She had a cell mate with the same pendant as JonLief's — a woman, sick, with a boy who wouldn't leave her side. Taken from the cell together; she doesn't know what became of them after. Cobb would have the records.
- **The drain route — stale, not false.** She went through it in the dark, half-starved, four weeks ago, and it was clear. She has no way of knowing Denrick found it and trapped it since. If she leads, she is telling the truth as she knows it — see `Denrick.md` and the Run Sheet's Branch B.
- **Bramblefen from the inside.** Layout, roughly, and the fact that Cobb is the only permanent slave who isn't stock or terms — everyone else moves through, he never has.

## Personality & how to run her

- **Fast, apologetic, over-explains.** She talks like someone bracing to be interrupted or hit, because for most of her life both were likely. She volunteers information nobody asked for because silence has never once protected her.
- **"I swear to the Emperor."** Her reflexive oath when frightened — sincere, a little old-fashioned, the kind of thing a circus family says.
- **Flinches from raised hands, and from nobles specifically.** Ysabel reaching for the ledger is the sharpest version of this in the chapter — play her flinch, not her words.
- **Never lies. She is simply out of date.** This is load-bearing for the Drain scene — if she's wrong, she's wrong in good faith, and she should find out she was wrong at the same moment the party does.
- **Her mother is still in her, underneath the fear.** She reads a room fast and she's usually right about who's dangerous and who isn't — she just hasn't had the standing, in thirty years, to act on what she notices. Let a player catch her clocking a guard's mood, or Vaskar's temperature, half a beat before anyone else does.
- **Her father is still in her too, and it startles her.** Once in a great while — ducking a blow, catching something before it falls, moving through a gap that shouldn't fit her — she does something with an old, unmistakable grace, and it visibly surprises her as much as anyone watching. She hasn't had reason to move like that in a long time.
- **She does not talk about Sask and Nessa unless someone earns it.** If a PC does — real kindness, not just a question — she'll tell the story once, plainly, and then not again. Wolfram or JonLief are the likeliest to get it; Luthien might get her trust fastest, given his own history with the Church.
- **The smell is a fact, not a shame.** Erwana doesn't apologize for being Tabaxi and doesn't perform hurt about the rumor — she's just tired of paying for something that isn't even true. Play her matter-of-fact about it, not wounded.

- **Skills** Insight +4, Animal Handling +4, Acrobatics +3, Persuasion +3
- **Senses** darkvision 60 ft. (Tabaxi blood), passive Perception 12
- **Languages** Common; a few scattered words of Tabaxi, her father's, never quite learned
- **Condition** begins with **1 level of exhaustion** (a month living on berries and nerve)

**Traits**

- **Reads the Room.** Advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks to sense danger or a lie directed at her. Her mother's trade, real or not — she's usually right about who's dangerous a half-beat before anyone else notices.
- **An Old Grace.** Once per scene, when it would help her dodge, catch something, or slip through a gap, she may treat a failed Dexterity check as a success instead. Narrate it as visibly startling — to her most of all.
- **Stale, Not False.** Any information Erwana gives about Bramblefen as she last knew it (the drain route, guard habits, the yard) is exactly true as of four weeks ago. She has no way to know what's changed since.

**Actions**

- **Feeble Strike.** *Melee:* +1 to hit, 1 bludgeoning. She has never fought anyone in her life and isn't going to start now.

## Hooks & aftermath

- **She is not a fighter and shouldn't be asked to be one.** The party will likely have to decide whether to bring a starving noncombatant into a slaver compound at all, or leave her at the camp with Ysabel's people — either choice says something about them, and Erwana will go along with whichever they choose without complaint.
- **The Cobb connection.** She is the only living person who can vouch for who Old Cobb really is — not the clerk Vaskar tortures, but the man who gave a stranger his one act of decency. If Cobb dies before the party reaches him, Erwana is what's left of the reason to care.
- **After Bramblefen.** Per the Aftermath section of `Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`, she's the one person among the freed who has been both a slave and free in the last month — the eighteen will listen to her before they listen to the party. That makes her a natural voice in the "where do the freed go" decision, and a recurring contact afterward if she stays near the group.
- **The half-Tabaxi injustice.** She's exactly as human as she is not, and the law has never once counted the human half. This is a quiet, specific wrongness distinct from what Anselm or the Terms men are dealing with — worth letting land once, without turning it into a speech.
- **A planted seed, not a debt to pay off.** Her mother's death at a mob's hands under color of heresy sits close to the Church's own machinery — the same kind of "the crowd did it, not us" deniability the Citadel used on Anselm's testimony about Wolfram. Nothing forces this to matter again, but if the campaign ever goes looking at how the Church handles the old gods and their believers, she's a door that's already open.
