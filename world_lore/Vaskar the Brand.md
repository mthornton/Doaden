# Vaskar the Brand

**Type:** NPC villain — slave master of **Bramblefen**; Chapter 7 boss. Runs the holdfast for **Lord Leofard Ironwake.**
**In brief:** born a slave, now the cruelest free man in the fens — as clever as he is strong. Where [[The Juggernaut]] is a force you survive, Vaskar is a mind you have to beat.

> Definitive version of the "Vaskar the Brand" boss sketched in *Chapter 7: The Chained Coast*. This supersedes the one-line stat note there.

## Born in the Pens

Vaskar has never been free a day he can remember — not really. He was born property, in the mud of a slaver camp much like the one he now runs, to a woman who was sold on before he could walk and a father who was never a name, only a rumor. His first cradle was a cage. His first lesson was that crying brought the lash, not comfort. He grew up in a world scraped utterly bare of tenderness, and a child raised without a single act of kindness does not grow into a soft man. He grows into Vaskar.

He learned the one truth the pens teach: **there is a ladder of suffering, and every rung you climb is somebody else's back.** The slaves who suffered least were the ones who made others suffer most — the informers, the trusties, the ones who held down a runaway while the overseer worked. Vaskar decided very young which end of that he intended to be on.

## The Making of a Master

He made himself useful to the men who owned him. He informed. He disciplined. When another slave planned an escape, Vaskar knew it before the overseers did — and traded the knowledge for a warmer corner and a fuller bowl. He broke recaptured runaways personally, because he understood, better than any free-born overseer ever could, exactly which small hopes to crush to make a person stop trying. He had tried every one of those hopes himself. That was his genius: **he ran slavery from the inside out.** No trick a slave could attempt was a trick he hadn't already thought of at nine years old.

There was, once, a girl in the pens who was kind to him. The only person who ever was. When the overseers came looking for who had stolen bread, Vaskar gave them her name — because kindness was a thing that could be spent, like anything else, and he was hungry. He watched them take her and felt the last soft thing in him close like a fist. He has told himself ever since that he did her a mercy: he taught her the truth of the world before it could disappoint her slowly. He does not think about her often. He does not let himself.

By the time he'd bought his own collar off — or rather, made himself too valuable to collar — he was no longer a slave who had risen. He was something the free men needed: a master who could not be lied to, because he had told every lie first.

## What He Is Now

Lord Ironwake owns Bramblefen. Vaskar *runs* it, and skims it, and is worth every coin, because a Bramblefen under Vaskar loses fewer "head of stock" to escape and fetches better prices than any Ironwake could staff with hired thugs. The two men use each other and neither pretends otherwise. Vaskar keeps insurance — ledgers, names, a letter or two under Ironwake's own seal — hidden where only he can reach, because he trusts his employer exactly as far as he trusts anyone, which is not at all.

He brands every slave personally. It is his signature and his need both — the one permanence he believes in is ownership, marked into the skin where it can't be argued with. He wears trophy brands and collars taken from those he broke worst, and he keeps the best food, the warmest room, and a living wall of other people's bodies between himself and any danger, because **the only comfort Vaskar has ever known is comfort taken from someone else, and he takes it constantly, the way other men breathe.**

## Personality & how to run him

- **Cold, not hot.** He never rages (that's Mott's job). Vaskar is calm, transactional, and patient. Cruelty is a tool he uses precisely, never in a temper.
- **He assumes everyone is him.** He expects betrayal, greed, and self-interest from every living soul, and he is almost always right — which makes him very hard to deceive and nearly impossible to appeal to.
- **He values his own hide above all.** If a fight turns against him he will grab a hostage and bargain, or cut a deal he fully intends to break, or simply run. He does not die for Ironwake, or for anything.
- **His flaw is the thing he threw away.** Vaskar genuinely cannot conceive of a person acting against their own interest — for love, for loyalty, for someone else. Real selflessness doesn't compute; it reads to him as a trick he hasn't spotted yet. The bond between [[Durgan Half-Ear]] and Mott baffles him. JonLief crossing a continent for a wife he can't profit from would strike him as *stupid* — and things Vaskar thinks are stupid are things he fails to plan for.

## Stat block — Vaskar the Brand

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful evil — CR 5 (1,800 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 16 (studded leather, scarred hide)
- **Hit Points** 102 (12d10 + 36)
- **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 (+4) | 12 (+1) | 16 (+3) | 14 (+2) | 15 (+2) | 15 (+2) |

- **Saving Throws** STR +7, CON +6, WIS +5
- **Skills** Athletics +7, Deception +5, Insight +8, Intimidation +5, Perception +8
- **Condition Immunities** charmed, frightened
- **Senses** passive Perception 18
- **Languages** Common, Thieves' Cant (the pens' argot)
- **Proficiency Bonus** +3

**Traits**

- **Expects Betrayal.** Vaskar can't be surprised, and he has advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks to detect a lie. Nothing left in him can be charmed or frightened.
- **Master of the Pens.** Creatures have disadvantage on ability checks to escape his grapple, his bonds, or his notice, and he has advantage on checks made to recapture or track an escapee.
- **Blind to Love (exploitable).** Vaskar cannot model selflessness. The **first time each round** a creature he can see takes the Help action, uses its action or reaction to shield/protect another creature, or otherwise acts against its own obvious self-interest, Vaskar is caught off-guard: he has **disadvantage on his next attack roll or saving throw.** (The party's compassion is a weapon against him — lean into it.)
- **Relentless (recharges after a short or long rest).** If Vaskar is reduced to 0 HP but not killed outright, he drops to 1 HP instead.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack.** Vaskar makes two attacks: two with his spiked chain, or one chain and one branding iron.
- **Spiked Chain.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +7 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 2d6 + 4 slashing. A Large or smaller target must succeed on a DC 15 Strength save or be pulled up to 5 ft. toward Vaskar and knocked prone.
- **Branding Iron (heated).** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 1d8 + 4 bludgeoning + 2d6 fire, and the target must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution save or be **Branded** until it finishes a long rest. While Branded, Vaskar has advantage on attack rolls against the creature, and it has disadvantage on saves against being frightened.
- **Feed on Their Fear (recharge 5–6).** Vaskar draws comfort from another's pain. When he uses this, choose a creature within 30 ft. that is frightened, Branded, grappled, or at 0 HP; Vaskar gains **10 temporary hit points** and advantage on his next attack roll this turn.

**Reactions**

- **Someone Else's Skin.** When Vaskar is hit by an attack he can see and another creature (a minion, a captive, or a creature grappled by him) is within 5 ft., he can interpose that creature. The attack hits the interposed creature instead, which takes the damage. He can use this once per round — the truest expression of the man: he has never once taken a blow he could make someone else take for him.

**Villain Actions (optional — for a solo boss vs. 4 PCs).** Vaskar takes 2 of these per round, one at a time, at the end of another creature's turn. Drop this block if you're running him with a full slaver crew and don't need the extra action economy.

- **Crack the Whip.** One creature within 15 ft. makes a DC 15 Dexterity save, taking 1d6 slashing and being pulled 10 ft. toward Vaskar on a failure (setting up *Someone Else's Skin* or a brand).
- **Bark Orders.** One allied slaver within 30 ft. that can hear him uses its reaction to move up to its speed and make one weapon attack.
- **Slip the Leash.** Vaskar moves up to his speed without provoking opportunity attacks, repositioning behind cover, a minion, or a captive.

## Running the fight

- **He fights from behind a wall of people.** Minions first, captives as shields. *Someone Else's Skin* means the party's damage keeps landing on the wrong targets until they thin his cover — which is exactly why **freeing the captives (or Mott, or the Silent One) mid-fight guts his defenses.**
- **He brands the biggest threat early** (the party's front-line martial), then leans on *Feed on Their Fear* to snowball once someone's frightened or dropped.
- **He will not die on principle.** Down to a third of his HP or stripped of his shields, he grabs a hostage and offers a deal — the ledgers, a name, safe passage — and means to break it the instant he's clear. Capturing him alive is worth far more than killing him.

## Plot leverage & weaknesses

- **The hidden insurance.** Vaskar keeps evidence against Ironwake — a sealed letter, the true sale-ledgers — squirreled away as leverage. It's the party's cleanest path to Ironwake, and Old Cobb (or a bribed minion) may know roughly where.
- **Love is his blind spot.** Mechanically (*Blind to Love*) and narratively, he cannot predict people who act for others. Durgan and Mott's bond, JonLief's search for his family — Vaskar will misjudge all of it, and that's how a smarter party gets inside his guard.
- **His shields are his weakness.** Everything he does depends on having people to hide behind and hurt. Take those away — free them, or turn them — and the cruel, clever master is just a frightened man who was never anything but a scared child with a brand.

## DM knobs

- **Scaling for a level-3 party of 4:** as written (CR 5) he's a *hard* solo boss — pair with the Chapter 7 Easy-tier minions but consider dropping the Villain Actions, or run him at **HP ~80** with 2–3 slavers. For a tougher table, keep everything and add a bodyguard.
- **Size:** built Medium (a big, powerful man) to contrast with Mott's literal Large frame. Bump to Large only if you want two giants in the arc.
- **Names/details** (Bramblefen, the pens' girl, the hidden ledgers) are yours to rename or cut.
