# What To Do With Rowan
### The guardianship problem — options, costs, and how to run it

> ⚠️ **DM ONLY.** Written for the moment the party gets the boy back.
>
> See `Elizabeth and the Green.md`, `Chapter 8 DM Notes - Planning.md` §5, `Baltus Thole.md`, `Anselm Vogt.md`.

---

## 1. The problem, stated plainly

**JonLief owes the Church of Azerai five hundred years.** He has no legal standing, no home, no income, no writ, and he is walking toward a confrontation with an extra-planar entity whose cult harvests children for suffering.

**A child cannot come on that road.**

**And JonLief, played honestly, will never hand him over.** He crossed a continent for this boy. He stood over his wife's bones in a burn circle six weeks ago. There is no version of that man who shrugs and gives his son to an institution he does not trust.

### 🔑 The principle to hold

> **JonLief decides. Every option costs him something. You never solve it for him.**
>
> The failure mode is not a child in a dungeon. **The failure mode is the DM confiscating the one thing he won.** If Rowan is taken out of his hands by fiat, the rescue stops being a victory and becomes a formality, and the player will feel it even if he never says so.

---

## 2. ⛪ The Church — use it as the clock, not the answer

**The policy is real and you should absolutely establish it.** A child whose sole guardian becomes a Penitent is, in the eyes of the Church, an orphan. Orphans are raised by the Church — **which in practice means placed in an apprenticeship** until fifteen, when a child in Doaden is considered grown and on his own *(`Doaden - World Rules.md`)*. Rowan is eight: seven years of indenture, to whatever trade has a place. One of those trades is Barney Soot's *(`Barney Soot.md` §8)*. That is not cruelty, it is **administration** — and it is run in exactly the same register as everything else this institution does. *You are not losing a son. He is being saved.*

**But do not let it resolve the question.** If the Church takes Rowan, JonLief's whole chapter arc is handed to a clerk.

**Use it as a deadline instead.** The moment the party walks that boy into a Church office, somebody applies the policy. So the party has until then — and they don't know how long that is.

### ⚠️ And Baltus is the one who has to say it

> ✅ **SETTLED (13 Oct 2026): the APPRENTICE delivers it, not Baltus.** Baltus is not in Solteres. The apprentice passes the warning on and says plainly that **he has never seen it done himself — he has heard the stories from Baltus.** Use the quote below as what Baltus told him, relayed. Everything in "What this does" still applies, with one change: the apprentice is no longer a witness to his master looking away, he is the one choosing to repeat it to Penitents, which is the first time he takes their side over his order's paperwork.

This is the best available version, and it costs you nothing to set up. **Baltus Thole is a Shepherd, the policy is his to enforce, and he is also the man who was visibly, uncomplicatedly glad that Bramblefen went well.**

**He warns them.** He should not want to.

> *"I'm going to tell you something and then I'm going to pretend I didn't.*
>
> *Don't take the boy to a Redeemer. Don't take him to an office, don't take him to a chapel, don't give his name to a clerk. A child with no guardian but a Penitent is an orphan, and orphans are raised by the Church, and that is written down somewhere in a hand very like mine.*
>
> *I've carried that order out. Twice."* (a pause) *"I don't recall either of their faces, and I have tried."*
>
> *"So find him somewhere. Quickly. And don't tell me where."*

**What this does:**

- JonLief gets a deadline instead of a confiscation.
- The Church stays menacing without becoming the villain of a chapter about a crime lord.
- ⚠️ **Baltus gets the best scene he has had** — his charter and his decency pulling opposite directions, and him choosing decency by *looking away* rather than by disobeying. That is exactly the man.
- **And the apprentice is standing right there**, hearing his master quietly decline to do his job. *(That is the new PC's first real data point about what this order is, and it should complicate him.)*

## 3. The options, costed

**Lay these out as the world offers them. Do not rank them out loud.**

### ⭐ A — Ald's Star, with Anselm *(recommended)*

**Thirty freed people walked there last week on Wolfram's name.** Anselm Vogt is with them: sixty years old, a retired sheriff of Westfield, owes this party everything, and has nothing left in the world to do.

| Why it works | |
|---|---|
| **The guardian** | **A retired lawman raising a boy in a village of people who were slaves last month.** There is no better guardian available in this setting and no institution that comes close. |
| **The place** | Outside Solteres jurisdiction. Nobody there will ask what a half-elf child is doing walking around free. ⚠️ **The locals are literally green from Ald's cabbage and proud of it** — "looks different" is a point of pride in that valley, not a danger. |
| **Reachable** | It is established geography the campaign already returns to. **JonLief can visit.** That matters enormously to a player being asked to give up a child. |
| **Debt** | Thirty people and a village both owe this party. ⚠️ **They will guard that boy without being asked.** |

**The cost:** he is not with his father, and JonLief has to be the one who says so.

> ### 🔑 Two things that make this better than it looks
>
> 1. ⚠️ **The Arc Plan already puts the finale at Ald's Star.** Send Rowan there and the last chapter of this campaign happens in the town where JonLief's son lives. **You do not have to build that. It is already standing.**
> 2. ⚠️ **Ald's Star is where the green rumour is going to land** — a valley of green-skinned farmers, in a country that has started whispering about a plague that turns people green. **And Rowan is immune, and nobody knows, including him.** The safest person in that valley will be the child they took in out of charity.

---

### B — Keep him. For one chapter.

**Do not forbid this. Allow it, and let the player live the reality.**

- Food, watches, somewhere to put him during every single conversation.
- He cannot be left anywhere, and the party will not leave him with strangers twice.
- A fight happens and somebody has to choose between a target and a child.
- ⚠️ **He gets taken again, or nearly does.** That is not a punishment. That is the thing JonLief is already afraid of, arriving.

**Then let JonLief be the one who ends it.** After a near-miss, under his own steam, in his own words.

> **This is the version with the most roleplay in it and it still gets you to a playable campaign.** A decision the player makes under pressure is worth ten of an exit you offered in Chapter 8. **Budget one chapter, maybe two.**

**⚠️ And the thing only you know:** **Rowan cannot be tortured by any servant of Brudda Death** — a term of his mother's bargain *(`Elizabeth and the Green.md` §5)*. If your remaining threats are largely cult-aligned, **the boy is strangely well protected**, and you can let JonLief keep him longer than looks wise without it being a death sentence. **Nobody at that table will ever know why the cultists keep failing to lay hands on him.**

---

### C — Ysabel *(only if they saved her)*

She has a house, money, staff, a walled garden and a debt she cannot repay.

**She also believes non-humans are beneath humans, and Rowan is half-elf.**

> **Safe, fed, lettered, and raised by a woman who thinks he is lesser.**

**It is appalling and it is genuinely on the table**, and JonLief weighing it out loud is one of the strongest scenes available in Chapter 8. **Do not sweeten her to make it easier.** Let her mean every word of the offer and let her be hurt when he hesitates.

---

### D — Underwood, with Thaddeus, Marion and Virgil

The Alchemical Order would take him. Marion is kind and childless. Virgil is *there*.

**And as far as JonLief knows, Underwood is the single most dangerous place in the world for a child** — a village two years into a plague that turns people into plants.

⚠️ **This is the Virgil/Rowan collision arriving three chapters early.** Virgil would want the boy there and would say so badly, and he would be right, and JonLief would be right to refuse. *(`players/Virgil.md` — Virgil now holds the vaccine path and Rowan is the only immune specimen in the world.)*

**Use it if you want that fight now. Hold it if you want it to land in Act III.**

---

### E — Barney Soot

He liked the boy, he was robbed of him, and he would take him back.

**He is also a chimney sweep who buys children and puts them up flues.**

**Offer it. Let the party say no.** It costs nothing and it tells them something about the city.

---

### ❌ Not options

- **Corbin or anyone in Solteres.** Ironwake lives there.
- **Baltus.** He will not take the boy and he will say so. ⚠️ **That is the point of §2** — he is declining to be the solution.
- **The freed thirty collectively.** Thirty traumatised strangers are not a household. Anselm is.

---

## 4. ⚠️ The option that belongs to the player, not you

**Rowan is eight. He has been sold twice. His mother went cold and then vanished. And he may not remember his father at all.**

**He might not want to go with JonLief.**

> **Do not engineer this.** It is the most honest thing in the chapter and it is also the cruellest, and it should only happen if the player reaches for it or the scene genuinely goes there.
>
> **But know the shape of it in advance**, because if a player asks *does he even know who I am*, you need an answer and you need it to be the true one.

**What Rowan actually has:** a name, a face he half-remembers, and the memory of a mother who stopped looking at him. *(`Elizabeth and the Green.md` §1 — it is exactly backwards and nobody alive can correct it.)*

**He will talk about her at the first chance he gets.** Let him try early and be interrupted. Let him finish it later, quietly, **when JonLief thinks the bad part is over.**

## 5. The recommended sequence

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Ch 8, after the rescue** | ⛪ **Baltus's warning, delivered by the apprentice.** *(§2.)* JonLief gets a deadline, not a decision. |
| **Ch 8 aftermath** | **The offers arrive on their own** — Ysabel if she lives, Soot out of guilt, Virgil by letter. **JonLief refuses all of them** and keeps the boy. Let that be allowed. |
| **Ch 9** | 🎒 **Rowan travels with them.** Run the friction honestly and without malice. ⚠️ **One near-miss**, late. |
| **Ch 9 end / Ch 10 start** | ⭐ **JonLief chooses Ald's Star.** His words, his decision, Anselm's hands. |
| **The finale** | 🏘️ **Ald's Star.** Already in the Arc Plan. **His son is in that town while it happens.** |

> **If the player refuses to let go even after the near-miss — let him.** Rowan survives; the cult cannot touch him. **You will be the only person who knows the campaign was never going to kill that boy.**
