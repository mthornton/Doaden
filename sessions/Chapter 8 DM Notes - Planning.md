## 2. ⚙️ THE ENGINE — Vaskar's commission

**This is the chapter. Everything else hangs off it.**

Vaskar goes to Ironwake, because he has nowhere else to go, and he **begs.** Nineteen dead guards, thirty slaves gone, the champion vanished, the fen lost.

Ironwake does not kill him. **Vaskar is a valuable employee** and Ironwake is a businessman before he is anything else. So he sets a price:

> **Bring me the heads of the party that took Bramblefen. And bring me Ysabel Rochefort — alive.**

Vaskar gets **Ironwake's money, Ironwake's men, and a head start.** He rides for Solteres ahead of the party.

### What Vaskar does with that head start

**He knows what they want.** He stood in that house and watched an elf ask after a woman and a boy. He does not need to guess where they are going or what they will look for.

So he does the obvious thing, and he does it first:

> ### ⚠️ Vaskar finds Rowan and takes him.
>
> **The boy is not a hostage. He is bait.**

### And the part that matters most

**Rowan is safe, and he is safe for the worst possible reason.** Vaskar needs him breathing, because a dead child draws nobody anywhere. The same arithmetic that made Vaskar sell the boy cheap now keeps him alive.

**Vaskar also needs Ysabel alive**, by contract.

**The only people on his list who need to be dead are the PCs.**

> **Which makes him exactly what he was at Bramblefen: a man who would far rather trade than fight, holding something you cannot let him keep.** Run him that way. He will offer terms, and the terms will be real, and taking them will be a catastrophe.

### What he told Ironwake, and what he didn't

⚠️ **Vaskar did not mention that the party were Penitents.**

He saw Gimble's brand. He can do the arithmetic on the rest. But saying it out loud means admitting that **Bramblefen was holding a branded servant of the Church** — which is the one fact that would make Ironwake kill him on the spot, and the one fact that could bring the Office of the Lost Flock up the fen road.

**So Ironwake is hunting four or five dangerous mercenaries. He does not know he is hunting Penitents.** Vaskar is sitting on that, and it is worth money to him, and he will spend it if cornered.

## 3. ⚠️ Ysabel wins, and that is the trap

Her plan works. Exactly as she described it.

She names houses out of Cobb's Ledger, those houses lean on Ironwake, and **Ironwake withdraws the accusation. The trial by combat is cancelled.**

> **Play her triumph straight and let it land.** She has outmanoeuvred a crime lord with a dead clerk's handwriting and she will be insufferable about it, and she has earned that. **The party should be glad for her.**

### ⚙️ Why Ironwake gave it up so easily — he wants the estate, not the verdict

**This is the villain logic of the whole chapter and it is better than a murder.**

A trial by combat kills her. **A dead convicted poisoner's property is seized** — by the crown, by the Church, by whoever gets there first — and Ironwake gets nothing but a corpse and a mess.

**A wife's property passes to the control of her husband.**

So the plan is:

> **Vaskar takes her alive. Ironwake forces her into a marriage to one of his own men. The mills, the warehouses, the whole Rochefort concern becomes his, lawfully, on paper, permanently.**

- **The accusation had to go away for this to work.** You cannot marry a woman who is about to be tried for poisoning her niece. **Her blackmail did not save her. It cleared the paperwork.**
- **It is legal.** That is the horror of it. There is no court to appeal to, because nothing illegal will have happened. Everything in Solteres runs on a book, and this will be properly entered in one.
- **He does not need a champion.** *(See §6.)* He never needed one. The trial was always his second-best option and the party handed him his first.

**The groom.** One of Ironwake's lieutenants — ⚠️ *suggested name, yours to change:* **Mercer Thane.** Thirty-odd, well-dressed, pleasant, and entirely untroubled by any of it. **Do not make him a brute.** The worst version of this man is courteous to her, and to the party, and genuinely cannot see what the objection is.

**The officiant.** A marriage needs blessing and a record. ⚠️ **Somebody in a Church of Azerai vestment is going to have to bless this**, and somebody is going to have to write it down — which gives you a priest who can be found, bought, frightened, or shamed, and a register that can be destroyed.

> 🔑 **So the climax has a date, a place, and witnesses.** That is a far better target than a dungeon. **The party is not raiding a cell. They are stopping a wedding.**

### 🔑 And here is the mechanical bite

**JonLief is an elf. Luthien is a half-elf. Gimble is a gnome.** In Solteres that is not a description, it is a **legal status** — property, on sight, by default. Anselm said it plainly at Bramblefen and he was right: that is why thirty freed people could not come to this city.

**They are protected from that by exactly one thing, and it is not Ysabel. It is the brand.** *(See §4.)*

**What Ysabel actually gives them is everything the brand cannot: access.** A noblewoman's word gets them through a door, into the wealthy quarter, in front of a magistrate, served in a taproom, answered when they ask a question. **Three branded non-humans alone in Solteres are legally untouchable and socially impossible.** Nobody will speak to them. Nobody will sell to them. Every single thing takes four times as long.

> **So when Vaskar takes Ysabel, he does not take their safety. He takes their ability to move**, and he forces them to fall back on the brand — which is where it gets interesting. See §4.

### ⚠️ AND THEY MAY NOT RESCUE HER. PLAN FOR THAT.

**Be honest about who she is to this party.** Ysabel owns non-human slaves, says out loud that non-humans are beneath humans, and believes holding them is a kindness. **Three of this party are non-human and four of the five are branded** — the apprentice is not *(§7)*. They have worked with her because she was useful, and they have never liked her.

**Told that she is to be married off into what is slavery in everything but the name, this party may well call that justice and let it happen.**

> **Do not build the climax on rescuing her and do not argue them out of it.** Let it be a decision with weight on both sides, and let them make it.

**What it actually costs — three things, none of which require sympathy:**

| | |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **The people she owns transfer with the estate** | This is the hook that refuses them the easy satisfaction. **Her mills and warehouses are worked by non-human slaves.** If the marriage goes through, those people pass from a woman who thinks they are children to a man who thinks they are stock. **Letting her fall frees nobody. It sells them downstream.** She is repellent and she is still the better owner, and that should be genuinely uncomfortable for a party of branded non-humans to sit with. |
| **Her household** | Perrin, Dov, Symon, Cael, Mina — five people this party was responsible for all last chapter. ⚠️ **Dov is property on Terms, bought at Bramblefen.** He transfers too. |
| ✂️ **Maelis Vire and the shears transfer too** | ✅ **SETTLED (13 Oct 2026): her fate is tied to the marriage.** Ysabel's blackmail works, the trial is cancelled, so **she never needs to sell workers** and the old "liquidate to fund her defence" clock is dead. **The marriage is the clock now.** If it goes through, Maelis passes to Ironwake with the dress house — the man who *runs* the trade Ashgate buys from. **Stop the wedding and the shears stay in Solteres; let it happen and they are one bad quarter away from the Herald's holding.** *(`The Art-Items.md` §5.)* |
| **Ironwake gets stronger** | Mills, warehouses, ready cash, and a legitimate face. **Chapter 10's boss is measurably harder** because of a thing the party chose not to prevent. They can find that out later, and nobody needs to lecture them about it. |

**Minor, but worth knowing:** Cobb's Ledger goes with her, and the first thing Ironwake does is burn it. **That costs the party nothing** — their lead is the Working Ledger, in their own pack. It costs Ysabel the only weapon she ever had.

## 3a. 📕 THE SECOND SECRET — the book Vaskar actually wants

⚠️ **Vaskar also did not tell Ironwake that the party has the Working Ledger.**

**Because it is his own book.** It records Bramblefen's real business — Ironwake's buyers, his household purchases, the whole trade in one hand. Admitting it is loose is not another failure on the pile. **It is the failure that makes Ironwake stop negotiating**, because Ironwake would mobilise everything to get it back and nothing about that response includes Vaskar surviving it.

### What that does to him

**Vaskar cannot ask anybody for help with the thing he actually needs.**

- **His crew think they are collecting bounties.** They have no idea a book exists.
- **Ironwake thinks he is buying revenge.** He is owed five heads and a bride.
- **Vaskar is running a private recovery operation inside his own employer's contract**, and he is the only living person who knows what is really at stake.

> 🔑 **He is not hunting the party. He is hunting the ledger. They happen to be carrying it.**

✅ **"Then why did he give it to us?" — SETTLED (13 Oct 2026).** The party has already read Vaskar as a man who will not fight if there is any other way out. **Trading the ledger for his life was in character** and nobody at the table found it strange. That night his life was worth more than the book; now that he has his life back, he wants the book too. Same man, same arithmetic.

### ⚙️ And this is how the party learns what they are holding

**They will not learn either secret.** They will learn something more useful: **what that book is worth.**

**When Vaskar confronts them, he demands it.** Not as an afterthought — as the thing he came for. Let the demand leak, in this order:

| Clue | What they definitely take away | What a sharp table might infer |
|---|---|---|
| He names the book **before** he names the bounty | This matters more to him than killing us | — |
| He will not say *why* he wants it, and gets visibly worse when pressed | It is not about the boy. It is not about Bramblefen. | — |
| He offers to **buy** it, with Ironwake's money, at a price that is insulting to his own contract | He is spending his employer's purse on his own problem | ⚠️ *His employer doesn't know* |
| He wants it **before** the heads, and he wants it handed over quietly | He does not want witnesses to the handover | ⚠️ *Including his own men* |

> **The guaranteed takeaway is the one that matters: they are carrying something people will kill for.**
>
> **They already had the evidence and never did the arithmetic.** Ysabel took a *copy* of the sister book and made a crime lord fold inside a week. The party has the master, and they have used it to look up one chimney sweep.

### 🧠 Be ready for what they do next

**This should open lines of thought, so have answers ready:**

- **Blackmail, the way Ysabel did it.** The Working Ledger names current buyers. ⚠️ It is *more* dangerous than hers, because hers stopped six weeks ago.
- **Trade it to Ironwake** to call the hunt off. He would far rather have it than five corpses. **It is the cleanest way out of §8 and it burns their lead.**
- **Give it to the Church.** ⚠️ Remember the canon trap: a heresy case converts seized property — **including people** — into Penitents. *(`Cobbs Ledger.md`.)*
- **Give it to Baltus.** He is the one person who has told them they have latitude.
- ⚠️ **Copy it.** **Cobb proved a copy is possible — that is the entire reason two books exist.** The party has an educated elf and a bard with good hands. **If they copy the ledger, Vaskar's leverage collapses and they can hand over the original and mean it.** This is the smartest available play and a clever table will find it. **Let it work.**
- **Actually read it.** The women. The barrels. The consignments. **It is what they promised Baltus they would do and nobody has opened it yet.**

## 4. ⚠️ THE BRAND IN SOLTERES — it cuts both ways

**Every PC is branded. All of them owe five hundred years.** In this city that single fact is both the thing keeping them free and the thing that can end them, and which one it is depends entirely on *who is asking.*

| | |
|---|---|
| 🛡️ **Against slavers** | **A Penitent is already owned — by the Church of Azerai.** A collared Penitent is a needle the Church can find, and no legitimate house in Solteres wants the Office of the Lost Flock asking where their stock came from. **Show the mark and a lawful slaver backs off fast.** |
| ⚠️ **Against the Church** | They are **two and a half months overdue**, have no orders in hand, no Redeemer within a hundred miles, and nothing on paper that says where they are supposed to be. **Show the mark to the wrong clerk and they are deserters.** |
| ☠️ **Against Ironwake** | **It does not protect them at all.** Vaskar collared Gimble knowing exactly what he was. Ironwake runs the illicit trade; the whole point of the illicit trade is that it does not care what the law says. |

### 🔑 And the sharpest consequence

⚠️ **Vaskar never told Ironwake the party were Penitents** *(§2)*. So every time one of them shows that brand in Solteres to get out of trouble, **they are spending Vaskar's secret for him.**

- It gets them past a slaver.
- It also puts the word *Penitent* into the city's gossip, where Ironwake's people are listening.
- And the moment Ironwake learns that **Bramblefen was holding a branded servant of the Church**, Vaskar is a dead man — because that is the one fact he is hiding to stay alive.

> **The party will not know they are doing this.** They will flash the brand because it works, and three chapters from now they may find out that it is why Vaskar came after them with everything he had left.

### For Gimble especially

**Dave's character is the one who was already collared in spite of all this.** He is the living proof the protection is conditional. He knows better than anyone at that table that the mark on his shoulder stopped nobody at Bramblefen.

> **Hand him that early.** The brand is the reason he owes five hundred years. It is also the only reason he is not for sale on a street in Solteres this afternoon. **And he has met the man who ignored it.**

## 5. 📕 The trail to Rowan

**The party's priority. Run it as a hunt that is already losing.**

| Step | What they find |
|---|---|
| **The Working Ledger** | A name, a trade, a city. **Barney Soot, chimney sweep.**  ✅ **JonLief has NOT read it yet — he reads it at the start of the next session** *(DM, 15 Oct 2026)*. **Full NPC: `world_lore/Barney Soot.md`** — a poor, stern, resigned sweep, **not tied to Ironwake.** |
| **Finding Soot** | Not hard. Sweeps are licensed, known, and work the wealthy quarter. Half a day's asking. |
| **⚠️ Soot's shop** | **They are late.** Men came, put coin on the table, and took the boy. Soot is alive, frightened, and *bitter* — and, ⚠️ **he liked Rowan.** A quiet boy who did what he was told and did not complain, in a trade that chews through children. **He is not a villain and he is not relieved.** He is a small man who was robbed and misses the kid. |
| **What Soot gives them** | A description of the men. A heavy older man who did the talking and never raised his voice. **The party will know exactly who that is.** |
| **The handover** | ✅ **He was forced to sell — for less than he paid.** Men he could not refuse put coin on his table and took the boy, and the coin was an insult. **That is robbery with a receipt**, and it is exactly how Ironwake's people do business. |

### What Rowan is like

**Eight years old, and nobody has asked him anything in two months.**

- He was isolated in a tower for a week, sold, put to work in chimneys, and has now been taken again by men who will not explain.
- ⚠️ **He remembers his mother going cold on him, and he WILL talk about it given any chance at all.** It is the only thing he knows about her and nobody has let him say it in two months. *(See `Elizabeth and the Green.md` §1 — it is exactly backwards and nobody alive can correct it.)*

  > **How to time it:** let him **try** to say it early — in the cart, in the dark, in the middle of something else — and let him be interrupted. Then let him finish it later, quietly, **when JonLief thinks the bad part is over.**
  >
  > He is not being cruel and he does not know what he is doing. He is an eight-year-old telling the only story he has.
- **He is immune to the green, and does not know it, and neither does anyone else.**
- 🔑 **He cannot be tortured by any servant of Brudda Death.** Vaskar is not one. **That protection does him no good at all here**, and that is worth knowing in case a player hopes otherwise.

## 6. Vaskar's crew — what Ironwake's money buys

**Do not give him twenty men.** He lost twenty men. Give him a small, expensive, competent crew, and make each of them a problem of a different shape.

⚠️ **And no heavy hitter.** Ironwake has not replaced Mott and is not looking to; his attention is entirely on the Rochefort estate. **Vaskar is working with a purse and a deadline, not an army.**

**Suggested shape — four or five, not more:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Vaskar** | CR 5, 102 HP, AC 17. Relentless. **He will not die on principle and he never has.** Taking him alive is still worth more than killing him — and the party already knows it, having tried. |
| **Two of Ironwake's professionals** | The six who came with Mott are dead; these are the next tier up. **Ironwake Thug, CR 1/2** — or better, one real duellist. |
| **A tracker** | How Vaskar finds people in a city he does not live in. Give him a face and a reason to be bought off. |
| ❌ **No champion** | ✅ **Ironwake has NOT replaced the Juggernaut.** He does not need one — the trial is cancelled and he is occupied with the marriage. **There is no monster in this chapter**, and that is deliberate: it keeps the climax a negotiation the party can actually win. |

⚖️ **Encounter balance is deliberately out of balance — SETTLED (13 Oct 2026).** Five PCs at level 4 (Easy 625 / Medium 1,250 / Hard 1,875 / Deadly 2,500). Vaskar alone (1,800 XP) is Hard; Vaskar plus his crew is roughly 4,000+ adjusted, well past Deadly. **That is by design.** A fair fight is not on offer; the party is meant to find a more interesting way through. Do not trim the crew to make option C comfortable.

**And the thing that makes the crew interesting:** none of them know the party were Penitents either. Vaskar has not told anyone.

## 7. 🧍 The apprentice — onboarding the new PC

**He is not a Penitent, and that is his whole value in this city.**

- **He is free, human-or-not-it-does-not-matter, and carries Church sanction.** He can walk into a magistrate's office, buy a thing, hire a cart, and ask a question without producing a noblewoman.
- ⚠️ **When Ysabel is taken, he is the only one who can still move freely.** The others are branded: showing the mark gets them past a slaver and into trouble with the Church, and spends Vaskar's secret *(§4)*. **The apprentice can walk into a magistrate's office and ask a question without any of that happening.** That makes him instantly indispensable.
- **Give him that job in his first hour.** The outsider problem solves itself if the party needs him immediately.
- **Baltus sent him to watch and to help**, and he knows it, and he was told so out loud. *(See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md` — apprentices are unbranded, feel no pull, and cannot be assigned files.)*
- ✅ **He delivers the warning about Rowan — SETTLED (13 Oct 2026).** Baltus is not in the city, so the apprentice says it, and says plainly that **he has never seen it done himself; these are Baltus's stories.** *(`What To Do With Rowan.md` §2.)* A secondhand warning from someone who clearly believes it is its own kind of frightening, and it is the first time the apprentice tells the party something about his own order that his order would rather he didn't.
- **He has not been taught the severance rite.** He does not have the thing that could free his friends. **That is his arc, not this chapter.**

⚠️ **Still needed before session 1:** name, backstory, and a reason he personally cares about going into Solteres. Ask the player for the third one — it is the one that makes him a character rather than an escort.

## 8a. ☠️ VASKAR'S BINARY — he kills them or he dies

**There is no deal where Vaskar walks away and the party lives. Ironwake wants five heads and Vaskar's life is the forfeit.** Do not write a negotiation that pretends otherwise — the party will see through it and it cheapens him.

**What he wants, in his own order of priority:**

1. **The Working Ledger**, quietly, before anyone else reads it. *(§3a — nobody knows he needs this.)*
2. **Five heads**, delivered to Ironwake.
3. **Ysabel**, alive, by contract.

**Four ways out. Only the first lets everybody live.**

### A — ⭐ Heads that are not theirs *(he proposes this)*

⚠️ **Ironwake has never seen the party.** He wants *heads*, not these heads.

**And Vaskar knows exactly where nineteen bodies are lying in a fen**, because they were his men, and he watched most of them die, and nobody has buried them.

> **Vaskar offers this himself**, because he is a manager and this is the profitable option: the book, five heads out of the marsh, and everyone walks away from a transaction.
>
> **It should cost the party something to say yes.** Those are nineteen men they killed, and now they are going back for the parts. **And Anselm's people helped kill them**, which means somebody has to decide whether to tell the thirty who went to Ald's Star what was done with the bodies.

**This is the version to aim at.** It is grim, specific, entirely in character, and it leaves Vaskar alive and compromised for Chapter 10.

### B — Buy the contract out with the book

Ironwake would far rather have the Working Ledger than five corpses. **Hand it over directly and the hunt is called off** — and the party burns the only lead they have, and the promise they made to Baltus. *(⚠️ Unless they copied it first. See §3a.)*

### C — They kill him

**Clean, honest, and tragic.** They spared him once on principle and now they have to do it anyway. ⚠️ **Do not let this read as "mercy was stupid"** — see §9. Ironwake learns Bramblefen's takers killed his man, and escalates.

### D — He defects

He cannot win and he knows it. **What he has left to sell is where the barrels went** — he ran a holdfast with one sitting in the Deeps. He trades the cult's route for protection. **Thin, but it feeds the cosmic plot directly** and gives Chapter 10 a man on the inside.

> ### How to run the confrontation
>
> **He does not ambush them in a street.** He names a place and comes to talk, because talking is what he is good at and he has three things to get and only one of them can be taken by force.
>
> **Open on the demand for the book.** Let the party's first realisation be *he came for the ledger, not for us.*
>
> Then the heads. Then the fen. **Let the party say no and mean it, and have C ready.**

## 9. ⚖️ Wolfram's mercy — how to run this without punishing virtue

**Vaskar is in Solteres because Wolfram would not let the party break their word.** The table will make that connection, and how you handle it decides what this campaign teaches.

**Do not let it read as *mercy was stupid*.** Run it as **mercy had consequences, and so would the alternative**:

- **Vaskar alive is the only reason they have a living trail to Rowan at all.** A corpse at Bramblefen names nobody and the Working Ledger gives them a chimney sweep who no longer has the boy.
- **Vaskar alive is why Rowan is alive.** The man needs a living child for bait. A different, colder agent of Ironwake would not have needed that.
- ⚠️ **And Vaskar would have sold Ysabel out the moment the party broke their word with him** — he is a manager, and he remembers who honours a deal.

> **The lesson is not "don't be good." It is "good is expensive, and it buys things you can't see yet."** Let Wolfram's player find that out by living it, and never say it out loud.

## 10. ✅ Corbin Wainwright — in, this chapter

**Confirmed: Corbin comes in now, to keep the cosmic side moving.** That is the right call — Chapters 5, 6 and 7 had almost nothing, and Luthien saw her two nights ago.

### How he arrives

**He finds Luthien, not the other way round.** With the trial cancelled, the Arc Plan's "sketching at the trial" entrance is gone, and the better version was always this:

> Luthien plays — in a taproom, a street, a courtyard, wherever the party ends up. **Somebody two rooms away stops working, puts down a brush, and comes to find out who is doing that.**

**That makes the guitar the cause rather than a coincidence**, and it means the cosmic plot walks up to the party because of something a player chose to do.

### What he is

- **A working artist in a city that has been quietly losing its artists.** He is one of the few still painting, he works fast, and ⚠️ **he burns his best canvases.** *(See `Corbin Wainwright and Orla Miller.md`.)*
- **Warm, generous, useful, and compromised.** All four at once. He should be the most likeable NPC in the chapter.
- **He has been in contact with an imprisoned Saraswati for years**, and he has a brush that is one of her art-items, and he destroys his finest work to keep anyone from looking too closely at how it was made. ⚠️ **Which starves her** — appreciation is her only food, so every canvas he burns is mana she never gets.
- 🎭 **RUN HIM FROM `world_lore/Corbin Wainwright and Orla Miller.md` §§1–8.** Young, would be handsome if he were not so thin *(he has money now and still forgets to eat — he got what he wanted and is not enjoying it)*. **Speaks like an aristocrat and is not one** — assembled over years in rich people's drawing rooms; **a superb mimic with no voice of his own, which is the exact flaw in his painting.** ⚠️ **Luthien would hear that the accent is learned — give him the read unrolled, and do not explain it.** **His hands give him away:** pigment in the creases, noble vowels, a tradesman's hands, a sign-maker's history. **Three successful cover stories before he ever killed anyone** — lying costs him nothing and he does not tell. ⚠️ **Never play him as a hypocrite.** He is sincere, he works hard, he spends his money on the mission. **That is what makes him dangerous.**

### What he gives the party, and in what order

**Do not let him deliver a cosmology lecture.** Three beats, spread across the chapter:

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 — Recognition** | He hears the guitar and **strongly suspects** what it is — ⚠️ **he cannot be certain, because his own test is “better than this person's history accounts for”, not a spell** *(`The Art-Items.md` §3)*. **He does not say so.** He goes still, asks where Luthien got it, listens very carefully, accepts whatever lie he is told while clearly not believing it, and changes the subject. **He will be back.** |
| **2 — The vessels** | Eventually: **there is more than one.** Art-items, made by her, scattered.
