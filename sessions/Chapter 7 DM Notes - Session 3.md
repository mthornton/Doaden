## 2. The shape of the session, in one sentence

> **The match is everybody's cover — Anselm's, the party's, and Vaskar's — and the first person to break it decides how the night goes.**

Everyone in that compound is looking at the same twenty feet of mud. That is the only reason a revolt is possible, the only reason Vaskar's house is empty, and the only reason four armed strangers can move. **Whoever spends that cover first, spends it for everyone.**

**Chosen ending: the revolt takes the compound.** Build toward that. Let the party's choices decide what "takes" costs.

## 5. 🎲 GIMBLE INTO PLAY — guaranteed, inside fifteen minutes

**Dave does not sit and watch. This is his first real session and it is not negotiable.**

**The mechanism: chaos frees him, and the chaos fires no matter what the party does.**

- **If the party acts first** — the ring is the loudest thing in the compound and everyone is facing it. Anselm's people take any commotion as the signal. **The revolt starts.**
- **If the party hesitates** — Vaskar gives the signal, Mott steps in, **and the revolt starts on schedule.**
- **Either way, within a round or two the yard is a brawl and nobody is watching the ring.**

**Gimble is unbound, unmasked, standing in an open timber circle with the crowd coming apart around him.** He does not need rescuing. He needs one round.

> **Hand Dave the reins the moment the first guard goes down.** *"Everyone's looking somewhere else. What do you do?"*

**Equipment:** he owns nothing. ⚠️ **Decide before the session.** Cheapest good answer: **the dropped timber from the ring build, and then a dead guard's short sword.** Let him arm himself on screen in his first thirty seconds — it is a much better introduction than being handed a pack.

**One thing to protect:** Gimble has been in that compound for two months and knows it better than anyone else in the party. **Let Dave be the one who knows where things are.** It makes him useful immediately and it costs you nothing.

## 6. ⚔️ THE REVOLT — this is the spine

**Anselm's plan, as he gave it:** when everyone is watching the fight, the work party attacks the guards and earns their freedom or dies trying. He asked for one thing: **weapons out of the armory.**

**Left alone, they lose.** Unarmed, starved, beaten people against fourteen fen guards and six professionals is not a fight. **The party is the difference, and every choice they make should visibly change the count.**

### The three levers

| Lever | Where | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 🗝️ **The armory** | Blacksmith building, chained and padlocked, forge cold. **Anselm knows where the keys hang.** | **The single biggest swing in the session.** Arms the revolt. Turns a massacre into a fight. |
| 🎚️ **The small prison** | Eight cells, iron doors, **one lever that works all of them at once**, and the key ring on the wall — **which the party has already identified as holding the armory key.** ⚠️ The cells are empty; every slave is out in the yard. | **Not more bodies. Two other things:** the keys *(§6)*, and **the only lockable room in the compound.** |
| 🧍 **Leadership** | Wherever a PC decides to stand | **Anselm can lead and will**, but he is one man and he is sixty. **A PC who takes a share of it is worth +1 on the Revolt Track every round** — see §7a. |

> ### ⚠️ The cells are empty — do not offer the lever as a prisoner release
>
> Every enslaved person was brought out to watch the match. **Pulling that lever opens eight empty rooms.** If a player reaches for it expecting bodies, say so plainly rather than letting them spend a turn on it.
>
> **What the room is actually worth is two things:**
>
> 1. 🔑 **The keys — and the party's decision about them.** They worked out that the armory key hangs on that ring, **and chose not to take it**, because a missing key ring would be noticed and cause more trouble than it solved.
>
>    **That reasoning was correct — and it has an expiry.** See the timing box below.
> 2. 🛡️ **A lockable room.** Eight iron doors and one lever that shuts them all at once, in the middle of a riot. **It is the only defensible space in Bramblefen** — for Mina and Cael and the wounded, for freed people who cannot fight, or for guards the party takes alive and does not want to kill. **A party that thinks of this has solved a real problem**, and it directly improves the casualty count below.

> ### ⏱️ When the keys become free — reward the thinking, then let the clock solve it
>
> **The party's worry was real and specific: someone notices the ring is gone.** Adjudicate it honestly rather than hand-waving either way.
>
> | When | Who would notice | Ruling |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Before the match** | The cell-block guard, at a glance, any time he walks past the wall they hang on. | **Stealth DC 13 to get in unseen, and then a real risk** — the ring is checked at lock-up and any guard passing the room sees a bare nail. **Their instinct was right.** |
> | **Once the match starts** | Nobody. Every guard in the compound is at the ring, including the one who minds that block. | ✅ **No roll. Take them.** The room is empty, the building is empty, and there is nobody left to notice anything until morning. |
> | **After the revolt starts** | Irrelevant. | Take whatever you like. |
>
> 🔑 **And give them this for free if anyone thinks of it: take the key off the ring and leave the ring.** One key gone from a ring of a dozen is not a bare nail. **It answers their exact objection**, and a party that proposes it has earned the armory outright.
>
> > **The point to land: they were not wrong, they were early.** Do not make them feel they missed something. **Make them feel they were right and then patient.**

### 🗝️ Adjudicating the armory — Luthien's key

**Established at the table:** Wolfram examined the lock, judged it unpickable, and reckons he can **break the door — DC 15 Strength.** The party did **not** take the ring of keys they saw in the small prison. Luthien is proposing to conjure a key.

> **The principle: do not adjudicate the spell. Adjudicate the fit.**
>
> "Does your magic work" is a boring question and the answer is always yes. **"Does the thing you made fit a lock you have never seen" is the real question**, and it converts a rules argument into a roll.

**Step one — ask which spell, by name and level.** Not a gotcha; you need to know what you are ruling on.

| What he actually has | Ruling |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Knock** *(2nd level — and it IS on the bard list)* | **It just works. No roll, no key, no argument.** Check his sheet before the session; he may already hold the real answer and not know it. **But it is LOUD** — a knock audible to 300 feet. In this compound that is not a drawback, it is a trigger. See below. |
| **Prestidigitation** *(cantrip)* | Creates a **trinket**, not a working key — and it lasts moments. **Allow it as a tool, not a solution:** a shim, a wire, a wax blank. It grants **advantage** on a lockpicking attempt, it does not replace one. |
| **Minor Illusion** | An image. It cannot turn a ward. **But it can hide a man working on a lock**, or put a guard's attention thirty feet the other way. Reward that use instead. |
| **Creation** *(5th level)* | Not available to a level-3 bard. Say so kindly and early, and offer the list above. |

**Step two — if it is not Knock, move the check to where the uncertainty actually is:**

> **He is not trying to copy a key. He is trying to avoid taking one.** The party already knows where the real key is and has decided that removing it is the risk. **Luthien's plan is a way in that leaves the ring on the nail** — which is good thinking and should be rewarded as such.
>
> **But he has never handled that key.** No impression, no wax, no idea of its shape. He is conjuring a key to a lock he has only looked at from outside.
>
> So: **Sleight of Hand or Dexterity (thieves' tools) — DC 15**, with advantage from whatever he conjured. **Same DC as Wolfram's door**, deliberately. Two players, two approaches, one number — nobody feels cheated.

**Never a flat no.** If the spell is wrong, the effort still buys something: advantage, a second attempt, or knowledge that the padlock is old and the *chain* is older.

### Four ways into that armory — they only need one

| Route | Check | Noise |
|---|---|---|
| **Wolfram breaks the door** | **STR DC 15** | 🔊🔊🔊 Very loud. Everyone in the yard could hear it if they weren't shouting at a fight. |
| **Break the chain, not the door** | The padlock is an object: **AC 19, 5 HP.** One good hit. | 🔊🔊 Loud, but brief and easily lost in a crowd. **Most players never think of this — reward it hard if they do.** |
| ⭐ **The key ring in the small prison** | **Stealth to get in, then simply take it.** During the match: **no roll at all.** Before it: Stealth **DC 13**. | 🔊 Silent. **They already know the key is there. The only question they have ever had is when it is safe — and the match is the answer.** |
| **The forge chimney** | Cold, no smoke, and it goes straight into the building. **Too small for anyone in the party — until Gimble is free.** | 🔊 Silent |

> ⚠️ **The chimney is a gift to Dave.** If Gimble is loose and looking for something to do in his first five minutes, the armory has a hole in the roof exactly one gnome wide.

### 🔊 The noise budget

The match is the party's cover *(§2)*. **Every loud option spends it.**

- **Before the match starts:** noise draws guards. A shattered door brings **1d4 fen guards** at a walk, curious rather than alarmed.
- **During the match:** the crowd is roaring at a giant. **Nearly anything is covered.** This is the window, and the party should be able to work that out for themselves.
- **After the revolt starts:** noise is irrelevant. Everything is noise.

> **So the correct answer to Luthien's question may not be a spell at all: wait ninety seconds.** If a player says that out loud, give it to them for free.

### Casualties — follow the plan, not a quota

**Do not decide in advance how many die.** Count it off what they actually do.

| What the party does | Roughly how the eighteen come out |
|---|---|
| Nothing — revolt runs alone | **Most die.** Anselm among them. *(Track drops fast — §7a.)* |
| Armory opened | **Most live.** Real casualties, but they win their own fight. |
| Non-combatants put somewhere safe | **Mina, Cael, the wounded, and the freed who cannot fight stop being casualties waiting to happen.** See the small prison, above. |
| A PC leads them — targets, order, a place to fall back to | **Fewest losses of any branch.** |
| Ironwake's six drawn off or held | **The main reason anyone survives at all.** Anselm says so himself: those six are why he hasn't risen yet. |

> ⚠️ **Wolfram earns this whether he fights or organises.** Getting eighteen people out alive is exactly as much of a claim on Anselm's loyalty as leading a charge. **Do not make swordplay the only currency.**

> **If he lives he is the most important NPC to come out of this chapter** — the only man alive who tried to tell the Church the truth about Wolfram's arrest and was ignored. **He should be savable, and it should be possible to fail.**

## 7. The twenty — and the rule that keeps this survivable

**Fourteen fen guards + six of Ironwake's = twenty.** Every one of them is in or near the yard, which is the most dangerous configuration this chapter has ever had.

> ### ⚠️ THE RULE: they must never all engage the party at once.
>
> Four level-3 PCs and a fifth in rags cannot fight twenty men. **They do not have to, and the fiction already solves it:**
>
> - **The revolt absorbs most of the fen guards immediately.** Eighteen desperate people grabbing at fourteen men is not a distraction, it is the battle. **Most of the twenty are busy with somebody else all night.**
> - **Ironwake's six have one job and it is not Vaskar's yard.** Their orders concern Mott. Two are on him at all times. **They will not chase a merchant's guards while the champion is loose in a riot.**
> - **The party fights in threes and fours**, in doorways, at the ring, on the Hall steps.

**Track the twenty as a pool and spend them.** Do not replenish a post you already emptied — the review flagged this and it matters more tonight than it ever has.

## 7a. 🎲 RUNNING THE MAYHEM — and tilting it toward the party

**Eighteen freed people, fourteen fen guards, six professionals, a giant, and five PCs in one muddy yard.** Rolling initiative for all of that will kill the session before it kills anyone.

**Do not run the revolt as combat. Run it as a track, and let the PCs move it.**

### ⚔️ THE REVOLT TRACK

**Ten boxes. Starts at 4.** *(The slaves are losing when it begins — they are unarmed and starving.)*

At the **end of each round**, adjust once for each line that is true:

| | |
|---|---|
| **+1** | 🗝️ **The eighteen are armed** *(armory open)* — this applies every single round |
| **+1** | 🧍 **A PC spent an action leading** — directing, rallying, shouting a target, opening a way out |
| **+1** | 🗡️ **Anselm is alive, free and fighting** |
| **+1** | 🚪 **The party took a post, shut a gate, or dropped a named guard in view** |
| **−1** | 🛡️ **Any of Ironwake's six are fighting the slaves** |
| **−1** | 💀 **A PC went down where the crowd could see it** |
| **−1** | 😶 **Nobody did anything for the revolt this round** |

> **10 → the guards break.** Fen guards drop spears and run for the gate or the fen. The compound is the party's.
> **0 → the revolt is crushed.** Survivors are driven back against the wall of the Deeps.

**⭐ Narrate the track every round, out loud, without numbers.** *"The line by the fence is holding now."* / *"They've got the gate."* / *"Three of them break and run for the pens."* **The party seeing their choices work is the whole point** — momentum they can feel is what makes a chaotic fight enjoyable instead of frightening.

### The casualty read-off

**Do not decide this in advance. Read it off the track when the fight ends.**

| Final track | Of the eighteen |
|---|---|
| **10** | **1–2 dead.** They will name the party for it. |
| **7–9** | 3–4 dead |
| **4–6** | 6–8 dead |
| **1–3** | 10–12 dead |
| **0** | Most of them |

### 👥 The Mob — how the eighteen act without initiative

**Treat all of them as one thing.** No rolls, no turns.

- **Any guard adjacent to the mob has disadvantage on attack rolls.** They are being grabbed, shoved, and swarmed by people with nothing to lose.
- **If the track rose this round, the mob takes one fen guard out of play.** Dragged down, disarmed, or run off. You do not roll for it.
- **If the track fell, one of the eighteen dies.** Narrate it as a specific person if you can. That is what the track costs.

### 🏃 Guard morale — use it freely

**Fen guards have no reason to die for Vaskar.**

- **When half of any group drops, the rest run.** Already the rule; tonight, apply it fast and apply it often.
- A guard who watches two mates go down makes a **DC 11 Wisdom save** or backs out of the fight entirely.
- **A drunk guard has disadvantage** — there were several at the whipping post and the fence, and they have been drinking since the fence went up.
- **Let them flee.** A fleeing guard is better drama than a corpse and removes a body from the yard for free.

### ⭐ Four thumbs on the scale — use all of them

1. **The party gets a surprise round if they position first.** They know the revolt is coming and the guards do not. **If they set up before the signal, they choose their targets and act before anyone.** Give this to them; it is genuinely earned.
2. **Ironwake's six are busy.** Their job is the champion, and the champion is either in the ring, loose, or raging. **Every round they spend on Mott is a round they are not spending on the party** — and it costs the revolt nothing.
3. **Never let more than five enemies act in a round.** The Run Sheet's rule, and tonight it is the only thing standing between the party and twenty men. Bramblefen is a *sequence* of small fights. Use the ring, the buildings, the dark, the mud, the torches.
4. **Vaskar cannot be everywhere.** He is one man trying to save an operation, and every minute the yard is chaos is a minute he is managing rather than fighting.

## 8. Vaskar

**He is standing in the yard having just thrown a dead man into it.** He is not expecting tonight to go wrong.

**His problem, the moment a brand is seen:** he has been holding a branded Penitent, and Penitents are the Church's property. He has always known that. **What he has not known until this moment is that four more are standing in his yard.**

- **He does not panic. He calculates.** Killing the gnome tonight was always the plan; killing four more witnesses is a much larger and more expensive plan, and he is a manager.
- **His first instinct is to close the gate**, not to charge. Contain, then decide.
- **He will not die on principle.** At a third of his HP or stripped of his shields, he grabs a hostage and offers a deal — the ledgers, a name, safe passage — and he means to break it. **Taking him alive is worth far more than killing him** *(§11)*.

### ⚠️ Why he does not simply detain Ysabel

The old notes assumed he would. Give him a constraint and adjudicate it honestly:

**He has no authorization.** Seizing a Solteres noblewoman on the eve of her trial is Ironwake's decision, not his, and **Ironwake's men are here for the champion, not to arrest anyone.** With the yard on fire he has neither the crowd cover nor the bodies to do it, and doing it wrong ends him. **He needs the riot put down first — which is exactly the thing the party can prevent.**

**Stats:** CR 5, AC 17, **HP 102**, Relentless (0 HP → 1 HP, once), Feed on Their Fear. **See §12 before you run him.**

## 9. Mott, Durgan — and the misread that is going to blow up

### ⚠️⚠️ THE PARTY HAS THE CAUSATION EXACTLY BACKWARDS

**What they saw:** a giant sitting in the dark playing with a straw doll, docile — and then a dwarf charging the door shouting *"Leave him alone, he never hurt anybody!"*, and the giant instantly roaring.

**What they concluded:** the doll keeps Mott calm, and **Durgan is the one who makes him dangerous.** So the plan is to **take Durgan out and get the doll.**

**What is actually true:**

| They believe | Reality |
|---|---|
| The doll pacifies him | **It is a toy Durgan made him.** It has no power of any kind. It is sentiment, not a leash. |
| Durgan winds him up | **Durgan is the only thing that can switch him off** — see and hear Durgan, one action, rage ends, no roll. It is trust, not magic. |
| *"He never hurt anybody"* is literally true | ☠️ **It is not.** Mott has torn men apart in the ring. Durgan means *he is gentle and they use him as a weapon* — which is true, and is not the same sentence. |
| Removing Durgan makes Mott safe | **Removing Durgan makes Mott unstoppable.** |

> ### ☠️ If they hurt Durgan: Grief-Rage
>
> **Instant rage. Cannot be calmed by anyone, including Durgan. Six rounds of Full Fury instead of four. He hunts whoever struck Durgan.**
>
> In Full Fury he has **resistance to bludgeoning, piercing and slashing**, 90 HP, and two slams averaging **32 damage** if both land. **Wolfram has 28 hit points.**
>
> **This is the single most lethal thing that can happen tonight, and the party is currently planning to cause it.**

### Running the backfire so it lands as tragedy, not a gotcha

**Do not warn them out of character. Do give them every fair chance in the fiction.**

- **Any guard will tell them the truth for free, if asked.** Ask what the Juggernaut's record is and you get a delighted, detailed account of men pulled apart. **Durgan's "he never hurt anybody" is contradicted by the first person they ask.** Nobody has asked.
- **Durgan will say it plainly if anyone speaks to him rather than about him.** He is not hiding the mechanism. Nobody has spoken to him.
- ⚠️ **Anselm's intel would make it worse, not better.** His line is *"the big one is gentle until the dwarf makes him otherwise"* — an outsider's read that **confirms their wrong model.** *(He never got to say it; the guard's boot cut him off.)* **If you deliver it now, you are choosing to harden the misread.** That is a legitimate and very cruel option. Decide on purpose.
- **The doll can still matter — as evidence, not as a tool.** Someone who looks at it properly sees that a slave with nothing made a toy for the thing everyone else calls a weapon. **That is one step from the right answer**, and it is available to any player who stops to think about what they are holding.

### If it goes wrong — escape valves that are not DM fiat

**Grief-Rage should be survivable, and it should be survivable for reasons on the page.**

- **He hunts the one who struck Durgan.** Not the party. **One PC**, specifically, and everyone else can act.
- **The Rage Clock still runs.** Six rounds of Full Fury, then Flagging, then **Burned Out — prone, incapacitated, 2 exhaustion, down 1d4 rounds, and melee hits within 5 ft. are critical hits.** A party that survives and separates wins by waiting.
- ⭐ **Ironwake's six will try to put him down.** He is their employer's investment and they are standing right there. **This is your best valve** — it turns a TPK into a spectacle the party can run from, and it costs Vaskar the guards he needs to stop the revolt.
- **Durgan hurt but alive can still fix it.** Grief-Rage is triggered by *killed or gravely harmed* — so **a party that stabilises the dwarf they just stabbed can get the switch back.** Give them that door and let them find it.
- The timber ring, the buildings, the crowd, and the dark are all real cover. **Use them.**

### The version where they get it right

**Durgan wants one thing and it is not freedom: he wants somebody to treat Mott as a person while it matters.**

- Protect Durgan and the most dangerous thing in the compound is either on their side or out of the fight entirely.
- **Luthien owes here.** He mocked Mott through a cell window an hour ago and got the roar for it. Durgan is not looking for an apology — he is looking for evidence. **Luthien is the PC best placed to provide it, and the least likely to be believed.**
- **Mott does not need to be a boss fight.** Talked past, walked away, left in the ring, or turned into the worst thing in the yard by somebody's bad hour. All four are good endings.

> **The best outcome for Chapter 8 is that those two leave together and owe somebody.**

## 10. Ysabel, her household, and the ledger

**Five extra bodies the party is responsible for, in a riot.** Perrin Aske (54, won't run), Dov Brandt (31, the closest thing to a soldier — and Bramblefen is where Ysabel bought him), Symon Reeve (26, drives the carts), Cael Ordway (18, terrified), Mina Dorn (40s, has bandages and knows how to use them).

- **Stat block:** AC 13, **HP 9**, shortsword +2. **They break** the moment one of them drops, unless a PC gives them something specific to do.
- **Symon and the carts are the evacuation.** Eighteen freed people have to leave the fen somehow, and he is the only person here who can move a loaded cart at speed in the dark.
- ⚠️ **Dov is going back into the place he was sold.** He has said nothing about it. If you want one quiet beat in a loud session, that is where it is.
- **The ledger:** promised to Ysabel **when everyone is clear of Bramblefen.** If she is taken or killed here the deal dies and the party keeps the book by default. **Still do not point that out to them.**
- **Ysabel's own read on tonight:** a riot is a catastrophe for her. She came to buy seamstresses and establish that she was here on ordinary business. **She will want to leave, early and loudly, and the party will have to decide whether to let her.**

## 11. 📕 ROWAN — the lead must survive the session

**Cobb is dead. Do not let the trail die with him.** The party needs a name and a city out of tonight, or Chapter 8 has no engine.

### Primary — the working ledger

**In Vaskar's house.** A different book from the one they already have: the current, ordinary, day-to-day record, and **it contains the sale of the boy.**

- **The Hall is empty.** Everyone is in the yard. **The match is the party's cover exactly as much as it is Anselm's** — that is the elegant version of tonight and it is worth signposting.
- ⚠️ **The chair with the tarp is still in there.** It is empty now. The blood on the wall is Cobb's. Let somebody notice.
- The entry gives **the buyer's trade, name, and city: a chimney sweep in Solteres — Barney Soot** — plus the price, which is low enough to be strange.

### Backups — use whichever the night leaves standing

| Route | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Vaskar alive** | He trades the name for his life. **This is the strongest reason to take him prisoner** and the party should be able to work that out. |
| **The guards who carried the boy** | Two of them walked Rowan out to the buyer. They remember *"a sooty little fella, come up from Solteres."* **Trade-and-city without the name.** Enough to start Chapter 8. |
| **The tower** | The room Rowan was kept in for a week. **Emotional, not informational** — scratches in the plaster, a knot of straw tied like the doll in Mott's hands. Pair it with a real lead; don't make it carry the plot. |

> **Rule for the table: JonLief leaves Bramblefen with a trade and a city, minimum.** How much more he gets is up to them.

## 12. ⚠️ Combat math — read before you run anything

The Run Sheet's tuned-Vaskar encounter is **unverified**. Do not trust its label.

- **Four PCs at level 3 have a Deadly threshold around 1,600 adjusted XP.** Vaskar alone is CR 5 / 1,800. Mott alone is CR 5 / 1,800. **Either one, solo, is already past deadly** — and with a fifth PC in rags with a stick, only barely less so.
- **Mott's two raging slams average 32 damage if both hit. Wolfram has 28 HP.** One round can drop a PC. Say it out loud to yourself now so you are not surprised.
- **Never run Vaskar and Mott in the same fight.** If they both end up engaged, one of them is fleeing, negotiating, burning out, or being held off by the revolt.
- **Keep the exits real.** Escape, surrender, cover, Durgan's intervention, and the crowd should all be live options every round.
- **If Vaskar plans to redirect a hit onto a hostage, telegraph the shield before it matters** — do not let an ordinary attack roll kill someone by surprise.

## 14. Landing it

**One main ending: the revolt takes the compound.** Everything else is texture.

**What "takes" can look like** — let the party's night decide which:

- **Held.** Guards dead or fled, gates shut, eighteen people standing in a yard they own, and nobody has any idea what to do next.
- **Emptied.** Nobody holds it. Carts out through the fen before Ironwake's men regroup. **Cleanest, and the best fit for Ysabel's timetable.**
- **Burning.** Somebody puts a torch in the Deeps. Very final, very hard to explain to a Church that will ask.

**Vaskar can escape, bargain, die, or be dragged out in a cart.** Any of the four closes the chapter. **Alive and held is worth the most** and the party should get to notice that.

## 15. ✅ Aftermath — the last twenty minutes

**Do not skip this and do not stack it. Pick two beats, not six.**

**Available:**

- 🕯️ **Cobb.** His body is in the mud where Vaskar threw it and nobody has moved it. **Somebody should.** Forty years of writing down other people's sales and he never had a line of his own.
- 📕 **The name.** JonLief reads it. Barney Soot. A chimney sweep, in Solteres, which is where they are going anyway.
- 🌱 **Virgil's last night as a PC.** He is carrying samples of the thing that grew out of a woman's ashes and he already believes he is right about all of it. **Give him one beat that points at Underwood** — it is what makes the Chapter 8 handover feel like the character's decision rather than the player's. *(See §16.)*
- 🎸 **Luthien.** The guitar has not answered anything yet and eighteen freed people are sitting in the dark not knowing what happens next. **If he plays, let it answer.**
- ⛓️ **Gimble and the party.** Five brands in one place for the first time. **Dave's scene — let him set the terms.** He is also the answer to their Chapter 1 orders and none of them have said so yet.
- 🧭 **Anselm, if he lives.** *"Who's in charge of you?"* is still unanswered.

**End the chapter on the road, not in the yard.** Carts, the fen, and Solteres somewhere ahead of them.
