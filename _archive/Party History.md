# Party History

*A recap of the Penitents' journey so far, for reference at the table. Covers only what the party themselves witnessed or learned — no DM secrets.*

---

## Chapter 1: Welcome to Doaden
**Session 1 — October 20, 2025**

Four strangers — JonLief, Virgil, Luthien, and Wolfram — came back to themselves in fragments, seated together at a rough bench in a cavernous stone dining hall crowded with dozens of other hollow-eyed prisoners. The stew was grey and bland, but it was the most food any of them had seen in weeks, and they ate it the way starving people eat. Only afterward did they look up and take stock of each other.

Virgil drew the eye first: a man only barely still recognizable as one, his body sprouting mushrooms and strange growths, cones of plant matter grown into the sockets where his eyes had been, a musty smell hanging around him. Luthien asked, without much ceremony, whether the mushrooms were edible. Virgil cheerfully snapped a piece off his own leg and dropped it in Luthien's bowl, explaining that he had engineered the symbiotic fungus to be exactly that. Luthien used it to sop up the dregs and ate it.

Before any real conversation could start, Church of Azerai guards came through the doors and called all four names — a deliberate grouping, though nobody said so out loud — and marched them to a blacksmith's forge.

There, one by one, each was held down and branded on the shoulder with the Mark of Atonement: a stylized sun with a single tear falling from its center. Luthien was seized from behind and branded first, without warning. JonLief, recognizing that resistance now would only waste a life he intended to spend on better battles, stepped forward and asked to go next. *"Smart, for an elf,"* the guard told him, and lifted his own pauldron to show the same mark on his own shoulder, faded to a silvered scar. Every Penitent leaves the smithy branded, he said — on their feet, or dragged out as a corpse. Virgil and Wolfram followed.

They were brought before **Vozruk the Redeemer**, who welcomed them as "new lambs for the flock" in a voice thick with irony and laid out the terms: each owed the Church five hundred years of service, a debt reduced only by missions successfully completed. Most sinners die with the debt unpaid; the four of them, he assured them, were the lucky ones. He had assembled them into a unit. They had the night to get equipped and settled. Their first mission began at dawn.

At the armory, an ancient halfling quartermaster named **Finnian Quickstep** — himself wearing a Mark of Atonement faded almost to nothing, and reportedly running that warehouse for over a hundred and twenty years — outfitted them one at a time, with one standing instruction shouted over his shoulder before he vanished into the stacks: *don't touch anything.* Wolfram took armor, a shield, a longsword, and javelins. Virgil asked only for a cloak and staff. JonLief was given a bow and arrows, a sword, light armor, and a bear-fur cloak that fit him perfectly.

Luthien, meanwhile, had spotted a guitar made of bone balanced atop a seven-foot pile of packs and cookware. He was told to wait his turn. He did not wait his turn. The guitar brushed a pot on the way down, and the entire pile came apart like a wave breaking, scattering gear across the armory floor and opening a cut on the arm of a guard who ran in to stop it. Finnian came out of the stacks at speed, leapt onto Luthien's back, and beat him about the head with both hands until a guard lifted the halfling off. Luthien was banned from the armory for life, issued cracked and broken castoffs — a dagger split down the blade, a short sword missing its tip, torn leather — and put to work righting the mess.

The other three ate dinner without him. Luthien rejoined the table before the meal was finished, in a good mood, and gave an account of the disaster in which none of it was his fault: a sharp-edged codpiece had cut his finger, he had dropped what he was carrying, one piece landed on his foot, he had hopped back into another pile, and Finnian had tripped over the wreckage and knocked over a third. He displayed the quarter-inch cut with some ceremony. Virgil, sympathetic, snapped a blue-green piece of mushroom off his own calf and gave it to him.

The next morning, Vozruk stood before a coastal map and laid out the mission: a small island called **Dol**, off the southern coast in the region known as the Shattered Coast. Unusual ship traffic, missing locals, and an island the Church's records list as uninhabited — meaning someone was operating there without the Empire's blessing. A team of Penitents had been sent weeks earlier and never returned; the local governor blamed a shipwreck, but had also recently arrested several heretics who all happened to be experienced sailors. The party's orders: reach the island, find out what happened to the first team, discover who was operating there, and eliminate them.

Vozruk then introduced **Fid**, the camp's blue-haired gnome inventor, who arrived bouncing into the room atop a malfunctioning contraption he called the Puff-a-loco and who firmly corrected Vozruk's description of him as a weaponsmith — he was, he insisted, the master inventor in charge of peace-keeping technology. Fid showed off two devices. The **Ball of Undeath**, a two-inch copper sphere that bursts into enchanted shards at the top of its arc: thrown to exactly the right height, the shards harm only undead; thrown wrong, they harm everyone. And the **knitting spider**, a one-use clockwork that senses fresh blood, leaps to the wound, and stitches it closed with a touch of magic.

Vozruk judged the party far too green to be trusted with the Ball of Undeath, and demonstrated the second device by drawing and throwing a knife faster than anyone could follow, opening a gash on JonLief's cheek. The gold spider pinned to Fid's jacket came to life on its own, bounded across the room, landed on JonLief's face, and knitted the wound shut in seconds before falling to the floor in pieces. Fid officially issued the party his one remaining spider, handing the box to Wolfram — and, whispering that he had lied and actually had two, slipped a second into Wolfram's belt pouch out of Vozruk's sight. Wolfram told the rest of the party he was holding both.

Luthien asked Vozruk for the shiny ball. Vozruk stared at him and returned to his papers. Outside, Luthien asked Fid instead, and Fid handed it over immediately, delighted, asking only that he not mention where he got it. Wolfram stepped forward as if to take it away; JonLief touched his arm and told him to let it be.

The party set out from the military camp on the Castur River aboard a modest thirty-foot ship, the ***Hreowan***, better suited to fishing than to a military voyage. The trip took three uneventful weeks — until the night before they were due to arrive, when a violent storm made navigation impossible and drove the ship onto partly submerged reefs. The *Hreowan* broke apart, and the party woke washed up on an unfamiliar beach.

## Chapter 2: Dol Island

The party woke scattered on the sand with no sign of the crew. Exploring, they came across puddles of blood but didn't investigate further, and disturbed a pile of old bones — two skeletons rose to attack, and the party made short work of them.

Footprints led to a break in the vegetation and a stone path buried under the sand, and the path led to the cliff face, where the lower rock had been carved into an ornate façade, aged and chipped but once beautiful. An archway opened onto stairs descending into darkness, their walls covered in glass mosaics the party paused to admire — landscapes, abstract patterns, and several multi-panel sequences that told a story.

The stairs opened into a great cave, its ceiling open to the sky, built around an island of soil and vegetation suspended over a drop of unknowable depth. A shrine stood at its center. Scattered around it were bones and body parts — roughly two dozen people, many in pieces, the flesh long rotted away — along with scraps of clothing and homemade jewelry of no value. The remains looked to be about two years old rather than ancient, and there were no weapons anywhere among them: these had been ordinary men and women, not fighters. A large apple tree grew on the island, and the party picked as many apples as their packs could hold.

A second bridge led to a large stone door with no handle, script in an unknown language carved above it and three symbols set into the door itself. The party puzzled out a way through by wedging an apple into place to trigger it open. Beyond, in the hall, a ring of runes carved into the floor made them suspicious enough that they used *mage hand* to tap each stone from a distance rather than trust it with their weight — which revealed a falling-stone trap and let them pick their way around it. Wary of a second trap on the bridge ahead, they sent Wolfram across first with a rope tied around his waist.

In the first chamber they found three cultists and two statues of mummified ancient warriors facing a third statue with a mirror set into it. Two of the cultists hurried out; the third told the party *"too late, we have what we need,"* smashed the mirror, and fled — and as the mirror broke, both mummies came alive and attacked. The party put them down. JonLief recognized the mirrored statue as imagery from the Vasant Panchami festival, and, on reflection, identified the figure as **Saraswati**, a goddess of the arts out of old mythology.

In the second chamber, a cultist was heard giving an order — *get the blood loaded for transport, I will delay the intruders* — before the party reached him. They captured him and tried to question him. He answered only with a cackled *"Hail Adephagia!"* before biting off his own tongue and choking to death on his own blood, smiling. On his body they found a map marking the location of an old church.

That same room held an ornate tomb with a hole chipped into its side and blood oozing out of it. An unnatural fear pressed against the party as they approached; Wolfram pushed through it to look inside, and saw a woman in a bloody white gown, four-armed, bound in chains, her body unmarked by decay and an open wound in her side where the blood was seeping. She was the most beautiful woman he had ever seen. Her eyes opened and found his, full of sorrow and terror. When he brought the others over, she — and the sense of dread — had vanished.

In the third chamber, a thick pool of dark blood on the floor drew itself up into a monster made of coagulated gore. The sight of it froze them; they fought it and destroyed it. Afterward they found a ten-foot circle carved into the floor, ringed with a spiral of runes in the same unknown language as the door above.

Following a break in the wall, they emerged onto a different beach in time to see four robed men loading barrels into a boat, with a larger single-masted ship waiting offshore. Two of them drew weapons and came at the party while the other two kept loading. The party killed both, but the barrels went aboard and the ship set sail regardless. Among the dead they found the map and an **amulet of transportation** set with three crystals — two clear, one red and burnt-looking. Examining it, JonLief touched a clear crystal and was instantly transported back into the third chamber underground. The party worked out that the remaining clear crystal would do the same thing once more, and that the burnt one was spent. **One charge left.**

With the fighting done, the party took a long rest on the beach, and the chapter ended.

## Chapter 3: Ald's Star

Stranded on the beach after clearing Saraswati's crypt, the party watched the cultists' ship recede into the distance with its cargo of barrels, the mainland's mountains a faint shadow across roughly three miles of open water. Too exhausted to give chase, and with night falling, they settled in to make camp instead. Wolfram suggested sheltering in the crypt below, but Virgil and Luthien overruled him — neither had any interest in bedding down among the cultists' corpses, or in risking the guardian statues stirring again. They built a fire on the beach from wood gathered at the jungle's edge, ate rations supplemented by berries Virgil gathered, and JonLief took the night watch, resting through meditation rather than sleep.

The next morning was clear and calm. Studying the map they'd taken off the dead cultist, the party recognized it as the stretch of coastline across from Dol Island, its towns marked but unlabeled except for one circled location titled "St. Pyotr." Reasoning that this was their only real lead — and that walking back to Vozruk's camp would take months even if they knew the way — they rowed to the mainland to find it.

The crossing was uneventful. They landed on a rocky beach, found a road matching the one on the map, and followed it east toward St. Pyotr's, expecting to pass through a small village along the way.

As they approached, a halfling working a nearby field spotted them, dropped his hoe, and ran for the village shouting "invaders!" JonLief called for the party to secure their weapons and look friendly. They were met at the village entrance by an older halfling named **Ald**, pitchfork in hand and furious, flanked by a red-haired halfling named **Red** — both with unmistakably green skin. Ald mistook them for another delegation from the Alchemical Order, come back to pressure him over three wagons of cabbages he wasn't ready to sell, and aimed the whole speech at Virgil. Virgil said he was no longer part of the Order; Ald didn't buy it, said his gran-poppy had always told him the Order couldn't be trusted, and moved with Red to run them off.

Wolfram stepped in front of the pitchforks with his hands raised and explained that they were Penitents of the Church, pursuing heretics — nothing to do with the Order. Both halflings stepped back. *"Heretics! We ain't no heretics,"* Red squeaked. *"We serve the Emperor, may he rise again!"* Ald stood down, introduced himself and the village — **Ald's Star** — and declared they'd find no better servants of the Church on the whole coast.

When Luthien bluntly asked why he was green, Ald was first insulted (Luthien had suggested half-orc) and then proud: his grand-poppy had cultivated a strain of cabbage generations back, the most nutritious and delicious in the world, grown only from the village's own seed. Eat it every day and you eventually turn that beautiful color of green. No magic, no wizards — just hard work and love of the land.

Ald was gathering a posse, not to run the party off but to hunt whatever had been killing the village's goats and sheep at night for the last few weeks — not eating them so much as tearing them apart, in a way nobody in the village had seen before. His son Bart usually handled predators, but this felt different, and he didn't want the boy out alone. Wolfram offered to help hunt it, free of charge, as a servant of the Church. Luthien objected — they had no money at all, not a single copper — so Ald offered a trade instead: help with the hunt, and he'd tell them what he knew about St. Pyotr's. Which was, he said, no church at all. *"That's no church, son. It's a loony bin."*

The party followed Ald to the dining hall inside the church at the center of the village, where a couple of dozen green-skinned halflings were finishing the day's work and setting out supper. Ald introduced the party to the room, told them the Emperor had provided in their time of need, and the whole hall answered *"may he rise again"* out of ingrained habit.

Virgil said flatly that he didn't trust them. Wolfram said he sensed they were good people and that Virgil was still bristling over the misunderstanding at the gate; when Virgil announced he was going to scout the village as a spider, Wolfram said he wanted nothing to do with it and walked off, and JonLief followed him. Only Luthien volunteered to help — he'd been wanting an audience.

Luthien took the front of the room and played without a word of introduction. The halflings had never heard music of that quality; his first song, a traditional folk tune about the satisfaction of a good harvest, brought the hall to cheers. Virgil slipped out under cover of the applause. Riding the response, Luthien launched into an original of his own — a fast tavern favorite called **"The Nuns of Nantucket"** — and it did not take long for the room to catch the lyrics. Ald rushed the stage, grabbed his arm, thanked him loudly for a wonderful performance, and announced that dinner was ready. Off to the side he demanded to know how a man who sang something so disrespectful could possibly represent the Church. Luthien snapped back that he didn't represent the Church — he served it, and not by choice, and it had never done anything for him but cause pain. JonLief smoothed it over, framing Luthien as a renowned musician from cities where piety was valued less highly, and apologized on his behalf. Luthien muttered about backwards pumpkin bumpkins as he was ushered away.

Virgil's scouting turned up nothing. The village really was what it appeared to be: a simple farming community. Supper was cabbage with a little spiced goat, and it was shockingly good.

After dark, the hunting party set out for the field where the last sheep had been killed: JonLief, Luthien, Wolfram, and Virgil, joined by Red, Ald's grown son **Bart** — the village's peacekeeper and protector — and Ald himself. And, over his objection, the village's sharp-tongued housekeeper **Alice**, a widow of middle age who informed the assembled company that there would be no discussion and not another word said on the matter.

They kept watch for about an hour before a deep growl came out of the darkness, and a great four-legged beast came bounding at them — wolf-like, larger than any wolf anyone had seen, and mutilated: flaps of skin hanging loose, blood seeping from open cuts, iron bars worked into its head and body, some visible and some sunk into the flesh, with iron spikes jutting out where they were anchored. Before anyone could react it hit Alice in the chest, drove her to the ground, and tore her throat out so violently that her head came away from her body.

As Bart and Wolfram closed on it, a second, unseen hound came in from behind, caught Red in its jaws around the middle, and shook him until he came apart. It dropped him dead and started creeping toward JonLief.

The fight with the first beast went back and forth — the iron bars turned more damage than anyone expected — until Wolfram's sword finally found unprotected flesh just above the shoulders and came down without stopping, leaving the beast in two pieces. The second turned on Luthien and Virgil; Luthien's rapier proved to have little effect, so he cast a spell that held the creature immobile long enough for Virgil to finish it with a blow from his staff, empowered by exploding fungus.

The party carried Alice's and Red's bodies back to the village, with Virgil dragging one of the hound corpses behind him. Ald wept and asked what the things were and whether more would come. Wolfram told him he had sensed they were not of this realm, that they had come from outside this world, and that now he had encountered them he would feel their presence from a couple of miles off — he was confident there were none left in the immediate area, but urged the village not to go out after dark for a while and not to travel alone. Ald said plainly that his people would not have survived the night without them.

Virgil, gore still on his hands, confirmed the corpse matched nothing he had ever seen or read about, and disposed of the remains in the village's fire pit. The party spent the night in Red's now-empty house, where they found and took six sealed jars of his pickled cabbage, having no coin and few supplies of their own.

The next morning, before moving on, the party attended the wake and burial for Alice and Red alongside the rest of the village, and spent the morning getting to know most of Ald's Star — a small enough community that they met nearly everyone, including William, who tended the horses and livestock, and Walter and Edith, whose young daughter Matilda kept the party entertained with constant chatter and wanted to know from Luthien whether the monster was really dead. Afterward, Ald made good on his promise: he told the party what he knew about St. Pyotr's — repeating his warning that it wasn't a real church, and mentioning that the place had stopped buying the village's goats and grain a couple of months back and had stonewalled every attempt to find out why — and gave them directions to find it.

## Chapter 4: St. Pyotr's

Following Ald's directions, the party made their way to St. Pyotr's — and even from a distance, something was clearly wrong. Dark clouds had gathered over the building, with tendrils of black fog visibly reaching down toward it. Before going any closer, someone conjured a bird to fly ahead and scout; it died the instant it flew into the haze, dropping out of the air.

The front doors were unlocked and unanswered. Inside the vestibule, under a smell of old wood and incense with rot underneath it, stood a life-sized bronze statue of Saint Pyotr, its placard reading *"In dedication to Saint Pyotr, healer of madness and consecrator of the wretched."* Luthien tried to push it over on a whim; it was far too heavy to budge.

Before they got much further, they heard cultists talking in the garden ahead and chose to strike first rather than wait to be found. The fight turned into a chaotic scrum as everyone tried to funnel through the same doorway at once — and it turned out the cultists were not alone. Among them was **another of the iron-barred hounds**, the same kind of creature that had killed Alice and Red two days' walk up the road, at the same place Ald said had stopped buying his cabbages. The party recognized it immediately, and killed it along with the cultists.

A locked metal grate set into the ground at the edge of the building resisted every attempt to get it open — a two-foot-square opening dropping straight down into darkness, with the stench of rotting meat coming up out of it. They moved on without finding out what was underneath.

In the dining room, a table was still set with plates and cutlery and food gone to rot two or three months old — whatever had happened here had interrupted the ordinary life of the place all at once. In the kitchen they found a heavy-set priest dead on the floor in his working robes, a blood-crusted knife still rigid in his hand, deep slashes across his body and his throat opened by the killing blow — and his distended, pulsing abdomen burst open as they watched, spilling a mass of gore-soaked rats that scattered for the corners. Nobody was shaken badly enough by it to lose their composure. In the library, a search turned up a book titled ***Myths of the Old Gods***.

In the south bell tower, they found the wooden stairway collapsed and no way up. Hearing a commotion above, they ducked behind a pile of rubble and hid. Two cultists dragged a man in priest's robes to the edge of the platform seventy feet overhead. As he begged for his life, one threw a noose around his neck and tied the other end to the bell's crossbeam; the second drew a dagger, slashed his belly open, and kicked him off the ledge. The noose caught. His intestines dropped and splattered onto the ground around the party's hiding place, and his spasms rang the bell in sharp clangs until he stilled. When they looked up again, the cultists were gone.

Deeper into the sanitorium, a locked cell held a prisoner who had been screaming himself hoarse begging to be let out. Something about him didn't sit right with the party, and they chose to leave him locked away rather than open the door.

In the torture chamber, they fought and killed one guard and used magic to charm the other — then had the charmed guard walk back into the chamber before locking him in and leaving him there. Among the room's clutter they found a stock of healing potions. They also found a second torture victim, a woman, still alive but barely sane after what had clearly been a prolonged ordeal — scarred all over, taken to the brink of death and healed back again and again. The party healed her wounds, but given her fractured mental state and their focus on the Church's mission, they made the difficult call not to bring her with them. They left her behind in an unlocked cell with a few days' worth of rations rather than risk taking her along — a decision made with real hesitation, weighing what little they could offer her against what the Church still expected of them.

In the sanitorium's upper office, they found the caretaker Father Ignatius dead, nailed to the wall, alongside his personal journal. It chronicled his long decline: what began as harsh but ordinary treatment of his patients — deprivation of food, water and sleep, whipping to "encourage sane behavior" — grew steadily crueler over the years as he became convinced they were infested by spiritual creatures from another realm, and that enough pain could drive the infestation out. By the end he was using torture devices, and his handwriting had degraded page by page along with his mind. Near the end he wrote of being able to feel the barrier between this realm and that other one, and of believing that if he thinned it far enough, the spirits infesting his patients would be drawn back through it once he drove them out. A ring of keys was found on his body.

The chapel held the worst of it. A heavy black mist hung in the air, thick as fog and sour-smelling, and it burned to breathe. Empty pews filled most of the room; at the front stood a stone altar with a bound and wounded figure laid across it. Beside the altar stood a stone ring about three feet across, standing three feet off the floor like a doorway, with black particles pouring out of it — the source of the mist. Behind the altar, a very tall, very pale man in an ornate headdress chanted in deep guttural tones and did not react to the party's entrance at all.

Five cultists advanced, and their leader stopped to address the party directly: *"How dare you interrupt. I can smell the stink of Saraswati on you. Are you here for revenge? Upset she was outplayed by Adephagia? Fools. You know nothing of the Old Gods. But don't worry — Brudda Death will tutor you. Books will be written on the suffering you will experience."* It was the first time the party had heard that name.

The fight was intense; JonLief was dropped to 0 hit points and clung to life through his death saving throws before the party turned the tide. They struck the herald hard enough to break the ritual, but he proved strong enough to escape out the back of the chapel before they could finish him off. The mist thinned and dispersed once nothing was sustaining it.

## Chapter 5: The Inn

After St. Pyotr's the party had no lead at all — the cultist's map had brought them to the sanitorium and marked nothing beyond it — so they spent roughly a month on the road, working where they could, asking after Vozruk's camp from people who had never heard of it, and generally trying to find out where in Doaden they actually were.

At the end of that month they came to a busy roadside inn called the **Calf & Ass**, and Luthien struck a deal to play music in the common room in exchange for food and lodging for the night — a performance that would end up mattering more than anyone expected.

The common room had its share of notable company. A table of slave merchants sat trading business nearby: racists who dealt only with human buyers, and who trafficked only in non-humans and half-humans — which was every member of the party except Wolfram. They wanted nothing to do with them and kept their distance.

Also staying the night were **Master Thaddeus and his wife Marion** of the Alchemical Order, waiting on a delayed shipment. Thaddeus's body ends at the waist in a long, ringed, boneless tentacle — worm-like at a glance — coiled about Marion's shoulders and torso; she carries him, and he rides her, and neither of them makes anything of it. When the party asked about the transformation, he gave them an explanation — later proven untrue — that it was a vanity experiment gone wrong. Marion said nothing, because she cannot; the party would learn later that she speaks only mind to mind.

Also present was **Lady Ysabel Rochefort**, traveling with her champion **Ulfric Dreadmarsh**, her lawyer, an assistant, and several guards. She had been accused by her uncle, **Lord Leofard Ironwake**, of poisoning her niece, and — having no way to prove her innocence — had called for trial by combat, with Ulfric set to fight as her champion in two days' time in Solteres.

That evening, a commotion broke out downstairs: **Baron Emeric Blackholt** arrived with two thugs and caught his own betrothed, **Lady Marguerite de Valpreux**, in a room with her lover, a young and near-penniless baron named **Corvin Ashwinter**, who was staying at the inn under the alias "Godric." A thug threw Corvin down the stairs and the room erupted into a brawl, with Marguerite sobbing *"you said you would not hurt him!"* as she threw herself between them. The party watched it unfold but chose to stay out of it; Luthien kept playing through the chaos.

It was only after things settled that Ulfric was found murdered upstairs — and the knife left in his body was recognized as Luthien's own, lifted from him at some point during the night's chaos. Luthien was accused on the spot. When the local Sheriff, **Eastwood**, arrived, he cleared Luthien quickly: half the room had watched him playing on stage at the time of the murder, an alibi nobody could dispute. Eastwood then cracked the case himself — a barmaid had drugged Ulfric with a sleeping tonic earlier in the evening and had also lifted Luthien's knife, and Emeric was identified as the one behind the whole scheme, the scandal downstairs having been staged as a distraction. Emeric was taken into custody. What became of him, the barmaid, or Corvin and Marguerite afterward, the party never learned.

While the inn sat locked down waiting on Eastwood, a very old man walked into the common room and sat down with the party — and strangely, nobody else in the room seemed to notice him at all, as though he simply couldn't be perceived by anyone but them. He thanked them for stopping the summoning of Brudda Death back at St. Pyotr's — something they had told no one — offered nothing about who he was or what he wanted, and simply encouraged them to keep up the good work before the moment passed.

The next morning, the party agreed to escort Lady Ysabel on the road to Solteres. Master Thaddeus and Marion joined them for the first leg of the journey — they were due to meet an acquaintance along the way in a few days' time before turning back toward home.

## Chapter 6: Swine Raid

The party set out escorting Lady Ysabel Rochefort toward Solteres, along with four male servants who doubled as guards and her lady-in-waiting. With Ulfric dead she had no real protection, and the party had access to carts, horses, supplies, and someone who knew both the road and the city. Master Thaddeus and Marion traveled with them for the first leg, planning to meet a colleague on the road before turning back toward Haleyon.

Somewhere on that road, Ysabel raised the obvious question with all four of them: would one of them stand as her champion at the trial. She asked more than once. All four of them said no.

A few nights into the trip, Thaddeus took Virgil aside and finally told him the truth about his transformation — the story he'd given at the Inn had been a lie out of embarrassment. In reality, Thaddeus came from a long line of alchemists and had spent years obsessed with extending human life, driven in part by his mother's death when he was nine. His research into the disease resistance and longevity of giant squids led him to test an experimental treatment on himself and on Marion, whose own work was on unlocking innate telepathy. The results weren't what either of them hoped for: they can speak mind to mind, but Marion lost her voice entirely, her mouth and throat reshaped into something like a squid's beak, while Thaddeus's body below the waist became the ringed, boneless length he now carries himself on.

Thaddeus also confided in Virgil about the real reason they were on the road: a farming village called **Underwood**, a day's ride outside Haleyon, has been suffering for two years from a strange affliction — green patches on the skin that thicken into nodules and then into spore-bearing pods, eventually consuming and killing those infected. Worse, the further it advances, the less the infected care; they grow protective of the pods and addicted to eating their fruit. The Church denied the village aid, citing the ongoing war in the north, and ordered a quarantine it never enforced. The Alchemical Order sent Thaddeus and Marion instead. They were on their way to meet a colleague, **Willowbread**, who claimed to have found something similar growing farther east and was bringing samples and notes. Thaddeus asked Virgil to come help directly, dangling the chance to earn back his standing with the Order — but Virgil turned him down, telling Thaddeus he needed to stay with the party, who were still under standing orders from the Church.

The next day, the road ahead erupted into screaming: a band of Porcine beastmen was in the middle of raiding a wagon of produce. Ysabel's servants drew their short swords and closed around her; Thaddeus and Marion took cover behind them. The party threw a bottle of oil into the fray and put fire to it, and between the two the fight went far more easily than expected. In the chaos, though, several of the beastmen broke through to Marion and beat her nearly to death before the party could drive them off.

The wagon turned out to belong to Willowbread's party — the very colleague Thaddeus was meant to meet. Willowbread himself was dying, his gut torn open and half eaten; with his last words, he gave Thaddeus a rough location for where the strange pumpkins had been found and warned him that a **coven of witches** was growing them. His group had avoided confronting the witches directly and had instead used a distraction to steal what they needed. Most of the cart's dozen pumpkins had been destroyed in the fighting, but Thaddeus salvaged what scraps he could.

The party stabilized Marion. Afterward, Virgil examined the surviving pumpkin material and found that the pumpkins themselves looked, smelled and tasted entirely ordinary — but that the magic wasn't in the flesh at all. It was concentrated entirely in the seeds: a blend of Enchantment, Necromancy, and Transmutation. With that information in hand, and Marion stable, Thaddeus gathered his notes and the pumpkin remains and set off back toward Underwood.

With the fight over, the party took a long rest before continuing on toward Solteres with Lady Ysabel. The following two weeks of travel passed without incident, leaving them still two days out from the city when the next leg of their journey began.
