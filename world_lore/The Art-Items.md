# The Art-Items of Saraswati

> **FINALISED October 2026.** This file is now the single source of truth for the roster. `Campaign Arc Plan.md` §8.6, `The Three Faiths.md` §6, `The Ashgate Purchases.md` §7 and `The Patient.md` §6 all defer to it.
>
> Background on why they exist: `Saraswati and Adephagia.md`. What they mean theologically: `The Three Faiths.md`.

---

## 1. What they are, in one paragraph

Before her last fight with Adephagia, Saraswati knew she was going to lose. She spent nearly all her remaining power making a set of tools for artists — and then scattered them. Each one takes a person **who is already good** and makes them legendary. It is not a shortcut for the talentless; it is an amplifier for the gifted. The wielder finds technique and inspiration arriving faster than practice can account for.

That is the entire strategy. Great work gets made, great work gets admired, **and genuine admiration is unwitting worship** — a trickle of mana back to a god in a cell. Per person it is negligible. But masterpieces last centuries and are seen by thousands, so the trickle never stops and it compounds.

**It is also agonisingly slow, and she has been in the Prison Realm for five hundred years.**

## 1a. 🦴 THE BONE — what they are made of, and the answer to the six-arms discrepancy

⚠️ **EVERY art-item has bone in it, and the bone is HERS.** *(Settled Oct 2026.)*

**The iconography of Saraswati shows six arms.** That is what the Church's own suppressed material says, it is what `What the Party Knows - The Book.md` records, and it is what every surviving image of her depicts.

**Wolfram looked through the chipped hole in that tomb on Dol Island and counted four.** So did Aldous, five chapters earlier, with the first Penitent team — ⚠️ **two independent witnesses, and the party has both accounts.**

> 🔑 **She took two of her own arms apart and made the vessels out of them.**
>
> That is what *"nearly all her remaining power"* actually cost. Not an abstraction, not a sacrifice in the poetic sense — she was going to lose, she knew it, and she dismantled a third of her own body on a workbench and scattered the pieces across the world so that strangers could make beautiful things with them for five hundred years.

**What this buys you, all of it from threads already in your files:**

| | |
|---|---|
| 🧩 **It closes a dangling thread the party can solve unaided** | The book says six. Two PCs saw four. **Nobody has to explain anything** — they have both halves and one of them will do the arithmetic. |
| 🔢 **It explains why the true number is unknowable** | She does not know how many pieces came out of two arms, or how many survived five centuries in a world that burns things. **Not even the god has the list** — which is exactly what §2 needs. |
| 🔍 **It gives you a second, physical tell** | See §3. The first tell is a career that outruns a life. **The second is a piece of bone that is five hundred years old and has not yellowed, scratched, or aged a day** — and that is not from any animal anyone can name. |
| 🔥 **It makes destruction obscene** | Open decision 6. Breaking a vessel is not losing an asset. **It is breaking a bone of a living god who is awake and chained and can feel it.** |
| ⚠⚠ **And it puts a knife to the Herald's holding** | The Tormentors bleed her for mana. **Bone would be concentrated.** If anyone in that building ever worked out what the chisel and the rib are, they would grind them — and they are *right there*, in bags, unlabelled. **The enemy's ignorance is the only thing keeping two vessels intact.** |

### Where the bone is, item by item

| Item | The bone |
|---|---|
| **The guitar** | ⚠️ **Already canon — it is *the bone guitar*.** The body and the soundboard. The largest single piece anyone has found. |
| **The brush** | The **ferrule** — the collar binding the bristles — and the handle. The part a painter's fingers never leave. |
| **The mask** | Carved from one flat plate of it. Pale, smooth, almost featureless, which is also simply correct for a stage mask. |
| **The shears** | The handles. *(`world_lore/Solteres.md`, `§5`.)* |
| **The chisel** | The handle — ⚠️ **unusual enough to notice**, since a mason's chisel is hafted in wood or horn, not bone. **A stonemason would remark on it.** Bosk Dunmar would remark on it. |
| **The potter's rib** | All of it. A rib is traditionally bone anyway, which is why **nobody has ever looked twice at the one in Hanne Vetter's hand.** |

### ⚠️ How to run the bone at the table

- **It does not yellow and it does not mark.** Five hundred years of daily use and it looks like it was cut last week. **To any craftsman that is the impossible part** — not that the tool is good, but that it is not worn.
- **It goes warm.** Already written into Arc Plan §8.6 Beat 1: *"the bone goes warm under his hands."* ✅ **That line was always the bone answering.** Keep it as the only overt magic any vessel ever shows.
- 🔬 **Virgil is your identifier, and this is a real use for him.** He is the one member of the party who would look at a bone and ask what it came off. **It matches no animal he knows, no animal in any book, and it is not ivory.** That is a conclusion he can reach without a spell and without the DM saying a word.
- **Say nothing about the family resemblance.** Six objects across ten chapters, all with bone in them, is a pattern a player will find on their own and feel clever about. ⚠️ **Do not point at it. Not once.**

## 2. ⚠️ THE ROSTER — six findable, more lost

**She made more than six.** Call it nine or a dozen in that last act; the exact number is deliberately not fixed and **nobody alive knows it, including Saraswati** — she has been in a cell for five centuries and has no idea what survived. Over that time items were destroyed, buried, burned in house fires, sank with ships, or are sitting unrecognised in a drawer in a country the campaign never visits.

> 🔑 **Why this matters mechanically: the six below are what SURVIVES, not what exists.** The party can never be told they failed to find a seventh. The mana dial stays honest, there is no completionist failure state, and you keep the freedom to add one later if a chapter wants it.

|:--:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The bone guitar** | Music | **Luthien** | Ch1 — Finnian's armory | ✅ **In the party's hands.** He does not know. 🦴 **Largest single piece of her anyone has found.** |

## 3. 🔍 HOW YOU FIND ONE — the rule is already canon, and it is Corbin's own method

**Do not invent a detection spell.** You already wrote the procedure, in `Corbin Wainwright and Orla Miller.md`, as a worked example. Corbin wanted the brush, so he found the three painters in the land who had risen fastest and checked each one:

| Painter | What Corbin found | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Orin Longacre** | A fraud. The masterworks were painted by a talented young man in his employ. | No item |
| **Gareth Rivers** | Genuinely that good, and always had been — a studio full of unreleased masterworks going back to his youth. | No item |
| **Fenna Pembrook** | Public work merely spectacular. **A hidden stash that was something else entirely.** | **The item.** |

> 🔑 **TELL ONE — THE CAREER. IT IS NOT "VERY GOOD." IT IS "BETTER THAN THIS PERSON'S OWN HISTORY CAN ACCOUNT FOR."**
>
> A prodigy has a childhood full of evidence. A fraud has someone else's hands. **An item-holder has a discontinuity** — a point where the work outruns the life. That is the only reliable test, it requires no magic, and it is done by asking about a career rather than casting a spell.

**And the holder usually hides it.** Fenna did — she sold the merely-spectacular and kept the real work private. Corbin learned that from her and does the same, destroying his best canvases so nobody traces the curve. ⚠️ **Which means the signature of an art-item in the world is not a famous artist. It is a good artist with a locked room.**

> 🦴 **TELL TWO — THE MATERIAL.** §1a: **there is bone in every vessel, it is hers, and it has not aged.** Five centuries of use with no yellowing and no wear is the physical giveaway, and it matches no animal alive. ⚠️ **Corbin does not have this test** — he found the brush by career alone and has never compared two vessels side by side, because he has only ever held one. **The party will be the first people in five hundred years to put two of them on the same table.**
>
> 🎸 **Run tell one against Luthien and the hair stands up.** He has been getting better since Dol — verses arriving whole, his hands finding shapes he never practised. **He is the discontinuity.** The moment the party understands the rule, they have the tools to diagnose their own bard, and one of them will.

## 4. 🎭 THE MASK — and the best scene it buys you

**Orla Miller does not have it yet, and that is the point.** She is written as an aspiring actress who takes unpaid roles with small troupes and earns her living serving tables. She is not good. An art-item amplifies the gifted, so she cannot simply have been holding one all along.

**So: Corbin gives it to her.**

His researchers turn up a mask — **one flat plate of bone, pale and nearly featureless** *(§1a)*. It is no use to a painter. He hands it to the woman who has stood by him through years of a story she has never once believed — and **overnight she is extraordinary.** Real roles. Paid roles. People weeping in a hall.

> 🔑 **This is how Corbin converts her, and he does it without an argument.** Orla's whole character is that she thinks the goddess is a fantasy and loves him anyway. **You cannot talk her out of that. You can hand her proof she feels in her own hands.** After the mask she is not humouring him. She is in it.
>
> And it retrofits the Arc Plan's note that *"she's the one who can be broken — and she still won't name him."* **Now there is a reason.** She does not protect Corbin out of romance. She protects him because he is the man who gave her the only thing she ever wanted, and she knows exactly what it cost him to give it away rather than sell it.

**Recovering it:** she is performing in Solteres in Ch8–10. The party can see her on a stage before they ever learn what she is to Corbin. ⚠️ **Let them admire the performance. That is worship, it feeds Saraswati, and it is the first time the party fuels the win condition on purpose by accident.**

## 5. ✂️ THE SHEARS — Maelis Vire, and the clock on her

**Settled Oct 2026: the dressmaking vessel is a pair of bone-handled shears, it is in Lady Ysabel Rochefort's dress house, and it is being used by one of her slaves.** *(Supersedes the earlier loom/shuttle proposal.)*

### Why the dress house and not the mill — your own timeline says so

| What she did | What happened | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Imported fine cloth, sold on to dressmakers | A good living | Ordinary trade |
| Built a **weaving mill**, Bramblefen slaves, her own cloth | **Margins doubled** | ✅ Exactly what vertical integration is supposed to do. **Nothing to explain.** |
| Opened a **dress house** | *"Better than anything else in the city"* — **every noblewoman and merchant's wife in Solteres within a season**, any price asked, **wealthy → one of the wealthiest women in the city** | ⚠️ **This is the discontinuity.** Nobody becomes the best in a capital city in one season. |

> 🔑 **The loom made her money. The dresses made her rich, and they did it overnight.** By the detection rule in §3, the vessel is in the second house. Leave the weaving mill entirely mundane — it is better that one of her two factories is just a factory.

### ⚠⚠ And she is already running the Tormentors' procedure

At Bramblefen she asked Vaskar for **seamstresses specifically**, and then: *"Some of the stock you do have may have worked as tailors. **I want to inspect them.**"*

**That is the same thing Ashgate's buyers did to Sera Oleander when they asked to see her hands** *(§6)*. Ysabel is selecting for artists on sight, for the same reason, with no idea — and **the party stood in the room and watched her do it.** Nothing needs to be said about this. Let a player remember it later.

### Maelis Vire

**Elf. Dressmaker. Slave.** Bought at Bramblefen for the dress house. **She has had the shears for about a hundred and fifty years**, which is longer than Ysabel's family has been in textiles.

- **She kept them through her own sale** for the same reason Sera Oleander kept her knife and her block of lime wood: **a seamstress needs shears, and nobody thought twice about it.** The most valuable object in Solteres is in a slave's workbasket because it looks like equipment.
- **She is in the ledger.** Sold out of Bramblefen like everyone else — but to a *named, respectable, tax-paying buyer*, so her line looks utterly ordinary. ⚠️ **The same book that records the cult's anonymous purchases records the legal ones**, and the legal one is the woman the party can walk up to and talk to.
- **One hundred and fifty years means provenance.** She knows who had them before her, and that person knew too. **This is how the party confirms what the shears are without a single spell** — they follow a chain of custody backward until it runs out of living memory, exactly the way Corbin found the brush by asking about careers.

### 🔑 She knows. She has never explained why.

**She knows she is better with those shears than without them, and she has worked out the economics of her own survival.**

> **The shears are the only reason she is valuable. Being valuable is the only reason she has not been sold.**

She does **not** know about Saraswati, the Prison Realm, mana, or any of it. She knows the tool is the thing keeping her alive, and she has never told a single owner, because the day Ysabel understands that the house's reputation belongs to a pair of scissors is the day Maelis becomes surplus and the scissors become the asset.

**So she has let Ysabel believe the house's genius is the house's.** Quiet sabotage by omission, maintained for years, by someone with nothing else to defend herself with.

> ⚠️ **This gives the party somebody to NEGOTIATE with instead of somebody to rescue** — and she will be sharper about her own situation than anyone they have met in a collar. **She will want to know what they are offering before she tells them anything**, and she is right to.

### ⏰ THE CLOCK — and nobody is driving it

> ✅ **SUPERSEDED (13 Oct 2026) — the clock is now the marriage.** The party's actions changed this: Ysabel's blackmail cancels the trial, so **she never needs to sell workers** and the liquidation clock below never starts. **Maelis's fate is tied to the forced marriage** *(`Chapter 8 DM Notes - Planning.md` §3)*: if Ysabel is married off, the dress house — and Maelis with it — passes to Ironwake, who runs the trade Ashgate buys from. Stop the wedding and the shears stay put. The text below is kept for the logic of the pipeline, which still holds.

`Solteres.md` already states her live wire: *"She is financially ruined and needs money for a defence. **Her workers are an asset.** If she has to liquidate, she will sell people — regretfully, with genuine reluctance, and she will do it."*

> 🔑 **Ysabel may sell the one person who is actually making her money, because she has no idea which one that is.**
>
> And a sale out of Solteres goes through **Ironwake's slave trade**, which is the pipeline **Ashgate** buys from — so Saraswati's shears would travel to the Herald's holding east of Underwood and join **the chisel and the potter's rib**. ⚠⚠ **Three of six vessels in one building, delivered by a frightened woman doing her accounts, and not one person in the chain would know what had happened.**

**That is a ticking clock with no villain pushing it**, which is the best kind. It also means the party's window is **Chapters 8–10, while Ysabel is still ruined** — if they resolve her money problem, the clock stops; if they ignore her, it runs.

### How this plays at the table

- **Taking the shears off Maelis repeats the harm.** She has been stripped of everything else; the party confiscating her last possession to save the world is a real and ugly option, and they should feel it. **Do not stop them.**
- **Freeing her is both the right answer and the optimal one.** Per §9, belief and work given freely outvalue an object taken. **A freed master dressmaker who keeps working for the rest of her life feeds Saraswati for two hundred years. A confiscated pair of shears in a sack feeds her nothing.** The party does not need to be told this — by Ch13 they will have the rule and can work it out.
- ⚠️ **And it is the hardest possible test of Ysabel.** She does not need to be argued down about slavery in the abstract any more. She needs to be told that the single best thing in her life was made by a person she owns, who she was about to sell, and that she never noticed. *(`Solteres.md`: she will not be argued out of it in one conversation. **This is not one conversation. This is evidence.**)*

## 6. 🪨 THE CHISEL — Sera Oleander, and why Ashgate bought her

**Settled: Sera Oleander has it, and the Tormentors took it off her without knowing what it was.** *(Supersedes the Arc Plan's earlier suggestion of Bosk Dunmar.)*

`The Ashgate Purchases.md` already says she was **the one the buyers came for** — the other ten women were the lot she arrived in — and that **they asked to see her hands.** She had a knife and a block of lime wood that Vaskar let her keep because it kept her quiet.

> ⚠️⚠️ **AND HERE IS THE THING THAT FALLS OUT OF THIS, WHICH IS BIGGER THAN ONE CHISEL.**
>
> The Tormentors have spent thirty-eight years buying artists off a slave block — sixty people, disproportionately artists, selected on sight. They buy them because an unwitting Saraswati-devout generates mana, and the drill needs a certain kind of material.
>
> **But an art-item is what makes an artist good enough to get noticed in the first place.** So the Ashgate purchasing programme has been accidentally harvesting art-item holders for four decades. **The Tormentors are the largest collector of Saraswati's vessels in the world and do not know it.** Nobody in that holding knows what is in the bags they took off sixty women.
>
> 🔑 **Which means the Herald's holding is not just where the chisel is. It is a reliquary.** How much is in there is yours to set — recommend **two recoverable** (the chisel and the rib, §7) **and strong evidence of more having passed through.**

🦴 **The chisel is hafted in bone, and that is wrong.** A mason's chisel is hafted in wood or horn — never bone, which splits. ⚠️ **Any stonemason who sees it will say so unprompted**, which makes this the one vessel whose material gives it away to a layman. *(§1a.)*

**Bosk Dunmar is not wasted.** The 90-year-old dwarf stonemason in Bramblefen's Pens who built two of the compound's walls as a free man is now the man who can **recognise** the chisel — and now he has two reasons to: he carved with one like it, **and he will take one look at a bone haft and tell them it should have split a century ago.** Optional and good: he carved with one like it decades ago, or knew the woman who did. ⚠️ **If the party freed him, they have a living appraiser** — which is a reward for Chapter 7 generosity that arrives six chapters later.

## 7. 🏺 THE POTTER'S RIB — Hanne Vetter, and the cruellest loop in the campaign

**The Patient is holding one of Saraswati's vessels and using it.** *(See `The Patient.md`.)*

Her file already says there is a wheel in her rooms and the work is extraordinary, and that **because making beauty is unwitting worship, she is still feeding the goddess her own order is burning as fuel.** The rib makes that literal instead of thematic.

**What it is:** a small curved rib of bone, the shaping tool a potter runs along a wall on the wheel. Unremarkable, the sort of thing nobody would steal. ✅ **All of it is bone, and per §1a that bone is Saraswati's own** — but ⚠️ **a potter's rib is traditionally bone anyway**, which is precisely why nobody in five hundred years has looked twice at this one. **It is the best-hidden of the six, and it is hidden by being exactly what it appears to be.**

**She had it before any of this.** It is how a provincial potter got good enough that Ashgate's buyers crossed a province for her — exactly as they crossed one for Sera Oleander. **The Tormentors bought her because the vessel worked.**

> 🔑 **THE LOOP, STATED PLAINLY — this is the best thing in the whole roster:**
>
> Saraswati's own tool made Hanne Vetter remarkable. Being remarkable got her bought. Being bought got her tortured. Being tortured got her Brudda Death's bargain. **And now, on her days off, the Herald of the Tormentors sits at a wheel and makes beautiful things, and every one of them feeds the goddess her order has chained open under Dol.**
>
> She is the single most productive worshipper Saraswati has, and she is working for the other side, and neither god has told her.

**At the table:** her file already notes that **if a player admires one of her pots, that is worship.** With the rib canonical, that line stops being a nice irony and becomes a mana transaction happening in the room during a conversation with a villain.

⚠️ **Open and genuinely interesting: what happens if they take it from her?** Recommend that the work simply stops being extraordinary, she notices within a week, and **she has no framework for grief about it** — she gave up the capacity to receive pain, not the capacity to lose something. *That is a second crack, and it is adjacent to the first one (§5 of her file: kindness).*

## 8. 📣 WHAT CORBIN KNOWS — fix this before Chapter 8

**Settled: he knows a set exists. He does not know what is in it or where any of it is.**

Sara's dream-flashes are described in `Saraswati and Adephagia.md` as weak, unclear, and mostly unnoticed. She told him *"of the items she created"* — a category, not a manifest. **He found the brush by deduction and murder, not revelation**, and he is spending his new wealth employing researchers precisely because he has no list.

> 🔑 **So the party and the villain are hunting the same thing with the same information, and the party is ahead and does not know it.** Corbin is paying men to search the whole country for an art-item. There is one in the room with him, in the hands of a bard he just bought dinner for.

**What he says in Ch8, and what he withholds:**

| He will say | He will not say |
|---|---|
| Mana is an economy, not a mystery — appreciation feeds a god | That he has one |
| **There is a set of objects that make artists great** | That he murdered a woman for it |
| They were made by a god who is imprisoned | How many there are — *he does not know* |
| He can date Vasant Panchami, which turns a footnote into a clock | That he has researchers hunting the rest |

⚠️ **And the thing he does at that first meeting: he notices the guitar.** He does not reach for it. He goes still, and asks pleasantly where it came from, and accepts whatever lie he is told while very clearly not believing it. **Per §3 he cannot be certain** — a bone guitar is not proof, and Luthien could just be good. **But Corbin invented the discontinuity test, and Luthien is a walking discontinuity.** He will be back.

## 9. The dial in the last room

How much of Saraswati stands up out of that pit is a function of **how many vessels the party gathered** and **how much good work got made and appreciated** along the way. Collect nothing and she comes out a ghost and the win is ugly. Collect most of six, with Luthien having played to real audiences for ten chapters, and a four-armed goddess gets up out of her own blood.

Which puts the brush in the finale as an object rather than a symbol:

- **Kill Corbin, take the brush.** She comes out stronger. Clean, and a little hollow.
- **Turn Corbin.** He hands it over. He is her most devout living worshipper — he has murdered for her — and **belief given freely is worth more than an object taken**, by your own mana rules. She comes out strongest.

**So the party can save the villain, and it is not sentiment. It is the optimal play.** Let them work that out for themselves with a god screaming in a pit.
