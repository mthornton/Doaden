# Solteres

Solteres is a the capitol of the Southern Diocese.  A large bustling city fueled by trade.  People come from all over Doaden and lands further off for the trade.  This city became important for trade because of the rich farming lands in the south. 
 
The Merchant class runs the city and at the upper echelons are obnoxiously wealthy.  The wealth does not trickle down and most residents here are as poor as any other city. 
 
The trade and money attracted criminal syndicates.  The crime lords are as powerful as the top merchants.  In many cases, it is hard to tell one from the other. 
 
Slave trade is common.  Slavery is illegae in Doaden, but you would never know that in Solares.  The buying and selling of people is out in the open with regular live auctions.  Most of the slaves are non-humans.  Humans are kept as slaves, but that is frowned upon.  However, there is a work around.  Humans convected of a crime with a sentence of 50 years or greater can be purchased.  This law is frequently abused by corupt officials.

**There is a second, lesser instrument, and it is the common one: Terms.** A human convicted of a crime carrying a shorter sentence may discharge it by offering himself for sale. The purchase price satisfies his restitution and his debts; he serves the term to whoever bought him instead of serving it in a cell, and at the end of it he walks away clear. It is voluntary. It is entirely lawful. Nobody involved calls it slavery, and the distinction is defended fiercely by people who have never been on either end of it.

**This is the whole architecture of bondage in Doaden, and it has two doors:**

- **For non-humans and half-humans — "Stock."** Ownership, outright and permanent. Bought and sold with no paperwork beyond a line in a ledger. The Church's ruling against slavery is read as applying to *people*, and these are held not to be people.
- **For humans — "Terms."** A sentence somebody purchased. Finite, on paper. Everyone insists this is a different thing.

The 50-year rule is how the first door gets opened for a human: a corrupt magistrate hands down a sentence long enough to be permanent, and the man is Stock for life. **Terms is the honest version, and it is how most humans in collars got there** — a debt, a fine, a bad year, a worse decision.

**Living example:** *Dov Brandt*, one of Lady Ysabel's guards, is a Terms man with three years left, sold at Bramblefen — see `sessions/Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`.

# Improtant People
 
## Lady Ysabel Rochefort 
Merchant specializing in textiles.  Mid-level merchant, but on the rise.  She started bringing in shipments of silks from across the sea.  To increase profits, she smuggled a large shipment into Soleres bypassing the required tax to Lord Ironwake.  Lord Ironwake learned of this before the shipment reached her warehouse.  The silks were intercepted and stolen.  Not satisified with just this, Ironwake arranged for the murder of Lady Ysabel's niece.  Evidence was planted to make it appear the murder was committed by Ysabel.  Ysabel is all but financially wiped out from the loss of the silks and must defend herself against the murder charges via trial by combat.  Her champion, Ulfic, was murdered at the Calf & Ass.  She does not know what to do now.  She considered fleeing, but has no where to go.  With no money or power, her friends have largely abandoned her. 

### She is a slaveholder, and she does not think of herself as one

Ysabel's mills and warehouses are worked by **non-human slaves**, bought outright, and she owns them the way she owns the looms. She also holds at least one human on Terms — **Dov Brandt**, one of the guards travelling with the party (see `sessions/Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`).

**Her position, stated plainly and without embarrassment when the party presses her:** non-humans are beneath humans. Keeping them is not a cruelty but a kindness, and a civic good. They are fed. They are housed. They are safe, which they would not be otherwise. And they are given something to do with their lives, which — she will say this in exactly these words, and mean it — *they would never have arranged for themselves.*

**She is not a villain about it. That is the whole point.** She does not raise her voice, does not enjoy anyone's suffering, has never had a worker beaten, and would be honestly distressed to learn one had been. She is a decent, frightened, courteous woman with an ugly belief she has never once had to defend, because nobody in her life has ever asked her to.

### Running her when the party pushes

- **Start with bewilderment, not defensiveness.** The first time a PC objects, she does not understand the objection. She thinks they have misunderstood the arrangement, and explains it again more clearly, as a kindness.
- **She reaches for the Church when cornered** — the ruling against slavery concerns *people*, and this is a category question, not a moral one. Every institution she has ever dealt with agrees with her. **She is not lying and she is not alone.**
- **The exception she makes for the party is the most damning thing about her.** She has travelled with an elf, a half-elf, a gnome, and whatever Virgil is, for weeks, with perfect courtesy — and she squares it instantly and without strain: *they are different.* They are named. They are servants of the Church. She will say this out loud, to their faces, as a compliment, and hear nothing at all wrong with it.
- **Never let her escalate to cruelty.** The moment she becomes a monster the scene stops working. The horror is that she is reasonable, generous with her wine, grateful for the escort, and would introduce any of them to her friends.
- **She will not be argued out of it in one conversation.** Do not let a good Persuasion roll convert her. Let it land, let her go quiet, and let her be a little colder afterwards — she now knows these people judge her, and she still needs them.

### What this costs the party

- **Three or four of them are exactly the kind of person she buys.** JonLief, Luthien, Gimble, arguably Virgil. They have been eating her food for two weeks.
- **Erwana's flinch was never trauma. It was accurate.** A half-Tabaxi escaped slave took one look at a noblewoman reaching for her book and read her correctly in a second. **If the party works that out, Erwana's judgement becomes worth something to them** — see `Erwana.md`.
- **They are Penitents of a Church that has ruled slavery illegal, escorting a slaveholder to safety.** Nobody has made them think about that yet. Bramblefen will.
- **She asked all four of them to fight for her.** Ask again after this conversation and see what the table says.

### Live wire

She is financially ruined and needs money for a defence. **Her workers are an asset.** If she has to liquidate, she will sell people — regretfully, with genuine reluctance, and she will do it.

 
## Lord Ironwake 
Powerful merchant up to his knees with the criminal syndicate.  He controls much of the transportation in and out of Soleres collecting a tax on each shipment in and out.  If an upstart tries to bypass his service, he quickly and harshly punished the offender.  He frequently works through cat's paws - sometimes using a criminal gang, sometimes through blackmail or manipulation. Ironwake also controls the slave trade.
