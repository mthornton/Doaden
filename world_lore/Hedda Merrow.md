# Hedda Merrow
### Shepherd of the Office of the Lost Flock

> **The Office's killer. Everyone says so. She has spent sixteen years making sure they keep saying it, because a reputation for bringing back corpses is the only thing that lets her quietly let people live.**
>
> See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md` for the brand, the pull, and the cloaks. See `Baltus Thole.md` for the man who trained her.

---

## First sight

Forty-one. Not tall. Black hair worn long and loose, which is a strange choice for someone who works in the dark and is not a choice at all — it is a decision she made about how she wants to be looked at, and she reviews it every morning.

The violet cloak, like all of them, and — like almost all of them — **nothing on it.** The Office issues a lamb for every Penitent a Shepherd loses; nearly nobody wears them. *(Baltus does. It is one of the things he is known for and one of the things she has never argued with him about.)*

Hedda keeps hers. **Forty-seven small silver lambs in a soft leather pouch**, and she has carried every one of them since the day it was struck, and she can find any of them by feel.

**Eleven of them are in a separate pocket**, and if the party ever sees her open both, that is the whole character, given away for nothing, by a woman who has been careful for sixteen years.

Two long daggers, worn where a working person wears tools rather than where a duellist wears weapons. Plain, well-kept, and the edges have been taken down past sharp into something closer to surgical.

She has usually been near the party for two or three days before any of them learns her name.

### ⚠️ Her tell — use this

**She wears her hair down while she is still deciding.**

When Hedda ties her hair back, she has made up her mind, and the talking part of the evening is finished. She does it without ceremony, with a cord off her wrist, mid-sentence.

**Never do it as a threat. Do it as an answer.** A table that sees it once and survives will watch for it for the rest of the campaign.

## What everyone believes about her

That Baltus Thole brings them home and Hedda Merrow brings back the mark.

That is true. It is in the record. **Forty-seven lambs struck in sixteen years** — against Baltus's thirty-one in twenty-two — and every one of them a Penitent she was assigned and did not deliver alive.

Shepherds who work near her are polite to her. Clerks in the Office schedule her paperwork for days she is not in the building. Baltus, who taught her the trade, has stopped writing.

**She has never once corrected any of it.**

---

## ⚔️ What is actually true

**Eleven of the forty-seven are alive.**

She knows which eleven. She knows their names, and where she left them, and what they said. She has never written any of it down.

### How it works

The Office accepts one proof of death: **the mark itself.** A hand's width of shoulder skin with the Mark of Atonement on it, brought back and matched to the file. Not a body — bodies are heavy and the diocese is wide. Just the brand.

**And it only counts if it was taken under the Shepherd's ceremony** — the rite the Office teaches to no one else, the one thing in the world that actually severs the bond. *(See the Office file. Cutting a brand out without the rite does nothing at all; the pull comes off the person, not the skin. This is precisely why no one but a Shepherd could do what Hedda does.)*

So the proof and the severance are **the same act**, which is the whole reason the Office designed it that way, and the whole reason it can be turned.

Which means the only difference between a Penitent Hedda killed and a Penitent Hedda freed is **whether she took the mark off a corpse or off someone breathing.**

**The proof she hands in is always genuine.** Real skin, real brand, real person, correctly matched. There is nothing to detect and nothing to catch. **The lie is entirely in the verb.**

### And it is not a kindness

**The rite severs the leash for good.** So the person she frees is genuinely free: no Shepherd will ever feel them again, no file will ever reopen, and the Church will not come. That is not a loophole. **It is the Office's own instrument, used correctly, on a living person.**

They are also **dead on paper, permanently, with no way back.** The debt cannot be discharged by someone who does not exist. There is no door at the end of it any more — and the door is the entire thing Baltus has organised his life around.

So what she offers is not mercy in any shape Baltus would recognise. It is **an exchange**: your name, your record, and any chance of ever being lawfully free — for your life, tonight, and a scar you will explain badly for the rest of it.

She does it on a table, or a stump, or the floor of a barn. It takes about four minutes. She is very good at it and it still goes badly about one time in five.

## Who gets the second option

**She is not consistent and she knows it, and it is the thing that keeps her awake.**

She does not free runners. Someone who left because service was hard gets walked back, and she has no patience for them at all.

She frees people **the system broke rather than punished.** A sentence bought by an enemy. A Redeemer selling his own unit's assignments. A woman who would be killed inside a month of being returned, by someone in the Church, and everybody knowing it. **The cases where going home is the punishment.**

That is why she gets close first, and it is the most important thing to understand about her: **the sneaking is not tactics. It is due diligence.** She has to know who you are before she can decide, and she does not trust a file to tell her. So she takes work in your village, drinks in your inn, walks a day behind you, and asks people about you.

By the time she introduces herself she has usually already decided. The hair says which way.

## Baltus

He trained her. Sixteen years ago she was a thief with a suspended sentence and he was the man who argued she should be given a coat instead of a cell, and for six years they worked the Southern Diocese together and she has never met anyone she respected more.

**He believes she stopped counting.** That she got tired, and got efficient, and let the work become a job — and that his tally is the difference between them.

She counts more carefully than he ever has. Forty-seven lambs, and eleven of them she can put a finger on and tell you what colour their kitchen door was.

**She has never told him, and she never will**, for one reason: he would have to report it. He is exactly that kind of man. And she has watched him carry thirty-one dead people on his shoulders for two decades and has decided he does not get to carry her as well.

He has come close to asking. Once, four years ago, in an inn at Hallowmere, he looked at her coat for a long moment and started a sentence and did not finish it.

> **If they are ever in a room together with the party, that is the pressure in the scene** — and neither of them will say a word about it.

## This party

**Her verdict is not one verdict. She judges them one at a time, and she will say so.**

- **They did not run.** They were shipwrecked, stranded, and have been walking toward their orders in the only direction available. That is not her category. Her instinct is *go in, you'll be fine.*
- **Virgil** is her category exactly. Rivals put him in that camp, not the crime. She will find that out early, because she asks, and it will change how she looks at him.
- **Gimble** is her category and then some. A branded Penitent bought and collared by a lord's chainmaster, walking back into the Church that let it happen. **She may not even offer him the choice — she may simply tell him what she is going to do.**
- **Wolfram** is a problem she has not met before. A man who wants to go in, who thinks the Church is worth serving, who would refuse her flat and might report her for offering.

**So the most likely shape of a first meeting: she offers the knife to one or two of them, in private, and not to the others.** That is a far better scene than a fight, and it splits the party along a line they have never had to look at.

## ⚔️ Stat block — Hedda Merrow

*Medium humanoid (human), neutral — CR 5 (1,800 XP)*

- **Armor Class** 16 (studded leather)
- **Hit Points** 78 (12d8 + 24)
- **Speed** 35 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 (+0) | 20 (+5) | 14 (+2) | 14 (+2) | 16 (+3) | 13 (+1) |

- **Saving Throws** DEX +8, INT +5, WIS +6
- **Skills** Stealth +11, Perception +6, Insight +9, Deception +7, Investigation +5
- **Senses** passive Perception 16
- **Proficiency Bonus** +3

### Traits

- **Assassinate.** Advantage on attacks against any creature that has not yet taken a turn. Any hit against a **surprised** creature is a **critical hit.**
- **Sneak Attack (1/turn).** +4d6 when she has advantage, or when an ally of hers is within 5 ft. of the target.
- **Evasion.** No damage on a successful Dex save; half on a failure.
- **Three Days Ahead.** She has been observing before the scene begins. Unless the party has taken specific, stated precautions, **she chooses whether the first round is a surprise round** — and she usually chooses no.
- **Reads the File and the Face.** Advantage on Insight. She knows things about the party she has no business knowing, because she asked their innkeeper.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** Two dagger attacks.
- **Long Dagger.** *Melee or Ranged (20/60):* **+8** to hit. *Hit:* **1d4 + 5 piercing.**
- **★ Take the Mark.** Against a creature that is **restrained, incapacitated, unconscious, or willing** and bears the Mark of Atonement, she performs the Shepherd's ceremony and cuts the brand away. **4d6 slashing** and the target gains **one level of exhaustion.** **The bond is severed permanently and the Office's file closes as a death.** *Not a combat action — it takes about four minutes and she says the rite aloud the whole time. This is the entire character in one move.*

### Reactions

- **Uncanny Dodge.** Halve the damage from one attack she can see.

### If it comes to a fight

**It almost never should.** She fights to leave, not to win. She opens with surprise if she has it, takes one target down hard, and then goes — over a wall, into a crowd, out a window. **She does not fight a party of four in the open and she is not stupid enough to try.**

If the party corners her and she cannot leave, she talks. She is extremely good at it and everything she says is true.

## Voice

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | Quiet, level, unhurried. Fewer words than the situation calls for. |
| **Habit** | Asks questions, and almost none of them are about the case — *what were you before this? who put you in? does anybody know you're alive?* |
| **Never says** | *Come home.* She has never once used the phrase. She thinks it is a lie Baltus tells himself. |
| **Tell** | **Hair down = still deciding. Hair tied back = decided.** |

**Three lines to have ready:**

> *"I've been three days behind you. I bought your innkeeper's wife a drink and she told me you paid in silver and left early. I want you to understand that I could have done this in your sleep, and that I did not."*

> *"There are two ways this ends and I'm going to describe both of them, and then you're going to choose, and I am not going to argue with you about it afterward."*

> *"He'll tell you there's a door at the end of it. There is. I've seen it open once in sixteen years and so has he, and he's built his whole life on that afternoon, and I love him for it, and it is the stupidest thing I have ever watched a man do."*

## Hooks

- **She is the counter-argument to Baltus in the same coat.** Two Shepherds, same charter, same violet, opposite conclusions — and the party can meet either first. Meeting her first makes Baltus's arrival almost a relief. Meeting him first makes hers a horror. **Both are good; pick deliberately.**
- **She is a way out of the campaign's central trap.** Since Chapter 1 the party has had no answer to the brand. She is one. It costs everything they'd get by going in clean, and it is real.
- **If she frees Gimble, Dave's character loses the leash and gains a scar** — and stops being the party's proof of anything to the Church.
- **Exposure.** Eleven living people are the evidence against her. One of them turning up somewhere the Office can see them ends her. **The party could become that evidence, on purpose or by accident.**
- **She could be pointed at Ironwake** — but unlike Baltus she would not go loudly. She'd go in the dark, alone, and Ironwake would simply be found.

## Open decisions

0. **Does she know Sault has done the arithmetic?** *(See `Emeric Sault.md`.)* **Recommendation: no.** She thinks her secret's only real risk is one of her eleven surfacing. It is not. The Provost is holding it, filed and unassigned, waiting for a day he needs her to do something she would refuse.
1. **Does Baltus ever find out?** *Recommendation: not before the party has met them both and formed an opinion.* His discovering it should be a consequence of something the players did.
2. **Is one of her eleven someone the party can meet?** A living, scarred, nameless person who was declared dead years ago is the cleanest possible proof to the party that her offer is real — **and the easiest way to accidentally destroy her.**
3. **Does she offer the knife to the whole party or to individuals?** *Recommendation: individuals, privately, and not to all of them.* The split is the scene.
4. **What was she before?** A thief with a suspended sentence, per the Baltus paragraph. Worth a sentence more if a player asks — including whether she was ever branded herself. *(If she was, and she took her own mark off, this character goes somewhere else entirely. Flagging it, not recommending it.)*
