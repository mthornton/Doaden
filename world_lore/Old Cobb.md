# Old Cobb

**Type:** NPC — enslaved former slaver's clerk at **Bramblefen**; rescue objective and the emotional payload of the slaver arc.
**Function:** the man who wrote down every sale — including [[JonLief]]'s wife and son — and the walking evidence against **Lord Leofard Ironwake**. Vaskar (see [[Vaskar the Brand]]) is torturing him when the party arrives.

> Reskin of "Warden Ingfar" from *Chapter 7: The Chained Coast*. Rename freely.

## The Man Who Kept the Books

Cobb was never a whip-hand. He'll tell you that, quickly, if you let him — *he never once laid a chain on anyone.* He was a clerk. A factor. A tidy man with a tidy hand who, for the better part of forty years, sat at a high desk in the corner of a slaver's yard and **wrote the names down.** Who was bought, who was sold, for how much, to whom. He told himself the ledger was a neutral thing — that a man who merely records a sin does not commit it. It is a lie he believed for a very long time, because believing it let him sleep.

He was good at it. Too good. A clerk who has recorded every buyer, every price, every quiet "household purchase" a great lord would rather no one remember, is a clerk who knows far too much. When Cobb grew old and slow and his eyes began to fail, the masters did the arithmetic he'd taught them: a man who knows your secrets is cheaper to collar than to pension. So the bookkeeper of the trade was written, at last, into his own ledger — a line item, sold to Bramblefen, where Vaskar keeps him because Vaskar keeps everything that might one day be useful.

## Why Vaskar Is Hurting Him

Someone has been using Cobb's old records against Ironwake — a name here, a date there, surfacing where it shouldn't. Vaskar suspects a leak, and Cobb is the likeliest source. He is not wrong. In his last years of freedom, sick with a conscience that arrived decades too late, **Cobb quietly kept a second ledger** — a true copy of the sales the masters most wanted forgotten — and hid it, meaning to do *one* right thing before he died. Vaskar wants to know where it is and who's seen it. Cobb has given him nothing. He has endured a great deal to give him nothing. It is the bravest thing he has ever done, and he clings to it like the only warm thing left in him.

## What He Knows

Everything. Cobb remembers every transaction he ever recorded, the way some old men remember every hymn from childhood. Names, faces, buyers, prices, going back decades. **He remembers a half-elf woman and a small boy, brought in years apart by men who called it "clan business," and he remembers exactly who bought them.** He can hand JonLief the truth no one else in the world can give him. He can also name, buyer by buyer, the full shape of Ironwake's slaving — the single most dangerous document in the fens, carried in a dying clerk's skull.

## Personality & how to run him

- **Precise, hedging, clerkish.** He speaks in careful qualifications even now — "the record would indicate," "if memory serves, and it does." Grief has not made him dramatic; it has made him exact.
- **Guilt-soaked and desperate to confess.** He will not give a torturer a syllable, but the moment someone shows him mercy — cuts him loose, offers water — the words pour out of him. He *needs* to be the man who finally told the truth.
- **A moral mirror, not a monster.** Play him as what he is: the small, tidy face of atrocity — the person who "just kept the records." Let JonLief (or the party) decide what he's owed.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 (−1) | 9 (−1) | 9 (−1) | 15 (+2) | 13 (+1) | 11 (+0) |

- **Skills** History +4, Investigation +4, Insight +3
- **Senses** passive Perception 11 (poor eyes)
- **Languages** Common, Dwarvish (trade tongue); literate in three scripts
- **Condition** begins with **2 levels of exhaustion** from torture (speed already halved above; disadvantage on ability checks and attacks) — recovers one level per long rest with care

**Traits**

- **The Ledger in His Head.** Cobb can recall any slave transaction he ever recorded — buyer, price, date. Freed and questioned, he can identify who purchased [[JonLief]]'s wife and son, and enumerate Lord Ironwake's "household" purchases. Treat this as the adventure's key intel, not a die roll.
- **Endured Worse.** Cobb automatically refuses to give information to a captor, slaver, or torturer, no matter the pressure. Kindness, not coercion, unlocks him.

**Actions**

- **Feeble Strike.** *Melee:* +1 to hit, 1 bludgeoning. He is not a fighter; he is a witness.

## Hooks & rewards

- **The rescue.** Cobb is who Vaskar is torturing in the Chainmaster's Hall — reaching him *is* the climax's objective, and freeing him mid-fight is a moral gut-punch (the tortured old man vs. the master who broke him).
- **The second ledger.** Freed and trusted, Cobb reveals where he hid his true copy of the sales — hard evidence against Ironwake, and the cleanest legal weapon the party could bring to bear (dovetails with Vaskar's own hidden insurance). He may ask, in return, only that they *use* it.
- **JonLief's truth.** Cobb is the one who can finally tell JonLief what became of Maren and Rowan — where they went, and to whom. Keeping this old man alive suddenly matters more than any coin.
- **Redemption or reckoning.** Whether Cobb earns mercy or judgment is a table decision. Either way he wants, at the end, to have told the truth once.
