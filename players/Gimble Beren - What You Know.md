# Gimble Beren — What You Know
### For Dave. Everything your character has lived through, in his own order.

> **Read this one first.** It is written in second person because it is *yours* — every word of it is something Gimble Beren personally saw, heard, or did.
>
> Two companion documents fill in detail if you want it: **`The Unit Before Us.md`** *(the story of your unit, written as prose)* and **`The First Penitents.md`** *(the DM's version, with names and dates)*. Neither is required. This page is enough to sit down and play.
>
> **What is NOT in here is anything Gimble could not know**, and that is deliberate. See §7.

## 1. Before all this

**That part is yours to write.** Whatever Gimble was — tinker, tracker, poacher, guide, someone's uncle — Michael has left it entirely open. Whatever you decide, one thing is fixed: **you ended up in front of the Church of Azerai, and they found you guilty of something.**

You do not have to have been innocent. Most Penitents were not.

---

## 2. The forge

You remember the room. Everyone who has been through it does.

The Church of Azerai does not execute and does not imprison. It does something it considers far more generous: **it lets you pay.** They call it penitence. The debt is measured in years, and the number they read out over you was **five hundred of them.**

Then they put you face down and pressed a consecrated iron into your shoulder.

**The Mark of Atonement.** A sun with a tear falling from it. It is the Church's property mark and its promise at the same time, and every priest who has ever explained it to you has explained it kindly: *you are not a prisoner, you are being saved.* There is supposedly a door at the far end of the five hundred years. Nobody you have ever met has walked through it.

You were one of **five** branded that season and put into a unit together:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ottilie Marsk** | Hard, capable, the one who took charge because somebody had to. |
| **Brother Aldous Penn** | A priest, once, before whatever it was. He had been allowed a pulpit and then not allowed one. |
| **Sabin Ferrow** | Half elf thief.  Loved to talk and give his opinion on anything and everything. |
| **Grigor Tell** | Half orc.  Big and spoke little. |
| **You** | The small one. It came up constantly. |

**Your orders came from a Redeemer named Vozruk.** Go to **Dol Island.** Find out what is on it. Report back.

## 3. Dol Island — late June, 1126

You landed in the last week of June.

**What you found:** an island with a hole in it, and under the hole, catacombs — old, wrong, and not empty. You fought your way down. You killed the things keeping the place.

**And then you found her.**

A woman, chained. Not a statue and not a corpse. **Four arms.** A gown that had been white a very long time ago. Alive, in whatever way something like that is alive — and someone had cut a hole in her side and was **bleeding her into barrels.**

Aldous had preached from a pulpit that the old gods never existed. He stood in that room and looked at her and did not say anything for a long while.

**You thought you had won.** The caretakers were dead, the mission was done, and there was no boat and no way to send word — so you stayed on the island and waited for someone to come and collect you.

### Then the harvest crew came

Three ships. Twenty-odd men. Black robes in the summer heat. And a tall, pale man in an ornate headdress who did not touch anything himself.

They were not there to fight you. **They were there to fill barrels**, and five healthy adults had just walked into their hands — people with strong hearts, and better than that, **people nobody would come looking for.**

Because who reports a missing Penitent? A Penitent is a debt with legs. If a unit stops writing, the Church files it and moves on.

**They took you alive on purpose.**

### The four days

You were chained to stakes driven into the sand above the tide line on the far beach, in the sun, in sight of the barrels and in sight of each other.

They bled you into the same casks as the goddess. **A little at a time**, because they wanted it fresh and they wanted it slow. The men doing it were not angry and did not enjoy it. It was work. They talked about other things while they did it.

**They left you for last because you were small.** Barely worth the walk to the barrel, one of them said, in front of you, the way you would say it about a chicken.

They also could not read you. You would not scream, you would not beg, and you would not stop watching them — and men doing that kind of work do not like being looked at steadily by something they cannot get the measure of. So they moved your stake further down the sand, away from the others, and got on with the ones that mattered.

**That is the only reason you are alive.**

### Getting out

Four days was three too many. You got out of the cuff.

**And then you did not run.** You went into the treeline and you stayed there **two more days**, watching, because you could not make yourself accept that there was nothing left to do.

There was nothing left to do.

When the ships went, you walked the shore to where a thousand years of reef has been stacking up broken ships, and you lashed together enough timber to be called a raft, and you put out into **three miles of open water** on it, alone.

You made the mainland. You do not entirely know how.

## 4. From free to owned

You wandered for days with no map, no supplies, and no idea which direction a Church camp lay in.

Then you found people who offered to help you. **They were slavers.**

You fought them. You fought them well and for a long time and you lost. You fought them again during transport. You fought them a third time when you arrived at the place they were taking you.

That place is called **Bramblefen** — a walled slave-holding compound out in the marsh, in the duchy of **Solteres**, run by a man called **Vaskar the Brand** on behalf of **Lord Leofard Ironwake.**

### What Solteres is

You have had two months to learn the rules of this country and they are simple and they are obscene:

- **Non-humans are property.** Full stop. Not criminals, not debtors — stock. Bought, sold, worked, bred, inherited.
- **Humans can only be bound by contract** — a term of years, for a crime or a debt they signed away.
- Everyone here explains this to you patiently, as though it is a kindness, and **the people who own slaves genuinely believe they are doing them a favour.** Food, a roof, a purpose. They will say it to your face and be hurt if you argue.

You have heard that register before. **It is exactly how the Church talks about Penitents.** You have thought about that a lot in the cage and you have not enjoyed where it goes.

## 5. Two months in a cage

### Why they have not sold you, and why they have not killed you

You worked this out early, and you were right.

Vaskar cannot sell you: you are too willful to break and too skilled to put in anyone's household. He cannot free you. And he cannot simply kill you, because **you are branded**, and a Penitent is the Church's property.

If it ever came out that Ironwake's chainmaster had collared a servant of the Church of Azerai, **not even a lord that powerful could stand in front of what the Church would do about it.**

**So you are a problem in a box**, and Vaskar has been keeping you in it for two months while he decides.

### Then the wagon arrived

A few weeks ago, one of Ironwake's wagons rolled in with a giant in it.

They call him **the Juggernaut**. His name is **Mott**. He is Ironwake's champion, he is enormous, and — this is the part everyone else at Bramblefen seems able to ignore — **he is owned, exactly like you are.** He is kept in the Deep Cages under guard, and his handler is a dwarf named **Durgan Half-Ear**, who is also a slave, and who is the closest thing Mott has to a person.

Since the wagon came, the place has changed. Twice the guards. Six of Ironwake's own men, who are a different quality from Vaskar's fen guards and know it.

**And Vaskar found a use for you.**

### The mask

You are Mott's sparring partner. Good enough to sharpen him, not good enough to hurt him.

They put an **iron mask** on you for it — so no visitor sees a gnome's face, and, more importantly, **so nobody sees your shoulder.**

**You cannot speak through it.** Bear that in mind; it matters for your first scene.

### Tonight

There is a **sparring match at sundown — eight o'clock.** In front of guests. You are the opponent.

**Nobody at Bramblefen expects you to be alive at dawn.** They have not troubled to hide this from you. There is talk of a hole outside the wall.

**That is where your character is when the session opens.** In the Deep Cages, masked, waiting for eight o'clock.

## 6. The other people in that compound

Everything here is what **you** have seen from a cage, over two months. It is not the whole truth about any of them.

| Who | What you know |
|---|---|
| **Vaskar the Brand** | Runs Bramblefen. Not a thug — a manager, and a genuinely dangerous man. He has never once been cruel to you for fun, which is somehow worse. |
| **Mott, "the Juggernaut"** | Enormous. Owned. You have fought him a few times. **You may know things about him nobody else in the party can know**, including whether there is a person in there. That is yours to decide. |
| **Durgan Half-Ear** | Dwarf. Mott's handler and keeper. A slave with a slave to look after. |
| **Old Cobb** | Ancient, permanent, the bookkeeper — he has recorded every purchase and sale here for longer than anyone remembers. **He wrote you into a book when you arrived.** He is the only person at Bramblefen who has ever put your name on paper, and he has been quietly decent to you in small ways that cost him nothing and could still get him killed. |
| **Ironwake's six** | Not fen guards. Professionals. Two of them stand on Mott's cell day and night. |

## 7. ⚠️ What you do NOT know

**This is the most useful part of this document. Guard it.**

Gimble has been in a hole in a marsh for two months. He does not know:

- **That anyone was ever sent to look for his unit.** As far as Gimble is concerned, the Church filed five people as lost and moved on, which is exactly what the Church does.
- **That there is a second unit of Penitents**, that they went to Dol, that they stood in the same crypt, or that they walked past the stains on that beach without knowing whose they were.
- **What the blood was for.** You saw a goddess drained into casks and you have never learned why, and you have had two months of nothing to do but wonder.
- **Anything at all about the people who are about to walk into Bramblefen** — who they are, why they are there, what they want, or who they came with.

> **Do not read the party's documents.** *(`Party History.md`, `The Penitents Chronicle.md`, `Chapter 7 — Session 1`.)* They are excellent and you will enjoy them enormously **after** your first session. Reading them first will quietly rob you of the best scene you are going to get.

## 8. How you join

You do not walk into the tavern. **You are found.**

Sometime tonight, someone is going to get close enough to a masked gnome to see one bare shoulder — **and the mark on it is the same sun and tear they are all carrying under their own shirts.**

They will learn **what** you are before they learn **who** you are. And the mask does not come off by itself, so your first scene is likely to be one you play **without a single line of dialogue.**

Play that. It is a gift. A character who cannot speak in his introduction gets to be enormously present anyway, and everything Gimble does with his hands and his eyes in that scene is the first thing four strangers will ever know about him.

**After the mask comes off, he is entirely yours.** Michael stops narrating him the moment it does.

## 9. What you bring, once you are out

You should know how much weight this character carries, because it is a lot for a first session:

1. **You are the answer to their orders.** They were sent to find out what happened to the first team. **You are what happened to the first team**, and producing you alive is the only part of that mission anyone has ever completed.
2. **You are proof that Ironwake's people collared a Penitent** — which is leverage on a lord, and it has nothing to do with anything else they are carrying.
3. **You saw the four-armed woman.** One of them — **Wolfram** — has been carrying that memory alone since the crypt, with nobody to confirm it. **You can.** That is a very good early scene and it costs you nothing.
4. **You have been inside that crypt**, including rooms the second unit never opened.
5. **You know Bramblefen from the inside** — the cages, the yard, the routine, and the people. Nobody else in that party has that.

## 10. Three things to decide before the session

1. **What was Gimble before the Church got hold of him?** Entirely open. Give Michael a paragraph.
2. **What does he want now?** Pick one and let it drive him — *(a)* to get word to the Church about what is on Dol, *(b)* to find the tall pale man in the headdress, or *(c)* to get four names remembered by somebody, anybody, before he dies. **They lead to very different characters.**
3. **What is he like when he is not in a cage?** You have only ever seen him at his worst. Two months in a hole is not a personality. **Decide who he was on a good day**, because that man is the one the party is going to meet, eventually.
