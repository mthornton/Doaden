# The First Penitents — the team lost on Dol Island

> **STATUS: DRAFT for Michael's approval.** Everything here is invention built to fit existing canon (`Chapter 2 Dol Island.md`, `Chapter 1`, `Saraswati and Adephagia.md`, `players/Gimble Beren.md`). Nothing in it has been said at the table. **Open decisions are listed at the bottom — settle those first, then this becomes canon.** Names are placeholders; change any of them freely.

**The one-line version:** Vozruk's first team landed on Dol in the same week the cult's 666-day wait ran out, killed the caretakers, thought they had won, and were still on the island when the harvest crew arrived. They were taken alive, because the ritual needed volume and nobody comes looking for a Penitent. They were bled into barrels on the beach over four days. **Gimble Beren got out. He is the only one who did, and he watched the last of it from the treeline.**

---

## Who they were

Five Penitents, assembled by Vozruk the Redeemer the same way this party was, and sent for the same reason.

| | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ottilie Marsk** | Human, late 40s. Team lead. | A real sergeant once, in a real army, before whatever put her in the camp. Mission-first, unsentimental, kept four difficult people alive for three weeks on an island that was trying to kill them. **Died last, and knew she was going to be last.** |
| **Brother Aldous Penn** | Human, 60s. Defrocked priest. | The team's healer and its conscience. **He is the one who looked into the tomb** — and it broke something loose in him that the rest of the team could not talk him back from. |
| **Sabin Ferrow** | Half-elf, 30s. Scout and thief. | Found the stair, read the mosaics, picked the locks. The one who kept saying they should leave. |
| **Grigor Tell** | Half-orc, 40s. The wall. | Barely spoke. Carried Aldous the last mile more than once. |
| **Gimble Beren** | Gnome ranger. | The newest, the smallest, and the one nobody on that team — or in that cult — took seriously. |

They were a unit with a number rather than a name. Gimble still uses the number sometimes, without noticing he's doing it.

## What happened

### Early June 1126 — the sailing

They went out of the Castur on a chartered boat with a hired crew, and the crew did not come back for them.

Whether that was arranged or simply cowardice, nobody has established. What is certain is that the governor's office later reported the team "last seen setting sail" and assumed a shipwreck, and that around the same time the same governor arrested several known heretics who all happened to be experienced sailors. **The cult moves people and cargo by boat, and it has crews.** Vozruk read those two facts side by side in Chapter 1 and did not join them up.

### Late June — three weeks too early and one week too late

The team landed clean, in daylight, on a beach that was already being used for something.

They found the same things this party found six weeks later: the stone path under the sand, the carved cliff face, the mosaic stair, and the sunlit cave with two dozen unarmed dead in it — men and women, no weapons, homemade jewellery, about two years gone.

Sabin worked out the door. Aldous prayed over the bones. Ottilie wrote it all down, because that is what a sergeant does, and her report is at the bottom of the sea or in a cult's fire.

**What none of them could have known:** Saraswati's 666 days ran out that same week. They had walked into the crypt in the one narrow window between the caretakers going about their business and the harvest crew arriving to open a goddess's side.

### Late June to early July — the mistake

They went down, and they won.

They fought the guardians. They killed the cultists they found — six, maybe seven, spread thin and not expecting anyone. They cleared the upper chambers, and then the lower ones, and by the end of the second week there was nothing left alive down there but them.

**And so they concluded they had completed the mission.** Find out who is operating here. Eliminate them. Done, both clauses, and no boat to report it on.

So they stayed. Ottilie's reasoning was correct and it killed them: they had no way off the island, their orders said *eliminate whoever is operating there*, and the only way to know whether they'd got all of them was to wait and see who else turned up.

### The tomb

Somewhere in that second week, Aldous looked through the chipped hole in the side of the ornate tomb, and a four-armed woman in a bloodied white gown opened her eyes and looked back at him.

He did not stop talking about it after that.

He was a priest — a bad one by the Church's lights, but a real one — and the Church teaches that the Old Gods never existed. He had just been looked at by one, and she was chained, and she was in pain, and she was frightened. Everything he had left of a vocation reorganised itself around getting her out.

Ottilie refused. Repeatedly. Her position was that they were four people and a gnome on a rock with no boat, that whatever was in that box had been put there by something strong enough to put it there, and that opening it was not in anyone's orders.

**They were still arguing about it when the ships came.**

> *This argument is the emotional core of the whole story, and it is worth playing out loud if Gimble ever tells it. It is also the argument this party will eventually have.*

### Early July — the harvest

Not one boat. Three, and something over twenty men, and at the front of them a very tall, very pale man in an ornate headdress.

The team was underground when they arrived and above ground when they were caught, and it was not a battle. Grigor put three of them down and took a crossbow bolt through the thigh doing it. Ottilie surrendered the moment she understood the arithmetic, because a sergeant who has actually served does not spend her people on a fight already lost.

**They were taken alive on purpose.** That is the part Gimble has never been able to put down.

The cult was not there to kill intruders. It was there to fill barrels, and it had just been handed five healthy adults nobody would come looking for — because who reports a missing Penitent? A Penitent is a debt with legs. If a unit stops writing, the Church assumes they died doing what they were sent to do, and files it.

### The beach — four days

They were kept chained above the tide line on the second beach and bled into the same barrels as the goddess, a little at a time, because the cult wanted it fresh and wanted it slow.

**Aldous went first**, and went badly, and was still trying to preach at them when he stopped.
**Grigor** lasted longest of the four; there was more of him.
**Sabin** talked the whole time, at everyone, about anything, and got quieter over three days.
**Ottilie went last**, and she went without giving them one word, and the last thing she did was look directly at the treeline where she knew Gimble was, and not look at it again.

The stains this party found on the beach in August and did not investigate are theirs.

### How Gimble got out

They put him last and they put him aside, for two reasons, both insulting.

He was small — barely worth the walk to the barrel. And they could not work out what he was for. He would not scream and he would not beg and he would not stop watching them, and men doing that kind of work do not like being watched by something they cannot read.

**So they left him chained to a stake at the edge of the sand and got on with the others, and it took them four days to get round to him, and four days was three too many.**

He got out of the cuff. *(How, exactly, is Dave's to fill in — dislocated thumb, a pin, an animal, the tide. Leave it open.)*

He did not run immediately. He got into the treeline and he stayed, for two more days, close enough to watch, because Ottilie was still alive and he had not yet accepted that there was nothing he could do. He was right that there was nothing he could do. He has not accepted it.

Then he went along the shore to where a thousand years of reef has been piling up broken ships, lashed enough timber together to be called a raft if you were being generous, and put out into three miles of water on it.

### July to August — the road to a cage

He made the mainland. He wandered for days — no map, no language for where he was, no idea which way a Church camp lay.

Then he found people who offered to help.

They were slavers, and he fought them, and he fought them well and for a long time and lost anyway, and then he fought them again during the transport, and again at Bramblefen. That is the whole reason he is where he is.

**Vaskar's assessment**, written in his own hand in the working ledger if the party ever reads it: too willful to break, too skilled to sell to a household, and — the line that matters — *a branded Penitent.* If it ever came out that Lord Ironwake's people had collared a servant of the Church, not even Ironwake could stand in front of what the Church would do about it.

So Gimble could not be sold and could not be freed and could not simply be killed without waste.

Then Ironwake's wagon arrived with a giant in it who needed something to hit.

**Vaskar's solution is elegant and it is why the mask is on his head.** A sparring partner good enough to sharpen Mott and not good enough to damage him. Nobody sees the face. Nobody sees the shoulder. And at the end of it there is a nameless hole outside the wall, and no Penitent was ever here.

## What Gimble can tell this party

This is the payoff. **He closes the oldest open thread they have** — Vozruk's first question, from Chapter 1, never answered.

**He knows:**
- **What happened to the first team.** Names, order, how long it took.
- **That the cult bleeds people into barrels and ships them out by sea** — which is exactly what this party watched happen from the second beach and never understood.
- **The woman in the tomb.** Four arms, white gown, chains, the wound in her side. **He can corroborate Wolfram**, who has been carrying that alone since Chapter 2 and has had no reason to be sure he didn't imagine it.
- **The name Adephagia**, shouted the way the party heard it shouted.
- **The layout of the crypt**, in detail, including the rooms this party never entered — which matters if they ever spend the amulet's last charge.
- **The tall pale man in the headdress** *(see decisions)*.

**He does not know:**
- What the blood is for, or that a ritual is what it's for.
- Who Saraswati is. Aldous may have known; Aldous is not available.
- The name Brudda Death.
- Anything about St. Pyotr's, Ald's Star, or Ironwake beyond the men who sold him.

**And there is one thing he cannot tell them, because he does not know it:** the party arrived on Dol six weeks after he left, walked past his team's blood on the sand, and did not stop to look at it.

> *Whether that ever comes up is a choice. It is a very sharp knife and it will cut whoever is holding it.*

## What this opens

- **The mission clause.** Vozruk's order was: find out what happened to the first team, and eliminate whoever is operating there. **Gimble is the answer to the first half, walking around.** Producing him in front of Vozruk is arguably the only part of Chapter 1's mission this party has ever completed — and it is worth something against five hundred years each.
- **The legal grenade.** A branded Penitent was bought, chained, and scheduled to die for sport by a lord's chainmaster. Vaskar is right that the Church would burn Bramblefen to the waterline over it. **The party is walking out with proof.** That is leverage over Ironwake that has nothing to do with Cobb's ledger, and it arrives at exactly the moment they need leverage over Ironwake.
- **Two survivors of the same crypt, in the same party.** Wolfram saw her through the stone. Gimble stood in the room while a priest argued for opening it. Neither has told anyone what it did to them.
- **The argument, again.** Ottilie and Aldous had it and it killed them. This party is going to have it, and Gimble already knows how it ends.

## Decisions for you

1. **Is the herald from St. Pyotr's the man who ran the harvest on Dol?** *Recommended: yes.* It costs nothing, it makes the escape at the end of Chapter 4 retroactively awful, and it hands the party a personal reason to hunt a man they currently think of as "the one who got away." If you'd rather keep him unlinked, cut the headdress from the beach scene and Gimble just describes "a tall one who gave the orders."
2. **Did the crew that dropped them betray them, or just run?** Betrayal ties to the arrested sailor-heretics in Chapter 1 and makes the cult look organised. Cowardice is bleaker and cheaper.
3. **The storm that wrecked the *Hreowan*.** It came up fast and wrong on a clear night. If the cult's reach extends to weather, or to a bought crew, this party's shipwreck stops being bad luck. I have deliberately not asserted either way.
4. **Team size and names.** Five felt right — it mirrors the current party and gives Gimble four losses rather than an anonymous crowd. All names are placeholders.
5. **Optional darker hook, currently NOT in the text above:** Gimble never saw Sabin's body. He assumes. If you want a thread out of Dol that isn't closed, that's where it is — but it does contradict "sole survivor," so it needs a deliberate ruling from you.
6. **How much does Gimble volunteer?** Written above as though he'll tell it all. He may well not. **This is Dave's character now** — the events are yours, the telling is his. Hand him this document, not a summary.
