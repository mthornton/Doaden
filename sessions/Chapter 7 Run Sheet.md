# Chapter 7 Run Sheet — Bramblefen

**Companion to `Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`.** The DM Notes are the *why*; this is the *at the table*.
**Party:** JonLief, Luthien Veynar, Wolfram Eisenherz, Virgil — **4 PCs, level 3.**
**Scope: this chapter runs 2–3 play nights and covers ONE in-game afternoon.** It ends when the match, the revolt, and Ysabel's detainment are all resolved — not before.

---

## HOW THIS RUNS — three nights, one afternoon

**The single most important consequence: there is no long rest in this chapter.** Sundown is hours away in the fiction even if it's three weeks away at the table. Whatever the party spends on night 1 is still spent when they meet Vaskar on night 3. **They will arrive at the boss depleted, and that is intended.**

| Night | Covers | In-game time | Curtain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — The Road and the Wall** | Erwana at the campfire (pendant, ledger, Ysabel's grab) → the morning decision → approach → entry branch → the Whipping Post | Previous night → arrival, mid-afternoon | **They're inside, and something in the compound now knows it.** End before anyone important has been talked to. |
| **2 — The Compound** | The Pens (JonLief's scene, Denrick, Anselm) → Deep Cages (Mott, Durgan, Prisoner X) → the Armory decision → the Hall and Cobb | Mid-afternoon → the hour before sundown | **Vaskar stands up and reaches for the door.** Cut there. Whether Cobb is alive is the question the table takes home. |
| **3 — Sundown** | The match → Anselm's signal → the revolt → Vaskar → Ysabel → aftermath and the eighteen | Sundown → dark | The chapter ends. Ysabel's trial is the next chapter. |

**Running it across weeks:**

- **Recap resources, not just plot.** Open each night with "you are still on the hit points you finished on, and Luthien has *these* slots left." Players who've had three weeks off will assume they healed. They did not.
- **Keep the hour visible.** Say the time out loud at the start of each night and every time they burn a scene. Three weeks of real time makes an afternoon feel like a week unless you keep saying it.
- **⏳ A short rest costs an hour — and the hour is what's killing Cobb.** They can take one. It advances the clock toward Vaskar leaving that office. That is the cleanest pressure in the whole chapter: *rest, or reach the old man.* Make them say which.
- **Don't end a night mid-combat.** Both curtains above are decision points, not initiative rounds.
- **Attrition is the difficulty dial.** If they're gutted by the end of night 2, that's what the three release valves are for (Anselm fires early / Mott loose / guards break) — see the combat section. Use them rather than softening Vaskar mid-fight.

## ONE-PAGE QUICK REFERENCE

### The clock
The whole chapter hangs on **sundown**. Say the hour out loud when the party arrives and again every time they burn time.

| When | Vaskar | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival (mid-afternoon) | **His office**, with Cobb | Yard running normally. Whipping post active. |
| ~1 hr out | His office | Guards rig the arena fence. Off-duty five wake and drift out. Cobb's interrogation goes quiet — **this is Vaskar deciding Cobb is empty.** |
| **Just before sundown** | **Leaving the office** | ⏳ **COBB DIES HERE** if the party never reached the Hall. Vaskar kills him on his way out, then walks down to the arena. |
| **Sundown** | **The arena** | Match: Mott vs. Prisoner X. Every eye on it. **Anselm signals.** |
| **After the match** | The arena → Ysabel | Detains Ysabel **only if all 3 conditions hold** (see below). |

**Vaskar can only be in one place.** He cannot kill Cobb once he's at the rail — so **anything that keeps him in his office keeps Cobb alive**: a buyer to receive, an alarm to answer, Ysabel's party arriving and having to be handled courteously. The party can buy the old man time without knowing they did.

**The dilemma to press:** the Hall and the arena are not in the same building. They cannot save Cobb *and* be standing in the yard when the match starts.

**⚠️ Ysabel is only detained if ALL THREE are true:**

1. **The match happened** — Mott fought. (Freed, hurt, burned out, or no Prisoner X = no match = no stage.)
2. **The revolt was put down**, or never fired. A yard full of freed men is not a yard where anyone gets quietly arrested.
3. **The party never engaged Vaskar.** The instant he's fighting, hiding, bargaining or running, he stops caring about her. He protects Vaskar.

**Break any one and she walks.** The party can win Ysabel's trial without ever agreeing to be her champion, and without ever knowing they did it. **Don't tell them** — let it land in Solteres.

**If the party stalls, don't stop the world.** The match happens without them and the revolt goes off badly (Row 3).

### The four levers the party may not know they're pulling
1. **Free or break Mott** → Ironwake has no champion for the trial in two days. Ysabel's trial is won in the Deep Cages, not the courtroom. **It also cancels the match — which means Vaskar never gets his moment to detain her.** One lever, two saves.
2. **Free Durgan and treat him well** → same result, and Mott never enters the yard fight.
3. **Talk to Anselm before sundown** → they choose when the revolt fires instead of being caught in it.
4. **Open the Armory** → decides which row of the revolt table you're on. This is the single biggest mechanical decision of the night.

## GETTING IN — three real branches

Roll or ask for the approach *before* they see the walls. Where Vaskar is when the shooting starts is decided here.

### A. The Buyers' Gate (bribe / Ysabel's cover)
The easy way in and the most dangerous one socially.

> **Read-aloud:**
> *"Two men lean on the gateposts with the particular boredom of men who have not been paid on time. One is eating something out of his fist. Neither straightens up as you approach. 'Fen's closed to travellers,' he says, without looking. 'Unless you're buying.'"*

- **Persuasion DC 10**, or advantage with drink. With Ysabel fronting it, they don't even roll — she is a known buyer and they wave her through.
- **The cost:** Vaskar is notified inside a minute that a buyer has arrived. He recognizes Ysabel on sight and assumes she is here to scout Ironwake's champion. He is *right*, or near enough.
- **Where Vaskar is:** in the Hall, expecting them, with Cobb still tied to the chair and a rag over him. He will receive them courteously and lie for ten minutes straight.
- **Live wire:** the party is inside, unsuspected, with total freedom of the yard — and a boss who is already counting them. Item 12 (detain Ysabel at the end of the match) is now on rails.

### B. The Drain (stealth)
Erwana's route. She swears it is safe. She is telling the truth as she knows it, and she is four weeks out of date.

> **Read-aloud — the grate:**
> *"The fen gives up a smell like a wound. The grate is where she said it would be, half-drowned, the bars sprung loose on one side. Beyond it the channel runs black and low under the wall. Erwana is already nodding — 'This way, this way, it's nothing, I did it in the dark and half-starved' — and she is not lying to you."*

Denrick sold the route to Vaskar after she ran and seeded it afterward. Run the three hazards in order, spaced by crawl:

1. **The spiked sump.** Dex **DC 15**, 2d6 piercing, and the lead crawler is stuck (Str DC 12 to pull free) — noise if they fail twice.
2. **The tar barrel.** Rigged to a trip cord on the incline. Dex **DC 15** or 2d6 bludgeoning and knocked prone/pushed back 10 ft.; **DC 10** halves. The reek marks them — disadvantage on Stealth until they wash.
3. **Rat swarm** — *only if they are carrying Denrick's "lucky" sack.* One swarm, CR 1/4. If they left the sack, skip it and let them feel clever.

- **Where Vaskar is:** in the Hall, unaware. **This is the only approach where Cobb's rescue can happen quietly.**
- **Exit:** the grate at the back of the Pens. They come up *inside* JonLief's scene.
- **If Erwana leads and eats the sump:** let her. Her being wrong, and knowing it, is worth more than the 2d6.

### C. Over the Wall / Through the Gate (force)
> **Read-aloud:**
> *"The gate takes three blows and gives on the fourth, and the sound of it goes across that yard like a struck bell. Somewhere behind the wall a voice starts shouting a name, over and over, and it is not a name — it is the alarm."*

- **Everything wakes.** All 13 guards converge in waves (below). Vaskar gathers the barracks five and comes personally.
- **Anselm does NOT signal.** He watches to see whether the intruders are winning. He gives the party **two rounds** of visibly beating guards before he commits — reward a party that fights *in front of the Pens* where the men can see it.
- **This is the only approach that can go badly enough to kill the party.** See the alarm math below.

## COMBAT — the math, and how to keep it from being a slaughter

### Budget (4 PCs, level 3)
| | Easy | Medium | Hard | Deadly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Party threshold | 300 | 600 | 900 | **1600** |

### The two blocks

**Fen Guard** — *CR 1/8, 25 XP.* Bramblefen's own. Underfed, unpaid, unenthusiastic.
- **AC** 16 (chain shirt, shield) · **HP** 11 (2d8+2) · **Speed** 30 ft.
- **Spear** +3, 1d6+1 piercing (1d8+1 two-handed). **Light Crossbow** +3, 1d8+1.
- Passive Perception 12. **Morale:** when half of any group of them drops, the rest run for the gate or the Hall. Let them run — a fleeing guard raising the alarm is better drama than a corpse.

**Ironwake Thug** — *CR 1/2, 100 XP.* The five in the barracks. Professionals, brought in with the giant.
- **AC** 11 (leather) · **HP** 32 (5d8+10) · **Speed** 30 ft.
- **Multiattack:** two mace attacks, +4, 1d6+2 bludgeoning each. **Heavy Crossbow** +2, 1d10.
- **Pack Tactics.** They do not break and they do not chase individuals — they hold the arena and the Hall door.

### Wave math — why you must stage this

| Encounter | Composition | Adj. XP | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate | 2 Fen Guard | 75 | Trivial |
| Whipping post + patrol responds | 3 Fen Guard | 150 | **Easy** |
| Pens + Deep Cages doors | 2 Fen Guard | 75 | Trivial |
| Barracks, **asleep** | 5 Thug (surprised, no rolls first round) | ~500 effective | **Medium–Hard** |
| Barracks, **awake** | 5 Thug | 1000 | **Hard+** |
| **Full alarm — all 13 at once** | 8 Guard + 5 Thug | **~1750** | **DEADLY — TPK range** |
| Vaskar as written (CR 5, 102 HP, Villain Actions) | solo | 1800 | **DEADLY** |
| **Vaskar tuned** (below) + 2 Thug | | ~1300 | **Hard** — correct for the finale |

**The rule for the night: never let more than 5 enemies act in the same round.** Bramblefen is a *sequence* of small fights, not a battle. Use the buildings — the party fights in doorways, in the Pens, in the Hall. The yard is the only place a full convergence can happen, and the yard is where you should be reluctant to put them before sundown.

**If the alarm goes up anyway**, you have three release valves, in order:
1. **Anselm fires early.** 18 men hit the yard. Guards split their attention. Instantly halves the pressure on the party.
2. **Mott gets loose.** He mauls both sides and is a wall the party can put between themselves and the Thugs. Subtract 1–2 guards per round; add 1d4 slave deaths.
3. **Guards break.** Fen Guards have no reason to die for Vaskar. Let them run.

### Vaskar — tuned for level 3
Run him from `Vaskar the Brand.md` with these changes:
- **HP 80** (not 102).
- **Drop the Villain Actions block entirely.** He has 2 Thugs instead; that's his action economy.
- **Keep:** Multiattack, spiked chain (Str DC 15 pull + prone), branding iron (Con DC 15), *Someone Else's Skin*, *Blind to Love*, *Relentless*, *Feed on Their Fear*.
- **Play the reaction hard.** Cobb is tied to a chair five feet from him. The first time Vaskar interposes the tortured old man to eat a crossbow bolt, the fight becomes about something.
- **Blind to Love is the win condition.** Every time a PC uses Help, shields someone, or does something obviously selfless — *say it out loud*: "Vaskar hesitates. He doesn't understand what he just saw." Disadvantage on his next roll. Let the players learn the trick.
- **He does not die on principle.** At ~25 HP: hostage, deal, run. Captured Vaskar is worth an arc. Dead Vaskar is worth a strongbox.

### Mott
Use the Rage Clock as written in `The Juggernaut.md` — it is already tuned for 4 PCs at level 3. **Do not put him in the yard while the party is also fighting Thugs.** He is either the sundown set-piece or the release valve, never both.

## SCENE ORDER & READ-ALOUD

### 1. The camp — Erwana *(the night before)*
The plea, the rope, the starving woman. **The pendant beat is the hinge of the whole session** — everything else follows from JonLief showing it.

> *"She goes still when she sees it. Not frightened — the other thing. 'I knew that,' she says. 'I knew that. She had one. She was in my cell and she was so hot with the fever you could feel it across the straw, and there was a boy, and she wouldn't let go of him.' She looks up at you. 'They took them out. I don't know where. But Cobb writes it down. Cobb writes all of it down.'"*

Then the ledger. Let her fetch it. **Let JonLief open it himself.** Play the drop: hope, then page after page of nothing, then the slow arrival of what they *are* holding — Ironwake, decades of him, in a dead man's handwriting.

> **If Ysabel reaches for it:** *"'May I?' she says, and her hand is already out. She is not being sly about it. She is a woman two days from a trial who has just seen a weapon land in the grass."*
> Erwana's face does the work here. Play her flinching from a noble's hand. **The story is better if Ysabel does not get this book** — and if she doesn't get it, she stays with the party to be near it.

### 2. First sight of Bramblefen
Use the boxed text already in the DM Notes — the lash, the chains, the shouting. Don't add to it.

### 3. The Whipping Post
> *"The guard is drunk and he is not very good at this, which is somehow worse. He is taking his time because he keeps losing his place. Three men are on their knees in the mud with their heads held up so they have to watch. One of them has stopped watching. He is looking at you."*

Cutting these four loose gets the party the layout and *"they've got the old clerk up at the house."* It is also the first time the compound learns something is wrong — decide whether the guard lives to shout.

### 4. The Pens — **JonLief's scene**
Give him the room. Slow it down. Other players can be elsewhere.

> *"Stone, and dark, and forty years of men. They come to the bars when they hear the door — not fast. Nobody in here moves fast. Someone asks if you're the buyer. Someone else tells him to shut up. And then, from the back, where it's darkest, an old voice that has clearly asked this before and stopped expecting an answer: 'Is there anyone out there still looking for us?'"*

- **Ask after the woman and the boy.** Nobody here knows. Every man says no. That is the point — make him ask five times.
- **The empty cell at the end.** Investigation **DC 12** finds the loose flagstone and the hollow under it. Dry, shaped, worn smooth by a book that isn't there.
  > *"There's a space under the stone about the size of a ledger, and it is empty, and the dust in it has been disturbed within the month. Whatever lived here, someone took it out and carried it into the fen."*
  > **Let them work out it's the book in their own pack.**
- **Denrick** is in a cell here and starts talking immediately. **Anselm** is at the back and does not.

### 5. Anselm — the Wolfram scene
Use the boxed text in `Anselm Vogt.md` verbatim. Then: **no Insight roll.** Tell the table plainly that this man is telling the truth. That contrast — right after Denrick — is the whole design.

He gives, freely: 13 guards and where each stands, the five asleep and why he hasn't moved, where the armory keys hang, that the big one is gentle until the dwarf makes him otherwise, that the masked man dies tonight, and that Denrick talks to the overseers.

**Then ask Wolfram the question Anselm actually asks:** *"Who's in charge of you?"*

### 6. The Deep Cages
> *"Two cells. In the first, a dwarf sits with his back to the bars, watching. In the second — and it takes a moment to understand the scale of what you are seeing — a man the size of a cart horse is sitting cross-legged in the straw, holding a bundle of twine and stalks in the shape of a doll, and he is walking it very carefully along his own knee."*

- **Durgan speaks first, and it's a warning:** *"Don't shout. He's having a good day."*
- **Prisoner X** says nothing, ever. Sitting perfectly still, centred, iron over the whole head. Every guard who walks past says he'll be dead by dark.
- **This is where Ysabel's trial is won.** If the party is kind to Mott where Durgan can see it, you have given away Ironwake's champion.

### 7. The Chainmaster's Hall — Cobb, then Vaskar
> *"The old man in the chair does not look up when the door opens. He has learned what it means when the door opens. His hands are wrong. When he finally does lift his head, one eye is swollen closed and the other is trying very hard to focus on you, and what comes out of him is not a plea. It's a professional's habit, forty years deep: 'I've told him. The record would indicate — there is no record. There's no record left to give him.'"*

He is telling the truth. He gave the book away and does not know where it went. **Vaskar cannot tell the difference between a man protecting a secret and a man who has none** — that is the tragedy sitting in the chair.

> ⏳ **This scene has a wall.** Vaskar kills Cobb on his way out of the office to the match — *before* sundown, not after dark. Once he's at the rail, the chair is empty. Anything that keeps Vaskar in the Hall (a buyer to receive, an alarm, Ysabel to be courteous to) buys the old man time.
>
> **If they get here late:** don't say "he's dead." Give them the room — the chair, ropes cut not untied, the working ledger still open on the desk where Vaskar had been reading entries aloud to a man whose eyes had stopped being able to check them.

Cut him loose and he pours out everything: Maren, the fever, the boy cleaned up and sold quickly, and **Barney Soot** — but only if they get the working ledger from the office, which is a different book and it stays in the Hall.

### 8. Sundown — the match
> *"They've dragged the fence into a circle in the middle of the yard, and every man in Bramblefen who isn't chained is leaning on it. The masked man is walked out. He doesn't struggle. He doesn't look at anyone. And then the doors of the Deep Cages open, and the crowd makes a sound you've heard before at fairs and hangings, and the ground moves."*

Anselm's signal lands here. So does whatever the party has already set up.

**Vaskar's move on Ysabel comes *after* the match, not during it — and only if the match happened, the revolt was put down, and nobody laid a hand on him.** Check the three conditions before you reach for it. If any one failed, he lets her go without spending a man, and the party may never learn how close it was.

**Sequence the minute:** the match starts → Anselm signals → the yard goes up → *then* the match's outcome and Ysabel. Don't resolve them simultaneously; resolve them in that order and let the party act between each.

## REVOLT — trigger and outcomes

**Anselm chooses the instant.** Any disruption *can* trigger it; he fires it deliberately if the party has talked to him. Two variables: **armed or not**, **party in the fight or not.**

| # | Situation | Result | Aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Party fights alongside | Guards break in a few rounds. 3–5 slaves die. | They follow Wolfram out of the fen. |
| 2 | Party arms them, then goes for Vaskar/Cobb | Costly win. 8–10 dead. | Respected, not adored. Some blame him for leaving. |
| 3 | Party stays out, unarmed | Slaughter in minutes. Examples made. | **Wolfram is a fraud.** Survivors turn on the party. |
| 4 | Party stays out, armed | They win barely, and are not gentle. | No debt owed. They hold Bramblefen, take no orders. |
| 5 | Resolved quietly before sundown | No uprising. Cages unlocked, walked out. | Rescued, not comrades. Grateful, dependent, useless in a fight. |

**Mott is a wildcard on any row.** Loose and raging: subtract guards fast, add 1d4 slave deaths, put the party's front line in reach. Durgan freed and treated well: he keeps Mott out of it entirely.
