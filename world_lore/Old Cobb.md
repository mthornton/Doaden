# Old Cobb

**Type:** NPC — enslaved former slaver's clerk at **Bramblefen**; rescue objective and the emotional payload of the slaver arc.
**Function:** the man who wrote down every sale — including [[JonLief]]'s wife and son — and the walking evidence against **Lord Leofard Ironwake**. Vaskar (see [[Vaskar the Brand]]) is torturing him when the party arrives.

> Reskin of "Warden Ingfar" (*Prison Breakdown*, the module Bramblefen is built from). Inverted: here the captive being tortured is the party's emotional payload, not a quest-giver. Current session file is `sessions/Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`; the old `Chapter 7: The Chained Coast` file was deleted and replaced by it.

## The Man Who Kept the Books

Cobb was never a whip-hand. He'll tell you that, quickly, if you let him — *he never once laid a chain on anyone.* He was a clerk. A factor. A tidy man with a tidy hand who, for the better part of forty years, sat at a high desk in the corner of a slaver's yard and **wrote the names down.** Who was bought, who was sold, for how much, to whom. He told himself the ledger was a neutral thing — that a man who merely records a sin does not commit it. It is a lie he believed for a very long time, because believing it let him sleep.

He was good at it. Too good. A clerk who has recorded every buyer, every price, every quiet "household purchase" a great lord would rather no one remember, is a clerk who knows far too much. When Cobb grew old and slow and his eyes began to fail, the masters did the arithmetic he'd taught them: a man who knows your secrets is cheaper to collar than to pension. So the bookkeeper of the trade was written, at last, into his own ledger — a line item, sold to Bramblefen, where Vaskar keeps him because Vaskar keeps everything that might one day be useful.

## Why Vaskar Is Hurting Him

Someone has been using Cobb's old records against Ironwake — a name here, a date there, surfacing where it shouldn't. Vaskar suspects a leak, and Cobb is the likeliest source. He is not wrong. In his last years of freedom, sick with a conscience that arrived decades too late, **Cobb quietly kept a second ledger** — a true copy of the sales the masters most wanted forgotten — and hid it, meaning to do *one* right thing before he died.

**He does not have it any more.** He gave it to **Erwana** the night she ran, told her about the drain, and asked her to get it out of the fen. He does not know where she is, whether she lived, or where the book is now. It is the only right thing he has ever done and he did not expect to survive it.

So when Vaskar asks him where the ledger is and who has seen it, **Cobb genuinely cannot answer** — and Vaskar cannot tell the difference between a man protecting a secret and a man who no longer has one. That is the tragedy sitting in the chair. No amount of pressure will produce anything, because there is nothing left in him to produce.

**The clock — and it is earlier than it looks.** Vaskar kills Cobb **shortly before the sundown match**, on his way out of the office to go and watch it. He does it himself, without heat, the way a man closes a book he has finished: Cobb has stopped being an asset, and Vaskar does not leave loose property tied up in a room he is walking away from.

The deadline is **not** "after dark" — it is *Vaskar's walk to the arena*. He cannot kill Cobb once he is standing at the rail, which means **anything that keeps him in his office keeps the old man breathing**: a buyer to receive, an alarm to answer, a noblewoman he has to be courteous to. And the party cannot be in the Hall and at the match at the same time. That is the choice the evening is built around.

## The two ledgers — keep these straight

The single most confusable thing in the session. They are different books, in different places, doing different jobs:

| | **Cobb's second ledger** | **The working ledger** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Already in the party's hands, via Erwana | Vaskar's office in the Chainmaster's Hall — current business, never leaves the Hall |
| Covers | Decades of old sales the masters wanted forgotten | The last months, including this one |
| Worth | **Ironwake.** Names, prices, buyers, the shape of the whole operation | **Rowan.** The name of the man who bought him: **Barney Soot**, chimney sweep, Solteres. Also the lines about women sold to buyers who refused to identify themselves and "paid well" |
| Useless for | Finding Rowan — it predates him by years | Leverage. It's a business record, not evidence against a lord |

**Optional, recommended:** the second ledger holds a *near miss* — an old entry for a half-elf woman and a boy child, "clan business," wrong date, wrong price, wrong buyer. Not them. It proves Cobb writes down exactly the kind of thing JonLief needs, so the hope transfers to *reach Cobb* instead of dying on an empty page.

## What He Knows

Everything, going back decades. Cobb remembers every transaction he ever recorded, the way some old men remember every hymn from childhood. Names, faces, buyers, prices. He can name, buyer by buyer, the full shape of Ironwake's slaving — the single most dangerous document in the fens, carried in a dying clerk's skull.

**He remembers a half-elf woman and a small boy, brought in a month ago by men who called it "clan business," and he remembers exactly who bought them. The woman died from sickness before the buyer could take her. Her body was burned.**

**What he cannot give from memory: the boy's buyer.** He can tell JonLief everything about **Maren** — that she came in starved and beaten and already burning with fever, that she would not let go of the boy, that she was dead within days and they burned her because of the sickness. That is the part that lives in him. But the boy was one line in a month of lines, written in a failing hand by a man whose eyes are going, and the sale was recent enough that it has not settled into memory the way forty years of older business has. He will say so, precisely, in the way that is most maddening to a man who has crossed a continent:

> *"The record would indicate — and it would, if I had it — a sale within the fortnight. A sweep, I believe. Solteres. I could not swear to the name. I would not give you a name I could not swear to."*

**That is the design.** The grief is free; the name costs a raid on Vaskar's office. Don't let Cobb short-circuit the working ledger.

**If the party never gets the working ledger:** [[Talwyn Ceth]] (Stock, slave #10) can read it fast and reproduce the Barney Soot entry from memory afterward — the backup copy for a chapter where the crucial clue is a book in a building that may be on fire.

## Personality & how to run him

- **Precise, hedging, clerkish.** He speaks in careful qualifications even now — "the record would indicate," "if memory serves, and it does." Grief has not made him dramatic; it has made him exact.
- **Guilt-soaked and desperate to confess.** He will not give a torturer a syllable, but the moment someone shows him mercy — cuts him loose, offers water — the words pour out of him. He *needs* to be the man who finally told the truth.
- **He qualifies everything, even now.** Under torture, bleeding, one eye swollen shut, he still hedges. It is forty years of professional habit and it is the last thing he will lose.
- **A moral mirror, not a monster.** Play him as what he is: the small, tidy face of atrocity — the person who "just kept the records." Let JonLief (or the party) decide what he's owed.

## Stat block — Old Cobb

*Medium humanoid (human), neutral — noncombatant (CR 0)*

- **AC** 10 · **HP** 7 (2d8 − 2) · **Speed** 20 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 (−1) | 9 (−1) | 9 (−1) | 15 (+2) | 13 (+1) | 11 (+0) |

- **Skills** History +4, Investigation +4, Insight +3
- **Senses** passive Perception 11 (poor eyes)
- **Languages** Common, Dwarvish (trade tongue); literate in three scripts
- **Condition** begins with **2 levels of exhaustion** from torture (speed already halved above; disadvantage on ability checks and attacks) — recovers one level per long rest with care

**Traits**

- **The Ledger in His Head.** Cobb can recall any slave transaction he recorded in his forty years of free service — buyer, price, date — and can enumerate Lord Ironwake's "household" purchases in full. Treat this as the adventure's key intel, not a die roll. **Exception: the last few months.** Recent business lives in the working ledger, not in his memory; he can describe [[JonLief]]'s wife and son and what became of Maren, but not the name of the man who bought the boy.
- **Endured Worse.** Cobb automatically refuses to give information to a captor, slaver, or torturer, no matter the pressure. Kindness, not coercion, unlocks him. **He also has nothing left to give** — the second ledger is gone and he does not know where.
- **Reads a Page Once.** Given light, time, and the working ledger in his hands, Cobb can pull every relevant entry out of it in a single scene — no Investigation checks, no page-by-page search. He is faster at this than any PC.

**Actions**

- **Feeble Strike.** *Melee:* +1 to hit, 1 bludgeoning. He is not a fighter; he is a witness.

## Hooks & rewards

- **The rescue.** Cobb is who Vaskar is torturing in the Chainmaster's Hall — reaching him *is* the climax's objective, and freeing him mid-fight is a moral gut-punch (the tortured old man vs. the master who broke him). Vaskar will also use him: *Someone Else's Skin* means the first crossbow bolt of the fight can land in the man they came to save.
- **The second ledger, closed.** The party already has it. What Cobb adds is the *provenance* — who wrote it, why, and what it cost him. A book nobody can vouch for is paper; a book with a living clerk beside it is testimony. **He asks only one thing in return: that they use it.**
- **JonLief's truth.** Cobb is the one who can finally tell JonLief what became of **Maren** — and point him at the working ledger for **Rowan**. Keeping this old man alive suddenly matters more than any coin.
- **Cobb and Anselm.** Two record-keepers a hundred feet apart, forty years and four months, opposite sides of the same page — [[Anselm Vogt]] has scratched a tally into the Pens wall for every man carried out; Cobb wrote the names of the men who bought them. **Put them in the same room before the session ends if you can.** It will be the quietest and worst scene of the night.
- **Redemption or reckoning.** Whether Cobb earns mercy or judgment is a table decision. Either way he wants, at the end, to have told the truth once.
