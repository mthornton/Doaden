# The Reckoning of Years

*A player handout. Two timelines: the age of the world as the Church keeps it, and your own road since the brand. Nothing here is secret — this is what an ordinary educated person in Doaden could tell you, plus what your own eyes have seen.*

---

## Part One: The Age of the World

The Church of Azerai keeps the calendar, and the Church keeps the histories. Every date below is the Church's date. Where common people tell it differently — and they do, quietly, in kitchens and taverns and nowhere near a cleric — that's set underneath in italics.

Draw your own conclusions. That is, after all, what got at least one of you branded.

---

### Before the Reckoning

No date. No record. Nothing before Year 0 survives in any form the Church acknowledges.

What is known, because it is impossible to hide, is that **people worshipped other things before they worshipped the God Emperor.** The Old Gods. Dozens of them, by some accounts, each with a domain and a festival and a following. Most are forgotten entirely. A few survive as songs, or as holidays that everyone keeps without remembering why — Vasant Panchami in the spring, and others like it.

The Church teaches that these were never gods at all. Stories. Superstition. Mythology, and not true.

> *And yet a man on Dol Island bit through his own tongue rather than say one word about Adephagia, and died smiling.*

---

### 0 PA — The Apocalypse

Year zero. The world ends, and begins again.

The Church's chronicle starts here because nothing before it can be verified.

> *A convenient place for a chronicle to start, say the uncharitable. You cannot be caught lying about a period no one can check.*

---

### 147 PA — The Church of Azerai is re-founded

Note the word. **Re-**founded. The Church holds that it existed before the Apocalypse, was scattered by it, and was gathered again in this year.

> *Nobody can produce a single document from the first Church. Not one. Ask a cleric where the founding writings are kept and you will be told they were lost in the Apocalypse, which is also where everything else inconvenient was lost.*

---

### 447 PA — High Bishop Eadgar I confirmed

The first High Bishop the Church names.

> *The first they name — not the first there was. Three hundred years passed between the re-founding and Eadgar, and the Church's own scribes admit no records from that period exist. Records don't simply stop. Somebody stops them.*

---

### 461 PA — Eadgar I dies of natural causes

Fourteen years in office.

> *"Natural causes" is the Church's account. It is the only account. There is no other.*

---

### 661 PA — High Bishop Dunstan I confirmed

> *Two hundred years. Between Eadgar's death and Dunstan's confirmation, the official history names not one single High Bishop. Either the seat sat empty for ten generations, or somebody has been struck out of the list.*

---

### 665 PA — The Sunlit Citadel declared the holy city of the Church

Dunstan's great act. The Citadel becomes the seat of the faith, and remains so today.

> *Declared by the Church — not offered by the city. The people who actually governed the Sunlit Citadel are said to have had a good deal to say about it at the time. Within a generation they had stopped saying it. Draw your own conclusion about how that was achieved.*

---

### 693 PA — Dunstan I passes peacefully in his sleep

He was very old. This one, most people accept.

### 693 PA — *Crimen Grave Hereses*

Silas's first act as High Bishop, in the same year he took the seat.

**The worship of any god other than the true God is heresy, and heresy is punishable by death.**

It has never been repealed. It is the law under which every one of you could be executed for the wrong conversation, and the law under which the cultists you have been fighting would be executed too, which is a thing worth sitting with.

---

### 694–702 PA — The Great Heresy Purge

Eight years. Thousands accused. Thousands burned alive.

The Church's position is that the purge cleansed Doaden of a genuine and spreading corruption, and that the faith the Empire enjoys today rests on it.

> *Thousands. Not hundreds. And a great many of them, people will tell you if you buy the second drink, were neighbours with land somebody else wanted, or debts somebody else owed, or opinions somebody else disliked. An accusation was enough. The fire did the rest.*
>
> *This is also, not by accident, the period in which the Old Gods stopped being worshipped openly and started being sung about instead.*

---

### 702 PA — Silas I dies under questionable circumstances

The Church does not elaborate, and does not like being asked.

> *This is the one entry where even the official chronicle declines to claim it was natural. Whatever happened to Silas, it happened to a man who had spent eight years burning people.*

---

### 702 PA — High Bishop Silas II confirmed

Same year. Same name.

---

### 703–707 PA — The War of the Five Kingdoms

Four years of war across Doaden, ending in the re-establishment of the Empire.

---

### 707 PA — Emperor Fredric, first of his name, is enthroned

The modern bond between Church and Empire is made here. Fredric takes the title of Emperor and makes it hereditary, and the Church proclaims his line **chosen by the eternal Emperor to carry his will until his return** — a return that will come **by birth, into this same bloodline**, and be known when an Emperor Fredric **does not die**: the Emperor is divine, the Church speaks for the divine, and the two have not been meaningfully separable since.

> *Ask a priest about the eternal Emperor before Fredric and you'll get a sermon about embodiment. Ask a second time and you'll get a look.*

---

### 710 PA — The Thirty Day War

A Beastmen uprising in the north. Put down inside a month.

> *Worth remembering that it happened at all, and that Beastmen have been raiding roads in the south this very season.*

---

### 1126 PA — Now

Four hundred and nineteen years since Fredric I. **Emperor Fredric IX** sits the throne. In every village, people pray *may he rise again* — that this Fredric is the one, and will prove it by never dying. *Every Fredric so far has died.*

---

### What this chronology does not say

Four gaps, all of them on the Church's own list, all of them noticeable to anyone who reads it twice:

1. **Nothing before Year 0.** Not one date, not one name.
2. **Three hundred years** between the Church's re-founding and its first named High Bishop.
3. **Two hundred years** between Eadgar I and Dunstan I with no High Bishop named at all.
4. **The eternal Emperor.** The Church teaches that the Fredric line was chosen by the eternal Emperor to carry his will until he is born again among them, and every village prays *may he rise again.* **But the chronology never names the eternal Emperor, never says when he was last seen as anything but a Fredric, and has no Emperor on it at all before 707.** Almost nobody in the Church believes there ever was one.

That fourth one is the strangest. A faith built on an eternal Emperor, and not one line about him on its own list.

## Part Two: Your Own Road

Everything below you were present for. Dates are as near as any of you can reckon them. *(Gimble: your own road to Bramblefen — the first team, Dol, the cage — is in `The Unit Before Us`.)*

**Total elapsed: about two and a half months. Missions completed: none. Debt reduced: none.**

---

### 22 July 1126 — The brand

You wake in a stone dining hall with no memory of arriving, four strangers at one table, and eat the best meal any of you has had in weeks. Guards read four names off a slip of paper. Yours.

You are branded at the forge with the Mark of Atonement. Luthien first, without warning. JonLief second, on his own feet.

**Vozruk the Redeemer** sets the terms: five hundred years of service each, reduced only by missions successfully completed. You are made a unit.

Finnian Quickstep outfits you, bans Luthien from the armory for life, and beats him about the head personally. Luthien keeps the bone guitar.

### 23 July 1126 — The *Hreowan* sails

Vozruk gives the mission: **Dol Island.** A previous team of Penitents went and never came back. Find out what happened. Find out who is operating there. Kill them.

Fid demonstrates the knitting spider by way of Vozruk opening JonLief's cheek with a thrown knife. Wolfram leaves with two spiders. Luthien leaves with the Ball of Undeath, against Vozruk's direct order.

You sail from the Castur River.

### 23 July – 13 August — Three weeks at sea

Uneventful until the last night, when a storm that had no business being there puts the *Hreowan* onto submerged reef.

The ship is lost. The crew is never accounted for.

### 14 August 1126 — Dol Island

You wake scattered on a beach. Skeletons in the dune grass. A carved cliff face and a stairway of glass mosaic. A sunlit cave holding two dozen unarmed dead, about two years old.

Below: mummified guardians, a cultist who chose to choke on his own tongue rather than answer a question, a woman with four arms chained inside a bleeding tomb who looked at Wolfram and then was not there, and a thing made of blood.

You reach the second beach as the barrels are being loaded. The ship sails anyway.

You take a map and an amulet. JonLief uses one of its two charges by accident.

### 15 August 1126 — Ald's Star

You row to the mainland and follow the road east.

Green halflings. A hostile welcome, a good dinner, and a song that went badly. Ald trades you what he knows about St. Pyotr's for help hunting whatever has been killing his livestock.

That night, two hounds with iron driven through their bodies come out of the dark. **Alice** and **Red** are killed.

### 16 August 1126 — The burial

The village buries them. You meet most of Ald's Star over two open graves.

Ald tells you what he knows: St. Pyotr's is no church, it stopped buying his goats and grain two months ago, and nobody there will come to the door.

### 18 August 1126 — St. Pyotr's

Black fog over the roof, and a conjured bird dead the instant it touched it.

In the garden, cultists — and a third iron-barred hound, the same as the pair that killed Alice and Red two days up that road. A locked trapdoor you never opened.

Inside: a table set for a meal three months old. A priest dead in the kitchen with a knife in his own hand. A priest hanged and opened over the south bell while you hid beneath him. A prisoner you chose not to release. A woman you could not save and did not take with you. Father Ignatius nailed to his own wall, and a journal in which a man talks himself all the way into hell.

In the chapel, a ritual, a standing ring of stone pouring black mist, and a herald in a headdress. JonLief goes down and nearly does not come back up.

You break the ritual. The herald escapes. And a dying man tells you three names you had never heard: **Saraswati**, **Adephagia**, and **Brudda Death**.

### 19 August – 19 September — A month on the road

The map ends at St. Pyotr's. You have no lead, no orders you can act on, and no idea how to reach Vozruk's camp.

So you walk. You work where there is work. You ask after a military camp on the Castur River from people who have never heard of it.

Nobody comes looking for you. That is worth noticing.

### 20 September 1126 — The Calf and Ass

An inn. Luthien plays for beds and supper.

Slavers at one table, dealing only with human buyers and only in everyone else. Master Thaddeus and Marion of the Alchemical Order, waiting on a boat. Lady Ysabel Rochefort, accused by her own uncle **Lord Leofard Ironwake** of poisoning her niece, travelling to Solteres with her champion **Ulfric Dreadmarsh** to answer it by trial of combat.

A staged scandal downstairs. Ulfric murdered upstairs with Luthien's stolen knife. Sheriff Eastwood clears Luthien in an hour and has Baron Emeric Blackholt in custody by midnight.

Then an old man sits down at your table, in a locked-down room where not one other person can see him, and thanks you for stopping the summoning at St. Pyotr's — which you have told nobody.

### 21 September 1126 — The road to Solteres

You agree to escort Lady Ysabel. Thaddeus and Marion ride along for the first stretch.

### 22 September — Ysabel asks

She asks whether one of you will stand as her champion. All four of you say no. She asks twice more in the days that follow, and gets the same answer.

### 23 September — Thaddeus tells the truth

He admits the story about his body was a lie, and tells Virgil the real one — and then about **Underwood**, a village a day outside Haleyon that has been turning green for two years while the Church refuses aid, citing a war in the north.

He asks Virgil to come and help, and offers to restore his standing with the Order. Virgil says no.

### 24 September 1126 — The swine raid

Porcine beastmen on the road. Oil, and fire, and a fight that goes better than it should have — except that three of them break the line and beat Marion nearly to death.

The wagon is Willowbread's. He dies telling Thaddeus where the pumpkins grow and warning him of a **coven of witches** tending them.

Virgil finds the magic is not in the fruit. It is all in the seeds: Enchantment, Necromancy and Transmutation braided together.

*"This isn't a disease. Someone built this."*

Thaddeus and Marion turn back toward Underwood.

### 25 September – 7 October — Two quiet weeks

The first stretch of nothing since the brand.

### 8 October 1126 — The last fire on the road

Two days from Solteres. Ysabel asks a fifth time for a champion, and tells you what she is up against: Ironwake's champion, **the Juggernaut**, three times Wolfram's size, in the coliseum on a festival day, as the last act after the acrobats. Then she tells you how she got here — the smuggling, Ironwake's tax, the mill, the dress house, the second cheat, and her niece.

The answer is still no. The plan is to see her to the gates of Solteres and report to the Church there.

### 9 October 1126, dawn — Erwana

One of Ysabel's men drags a starving woman out of last night's leavings on a rope. Ysabel calls her *it*. JonLief kneels down and asks where she came from.

**Erwana**, half-Tabaxi, escaped from a slaver holdfast called **Bramblefen**. She carries a book an old clerk named **Old Cobb** gave her the night she ran. JonLief opens it and finds two names, two months old: **Elizabeth. Rowan.** She knows his pendant. The woman wore one. She was very sick.

Every woman at Bramblefen was sold in one lot to hooded men **covered in scars.**

Over breakfast Ysabel explains, reasonably, that **Dov is hers for three more years.**

You give Erwana food and five silver and send her to **Ald's Star**: *tell Ald that Wolfram sent you.* Ysabel offers to walk you in through the front gate as her staff, for the price of the book once everyone is clear. You take the deal.

### 9 October 1126, evening — Bramblefen

**5:30.** A walled valley in the fen. Not four or five guards, as you were told — **eight in the open**, and slaves building a ring of sharpened stakes. **The Juggernaut is here** for a match at sundown, against a gnome.

Inside, a building called the Deeps that feels like St. Pyotr's, only weaker. A slave in the yard who knows Wolfram's face: **Anselm Vogt**, who served Wolfram's father, went to the Bishop after Wolfram's arrest, and was never written down. The slaves will rise during the fight. They need the armory.

Vaskar receives Ysabel over a man under a tarp, breathing. He sold every woman he had to men in dirty robes with scarred skin, and charged them a premium.

From an elf in a cell and a guard who liked Luthien's company, you learn the rest. The woman stopped caring about anything, even the boy. Then **green patches** came up on her skin, and Vaskar had her taken out past the wall and **burned**. *"Don't want the Green here."* The boy was kept alone in the tower for a week, and then sold.

Behind a door in the Deeps, an enormous man plays with a straw doll, and a dwarf shouts at Luthien to leave him alone.

Outside the north wall, in a scorched circle, the only green thing in the valley is growing. Virgil digs to its roots and finds **Elizabeth's bones.**

**At sundown Vaskar throws Old Cobb's body into the yard.** In the ring, they take the mask off the gnome. On his shoulder is **the Mark of Atonement.**

### 9 October 1126, night — The fen falls

The Juggernaut will not kill the gnome. He throws a section of the fence into Ironwake's own men instead. **Gimble Beren** goes through the hole. Wolfram puts his shoulder through the armory door. The dwarf tells the giant to kill the guards.

**Nineteen of twenty guards die.** Gimble opens every cell in the small prison.

**Vaskar takes Ysabel hostage**, and trades her and the hiding place of his own ledger for his life. Some of you want to kill him anyway. **Wolfram will not let anyone break their word.** Vaskar walks out the gate, saying Ironwake will probably kill him for this. Luthien takes his money; Wolfram gives most of it to the freed.

In the Deeps: **a barrel of the same blood** you saw on Dol and at St. Pyotr's.

Luthien plays for the freed, better than he has ever played, and at the edge of his sight there is **a glowing woman with too many arms.** Four. Then six. When the song ends, so does she.

**Thirty people walk free.** They cannot go to Solteres. You send them to **Ald's Star**, on Wolfram's name. The giant and the dwarf go north. Ysabel gets Old Cobb's book, as promised. **You keep Vaskar's — it names the man who bought Rowan.**

Then the gate comes in under a hammer, and two men in violet cloaks walk into the yard. **Baltus Thole, a Shepherd.** You tell him everything. He is *pleased*. He tells you something no one in the Church ever has: **a Penitent may act beyond his orders when the chance comes to serve the will of the Emperor.**

**Virgil leaves for Underwood and Master Thaddeus**, to find a cure for the Green, and Baltus approves. Baltus leaves his apprentice with you.

### 10 October 1126 — Now

Solteres, two days off, with Lady Ysabel and her household.

Vozruk still has not heard from you. But the Church has found you at last, and **for the first time, it has told you that you are allowed.**

You are carrying a book with a name in it that none of you has read yet.
