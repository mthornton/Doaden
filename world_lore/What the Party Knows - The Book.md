# What the Party Knows — *Myths of the Old Gods*
### ⚠️ THE BOOK HAS BEEN READ. Stop writing "nobody has opened it."

> **JonLief read it on the road in the weeks before Bramblefen (late September 1126) and told the party what was in it.** Everything on this page is **player knowledge**, in character, available to be quoted back at you at the table.
>
> Source: `Myths and Legends of the Old Gods.docx` by **Abbot Valerius Thorne** — a Church volume, written for "the Watchers of the Church," which is itself worth noticing *(see §5)*.

---

## 1. The frame

- The **Old Gods** were real, physical, and ran the world before the **Ascension of the God Emperor.**
- They live on **mana**, generated by worship, belief, and ritual. **Starve them and they fade.** That is exactly what the Ascension did — the Great Secularization tore down the temples and the **Edict of Silence** forbade saying the names, and the pantheon went quiet.
- Modern doctrine calls them folklore. **The book, written by an Abbot, says plainly that they are not.**

> **The party now holds a Church document that contradicts the Church's public position.** They may not have registered that yet. It is true.

---

## 2. The four that matter, and what the party can now say out loud

### 🍖 Adephagia — *The Great Consumer*
- **She does not need worship.** She feeds on gluttony, greed and lust themselves, which is why she survived when the others starved. **The most dangerous thing still active.**
- Appears as she pleases — usually a beautiful, heavy-set woman, an image of abundance. Presence smells of **roasting meat, heavy perfume, expensive wine.**
- Her cults are **not temples.** They are gourmand clubs and merchant syndicates, and solitary pacts made by people already in power. **Her followers hollow out their own communities to feed her.**

> **The party can now identify one of her cells by its social shape rather than by robes**, and that is a real, usable tool. It also means every rich man in Solteres is a candidate.

### 🎨 Saraswati — *the Gentle Mother of the Muse*
- Kindest of the pantheon, and **naive** — repeatedly outmanoeuvred by crueller gods, protected only by the size of her mana.
- **Six arms.** Radiant, in yellow silks, holding the tools of trades — a lute, a chisel, a brush.
- Her power came from **creation itself**, not sacrifice, which made her the patron of peasant and architect alike.
- **Vasant Panchami** — the fifth day of spring — is her one surviving ritual: communal art-making on a mass scale, which floods her with mana. Still celebrated in remote places as "cultural heritage."

> ### ⚠️ Three things this hands the party, and one it hands you
>
> - **The four-armed woman in the white gown** they saw is now identifiable. *(Six in the iconography — that discrepancy is a gift, not an error. Let a player notice.)*
> - **"Her power comes from art"** puts them one step from Luthien's bone guitar. *(See `Saraswati and Adephagia.md`. Do not confirm. Let it itch.)*
> - **They know why a cult would harvest her specifically**: her mana is the largest untended reservoir left in the world, and she is the only god weak enough to catch and strong enough to be worth catching.
> - 📅 **VASANT PANCHAMI IS A CLOCK.** It is early October. The fifth day of spring is roughly **five months out.** If Saraswati is still alive and chained on that day, something happens — either she is replenished, or the cult is standing over her with a tap when it does. **Decide which, because a player who read that section will ask.**

### ⛓️ Brudda Death — *and this is the big one*
- **Not an Old God.** An extra-planar entity, the **jailer** of the pantheon — the Hand of Sentence, ruler of the **Prison Realm**, which exists purely for torture and permanent incarceration. Almost nobody has ever come back out.
- **Physiology:** massive, an oversized maw that swallows a man whole, and **his stomach has been torn out and replaced with iron bars.** The swallowed are not killed — they are **kept**, pressed against the bars, visible from outside, suffering indefinitely.
- **He cannot exist naturally in the mortal world.** He can only be partly pulled through as an **Aspect**, and even then only briefly before reality drags him back.

> #### 🔑 The summoning requirements — the most operationally useful thing in the whole book
>
> **Summoning an Aspect needs two separate things:**
>
> 1. **Massive mana** — beyond a mortal sorcerer, so: an ancient artifact, or a tapped god.
> 2. **The Catalyst of Suffering** — *"an ample amount of human suffering."* Large-scale tragedy, prolonged torture, or **the concentrated despair of a condemned population.**
>
> **The party can now assemble this themselves:**
>
> - The blood in the St. Pyotr's stone circle was requirement 1.
> - **The tortured prisoner in the cell downstairs was requirement 2** — and they walked past dozens of smaller horrors in that building without knowing they were reading a shopping list.
> - **⚠️ And it tells them what to look for next: a place where large-scale human suffering is being manufactured and held.**
>
> **Which is a description of a slave compound with a fighting pit in it.**
>
> **Handle this deliberately.** Bramblefen almost certainly is *not* a cult site — but a player who read that chapter may well decide it is, and the wrongness of that suspicion is far more interesting than being right. *(And if you ever want it to be true, the door is standing open and you did nothing to open it.)*

### 🐺 The rest — background, but they have the names now
- **Coyote-Blue**, the Star-Thief — chaos, adaptation, the edge of civilisation. Spilled Saraswati's bag of Light-Seeds across the void and made the stars by accident.
- **Jormungand** — bound at the Ascension. *(Rumoured to have a Black Scroll in his stomach. See below.)*
- **Shamash**, the Radiant Eye — sun, justice, absolute truth. Nothing could be hidden from him.
- **Thoth-Anubis**, the Silent Scrivener — records, the dead. **Gains a pulse of mana whenever a hidden truth is uncovered or a lost grave is identified.** *(That is a live mechanism and this party opens graves for a living.)*

## 3. 📜 The Black Scrolls — the political bomb

The party has now read Book Two, and it is arguably more dangerous to them than the gods are.

- **Divine ledgers** written by Thoth-Anubis in "the Jackal's Gall," distilled from the memories of the dead. Stygian black, cool to the touch, cedar and old earth. **The ink shifts and shows the reader "the truth that is feared" rather than the truth that is sought.**
- They were the **evidence of record** in Shamash's judgements: once written, a fact of reality that cannot be argued.
- 🔑 **They are the key to the Prison Realm.** Without a scroll detailing the sentence, **Brudda Death will not open the cage to receive a soul.**
- **The God Emperor purged them** — the Night of Ash, forty days of burning — because *"a perfect record was intolerable"* to a man rewriting history around himself. The **Golden Chronicles** were commissioned to replace them.
- Fragments still surface. A genuine scrap can **un-write a secret** — delete a crime or a memory from the world. Most on the market are dyed goat skin, used by Adephagia's cults to sell fake immunity.
- A **"Primal Copy"** containing the God Emperor's own secret history is rumoured to have been smuggled out — hidden somewhere he cannot reach. **Inside Jormungand.**

> **Two things fall out of this that the party can act on:**
>
> 1. **The Church's founding history is contested in the Church's own literature**, and there is allegedly a document that proves it. That is the endgame lever on Vozruk, on the Office, and on the brand itself.
> 2. **Thematically it rhymes with everything they are already carrying.** Old Cobb kept a true record because the men in power preferred it forgotten. The God Emperor burned the true record for the same reason. **Nobody needs to say this at the table. Let it sit.**

## 4. The warning at the front

Valerius Thorne opens the book by telling the reader to stop reading:

> *"If you find your heart beating faster as you read of Saraswati's spring-songs, or if you feel a sudden, cold weight in your gut at the mention of Brudda Death, close this book at once. **To know the Old Gods is to be noticed by them.** And to be noticed by a starving thing is to become its next meal."*
>
> *"Mana is a persistent parasite; it does not need a temple to thrive, only a soul with a void in its center."*

**JonLief read that aloud and then kept reading.** The whole party heard it.

> **You never have to make this true.** But it is on the table now, in their own words, and **the cheapest possible payoff is Luthien** — who is carrying one of Saraswati's art-items and has been getting better with it faster than practice explains.

## 5. Housekeeping

- **It is a Church book.** Written by an Abbot, for the Watchers, and it contradicts the doctrine the Church teaches publicly. **Carrying it into a Church office is not neutral.** Worth remembering when the party finally reports in — and worth remembering when a Shepherd searches their packs.
- **Corbin Wainwright's scene changes.** `Campaign Arc Plan.md` had him reading the book *for* them in Chapter 9. He can't — they've read it. **What he can do is better:** explain what mana actually is as an economy, tell them what the book leaves out, and be visibly unsettled that four Penitents are carrying a Watcher's manual.
- **Naming drift:** the book says "Church of Azaria" / "Church of the God Emperor." The campaign uses **Church of Azerai.** Treat as the same institution; fix on any reprint.

4. **Where is the book physically right now?** It has been in JonLief's pack since St. Pyotr's. **It is going into Bramblefen with them.** Losable, burnable, and very bad to be caught holding.
