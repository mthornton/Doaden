# The Blood of Saraswati
### The barrels — where they came from, what the party knows, and where they go next

> **The single most important unaccounted-for thing in the campaign.** The party has seen this blood twice, in two places, two chapters apart, and has correctly worked out what it is for. What they have not worked out is **how much of it is still moving.**
>
> See `Saraswati and Adephagia.md`, `The First Penitents.md`, `Campaign Arc Plan.md` §5, and `The Second Ledger.md`.

---

## 1. What it is

**Saraswati is chained in the catacombs under Dol Island, alive, with a hole cut in her side.** Adephagia's cult has been bleeding her — not killing her, *harvesting* her — and casking it.

A god's blood is a power source. *Rise of the Jailer* is explicit that the cult needs an energy source of sufficient magnitude to give **Brudda Death** physical form in Doaden, and this is it. Everything else in the cosmic arc is logistics around this one fact.

**Volume matters.** They have been at this for a long time and the operation is industrial: a crew, a schedule, casks, boats, a ship. This is not a ritual component measured in vials. It is freight.

---

## 2. What the party has actually seen

| When | Where | What they saw |
|---|---|---|
| **Ch 2** — 14 Aug 1126 | **Dol Island, second beach** | Four robed men loading barrels into a boat, two still on the sand, a ship waiting offshore. A cultist ordering them finished and away. They took the beach and let the ship go. |
| **Ch 2** | **The crypt below** | Saraswati chained, opened, and being drained. |
| **Ch 2** | **The first beach** | Bloodstains in the sand they never investigated. *(Those are the first Penitents' — see below.)* |
| **Ch 4** — 18 Aug | **St. Pyotr's, the chapel** | A stone circle three feet across, three feet off the floor, **filled with blood**, pouring the black fog into the room. **And a couple of barrels standing in the same room.** |
| **Ch 4** | Same room | A cultist naming **Saraswati**, **Adephagia**, and **Brudda Death** out loud, in one speech. |

### ✅ What the party has correctly concluded

- The blood is Saraswati's.
- It is being shipped by sea in barrels.
- It is the fuel for a ritual to bring **Brudda Death** through.
- They stopped that particular ritual by killing everyone in that particular chapel.

### ❌ What they have not realised

**That there were never only a couple of barrels.**

They saw two on a beach, a boat's worth going aboard a ship, and two in a chapel — and every one of those was a fragment of a supply chain that has been running for a long time. **The rest of it is out there, in casks, in warehouses, on carts, in cellars, and it is all still good.**

> **This is the single best "oh no" available to you in the next twenty sessions, and it costs one prop to deliver.** See §4.

---

## 3. Why nobody in Solteres has noticed

**Because the cult does not move it themselves.**

A caravan of robed men hauling sealed casks across a duchy draws exactly the attention they cannot afford. So they do the sensible thing: **they move it as contraband, through the contraband channels that already exist**, and they pay the tariff like everybody else.

**Lord Leofard Ironwake controls all illicit transport in and out of Solteres and taxes every shipment.** *(See `Campaign Arc Plan.md` §5 — the barrels are in his tax records: weight, date, destination, and the name the buyer gave.)*

### ⚠️ Handling the obvious objection

**"Why would a cult tell a crime lord they have a god's blood?"** They wouldn't, and they didn't.

- **Ironwake's business is not asking.** His men are paid for tonnage and route. A manifest says wine, oil, or tallow, and the tariff is assessed on weight, and nobody lifts a lid, because lifting lids is how a smuggling operation loses customers.
- **The cult pays well and ships boring.** Regular consignments, standard casks, unremarkable destinations, a buyer name that means nothing. **Being ordinary is the whole disguise.**
- **The safest place to hide freight is inside a system whose entire premise is not looking at freight.**

### 🔥 But Ironwake is not stupid, and this is the lead

He does not know what it is. **He knows something is wrong with it**, and he has known for a while, and he has decided very deliberately not to find out:

- Casks that come in heavier than the manifest says they should.
- A standing instruction, paid for at a premium, that these particular consignments are **not to be opened, not to be transferred, and not to be left overnight in a warm building.**
- One dockhand who opened one anyway, four or five years ago. Ironwake will not say what happened to him. **He remembers the man's name and he will not say it.**

> **So Ironwake has a fear-shaped hole where knowledge would be, and it is exactly the right kind of lead:** he has the *records* — dates, weights, destinations, the buyer name — and he has the *unease*, and he has never put the two together because putting them together is not profitable.
>
> **He also does not need to betray his client to be useful.** He can hand the party a ledger page and honestly say he has no idea what is in the barrels. Every word of that is true, and it blows the arc wide open.
>
> **And it makes him a better villain** — a man who has been paid, for years, to be the reason a god's blood crosses a continent, and has stopped sleeping well about it. *(Arc Plan §5 already says this. This file is why it is true.)*

## 4. 🎯 PLANT ONE IN BRAMBLEFEN

**Do this in Session 2. It is one prop, it takes ninety seconds, and it restarts the cosmic plot in a chapter that currently has none.**

*(See `Campaign Arc Plan.md` — last real Brudda Death content was Chapter 4, and Chapters 5, 6 and 7 have had none. This fixes that without changing a single thing about Chapter 7's plot.)*

**Where.** Vaskar's warehouse or the goods yard, among legitimate freight. **Not** hidden, **not** guarded, **not** special. It is stacked with the tallow and the salt fish, because to everyone at Bramblefen that is what it is.

**How they notice it.** Do not point at it. Put it where they will already be — the stores, the cover for an approach, the route out — and let the description do the work:

> *Standard cooperage, iron-hooped, sealed hard with pitch. Heavier than the ones beside it. Stencilled for a Solteres carter, with a tariff-mark burnt into the head and a buyer's name that means nothing to you.*
>
> **And nothing else at all.** No smell, no seep, no hum. The horror is that it is completely ordinary.

**If they open it.** Blood. Fresh, and it should not be, and it is faintly warm. If anyone in the party has been near Saraswati's crypt, they will know it on sight and should not need a roll.

**If they don't open it.** Perfect. **Let them leave it.** They will remember, and remembering it themselves in three sessions is worth more than being told now.

**What it gives them, in order:**

1. There is more of it. *(The realisation.)*
2. It moves by ordinary freight, with a tariff-mark on the head. *(The method.)*
3. Ironwake taxes all of that. *(The lead — and Ysabel can supply this line if nobody in the party does. It costs her nothing and it makes her useful.)*

> **Bonus, free:** Cobb's second ledger and this barrel are the **paper** and the **cargo** of the same trade. The ledger names buyers filed under *Ashgate*; the barrel carries a tariff-mark and a buyer name. **They are two halves of one trail, and the party will hold both by the end of Chapter 7 without anyone telling them they match.**

## 5. 🩸 The open thread: the Penitents' blood

**Do not resolve this yet. It is written down so it stays consistent when you do.**

The cult did not only bleed the goddess. When Vozruk's first team walked into the harvest in June 1126, four of them were chained above the tide line and **bled into the same barrels**, a little at a time, over four days. *(See `The First Penitents.md` and `The Unit Before Us.md`.)*

**The stated premise:** ordinary human blood has no power. **A Penitent's does** — because the Mark of Atonement is consecrated, and the brand makes the whole person a piece of live Church magic. That is the same fact the Shepherds' leash runs on. *(See `The Office of the Lost Flock.md`.)*

### Three ways this can land — pick later

| | The reason | What it costs, what it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **A tracer.** A few drops of Penitent blood in a cask makes the cask *findable*, by roughly the mechanism a Shepherd uses. The cult tags its own freight so it never loses a barrel. | **Cheapest and best.** It hands the party a way to hunt every remaining barrel in the world — **but only with a Shepherd's help, or a Penitent's blood, or both.** It makes the party's own brands an asset for the first time in the campaign. |
| **2** | **Research.** The cult was studying the brand to learn how the Church tracks people — and has learned it. | **Nastiest.** It means someone other than the Office can feel this party at dawn, and it retroactively explains any pursuit you ever want. Use with care: it partly duplicates the Shepherds. |
| **3** | **Nothing.** The blood has no power. They had the barrels, the chains, and the knives already out, and five people fell into their hands, so that is how they did it. | **Bleakest and entirely defensible.** It costs the plot nothing and it makes the cult worse. **If you never pull this thread, this is what it was.** |

> **Recommendation: keep all three live until Chapter 9 or 10.** Nothing in the campaign currently depends on the answer, and option 1 is the one that turns a horror into a tool exactly when the party needs one. **Gimble is the only living witness to any of it**, which means Dave's character is holding a piece of the finale and does not know it.

## Open decisions

1. **How much is still out there?** *Recommendation: enough that it cannot be destroyed by hand.* A number the party can count is a number they can solve; a warehouse is a problem.
2. **Does destroying a barrel hurt Saraswati?** She is alive and chained. **If the blood is still hers, burning it may be doing something to her**, and the party has already burned a chapel. *(Very good, very cruel, entirely optional.)*
3. **What buyer name is on the head of the barrel?** Make it the same one as in Cobb's ledger. *Ashgate* is right there and already means "a buyer who would not give a name."
4. **Does Ironwake ever say the dockhand's name?** *Recommendation: only once, and only if the party earns it.*
