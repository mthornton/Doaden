# Elizabeth and the Green
### The bargain she made, what it did to her, and what it bought her son

> ⚠️ **DM ONLY, AND DEEP.** The party does not know Elizabeth is dead. They think they are running a rescue.
>
> This page holds the true version. **Nobody living witnessed the bargain** — see §8 for the only ways it ever comes out.
>
> See `Underwood.md`, `Old Cobb.md`, `What the Party Knows - The Book.md`, `Chapter 7 DM Notes - Session 2 Onward.md` §5 and §5b.
>
> 🟢 **In the world it is called *the green*** — lowercase, definite article, and that is what ordinary people say. The Church has no word for it at all. See `Underwood.md`, *The name*.

---

## 1. 💀 The one-paragraph truth

**Elizabeth sold herself to Brudda Death to save her son, and the price was that she stopped being able to love him.**

She was tortured by cultists. He came to her that night, unconscious and bleeding, and offered a trade: no more torture for her, and **no torture ever, from any servant of his, for Rowan** — in exchange for accepting a gift of infection. She hesitated, and asked the only question that mattered: *would it kill the boy?* He agreed to make Rowan immune. She would die of it eventually; Rowan would live, free of the cultists and of the pandemic that is coming.

**She said yes.**

He put it in her directly. Because it came from him and not from a seed, it is a **more potent strain that never shows on the skin and still spreads** — and its only symptom is **apathy.**

> ### ⚠️ Build the whole thread around this
>
> The thing everyone at Bramblefen saw — **a dying mother who stopped caring about her child** — is the *only visible symptom of the price she paid for him.*
>
> **Cobb is going to tell JonLief that his wife stopped looking at their son.** It will be true, and it will be exactly backwards, and neither of them will ever know that unless you decide otherwise.

## 2. The night

**Where.** On the road, in her long aimless flight around Doaden staying ahead of the warriors the clan leaders sent after her. **In the region of Haleyon.** *(Which is also Underwood's region — a day's ride. That is not a coincidence, see §6.)*

**What happened.** She and Rowan were taken by **cultists**. It was a brutal night of torture for her.

**Rowan was not touched.** They had other plans for him. *(⚠️ Decide what those were — see Open Decisions. Whatever they were, the bargain cancelled them.)*

When they had finished with her for the night she was left bloodied and unconscious, and it was in that state — not asleep, not awake — that **Brudda Death came to her in a nightmare.**

### How to run it if it is ever seen

He does not threaten and he does not gloat. **He offers, plainly, and he keeps every word of it.**

- She hesitated. **She did not leap at it.** Her hesitation was not fear for herself, it was one question: *does this kill my son?*
- **He answered her honestly, and then improved the offer**, because a mother who agrees is worth more to him than a mother who is broken into agreeing.
- Terrified and worn down past argument, **she accepted.**

> **She was not tricked.** She understood the terms, she negotiated one of them, and she got what she was promised. **That is what makes it unbearable** — it was the best deal available and she took it, and it worked.

## 3. Two strains

| | **Wild strain** — Underwood, the pumpkins | **Elizabeth's strain** — direct from Brudda Death |
|---|---|---|
| **Carried by** | **Seeds** *(party confirmed this in Ch6)* — and by **spores from burst pods**, which the party has **not** worked out | Her. Breath, touch, proximity — nobody knows |
| **Visible signs** | Patches → nodules → pods, over about two years | **None. None at all.** |
| **Apathy** | Arrives late, once the pods are thick | **The only symptom, from the beginning** |
| **Spread rate** | Slow, local, one village | Carried everywhere she went for months — **but see §4. It only ever took where the ground was already thin.** |
| **The end** | The body is consumed; they walk to a field, lie down in the dirt, and become part of the land | The strain finishes with her: **the green erupts in her final week and kills her fast** |

> ### 🔑 The seed / spore blind spot — this is the party's next step and they are one sentence from it
>
> **What they know (Ch6, Virgil, at the table):** the pumpkin flesh is inert. **All of it is in the seeds** — Enchantment, Necromancy and Transmutation braided together, which is not how a blight behaves. *"It does not organize itself by school, like a scholar's notes."*
>
> **What they have not put together:** Underwood's pods **burst and throw spores.** *(Thaddeus told them. It is in `Underwood.md` and in the Chronicle.)*
>
> **A spore is a seed.** Same job, same vector, and the entire village has been standing in clouds of them for a year.
>
> **This is not a retcon and it does not need one.** It just needs one player to say it out loud, and it should be a player — put Thaddeus's description and Virgil's finding in the same conversation and step back.

### Why Elizabeth's version showed nothing

Because it did not need to. **The visible half of the wild disease is its reproductive machinery** — patches, nodules, pods, spores. She was not a plant trying to make more plants. **She was a delivery system**, and a delivery system with green nodules on its throat gets isolated in a week.

**She showed nothing for months. That is what made her a super carrier**, and it is not an accident. It is a design.

**But a carrier is not an epidemic.** What she was carrying could only take hold on ground the rift had already reached — **see §4, which is the rule that keeps this thread from eating the campaign.**

## 4. 🔒 THE CONTAINMENT RULE — this is the fix, and it is load-bearing

**The infection only takes root where the rift has already thinned the barrier. And where it takes, it lies dormant until the rift opens.**

Everything else follows from that one sentence.

| | |
|---|---|
| **On ordinary ground** | Nothing happens. She breathed on wagon drivers, slept in camps, shared straw in pens — **and none of it took.** The seed lands on ground that will not hold it. |
| **On thin ground** | It takes, silently, and then **does nothing at all.** No patches, no fever, no symptoms. It waits. |
| **When the rift opens** | Everything that took, wakes. **All of it. At once.** |

### What this buys you

- **There is no clock running right now.** Nothing she touched between Haleyon and Bramblefen is going to bloom next month. The fen is not thin ground; **Bramblefen is fine**, and it will stay fine.
- **It retires the awkward question instead of opening it.** If a player asks who else was in those pens two months ago, the party can go and look, and find them *well*. **That answer ends the thread** rather than starting a side campaign.
- **The disease becomes an instrument of the main plot instead of a rival to it.** Outbreaks are not a public-health problem. **They are a map.** Where the green grows, the barrier is thin — which is exactly what `Campaign Arc Plan.md` §3.5 already says: *Underwood's plague is a progressive rift, and it is your first visible rift sign.*
- **It explains Underwood without inventing anything.** There is a rift near Haleyon. **That is where the cultists took Elizabeth**, and a day's ride away is a village that has been turning green for two years. The geography closes on itself.
- **And the pandemic becomes the stake rather than the subplot.** It is not something to manage. **It is what happens if they fail.**

> ### ⚠️ How to say this at the table
>
> **Do not explain the rule.** Let them observe it: they check on someone Elizabeth was held with, and that person is fine, and the party quietly relaxes — and then, chapters later, somebody notices that every place the green *does* grow is a place where something else is also wrong.
>
> **Virgil is the one who will formalise it.** See §10.

## 5. 🌱 Rowan

**He is genuinely, permanently immune.** Not a carrier — **resistant**, because his mother bought it for him by name.

**Also true, and worth remembering when the cult reappears:** he cannot be tortured by **any servant of Brudda Death.** Not restrained from, not warded against — **cannot.** It is a term of a bargain the entity keeps.

**Note the shape of that protection carefully:**

| Protected from | NOT protected from |
|---|---|
| The disease, permanently | Ordinary harm, hunger, or a bad master |
| Torture by any servant of Brudda Death | Vaskar, slavers, anyone not in that service |
| Being made a Catalyst of Suffering *(see below)* | Being sold, worked, or killed outright |

> ### 🔑 Which quietly makes him useless to the cult
>
> Summoning an Aspect of Brudda Death requires **the Catalyst of Suffering** *(`What the Party Knows - The Book.md` — the party has read this)*. **A child who cannot be made to suffer by any of his servants is worth nothing to that ritual.**
>
> Whatever the cultists' "other plans" for Rowan were, **his mother ended them in one sentence, in a dream, while unconscious.**

**What Rowan knows:** nothing. He was small, he was untouched, and his mother went cold on him over a period of months and then was taken out of a cell and did not come back. **From where he stands, she stopped loving him and then left.**

## 6. ⚡ The rift is the cause

**The corruption in the plants and the disease are the same thing, and both come from the growing rift.**

This is the answer to two questions the party has already written down *(`Open Questions.md`)*: *what is doing this*, and *designed by whom.*

**The answer is: nobody designed it, and it is designed anyway.** It is what happens when the Prison Realm bleeds into biology. Reality is leaking, and what leaks through carries its author's grammar.

> ### 🔎 The signature, for whoever works it out — and it should be Virgil
>
> **Brudda Death's prisoners are kept alive, behind iron bars, inside his stomach.**
>
> **A pumpkin seed takes root in a person's stomach**, cannot be removed, grows a cage of itself through them, takes their will with a narcotic, makes them defend it — and then walks them out to a field to lie down in the dirt.
>
> **It is the same act.** It imprisons you inside your own body and puts you in the ground alive. *(`Chapter 6 Swine Raid.md` and the Ch4 chapel are the two halves of this and they are two chapters apart.)*
>
> Virgil already said the seed is *"three, braided — they have to be made to."* **He was right about everything except who made it.**

**And the rift is why Brudda Death could reach a woman in a nightmare at all.** He cannot exist here naturally. He is not supposed to be able to touch anything. **He touched her.** That should frighten the party more than the disease does.

> **Father Ignatius spent years at St. Pyotr's deliberately thinning the barrier**, and wrote that he could feel it going. **He was not raving.** *(Whether he caused the rift, widened it, or simply felt it, is `Open Questions.md` — and it is worth deciding, because Underwood started turning green about two years ago.)*

## 8. How the truth could ever come out

**Nobody living saw it.** These are the only doors:

1. 📜 **A Black Scroll.** *(Strongly recommended — it is already planted.)* The scrolls are divine ledgers of the absolute unedited truth of every soul and event, they are the instrument that makes Brudda Death honour a sentence, and **the party read about them a fortnight ago.** A bargain with him is exactly the kind of thing written in one. **This is the mechanism. Use it.**
2. **Rowan.** He was there, awake, untouched, in the room. He remembers fragments and does not understand any of them.
3. **The cultists.** Somebody in that camp watched a prisoner they had been told to break get taken off their hands. They will not know why.
4. **Brudda Death.** He has no reason to lie about it and might well say it to hurt someone.

> **Where this lands:** JonLief spends the rest of the campaign believing his wife abandoned their son at the end. **The scroll is how he finds out she bought him.** Hold that until it costs something to get.

## 10. 🍄 VIRGIL'S PATH — the cure, and what it costs

**Rowan is not just a rescued child. He is the only immune specimen in the world, and Virgil is the only person who could do anything with him.**

This is an **alternate victory condition**, and it is a real one.

### Why it works even though the immunity is divine

Brudda Death is not a metaphor. **He had to implement that promise in a living body** — and whatever he did to a small boy's blood to make it refuse the infection is *written down*, in the boy, in a language that is fundamentally biological.

**A god wrote it. Writing can be read.** That is the whole justification and it is enough.

### The tiers

| | What it is | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic** | A test that says whether ground is thin, or a person has taken it. **Turns Virgil into the party's rift-detector.** | Achievable in Act II |
| **Vaccine** | Prophylaxis. Protects the uninfected. | Act III, with a lab, time, and Rowan |
| **Cure** | Reverses a dormant infection. **Underwood is not saved by a vaccine** — they already have it. | Very hard. Recommend leaving it *possible and unfinished* |

> ### 🔑 Why this is a genuine second way to win
>
> Under §4, **the pandemic is Brudda Death's doomsday weapon and it only fires when the rift opens.** Every person the green has quietly taken is ordnance he has already placed.
>
> **A vaccine defuses it before it is fired.** Which means the party could lose the fight at the Hecatomb and still save the world — or win it and find the ground was already safe because Virgil got there first.
>
> **Say that out loud to Virgil's player at some point.** A character who has been told since Chapter 1 that his life's work is blasphemy should get to find out it was the answer.

### What it costs, and this is the good part

**It costs Rowan.**

Not his life. **Repeated access to a traumatised child** — blood, tissue, time, and a great many hours of a boy sitting still for a man who is not gentle about it and does not really see him.

> ⚠️ **And JonLief crossed a continent for that boy.**
>
> **This is the best PC-versus-PC pressure available in the campaign**, and neither man is wrong. Virgil can save thousands of people. JonLief has one son and has already lost the boy's mother to something that used her as material.
>
> **Do not resolve it for them.** Put Rowan in a room, put both of them in it, and let them negotiate.

### And what it means for Virgil

- **He was right.** He has believed since Chapter 1 that fungal symbiosis is humanity's next stage, and the Church called it blasphemy and gave him five hundred years for it. **A vaccine here is giving people a controlled version of the thing** — which is, precisely, his thesis, working.
- **It closes the Alchemical Order loop.** Thaddeus dangled earning back his standing and Virgil turned him down to stay with the party *(Ch6)*. **This is that offer coming back on his terms.**
- ⚠️ **And it is the one thing that could plausibly buy a discharge.** Five hundred years is not shortened by good conduct. It might be shortened by handing the Church the end of a plague. **Baltus Thole has watched exactly one person walk through that door in twenty-two years** *(`Baltus Thole.md`)*. **Virgil could be the second**, and that would mean something to a man in a purple coat covered in silver lambs.
