# Denrick

**Type:** NPC — enslaved overseer / "trusty" at **Bramblefen**, currently locked in a cell in the Pens; a treacherous informant the party will be tempted to trust.
**Function:** the poisoned mirror of [[Anselm Vogt]]. Same collar, same pens, opposite answer. He trapped the drain the party is probably about to crawl through, and he will offer to sell them things he has already sold to somebody else.

> Reskin of "Grolo" (*Prison Breakdown*, the module Bramblefen is built from). Current session file is `sessions/Chapter 7 DM Notes.md`; the old `Chapter 7: The Chained Coast` file was deleted and replaced by it.

## The Small Betrayer

Denrick is what most people become in a place like Bramblefen, if they lack the intelligence to become a Vaskar and the heart to become a [[Durgan Half-Ear]]. He is a slave still — collared, owned, disposable — but he bought himself an inch of comfort the only way the pens sell it: with other people. He points out the ones planning to run. He names the weak, the sick, the ones hiding food. In return he gets a dry corner, a fuller bowl, and the overseer's small, poisonous privilege of standing one rung above the wretches he informs on. He tells himself everyone would do the same. He is genuinely puzzled by those who don't.

He is not brave and he is not clever — not really. He is *quick*: quick to read who holds power in a room, quick to grovel, quick to threaten someone weaker, quick to find the angle. He has survived a long time on quickness. He mistakes it for wisdom.

## Where he is, and why he's in a cage

Four weeks ago **Erwana** walked out of Bramblefen and nobody could work out how. Vaskar (see [[Vaskar the Brand]]) does not accept "nobody could work out how." He put every trusty in the Pens pending an answer, Denrick among them — which is how a man with a dry corner ended up back on the stone.

Denrick bought himself most of the way out by being the one who *found* the answer: he retraced her, worked out the drainage channel, and sold Vaskar the location. That was worth enough to keep him breathing. It was not worth enough to get him out of the cell, because Vaskar's actual question — *who gave her the ledger* — is still unanswered, and Vaskar keeps everything that might one day be useful. So Denrick sits in the Pens with a grievance, an unspent bargaining chip, and nothing to do but wait for someone new to walk in.

**That someone is the party.** Play him coming to the bars fast, talking before they've decided whether to listen. [[Anselm Vogt]] is at the back of the same building and says nothing at all.

## The Drain — the trap he laid

Once he had the route, Denrick hedged the way he always does: he seeded the run with traps and stashed a little loot in a niche off the channel, betting that sooner or later *someone* desperate would try it and die there, and dead men's purses are the overseer's tip.

**The route (correct version — supersedes older notes):** a smuggler's drainage channel from the fen outside, under the wall, surfacing at a grate at the **back of the Pens.** It bypasses the yard entirely and comes up *inside* JonLief's scene. (Older text had it exiting behind the Chainmaster's Hall — that was the source module's layout, not Bramblefen's.)

**The hazards, in order:**

| # | Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spiked sump** | Dex **DC 15** | 2d6 piercing; lead crawler stuck (Str DC 12 to pull free). Noise if they fail twice. |
| 2 | **Tar barrel** on a trip cord, on the incline | Dex **DC 15**, half on **10** | 2d6 bludgeoning, prone / pushed back 10 ft. The reek marks them — disadvantage on Stealth until they wash. |
| 3 | **Rat swarm** (CR 1/4) | — | **Only triggers if the party is carrying Denrick's "lucky" sack.** No sack, no swarm — let them feel clever. |

**The cruel bit:** **Erwana will swear the drain is safe, and she is telling the truth.** She went through it in the dark, half-starved, four weeks ago — before Denrick found it. Her information isn't a lie, it's *stale*, and the party has no way to know that until the sump. If Erwana leads and eats the spiked sump, let her; her being wrong and knowing it is worth more than the 2d6.

**Denrick's stash:** ~500 gp in a purse tucked into a side niche partway along the channel. Bait for the dead. A party that survives the route, searches properly, or wrings the location out of him earns it — and it is the first real coin this party has had.

## What he actually sells them

He can't sell the way *in* — Erwana already gave them that for free, and he's on the wrong side of the wall to broker it. So he sells three other things, and every one of them is real enough to be dangerous:

1. **The way out.** *"Eighteen men through the front gate? Friend. Friend. There's a channel."* He'll route them and their freed slaves back down the drain he trapped — and if the traps have already been sprung, he'll take credit for the ones they survived. He'll also press the lucky sack on them "for the dark."
2. **The revolt.** He knows something is coming and he knows Anselm is the hand on it. He'll offer to name the ringleader — to the party, if they're buying, and to the overseers the moment they aren't. **If Denrick is left loose and unwatched after the party talks to Anselm, Vaskar knows within the hour.**
3. **Where the old clerk is.** True: Cobb is tied to a chair in the Chainmaster's Hall. What he leaves out is the guard on the door and that Vaskar is *in the room*. Useful information, shaped to get them killed at no cost to himself.

**Cracking him: Insight DC 13.** He lies smoothly about big things and fumbles the small ones — he'll swear the channel's clean while not quite meeting an eye, or oversell how much he "hates the masters, truly." Cross-checking with **Erwana** (who was there) or **Anselm** (who read him inside a week) exposes him without a roll at all.

**The design, and why it matters:** Denrick comes first and talks immediately; Anselm is at the back of the same room and has to be *asked*. When the party reaches Anselm, tell them plainly — no Insight roll — that this man is telling the truth. Earning good information honestly, right after being handed a poisoned version of the same thing, is the whole point of both these characters.

## Personality & how to run him

- **Ingratiating and twitchy.** He fawns on whoever's winning and sneers at whoever's losing, and the switch can flip mid-sentence. Lots of "friend," "of course," "anything you need."
- **A liar, but not a perfect one.** Smooth on the big lie, clumsy on the detail underneath it.
- **Faithless by policy.** Any deal he makes, he's already planning to break if a better one comes along. Trust him and he betrays you at the worst possible moment; corner him and he grovels and sells out whoever's next.
- **He never stops trading.** Bleeding, disarmed, on his knees in the mud with the compound burning — there is still an offer coming. It is the last thing about him that works.
- **The cautionary mirror.** He's the arc's third answer to "how do you survive slavery" — betray everyone, a little at a time. Set beside Durgan, Anselm and Vaskar, he's the most human and the most pitiable.

## Stat block — Denrick

*Medium humanoid (human), neutral evil — CR 1/2 (100 XP)*

- **AC** 12 (leather) · **HP** 22 (4d8 + 4) · **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 (+0) | 13 (+1) | 12 (+1) | 12 (+1) | 10 (+0) | 14 (+2) |

- **Skills** Deception +6, Persuasion +4, Insight +2, Stealth +3, Sleight of Hand +3
- **Senses** passive Perception 10
- **Languages** Common, Thieves' Cant

**Traits**

- **Silver-Tongued Traitor.** Denrick has advantage on Deception checks made while trading information or striking a bargain he means to break. **Insight DC 13** to catch him.
- **Knows the Ground.** He knows Bramblefen's layout, guard rotations, and the drainage channel intimately — but has already sold the channel to Vaskar and trapped it (see **The Drain** above). Any "safe route" he offers routes through danger he profits from.
- **Coward's Instinct.** The instant a fight turns against him or he drops below half HP, Denrick surrenders, grovels, or bolts — always with a fresh deal on his lips.

**Actions**

- **Cudgel.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +2 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 1d6 bludgeoning. He fights only when cornered, and badly.
- **Light Crossbow.** *Ranged:* +3 to hit, range 80/320. *Hit:* 1d8 + 1 piercing. Prefers to shoot from behind someone else.

**Reaction**

- **Shove the Weak.** When Denrick is targeted by an attack he can see and another creature is within 5 ft., he can shove that creature into the path of the blow; the attacker may redirect the hit to that creature. (A pettier echo of Vaskar's *Someone Else's Skin* — he learned it from the master.) Usable once per round.

## Hooks & how he plays out

- **Turnable, briefly.** Scare him badly enough, or outbid Vaskar (real freedom, real coin), and he'll flip — but only until the wind changes. Turned, he gives up true intel: the three hazards in the drain and how to disarm them, the location of his own stash, Vaskar's routine, the guard on the Hall door, and that the five in the barracks came in with the giant and aren't Bramblefen men.
- **The leak, if they leave him.** A Denrick left in an unlocked cell with a grievance and a story to sell is the fastest way for Vaskar to learn the party is inside. If the table wants a complication and hasn't earned one yet, this is where it comes from.
- **The reckoning.** When the collars come off, **the freed men will want him dead**, and they will not be wrong. He named half of them at one time or another. [[Wendel Crook]] is the moral bill for a guard who became a slave; Denrick is the harder one — a slave who stayed a slave and sold the others anyway. Make the party say it out loud, in front of eighteen men who are watching to see what kind of masters these are.
- **He should survive if he possibly can.** He is worth far more alive and loose in Solteres, turning up three sessions later with a fresh deal, than dead in a fen. The Chapter 7 threads-out list already assumes this — if the party executes him, note who swung.
- **Contrast, deliberately.** Four enslaved men, four answers: **Anselm** organizes, **Durgan** protects, **Cobb** records and finally refuses, **Denrick** sells. Get at least three of them on screen in the same session and the theme does its own work.
