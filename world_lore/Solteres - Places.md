## The city at a glance

- **Capital of the Southern Diocese**, fat on the trade of the southern farmlands. **Merchant-run**, and at the top obnoxiously rich; most people are as poor as anywhere. **Crime lords are as powerful as the top merchants**, and often you can't tell one from the other.
- **People are bought and sold in the open**, at regular auctions, despite the Church's ruling. Non-humans are **Stock**; humans serve **Terms** *(`Solteres.md`)*.
- **Layout** *(from your city map — `Solteres.md` has the link)*: a **river** through the middle, a **citadel**, a great **plaza**, a **cathedral**, and a **shantytown**. ⚠️ **The map has no city walls**, so "the gates" are really **the toll posts on the bridges and roads** — which is exactly why Ironwake can tax everything that moves.
- **For three branded non-humans, the whole city is a closed door** unless Ysabel or Cassian opens it *(Ch8 plan §3–4)*. Nobody serves them, nobody answers them, and every slaver looks twice. **The brand gets them past a lawful slaver; it doesn't get them a drink.**

## 1. The Toll Bridge
**Arrival. Ironwake's toll post. The first test of the brand.**

> **Read-aloud —** The road comes down to the river and stops at a stone bridge wide enough for two carts, with a toll house squatting at the near end like a dog at a gate. Beyond it Solteres goes on further than you can see: roofs, smoke, a cathedral spire, and the noise of more people than any of you has heard in one place since the camp. A man with a slate and a pencil is writing down everything that crosses — carts, crates, animals. Then he looks at you, and starts writing again.

**What it is.** The main road crossing. **Ironwake's tariff men** sit here, and at every other bridge, recording weight, goods and names — the same records that hold the barrels *(`The Barrel Trail.md` §8)*. Respectable travellers pay a toll; freight pays a tariff; **people pay both**, because people are freight here.

**Who's there**
- **Sergeant Wilm Corrow** *(new)* — Ironwake's toll sergeant. Bored, thorough, unbribeable in small amounts, very bribeable in large ones. Writes down *everything*, which is his whole value to Ironwake.
- **A watcher of Vaskar's** — Vaskar is watching the crossings for the party *(Ch8 §8)*. Probably **the tracker** *(Decision 7)*: a lean man on the far side, eating an apple, not looking at anyone.
- Carters, pilgrims, farmers bringing produce, a slaver's wagon with a canvas side.

**What can happen**
- **IF Ysabel vouches for them** — they're her staff; the sergeant writes "Rochefort, household, six" and waves them through. **This is the clean entry.**
- **IF anyone non-human enters without her** — the sergeant asks whose they are. **Showing the brand works** ("Church property") — and **spends Vaskar's secret** *(Ch8 §4)*: the word *Penitent* goes into the slate and into gossip.
- **IF Cassian speaks for them** — a Church apprentice is enough; the sergeant doesn't want trouble with the Office.
- **IF they look for the watcher** — Insight or Perception: the apple-eater leaves before the party is across. He's seen them. **The clock has started.**
- **Chapter 10 note:** every toll post keeps a slate, and every slate goes to the **Customs Wharf** (§16). That is where the barrel records live.

## 2. The Rented House on Lantern Row
**Where Vaskar is holding Rowan.**

> **Read-aloud —** Lantern Row is a street of narrow, tall houses that used to belong to people with money and now belong to people who rent to them. The one at the end has its shutters closed in the middle of the day. Its chimney is smoking. None of its neighbours' are.

**What it is.** A quiet rental in the wealthy quarter *(new street name)*, paid for with Ironwake's money. **Vaskar needs Rowan alive** — a dead child draws nobody anywhere *(Ch8 §2)* — so the boy is fed, locked in, and bored. **The tell is the lit chimney in a house that should be empty** — exactly what a sweep's boy notices *(`Barney Soot.md` §6)*.

**Who's there**
- **Rowan**, eight, in an upstairs room with the shutters nailed. Quiet. Does what he's told. **Will try to tell anyone who's kind to him about his mother** *(Ch8 §5 — let him be interrupted)*.
- **One or two of Vaskar's crew** on watch — Ironwake's professionals *(Ch8 §6)*. They think they're guarding a hostage for a bounty. **They don't know there's a book, or that the party are Penitents.**
- **Vaskar** — only sometimes. He prefers to be elsewhere and send messages.

**What can happen**
- **IF they come in by the front** — the guards fight, and one runs for Vaskar. **Rowan gets moved** before they reach him.
- **IF they come down the chimney** — Gimble, taught by Barney *(§3, §5)*. The flue lets out in Rowan's room. **The best version of the rescue: quiet, small, and Dave's.**
- **IF they find the house through Barney's boys** — Pip, Tam or Wick spot the smoke; Pip may overhear the guards *(podcast idea, not canon)*.
- **IF Vaskar is there when they arrive** — he doesn't fight. He talks, and names his price: the book *(Ch8 §3a, §8a)*.
- **IF they get Rowan out** — the guardianship question starts *(`What To Do With Rowan.md`)*, and Cassian's warning applies: don't take him to a Church office.

## 3. The Rooftops of the Wealthy Quarter
**The chimney route. Gimble's place.**

> **Read-aloud —** From up here the city is a sea of slate and soot, chimney pots in ranks like soldiers, gutters you can walk along if you don't look down. Smoke rises in the cold air from every third stack. Below, the streets are full and loud. Up here there's nobody but pigeons — and, three roofs over, a filthy little boy with a brush who waves.

**What it is.** The wealthy quarter's roofline: joined houses, shared parapets, and flues every sweep's boy knows by heart. **It connects Barney's shop to Lantern Row** without touching a street.

**Who's there:** Barney's boys at work *(`Barney Soot.md` §7)* — Pip, Tam, Wick.

**What can happen**
- **IF Gimble learns to climb from Barney** — ten minutes, knees and elbows. Give him **advantage on flues** for the chapter.
- **IF they cross the roofs at night** — Dex (Acrobatics) checks on wet slate; a fall is a street-level problem in front of witnesses.
- **IF they want to watch a house** — a chimney stack is a perfect blind. They can see who comes and goes on Lantern Row.

## 4. The Old Corn Exchange
**Vaskar's chosen meeting place.** *(new — swap for any neutral ground)*

> **Read-aloud —** The Old Corn Exchange has been shut since the new one opened by the river. Its great doors are chained, but the small one at the side isn't. Inside, dust lies on the trading floor in pale drifts, and the light comes down in bars through the high windows. In the middle of the floor someone has set out a table and two chairs. One of them is taken. The heavy, grey man in it doesn't get up.

**What it is.** A closed market hall in the merchants' quarter — big, empty, echoing, with galleries above and three ways out. **Vaskar chose it because he'd rather talk than fight**, and because it's hard to ambush a man who's sitting in the middle of an empty floor *(Ch8 §8a)*.

**Who's there**
- **Vaskar**, at the table. Calm. He has done this before.
- **His crew** in the galleries — two professionals and the tracker *(Ch8 §6)*. ⚠️ **Well past Deadly if it comes to a fight**, on purpose.
- **Rowan is not here.** He's leverage; Vaskar keeps leverage elsewhere.

**What can happen**
- **He opens with the book, not the bounty** *(Ch8 §3a)*. Let the clues leak in order: book before bounty; won't say why; offers to buy it with Ironwake's money at an insulting price; wants it quietly, no witnesses.
- **IF they listen** — he proposes **Option A**: the book, plus five heads from the fen *(Ch8 §8a)*.
- **IF they offer the book** — Option B; the hunt's off. ⚠️ Unless they've copied it.
- **IF they attack** — he signals the galleries and goes for a door. He doesn't die on principle *(`Vaskar the Brand.md`)*.
- **IF they've turned the tracker** *(podcast idea)* — the galleries are one man short, and Vaskar notices.

## 5. Barney Soot's Shop
**Where the trail to Rowan arrives too late.** Full NPC: `Barney Soot.md`.

> **Read-aloud —** The shop is two narrow rooms on a lane behind the wealthy quarter, and it smells like the inside of a cold fireplace. Sacks of soot are stacked to the ceiling, waiting to be sold to farmers. Brushes hang on the wall in order of size. A small, grey-faced man with soot in every line of his hands looks up from a sack, coughs, and says, "Sir."

**What it is.** A licensed sweep's shop with a cellar where the boys sleep on the sacks. **The beam over the cellar stairs** — about forty marks, eleven crosses *(`Barney Soot.md` §3)*. Don't point at it.

**Who's there**
- **Barney Soot** — stern, resigned, robbed. **Not tied to Ironwake.** He liked the boy.
- **Pip, Tam and Wick** *(`Barney Soot.md` §7)*. Pip is too big and goes out the door this month.

**What can happen — two visits** *(DM, 21 Oct 2026)*
- **By day: nobody's there.** Sweeps work daylight hours. The shop is locked, the lane is quiet, and a neighbour says *"They're all out on jobs. Back at dark."* **The party has to come back.**
- **In the evening: the full scene** — everything below — **plus Tam.** Barney is sitting on a soot sack with his cap in his hands. **Tam went up a chimney today and never came back down.** Barney doesn't know what's in that house. He's already counting: **another cross on the beam.** He won't ask the party for help. He doesn't expect anyone to help. *(→ **Side fight C**, and §22.)*
- **They're late.** Vaskar's men came, paid less than Barney paid, and took the boy *(Barney §5)*. He describes a heavy grey man who never raised his voice — **they'll know it's Vaskar** — and a lean man who studied the shop *(the tracker)*.
- **"Sprig."** Barney uses his name for the boy without thinking.
- **"Mama, look at me."** As they're leaving. As a kindness. *(Barney §4.)*
- **IF they pay Barney back what he was cheated of** — his boys go on the rooftops for them *(§3)*.
- ⭐ **IF they go after Tam first** *(Side fight C)* — and bring him home — **Barney and all his boys join the search for Rowan**, for the boy they liked and for the debt. That's the strongest version of the rooftop route.
- **IF they go down to the cellar** — describe the beam. Let someone count.
- **IF they offer to take Pip** — Ald's Star. A practice run at the Rowan question.
- **Church apprentices.** Some of Barney's human boys came from the Church *(`Barney Soot.md` §8)* — this is where "raised by the Church" leads.

## 6. Ysabel's Townhouse
**Her home. Where the triumph lands, and where she's taken from.**

> **Read-aloud —** The Rochefort house stands on a curved street of pale stone, three storeys and a walled garden, with lamps already lit in the windows at dusk. It's the kind of house that has a man whose only job is to open the door. Inside it smells of beeswax and lilies. Ysabel walks through it like a woman who had begun to believe she would never see it again.

**What it is.** Her town residence on the Crescent *(new)*. She was nearly ruined, so it's a little emptier than it should be — paintings gone from the walls, a dust sheet over a harpsichord. **It's where she plans the blackmail, and where she celebrates when it works.**

**Who's there**
- **Ysabel**, triumphant *(Ch8 §3)*. Generous with her wine. Insufferable, and earned it.
- **Her household** — Perrin, Dov, Symon, Cael, Mina *(`Ysabel's Household.md`)*.
- **Non-human house slaves** in the kitchen and stables, whom she does not mention.

**What can happen**
- **IF they stay here** — the party has a base, beds, and a door that opens to her name. It's also where Vaskar knows to find her.
- **The turn** *(Ch8 §8)* — **Ysabel disappears.** Decide whether she's taken from here (a broken garden door, Perrin beaten, Mina hiding) or on the street. **The wedding date arrives the same way: one piece of paper, two pieces of bad news** *(Decision 2)*.
- **IF they press her about slavery here** — she meets it with bewilderment, then the Church's ruling about *people*, and goes a shade colder *(`Solteres.md`)*.
- **Cobb's Ledger is here** until she's taken — then Ironwake has it, and burns it *(Ch8 §3)*.

## 7. The Rochefort Dress House
**Where Maelis works. The best house in the city "within a single season."**

> **Read-aloud —** The front of the house is all light: tall windows, gowns on wooden forms like a row of very elegant ghosts, a merchant's wife standing on a box while two girls pin her hem. Ysabel sweeps through it as though she built every stitch. Through a half-open door at the back you can see the workroom — a dozen women at benches under the windows, heads down, the only sound the hiss of cloth being cut.

**What it is.** Ysabel's second business and the source of her fortune *(`Solteres.md`, `The Art-Items.md` §5)*. **Its genius is one woman's hands, and she's a slave.**

**Who's there**
- **Maelis Vire** — elf, older than she looks, cutting silk with **bone-handled shears that look new**. Has had them about 150 years. **She knows they make her valuable**, and has let Ysabel believe the genius is the house's *(`The Art-Items.md` §5)*.
- **A dozen seamstresses**, mostly non-human, all Ysabel's property.
- **Ysabel**, showing off: *"My best girl. Best house in the city within a single season. I have an eye."*

**What can happen**
- **Session 1, in passing** *(Ch8 §10b)* — the tour after the blackmail lands. Maelis says one line if spoken to — *"Longer than this house has stood"* — and looks once at JonLief.
- **Optional tell** — a crafter notices the handles are bone and **unworn** *(`The Art-Items.md` §1a)*. Say what they see, not what it means.
- **After the Emperor's visit** *(Ch8 §10a)* — they come back knowing. **Maelis negotiates**; she wants to know what they're offering first.
- **IF the wedding goes through** — this house and Maelis pass to Ironwake. **The clock** *(`The Art-Items.md` §5)*.
- **IF they take the shears** — allowed, and ugly. **IF they free her** — right, and optimal.
- ⚠️ **Never put Corbin here.** If he hears about a dress house that got too good too fast, he gets there first.

## 8. The Rochefort Mill
**Where Ysabel's cloth is woven. Where her Stock lives.**

> **Read-aloud —** The mill sits on the river where the current is strongest, a long brick building with a waterwheel turning slow and patient at one end. Inside it is deafening: rows of looms clacking in time, lint hanging in the air like snow that won't fall. The people at the looms don't look up. Most of them aren't human. All of them have a collar mark at the throat.

**What it is.** Her first business — the weaving mill that doubled her margins *(`Solteres.md`)*, worked by **non-human slaves she bought at Bramblefen**. Ysabel thinks of them the way she thinks of the looms, and would be genuinely upset if one were beaten.

**Who's there**
- **Forty-odd Stock workers** *(new number)* — non-humans, some from Bramblefen, who might know the names of people the party freed.
- **An overseer** — human, a Terms man himself *(new)*: Ysabel likes Terms men in charge because "they understand obligation."
- **A child among the looms** — the small one who crawls inside to fix them, the job Ysabel was shopping for at Bramblefen when she asked Vaskar for "a skinny child." *(Played: Ch7 Session 2.)*

**What can happen**
- **IF the party visits** — this is what "letting Ysabel fall" sells downstream *(Ch8 §3)*: every person here passes to Ironwake with the estate.
- **IF they talk to the workers** — someone knew Erwana, or Anselm, or was at Bramblefen. A face for the abstraction.
- **IF a non-human PC walks in with Ysabel** — the workers notice the brand before anyone else does, and draw their own conclusions about which kind of property he is.

## 9. The Assize House
**The hall of records and the magistrates' court. Where the marriage becomes legal.** *(new name)*

> **Read-aloud —** The Assize House is a square stone building on the plaza with columns it can't quite afford and a queue that starts on the steps. Inside, clerks at high desks write in enormous books: sales, sentences, deeds, marriages, all of it in the same brown ink. A bell rings somewhere upstairs, and a man in a magistrate's chain crosses the hall without looking at anyone.

**What it is.** Everything in Solteres runs on a book, and this is where the books live. **Terms sentences are handed down upstairs. Marriages, sales and deeds are registered downstairs.** If Ysabel's marriage is to be lawful, it's entered here *(Ch8 §3)*.

**Who's there**
- **Magistrate Belden Roke** — signs the sentences that turn humans into Terms, and guaranteed six Ashgate drafts *(`Cobbs Ledger.md` §7)*. **Guilty twice.** A candidate to register the marriage *(podcast idea)*.
- **Clerks** — one of whom, **Brother-turned-clerk Edmund Pryce** *(new)*, is fussy, honest, and keeps the marriage register.
- **People queuing to be sold on Terms**, paperwork in hand.

**What can happen**
- **IF Cassian walks in** — a Church apprentice can ask to see a register. **The only party member who can.**
- **IF they look for the marriage entry** — the banns are posted here before the wedding; that's how they can learn the date if they haven't.
- **IF they destroy or steal the register** — one of the wedding's five soft points *(Ch8 §11)*.
- **IF they lean on Roke** — he's in Cobb's Ledger, which is in Ironwake's hands now. They need another source, or a bluff.
- **Terms in the flesh** — watch a man sell himself to pay a debt, legally, cheerfully, with a clerk asking how he spells his name. *(Like Cobb with Dov.)*

## 10. The Wedding Venue
**Where Ysabel is married to Mercer Thane.** *(Decisions 2–3: the date, the groom, the priest.)*

Two versions — pick one:

**A. The Chapel of the Plaza** *(new)* — public, small, respectable. A real wedding, with guests who think it's a love match.

> **Read-aloud —** The chapel is full of flowers somebody paid a great deal for. The pews are full of merchants in their best and their wives in Rochefort gowns. At the front, the bride stands very straight in grey silk, and does not look at the groom, who smiles at everyone as though they're all old friends.

**B. Ironwake's house** — private, guarded, with a household chapel. Fewer witnesses, more guards, no illusions.

**Who's there:** **Ysabel**; **Mercer Thane**, courteous and untroubled *(Ch8 §3 — not a brute)*; **a priest** in a Church vestment *(Decision 3 — possibly someone Cassian knows, podcast idea)*; **a clerk** with the register; **Vaskar**, at the back; Ironwake's men.

**What can happen**
- **Five soft points** *(Ch8 §11)* — the ceremony, the groom, the priest, the register, Vaskar. **They only need to break one.**
- **IF the priest objects** — the blessing fails; the marriage isn't lawful.
- **IF the register disappears** — it's not recorded; Ironwake's claim to the estate has no paper.
- **IF Ysabel says no at the altar** — she's brave enough to, if she thinks anyone will catch her.
- **IF the party doesn't come** — it goes through. Maelis, Dov and the mill pass to Ironwake. **Nobody comes back to say it twice.**

## 11. House Dunmoor
**Ysabel's rival — and a knowing Ashgate guarantor.**

> **Read-aloud —** Lady Hesper Dunmoor's dress house is older than Ysabel's and wants you to know it: dark wood, gold leaf, a doorman in livery, and gowns in the window a season behind. Lady Dunmoor herself receives you in a room with no windows at all, which is a choice.

**What it is.** The old house Ysabel overtook in a season. **Lady Hesper Dunmoor guaranteed eleven Ashgate drafts, most recently last spring — and knew** *(`Cobbs Ledger.md` §7)*. Not a believer; paid to be a respectable name.

**Who's there:** **Lady Hesper Dunmoor** — cold, clever, frightened under it. Her seamstresses. Possibly a visitor from Vance & Oltmer (§18).

**What can happen**
- **Ysabel squeezes her first** — she's the obvious name in the book *(Ch8 §3)*.
- **IF Hesper is squeezed** — she leans on Ironwake, as Ysabel planned. ⚠️ **She is also the person most likely to warn Ashgate's people that a book exists** — a second threat to Ysabel, from the cult.
- **IF the party visits her** — she wants to know what Ysabel holds and will pay to find out.
- **Chapter 10:** her name is a thread from Ironwake to the cult's money.

## 12. The Cathedral of the Embodiment
**The great church of Solteres — and the Church that looks the other way.** *(new name)*

> **Read-aloud —** The cathedral fills the north side of the plaza: white stone, a spire you saw from the bridge, and over the doors a carved sun with a man's face in it — the Emperor, as the Church shows him now, crowned and young and looking very much like the coins. Inside it is vast and cold and full of light, and every step echoes. Somewhere a choir is practising the same line over and over. *May he rise again.*

**What it is.** The seat of the Southern Diocese. Named for the doctrine that **the Fredric line is the mortal representation of the eternal Emperor** *(`Doaden - World Rules.md`)*. **It looks the other way about slavery** — Old Cobb wouldn't trust it, and told Erwana to take the book to any Church but this one *(`Cobbs Ledger.md`)*.

**Who's there**
- **Bishop Aurelian Crest** — *optional* *(Decision 54)*: the only Church name in Cobb's Ledger, one Ashgate draft nineteen years ago. Hold in reserve.
- **A diocesan clerk**, **Brother Lucan Feyre** *(new)* — keeps the register of Church personnel passing through. **The man who asks the party for their orders.**
- Pilgrims, beggars on the steps, clergy who don't believe in the Emperor they preach.

**What can happen**
- **The brand encounter** *(Ch8 §4, Decision 4)* — **one, early**: Brother Lucan wants to see their orders, their Redeemer's seal, their mission. **They have none.** He writes down their names. Whether he does anything with them is yours.
- **IF Cassian vouches for them** — a Shepherd's apprentice outranks a clerk's curiosity. Barely.
- **IF they report in, as they once planned** — this is the Church in Solteres. **Consider what Rowan's warning means here** *(`What To Do With Rowan.md` §2)*: don't give the boy's name to a clerk.
- **IF they try to use the Working Ledger as heresy evidence here** — it works, and the seizures follow: **hundreds of collars off, hundreds on** *(`Cobbs Ledger.md` §6)*.
- **IF Cassian prays here** — he finds it too big and too loud to hear anything. He'll want somewhere smaller (§13).

## 13. St. Wystan's
**A small, old church where Cassian can pray.** *(new)*

> **Read-aloud —** St. Wystan's is tucked between a cooper's and a bakery, so the whole street smells of bread and sawdust. It's old — older than the cathedral — low and dim and smelling of candle wax, with worn stone floors and a dozen pews. There is no gold anywhere. A brass sun hangs over the altar, dented on one side. It's empty, except for an old woman sweeping, who nods and leaves you alone.

**What it is.** A parish church that predates the cathedral, named for a soldier saint — *Wystan*, "battle-stone." **It's the kind of place Cassian prays best**: cold, quiet, nobody watching. It looks like the training-house chapel where he met Osric *(`players/Cassian Zev - The Bishop in the Chapel.md`)*.

**Who's there**
- **Sister Agnete** *(new)* — elderly, sweeps the floor, has been here forty years. Doesn't ask questions. Believes, quietly and without politics, in an Emperor nobody at the cathedral believes in. ⚠️ **One of the very few people whose worship actually reaches the true Emperor** *(`The Emperor.md` §3)* — which is why this place feels different to Cassian.
- Usually no one else.

**What can happen**
- **IF Cassian prays with the beads here** — this is where guidance is likeliest to come *(`The Emperor.md` §6)*. Have an answer ready: probably a pull toward **the dress house and Maelis**, which is what the Emperor wants this chapter *(podcast idea)*.
- **IF Osric appears** — this is the place. ⚠️ Only if you choose; he can barely afford it.
- **IF another PC comes with Cassian** — they feel nothing, or something faint and kind. Wolfram, who senses extraplanar presences, **might notice the room is not entirely empty.**
- **A safe place** — Sister Agnete would hide a child, if asked, and tell no clerk.

## 14. The Tavern — The Gilded Hook
**Where Luthien plays, and where Corbin hears him.** *(new)*

> **Read-aloud —** The Gilded Hook sits on the river side of the plaza, with a long bar, a fire at each end, and a crowd of clerks, carters and second sons who can afford it but not much better. It's loud, warm, and smells of beer and wet wool. There's a corner by the fire with a stool where somebody might play, if somebody wanted to. Chalked on a board by the door, in big uneven letters: **SINGING CONTEST — TONIGHT — FIRST PRIZE 10 GOLD.**

**Side fight:** **B — the sore loser** follows the contest.

**What it is.** ⭐ **Tonight the Hook is holding a singing contest — first prize 10 gp** — the landlord's way of filling the room on a slow night. **Instruments welcome**, so Luthien and his guitar qualify. *(DM, 21 Oct 2026: a thumb on the scale to make sure Luthien plays here.)* A mid-priced tavern that will serve the party — **as Ysabel's staff, or with Cassian.** On their own, three branded non-humans get *"we're full"* at any tavern in the city.

**Who's there**
- **The landlord, Hobb Grayle** *(new)* — knows everyone, sells gossip with the ale.
- **Corbin Wainwright** — two rooms away, the night Luthien plays *(Ch8 §10)*.

## A. The Catchers in the Wash
**Where:** the Wash (§20). **Difficulty:** Hard. **Stakes:** a runaway girl.

**The setup.** Slave-catchers have run a girl to ground in the Wash. **Wren** *(new)*, a half-elf, about fourteen, a seamstress who fled **House Dunmoor** (§11) three days ago. She's backed against a shack wall with a fish knife. The Wash has gone silent; nobody dares step in. The catchers carry **a bill of ownership from House Dunmoor**. By Solteres law they're in the right.

> **Read-aloud —** The noise of the Wash stops all at once, the way birds go quiet. In the gap between two shacks a girl with half-elf ears and a seamstress's apron is holding a gutting knife in both hands. Five men have her boxed in. Two have nets. The one in the middle has a paper in his fist and is reading from it, loudly, to nobody: *"— property of the House of Dunmoor, lawfully acquired —"*

**Roster** — 850 XP ×2 (five monsters) = **1,700, Hard**
- **Garr Hask** *(new)*, catcher captain — **bandit captain** (450 XP). Wants her alive; she's worth money undamaged.
- **Four catchers** — **thugs** (100 XP each). Two carry **nets** (thrown, range 5/15, restrains on a hit; DC 10 Strength to escape).

**Tactics.** Nets first, on whoever is quickest — they want everyone tied, not dead. Hask stays out of reach and shouts the law. **Morale:** once Hask drops, or two thugs are down, the rest run. They know the Wash will close behind them.

**IF lines**
- **IF a PC shows the brand** — Hask laughs: *"Church dogs. I've got paper."* The brand is worth nothing to a man whose job is property.
- **IF Cassian invokes the Church's ruling that slavery is illegal** — Hask: *"It says* people*."* That's the whole of Solteres in four words. Watch Cassian.
- **IF they talk instead** — DC 15 Persuasion or Intimidation, or about 30 gp, to buy her bill. Legal, and it makes them her owners.
- **IF Wren is freed** — she has nowhere to go but the Wash, or Ald's Star.

**Payoff**
- **Old Ness and the Wash owe them.** Ness will hide one person later — Rowan, Pip or Maelis — no questions asked.
- **Wren knows House Dunmoor's back rooms**, and she saw **men with scarred hands** come to see Lady Hesper at night. *(Ashgate — `Cobbs Ledger.md` §7.)*

## B. The Sore Loser
**Where:** the alley behind the Gilded Hook (§14), after the singing contest. **Difficulty:** Medium. **Stakes:** 10 gp and a bruised ego.

**The setup.** If Luthien beats **Valentin Sorrell**, House Dunmoor's tenor, Valentin is gracious in public. **His friends are not.** Six of House Dunmoor's footmen and hangers-on wait in the alley to take the prize back and teach the branded half-elf his place. Valentin isn't there. He'd never be seen anywhere near it.

> **Read-aloud —** The alley behind the Hook is narrow, wet, and smells of the kitchen's slops. Halfway down it, six men are waiting with the patience of men who have been paid to wait. One of them wears a footman's coat in Dunmoor green. He holds out his hand. *"The purse. And then we'll discuss your manners."*

**Roster** — 600 XP ×2 (six monsters) = **1,200, Medium**
- **Six thugs** (100 XP each), armed with **cudgels** — play it as a brawl. Nobody needs to die; **knocking out** is the norm (any melee hit can be nonlethal).

**Tactics.** Crowd Luthien. They didn't count on the rest of the party.

**IF lines**

- **IF the party kills one** — it stops being a brawl. A dead Dunmoor footman is a magistrate's matter, and the Assize House (§9) wants names.
- **IF Luthien hands over the purse** — they take it and leave. Pride is the only casualty.

**Payoff.** **Lady Hesper hears** that the branded half-elf who beat her singer can also fight, and so can his friends. She's the person most likely to warn Ashgate *(§11)*.

## C. Tam and the Otyugh ⭐
**Where:** the Marlowe House (§22). **Difficulty:** Hard. **Stakes:** a seven-year-old boy — and Barney's help finding Rowan. *(DM, 21 Oct 2026: expanded, and Tam is rescuable.)*

**The setup.** When the party comes back to Barney's shop in the evening *(§5)*, they learn that **Tam went up a chimney at the Marlowe house today and never came down.** Barney doesn't know what's in there. He's heartbroken, and resigned: there will be another cross on the beam. **He won't ask.** If the party offers, he gives them the key and tells them which flue.

⚠️ **The choice is the scene.** Going after Tam means **delaying the search for Rowan by a few hours**. Let JonLief feel that. *(The real cost is small, because Rowan is safe for the worst possible reason — Vaskar needs him alive. Don't tell them that.)*

**What happened to Tam.** He cleared the upper flues fine. The last flue was the old cellar kitchen's: he went up it from the hearth, the rotten brick gave way, and he fell back down into the flooded cellar — and the thing living there. He got one bite before he scrambled into **the bread oven** beside the hearth: a brick alcove too small for anything big to get into. **He's been in there for eight hours.** Bitten, feverish, coughing, and trying not to.

> **Read-aloud —** The cellar stairs go down into water. It's black, knee-deep and thick with floating rubbish, and the smell is so strong it has a taste. Your light shows a vaulted kitchen: a great hearth at the far end, copper pots green on their hooks, a bread oven like a small dark mouth in the brick beside the fire. Something is coughing in there — small, wet, and stifled. Then the water in the middle of the room moves, though nothing has touched it. And a voice that isn't a voice speaks inside your head: ***food. more food. small food is in the warm box.***

**Roster** — **1,800 XP, Hard**
- **Otyugh** (MM; CR 5) — Large, AC 14, **114 HP**, speed 30 ft, darkvision 120 ft.
  - **Multiattack:** bite and two tentacles.
  - **Bite:** +6, 2d8+3 piercing; DC 15 Constitution save or **diseased** — poisoned, and each 24 hours another save or lose maximum HP until cured.
  - **Tentacle (×2):** +6, 1d8+3 bludgeoning + 1d8 piercing; the target is **grappled** (escape DC 13) and restrained. Each tentacle can hold one creature.
  - **Tentacle Slam:** slams the creatures it's holding into each other or into a wall; DC 14 Constitution save or 2d6+3 bludgeoning and **stunned** until the end of its next turn (half on a success, no stun).
  - **Limited telepathy** (120 ft): it can push simple words and images into the head of any creature that understands a language. **It can't read minds.**

**The room**
- **Knee-deep refuse water** — difficult terrain for everyone except the otyugh.
- **Darkness** — the otyugh sees fine.
- **The bread oven** — Tam fits; nothing Large does. The otyugh can reach one tentacle in, but can't pull him out through the mouth. **Small PCs (Gimble) can get in beside him.**
- **The broken flue** — goes up three storeys to the roof. A way in for Gimble, and a way out for Tam if someone carries him.
- **Copper pots and hooks** — improvised weapons, or noise to draw it off.

**Tactics.** It isn't clever and it isn't evil. It's hungry. It grabs whoever is nearest, slams the two it's holding together, and bites what's stunned. **Below 40 HP it retreats down the collapsed drain** to the river sewer and doesn't come back.

**Make Tam rescuable**
- **He's alive when they arrive**, at 1 HP, diseased, as long as they come that night.
- While the otyugh is fighting the party, **it ignores the oven.** If nobody is engaging it for a full round, it reaches in for Tam: give a PC a reaction or a turn to stop it.
- **Getting him out:** Gimble crawls in and passes him out, or someone carries him up the flue.

**The other way** — otyughs can be **bargained with**. It's telepathic, simple, and hungry. **Offer it more food than a small boy** — a cart of offal from the shambles, the Hook's kitchen slops, the rubbish of a whole street — and it lets Tam go. Luthien or Cassian can "talk" back to it. *(A good option for a party that doesn't want to wade into that water.)*

**After**
- **Tam has the otyugh's disease** on top of his cough. ⭐ **Wolfram's Lay on Hands** can cure it (5 points from the pool) — **a paladin kneeling in a sweep's cellar to heal a Stock child who will never be anyone's priority.** Let that be a moment.
- **Barney**, when they bring Tam home: he doesn't cry, and doesn't thank them in words. He takes his cap off. Then: *"Right. Where do we look for Sprig."*

**Payoff**
- ⭐ **Barney and all his boys join the hunt for Rowan** — for the boy they liked, and for the debt. Rooftops, flues, every house in the wealthy quarter *(§2, §3)*.
- ⭐ *(Recommended)* **Tam saw something before he fell.** From the roofs of Lantern Row this morning, he saw **a face at a nailed-up shutter, three doors down**, and he'd swear it was **Sprig**. *"I waved. He didn't wave back. I don't think he's allowed."* **The rescue of one boy finds the other.**
- **One cross that never goes on the beam.**

## D. Festival Day at the Coliseum
**Where:** the Coliseum (§21). **Difficulty:** Hard (team) or a dangerous duel. **Stakes:** prize money and glory.

**The setup.** With the trial cancelled and the Juggernaut gone, the arena master has an empty slot at the end of the festival card. **Open bouts, all comers, one gold to enter. Bouts end at a fall or a yield — killing is for trials only.** The crowd is drunk, loud, and wants a show.

> **Read-aloud —** A man in a scarlet coat walks out onto the sand and spreads his arms, and the whole bowl quiets. *"The trial is off! The Juggernaut is gone! And so tonight — for the first time in a year — the sand is open to any man, woman or beast with the stomach for it! One gold to enter! Fifty to the team that's standing at the end!"* Somewhere above you, ten thousand people start chanting a number you can't make out.

**Option 1 — The Duel** (one PC). 25 gp purse.
- **Brannoc the Ox** *(new)* — **veteran** (CR 3, 700 XP). ⚠️ **Deadly for one level-4 PC**, which is why the yield rule matters: he wins by putting people on the sand, not in the ground. Wolfram's natural arena.

**Option 2 — The Team Bout** (up to five PCs). 50 gp purse.
- **The Silver Hand** *(new)* — **gladiator** (CR 5, 1,800 XP). **Hard.** A crowd favourite with a shield and a net, who plays to the stands and will happily take the party apart one at a time.

**Tactics.** The gladiator works the crowd: he parries showily, picks off whoever looks weakest, and shoves people out of the ring if there is one. The veteran just hits very hard.

**IF lines**
- **IF a PC wins** — the crowd chants a name. **They need to give one when they enter**, and a branded one is now famous in the city.
- **IF they show the brand on the sand** — every eye in Solteres sees a Penitent fighting for coin. ⚠️ **That spends Vaskar's secret in the loudest way possible** *(Ch8 §4)*, and it's exactly the kind of thing the Church clerk at the cathedral (§12) will hear about.
- **IF Wolfram is the star** — *(proposal)* **the arena master, Bertram Vane, asks him to fight again.** Ironwake isn't looking for a new champion; his arena master is.
- **IF they bet on themselves** — bookmakers give 3 to 1 against strangers.

**Payoff.** Money (25–50 gp, more in bets), the crowd's love, and a public name. A cost too, if they show the brand.
